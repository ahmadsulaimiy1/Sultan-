Our Academic Philosophy

Faith and Scholarship, Held to One Standard

A child raised well in both faith and scholarship will want for neither. That single conviction is the reason Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools exists, and the reason we refused to make families choose between a strong secular education and a serious Islamic one.

So we built five institutions rather than one. Each carries its own age range, curriculum and learning model: a Nursery and Primary School, a secondary Royal College, a School of Islamic and Arabic Studies, a Qur'an College, and a new Online & Distance Learning School. What they share is more important than what separates them. One Board of Governors. One CLEVER standard. One campus in Ikorodu, Lagos that welcomes Muslims and non-Muslims, sons and daughters, alike.

The result is a continuous path. A family can begin with a two-year-old learning to read and to pray in the same week, and stay with us through examinations, memorisation of the Qur'an, and beyond, never once stepping outside a community that holds academic excellence and Islamic character to the same measure.

The CLEVER Standard

One Standard, Held in Six Values

CLEVER is the measure every teacher is held to, in all five of our institutions, from a two-year-old's first morning to the day a student is granted an Ijazah: the classical licence to teach the Qur'an, awarded only after reciting the whole of it from memory to a named scholar.

Creativity

Children are taught to build things. Entrepreneurship, applied technology and problem-solving are timetabled subjects from the early years upward, and Coding sits on the Junior School's own assessment schedule.

Leadership

Prefectships, competitions and service give students the chance to lead early — and the character formation to lead justly, as Islam asks of them.

Engagement

Parents are treated as partners and kept close to the life of their children. Any safety incident, any illness beyond minor first aid, and any substantive disciplinary matter reaches the guardian the same day.

Versatility

Two national curricula, five institutions and flexible timetables let a family shape a path that fits their child — day or boarding, weekday or weekend, on campus or online.

Ethics

Daily Islamic character formation is the ground everything else is built on. Honesty, worship and adab are taught explicitly, modelled by staff, and expected in a child's daily conduct.

Reliability

A verifiable credential system, a published calendar and a governance charter mean families can trust what the school says — and check it.

How the Two Curricula Meet

What Integration Looks Like on a Tuesday

Any school can say it holds faith and scholarship together. The question a sceptical parent should ask is where that claim is written down and who signs it. Three places.

In the published timetable. Take the Junior School's First Continuous Assessment week, issued by the Registrar's Office. Monday assesses Maths, Garment Making and CCA. Tuesday, English & Diction, Business Studies and Social & Citizenship. Wednesday, Integrated Science, Yoruba and Digital Technology. Thursday, History and Coding. Every one of those days also carries Revision periods, a Break & Solat window and Islaamiyyah, printed once at the head of the schedule because they are fixed on every day of the week. Prayer is not a gap the academic day leaves open for it. It is a period in the same document as the examinations.

In the department structure. At Royal College, Arabic and the Islamic Sciences are academic departments, listed and staffed alongside Science & Technology and Mathematics & ICT. They carry their own subject sequence, their own specialist teachers and their own place in the examination calendar. A student strong in Chemistry and weak in Tafseer is a student with a weakness, and is taught accordingly. That structural decision is the school's entire argument, and it is made in a timetable rather than a motto.

In the evening, for boarders. The Qur'an College boarding routine sets a dedicated memorisation and Muraja'ah (revision) session inside the same evening as supervised study hours for a boarder's wider academic work. Both are published to guardians as part of a written daily routine covering wake time, prayer times, meals, study, free time and lights-out.

The assessment week above is published in full on the Academic Calendar; the department lists on the Royal College page; the boarding routine in the Hifz Regulations and the Boarding Policy.

Royal College

The Departments, Named

Royal College teaches the Nigerian National Curriculum from JSS 1 to SSS 3, to the BECE and then to WAEC and NECO. Below are its academic departments and the subjects each one carries. Two of them are Arabic and the Islamic Sciences.

Languages

English and Yoruba, taught to examination standard. French, Hausa and Chinese are planned additions and are named here as future subjects until they are timetabled.

Mathematics & ICT

Mathematics, Further Mathematics, Computer Studies, Data Processing, and Programming and Coding. Coding is a school subject with its own place on the assessment schedule.

Humanities

Geography, History, Government, Civic Education, Art, Literature in English and Social Studies.

Science & Technology

Biology, Physics, Chemistry, Agricultural Science, Food & Nutrition, Technical Drawing, Basic Technology, Home Economics and PHE. Biology and chemistry are taught in dedicated laboratories on campus.

Commerce & Management

Financial Accounting, Commerce, Economics, Bookkeeping, Marketing and Business Studies. Led by Mr. Afolabi Morufu Olalekan, Head of Department.

Arabic

Arabic, Nahwu & Sarfu (Grammar), Aruud (Poetry), Balaghah (Rhetoric), Al-Adab-Al Arabiy (Literature) and Al-Inshaw (Composition).

Islamic Sciences

Fiqh, Usul-Fiqh, Tawheed, Seerah, Tajweed, Hadith, Ulumul-Hadith, Ulumul-Tafseer, Tafseer and Ilmu Qiraat.

Read the Arabic and Islamic Sciences lists beside the others. They have the same shape: a named discipline, a sequence of subjects, a syllabus that runs for years. That is the point. A parent deciding between a strong secular school and a serious Islamic one is being asked here to look at one page and see both, examined on one calendar, under one Principal.

Outcomes

What a Student Leaves With

A school is judged on what its graduates can show, and on how much of it a stranger can check without asking the school’s permission. Four things.

A national qualification. Royal College students sit the BECE at the end of junior secondary and the WAEC and NECO examinations at the end of senior secondary. Those are the same papers, set and marked by the same national bodies, as every other candidate in Nigeria. There is no internal certificate standing in for them, and no separate, gentler track for students who carry a heavy Arabic and Islamic Sciences load.

A scholarly licence, where it is earned. Qur'an College students who hold all thirty Juz', pass the retention-verification period, meet the Tajweed standard and are then examined by named scholars receive an Ijazah. It is signed by the examining scholars and entered permanently in the register under a unique reference number, which is why a graduate can have it verified twenty years later.

A record of competing outside these walls. Five Sultan Hanafi students represented the school at the SPELL Africa International Spelling Leaders Competition and each returned with a medal, gold among them.

A cohort still in the building. Royal College opened in 2021, and a pupil entering at JSS 1 spends six years reaching SSS 3. Aggregate pass rates and destination lists are published once a full cohort has passed through the school end to end, so that the figure describes a Sultan Hanafi education rather than a fragment of one. Until that cohort completes, the Registrar's Office answers the question directly, in writing, for any family that asks.

Body Typeface

The face running text is set in. The serif is the institution’s own; the humanist sans is set slightly wider and suits screens read at a distance.

Leading

The space between lines. Generous leading helps a long passage; tight leading fits more on the screen.

Tracking

The space between letters. Open tracking is recommended for readers with dyslexia.

Text Alignment

Running prose is justified to both margins, as a printed page is. Ragged-right removes the word-spacing that justification requires.

Drop Capitals

The three-line versal that opens a chapter. Some screen magnifiers reflow awkwardly around it.

Arabic Script Size

Arabic carries its meaning in marks that sit above and below the line, and is set larger than Latin at the same nominal size.

Contrast

Raises every text colour, border and rule to the strongest ratio the palette allows.

Links

Underlining every link in running text, rather than only on hover.

Focus Ring

The outline that marks where the keyboard is. A strong ring is recommended for anyone navigating without a mouse.

Interface Sound

The soft mechanical response on buttons, panels and confirmations. Silent by preference, and silent by default on any device that asks for reduced motion.

The Spoken Introduction

The school's recorded welcome, played once on a first arrival and never again in the same visit. It has its own switch, so quietening the interface does not take the voice with it.

Imagery

Lean loads photographs only when they reach the screen and holds decorative layers back — for a slower connection or a metered one.

Read This Page Aloud

Puts a listen control in the masthead. It reads the page's own headings and paragraphs using the voice already installed on your device — nothing is fetched and nothing is sent anywhere — and lights each passage as it is spoken.

Ornament

The engraved furniture — corner rules, travelling-light rings, guilloche, watermarks and letterhead rules. Reduce it for a plainer page; remove it for a document.

Corners

Softened corners read as objects you could pick up; square ones read as printed matter.

Depth

How far the cards and panels stand off the page.

Paper Grain

A fine laid texture across the pale bands, the way a good stock is not perfectly smooth.

Raking Light

The gilt highlight that follows the pointer across headings, rules and figures — the difference between gold-coloured and gilded.

Parallax Depth

How far the layers drift against each other as the page scrolls.

Measure

The width of the reading column. A narrower measure is easier on the eye; a wider one fits more to the screen.

Letterpress
