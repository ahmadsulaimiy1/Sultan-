A Letter to Parents

Why We Built This

Sultan Zakaria, Founder

Since 2016, Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools has taken children from the Imowonla community and given them access to the kind of education that was previously available only to the most privileged families in Nigeria. That sentence is easy to write. Living it, one child at a time, is the actual work.

Every parent who entrusts us with a child is trusting us with something larger than a curriculum — a formation of character, faith, and confidence that will outlast every exam they ever sit. This edition is our attempt to show you, not just tell you, what that formation looks like.

“Together, let us embark on a journey where every child discovers not only what they know, but who they are called to become.”

Zakariya Olanrewaju Anofi · Founder & Head of Schools/Administrator

With an MSc from Edinburgh Business School, Heriot-Watt, a BSc from Oxford Brookes, and fellowships with the ACCA UK and ICAN, our Founder brings the same rigour he learned in finance and governance to the question every parent ultimately asks: will this school actually change my child's life? The chapters that follow are our honest answer.

A Day in the Life

One Ordinary Tuesday

Morning begins the same way for every child on campus, whichever of the five schools they belong to — with the day's first prayer, before the first lesson of any kind. From there, the paths diverge, and that divergence is the point: no two children at Sultan Hanafi walk exactly the same route through the day.

1
The Nursery Child
Play-based learning woven with numeracy, literacy, and Islamic values — a morning built around curiosity, not worksheets.
2
The Basic School Pupil
National curriculum lessons enriched with entrepreneurship and digital fluency, alongside the ICT & Computer Laboratory.
3
The Royal College Student
A full academic timetable across seven departments, from Mathematics to Islamic Sciences, taught by PhD- and PGDE-qualified staff.
4
The Qur’an College Student
Hours of memorisation and Tajwid practice with a Muhaffiz, working steadily toward the next verified Juz’.

The Weekly Rhythm

A Week Has Its Own Shape Too

Beyond the daily timetable, the week carries its own rhythm — anchored by Jumu’ah and the weekend Islamic and Arabic Studies programme.

1

The week's spiritual centre — congregational prayer that reorders everything around it, for students and staff alike.
2
Weekend Islamic and Arabic Studies
A dedicated weekend programme at School of Islamic and Arabic Studies, open to students beyond the Qur’an College's weekday track.
3
The Ramadan Season
The single busiest stretch of the school calendar — culminating in the annual Qur’an Competition at the Royal College Auditorium.

“And say: My Lord, increase me in knowledge.”

Surah Taha · 20:114

Whichever school a child belongs to, this weekly rhythm is the same shared clock every family on campus lives by — a small, steady reminder that faith and learning are never scheduled around each other here. They happen together.

Carrying the Qur’an, One Juz’ at a Time

Daily recitation practice

The Hifz Journey, Stage by Stage

Five Stages, One Destination

1
Enrolment & Assessment
A student's existing memorisation and Tajwid level is assessed before their personalised Hifz plan begins.
2
Foundational Memorisation
Building the first Juz', with daily Muhaffiz-supervised repetition and correction.
3
Sustained Progress
Steady accumulation of verified Juz', balanced against Murajaah — the continuous revision that prevents forgetting.
4
Completion & Review
All 30 Juz' memorised and formally reviewed against the school's Ijazah standard.
5
Ijazah
A certified, permanently recorded credential — verifiable years later, wherever that student's life takes them next.

“Allah will raise those who have believed among you and those who were given knowledge, by degrees.”

Surah Al-Mujadilah · 58:11

No two students move through these five stages at the same pace, and that is by design — the standard is complete, verified memorisation, not a fixed calendar. A student who needs an extra term on Murajaah gets it, without penalty, because forgetting a Juz’ later would cost far more than a slower fourth stage now.

Leadership & Character

Growing Into Leaders

Guided by the CLEVER framework, every student is mentored to lead — not someday, but starting now.

1
Student Representatives
The formal link between the student body and school management — real responsibility, not a ceremonial title.
2
School Prefects
Head Boy, Head Girl, Senior Prefects, and House Prefects — a structured leadership pathway across the Royal College.
3
Class Captains
The leadership tier closest to individual classes — the first point of peer accountability in daily school life.
4
Student Clubs
The broadest tier of the leadership pathway — voluntary, interest-led groups open to every pupil.

The CLEVER Framework

Creativity, Leadership, Engagement, Versatility, Ethics, Reliability — the shared language every teacher uses to describe growth that isn't captured on a report card.

The ALA Endowment Prize

Awarded to the top three academic performers in each class every term, established in 2024 by Mr Lukman Anofi, Board Member, in honour of academic distinction.
