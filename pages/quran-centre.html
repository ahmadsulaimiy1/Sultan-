Digital Qur'an Centre

A Daily Qur'an Companion

Our goal for this space: a student, parent, teacher, or ḥāfiẓ should be able to use SHRS as a daily Qur'an companion — not just a page of links. Here's exactly what that means today, and what a proper build requires next.

The full 604-page Mushaf reader, verse-by-verse audio, and Hifz repeat-loop tools are not live yet. This page is honest about that rather than shipping a reader with fabricated or unlicensed text. Below is exactly what's real today, what the legally-clean path forward looks like, and why it takes real data access to finish — not a Claude Code session working from memory.

What's Real Today

The Adhkār Centre

Morning & Evening Adhkār with a Smart Tasbih Counter, Arabic recitation read aloud via text-to-speech, personal streaks, and achievement badges — fully built and live. See the Adhkār Centre.

Qur'an College's Real Hifz Tracking

Enrolled Qur'an College students already have a genuine Hifz & Ijazah tracker — per-juz' memorisation status, muraja'ah notes, and Ijazah progress — visible to their guardian in the Parent Portal. See Qur'an College and the Digital Campus.

Today's Wird, on the Homepage

A daily "Today's Adhkār" and "Today's Islamic Reminder" (a well-established verse or hadith, with its citation) greet every visitor on the homepage. See Today's Wird.

Why the Full Reader Isn't Here Yet — and What It Needs

Building the reader properly means the actual verse text and audio have to come from a real, licensed data source fetched programmatically — never typed from memory, even for a single well-known verse. That's a firm rule on this site (the same one behind the Personalisation Centre's Verse-of-the-Day: English translation and citation only, never the Arabic script from memory). Research into what a compliant build needs:

Researched

Qur'an Text

Tanzil Project's Uthmani text is available under Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 — verbatim copying and redistribution is permitted with attribution and a link back to tanzil.net. This is the standard source most Qur'an apps use.

Researched

Madinah-Style Typesetting

The King Fahd Glorious Qur'an Printing Complex's own KFGQPC Uthmanic Script font is freely distributed for non-commercial typesetting use — this is how to get an authentic Madinah-Mushaf look. The scanned photographic pages of the actual Madinah Mushaf are a separate matter and are not under a comparably clear open licence — reproducing those specific page images would need direct written permission from the Complex first.

Researched

Recitation Audio

Reciter audio hosted on sites like EveryAyah.com is generally under Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial terms — usable for a non-commercial educational site like this one, with attribution, but each reciter's exact terms should be re-checked individually before enabling their voice specifically.

Blocked, Not Skipped

What Stopped This Build Going Further Today

This working session has no outbound network access to Qur'an text/audio APIs (a sandboxing restriction, confirmed by testing), so the actual verse data could not be fetched and verified here. The reader's navigation architecture (page/juz'/hizb/surah, manual and auto-scroll modes, Hifz repeat-loop controls) is designed and ready to receive real data the moment a session with that access — or the school's own developer — wires in a Tanzil-sourced feed.
