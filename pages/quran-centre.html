The Digital Qur'ān Centre

A Daily Companion to the Book

Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools was founded on the conviction that the Qur'ān is not a subject on a timetable but the discipline around which a life is ordered. The Digital Qur'ān Centre carries that conviction into the instruments a household actually uses: the adhkār of the morning and the evening, the record of a child's memorisation, and the daily portion that opens the school's own front page.

In Service Today

The Adhkār Centre

The adhkār of the morning and the evening, each with its Arabic, its transliteration, its meaning and its citation, and each set to the number of repetitions the Sunnah prescribes. A smart tasbīḥ counter keeps the tally; recitation is read aloud; the household's own consistency is recorded in streaks and marked in attainments. Enter the Adhkār Centre.

The Ḥifẓ and Ijāzah Record

Every pupil enrolled in Sultan Hanafi Qur'ān College carries a formal record of memorisation: status by juz', the teacher's murāja'ah notes, and progress toward Ijāzah. That record is kept by the College and is visible to the pupil's guardian in the Parent Portal, so that a family may follow the journey without having to ask for it. See Qur'ān College and the Digital Campus.

Today's Wird

A daily portion — the adhkār appointed to the hour, and a well-established verse or ḥadīth with its citation — greets every visitor on the school's front page, before any word of admissions or fees. It is placed first because it is first. See Today's Wird.

The Sultan Royal Annual Competition

The institution's own annual competition, held for excellence in the Qur'ān, in the Arabic language, and in the adhān. It is the occasion on which the school measures publicly what it teaches privately.

The Standard This Centre Holds Itself To

A school that teaches the Qur'ān may not publish its text carelessly. Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools will place a complete Muṣḥaf reader in this Centre — the full six hundred and four pages, verse-by-verse recitation, and the repeat-loop instruments a ḥāfiẓ needs for murāja'ah — and it will do so only from a text of established provenance, typeset in an authentic script, and reviewed by qualified scholars before a single āyah is displayed. The standards below are the ones the institution has adopted for that work.

Standard 01 · Text

Provenance of the Muṣḥaf Text

The Arabic text will be taken from the Tanzil Project's Uthmānī edition — the verified text on which the established Qur'ān applications are built, released for verbatim redistribution under Creative Commons Attribution with a link to its source. No text of uncertain origin is displayed in this Centre.

Standard 02 · Script

Typesetting in the Madīnah Hand

The reader will be set in the King Fahd Glorious Qur'ān Printing Complex's own KFGQPC Uthmānic script, which the Complex distributes for non-commercial typesetting — the authentic Madīnah letterform rather than an approximation of it. The photographic pages of the printed Madīnah Muṣḥaf stand under separate terms, and would be reproduced only upon written permission from the Complex.

Standard 03 · Recitation

The Voice of the Reciter

Verse-by-verse recitation will be served from licensed archives, and each reciter's own terms confirmed individually before his voice is carried on this site. A reciter is a scholar, and his recitation is his work.

Standard 04 · Review

Scholarly Confirmation

No portion of the Muṣḥaf enters this Centre until it has been read against a printed Muṣḥaf by the faculty of the School of Islamic and Arabic Studies and the Qur'ān College. Until it is, this Centre carries the instruments of daily practice and directs the reader to the printed Book and to the established Qur'ān applications for the text itself.
