The Class Architecture Proposed — For Institutional Review

Because every age enrols together, Islamiyyah School's classes have never been divided into fixed levels the way the Basic School's and Royal College's have. What follows is a proposed three-stage academic structure — Marāḥil, in the tradition of Saudi Arabia's own Ibtidā'ī–Mutawassiṭ–Thānawī system, adapted for a school a student can join at any age — set out here for the Principal and the Board of Governors to review, not yet an adopted level system.

Foundational Marhalah
1

Arabic Foundations

Letter formation, core vocabulary and simple sentence structure — the first steps toward reading and writing Arabic with confidence.

2

Islamic Foundations

The basic articles of faith (Aqeedah), the pillars of Islam, and a first companion study of short Surahs and their meaning.

Intermediate Marhalah
3

Arabic Grammar & Composition

Nahw and Sarf studied formally; students begin reading connected Arabic texts and writing short compositions (Inshā').

4

Fiqh & Hadith

The practical rulings of worship and daily life, and a structured first study of Hadith and its sciences.

Advanced Marhalah
5

Arabic Literature & Rhetoric

Balāghah, Arabic literature and more demanding composition, carrying a student toward genuine fluency.

6

Seerah, Usul & Advanced Fiqh

The Prophet's biography studied in depth, the principles of Islamic jurisprudence (Usul al-Fiqh), and more advanced questions of Fiqh and Tafseer.

This structure is a proposal, not a currently taught curriculum. It draws on Saudi Arabia's own three-tier Marhalah system and on the subjects Royal College already lists for its Arabic and Islamic Sciences departments, so that nothing above is invented — only the grouping into three stages is new, and it awaits the Principal's and Board's adoption before it is taught as such.

Islamic & Qur'anic Studies

Our Islamiyyah School and Qur'an College adopt the Saudi Arabian educational curriculum, with Saudi-approved textbooks. Extra-curricular activities span sports & games, debate, and Islamic arts & culture.

A Mixed Curriculum Practice

The Royal College and Basic School adopt the Nigerian National Curriculum for the secular programme, infused with entrepreneurial skills, financial intelligence, leadership, broad-based technology, personal development, career planning, and work-ready elective subjects.
