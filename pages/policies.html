Academic

Introduction

At SULTAN, assessment is the process by which the quantity and quality of teaching and learning are measured through various techniques, and organised into interpretable forms across a number of variables.

Objectives
  • Evaluate teaching and learning practice against specific objectives.
  • Give learners regular performance feedback and ownership of their learning.
  • Give parents a clear idea of progress needed.
  • Benchmark against local and national standards.
  • Identify whole-school professional development needs.
  • Ensure underperforming groups are identified for intervention.
  • Enable educators to plan support to fill knowledge gaps.
  • Uphold the school's high expectations and standards.
  • Report clear information to management and the board of governors.
  • Integrate assessment into planning and teaching.
Forms & Techniques
  • Forms: Diagnostic, Formative, Summative.
  • Techniques: Oral questions, classwork, assignments, quizzes, project, test (POP/CBT), exams (POP/CBT).
Feedback
  • Learners get constant feedback.
  • Parents receive broad sheets and oral feedback on Open Day each half term.
  • Report cards issued at the end of every term.
Report Card Components
  • Learner's individual information and attendance record.
  • Continuous Assessment (CA) score — 40 marks; Examination score — 60 marks; Total — 100 marks per term.
  • Cumulative 200 at 2nd term, 300 at 3rd term; class average.

Safety & Welfare

Introduction & Purpose

SULTAN provides adequate first aid to learners, staff, parents, and visitors during accidental or medical emergencies — ensuring safe use, storage, and administration of medicines, and preserving life.

Scope

Covers all members of the SULTAN community, in school and at off-school activities.

First Aiders' Responsibilities
  • Act as first responders; apply appropriate incident responses.
  • Send learners home for recovery if necessary.
  • Complete an accident report form the same day.
Medical Management
  • Handled by trained first aider and school nurse (sick bay).
  • Allergy/medical info disclosed at admission and recorded in sick bay.
  • Medicine must be original-packaged with dosage and name; no written parental permission, no administration.
  • Hospitalisation: parents contacted; staff stays with learner until arrival.
  • Infectious learners excluded unless certified fit by a doctor.
First Aid Kit Locations
  • Medical room/sick-bay · Reception · Sports Hall/field · Kitchen · Dining hall · Laboratories · School vehicles
Personnel & Training
  • Every institution maintains at least one currently certified First Aider present during school hours.
  • First Aider certification is renewed on a defined schedule; a First Aider recognising an incident beyond their training escalates immediately rather than exceeding it.
  • Boarding wardens apply the same procedures overnight, coordinating with the Emergency Response Plan where a nurse or First Aider isn't immediately on site.
Escalation & Transportation
  • Anything beyond minor first aid: school nurse notified immediately.
  • A serious incident (hospitalisation, emergency transport): guardian contacted without delay, with a staff member remaining until arrival or transport.
  • Every response — minor or serious — is logged on an accident report form the same day, without exception.
Conclusion

Final interpretation is the sole responsibility of the Principal, VP Administration, and Registrar.

Introduction & Objectives

SULTAN strives to provide every child with total education in a comfortable, safe environment. Objectives include fostering a safe environment, minimising injury potential, and preparing for and managing hazards.

Likely Hazards
  • Tripping, slipping, falling · unprotected wiring · aggression from visitors/intruders · machinery noise · pests · sunburn · dangerous play · fire outbreak · psychological hazards (bullying, harassment).
Safety
  • Fire-danger education; occasional fire drills and evacuation procedures.
  • Fire-prevention equipment placed and inspected regularly.
  • Clearly indicated fire escapes and safety exits.
  • Weekly walk-arounds by the health and safety committee.
Security
  • Effective learner supervision in and around the school.
  • Vehicle monitoring and documentation.
  • Visitors sign the visitors' book and wear identification badges at all times.
  • Threats professionally dispatched by the security unit.
Environmental & Other Measures
  • Occasional fumigation; high hygiene standards throughout.
  • Facilities for the physically challenged.
  • Managed access to offices, labs, classrooms.
  • PPE and minimal-risk practice for workers/contractors.
Area-Specific Safety
  • Laboratories, sports and physical activity, and boarding houses each carry their own elevated-hazard procedures, layered on top of the standing site inspection.
  • Electrical wiring, food-service hygiene, and contractor PPE are checked as part of the same recurring inspection cycle, not treated as one-time fixes.
Incident Reporting
  • Any hazard-related incident or near-miss is reported the same day to the Health and Safety Committee and logged.
  • The weekly walk-around produces a written, logged checklist result, with any hazard assigned an owner and a resolution deadline.
Conclusion

Correct and final interpretation is the sole responsibility of management staff.

Overview

Visitor safety and site security fall under the Health & Safety Policy's security provisions. Every visitor signs in and out at the security post, and wears an identification badge at all times on the premises.

Visitor Categories
  • Parent Visitor — a guardian of a currently enrolled student; follows the same registration procedure as any other visitor.
  • Contractor / Vendor — engaged for site work or deliveries; issued a distinct log category with a defined access scope agreed in advance.
  • Government Official — a regulatory, educational, or security representative visiting in an official capacity.
  • Emergency Visitor — a medical responder, fire service member, or law enforcement officer attending an active emergency; admitted immediately, logged retrospectively.
Registration & Screening
  • No visitor enters any classroom or office without an identification badge, issued only after registration and a purpose check at the security post.
  • Vehicles coming and going are monitored and documented.
  • Security personnel maintain constant orientation and professionalism.
Restricted Areas & Safeguarding
  • Boarding houses, the medical room, and staff-only offices are never entered by a visitor without specific, logged authorisation from the relevant owner.
  • No visitor is left unsupervised with a student at any time, regardless of category or stated purpose.
  • Visitor photography or recording of students requires explicit, specific authorisation — never a blanket default.
Misconduct & Removal
  • Any visitor deemed a potential threat, or who does not comply with a reasonable security instruction, is professionally dispatched by the security unit.
  • A visitor who refuses to leave when asked is referred to the Nigeria Police Force, consistent with the school's Emergency Response Plan.

Introduction & Purpose

SULTAN protects every child — from age 2 through boarding students at Qur'an College and Royal College — from abuse, neglect, exploitation, and harm, whether the risk originates inside the school, at home, online, from a peer, or elsewhere. Every adult connected to SULTAN has a clear, unambiguous duty to act, without delay, on any concern.

Our Commitment
  • A child's welfare is paramount, above institutional reputation or any relationship.
  • Every adult has an individual duty to report a concern the same day it arises — no one assumes "someone else will."
  • Confidentiality is need-to-know, never blanket; a child raising a concern is never promised secrecy.
  • Safer recruitment applies to every adult with unsupervised access to children, not teaching staff alone.
  • Boarding students, and any concern between peers, are addressed with the same seriousness as any other safeguarding matter.
Responding to a Concern
  • Any adult receiving a disclosure listens, does not promise confidentiality, and does not investigate personally.
  • The concern is reported the same day to the Designated Safeguarding Lead or a Deputy DSL.
  • Where a concern indicates a child may be at risk requiring statutory intervention, the DSL refers to the relevant external authority without delay.
Safer Recruitment & Visitors

Every adult with unsupervised access to children is vetted before appointment. No visitor or contractor is left unsupervised with a child, regardless of sign-in status, per the Visitors Policy (SW-08).

Digital Safeguarding

The Parent Portal and the website's AI assistant are treated as safeguarding surfaces, not just IT systems — access is restricted to a child's own guardians and authorised staff, and the AI assistant is configured to direct any safeguarding concern to a named human contact rather than attempt to handle one itself.

Conclusion

This policy is fully defined and awaiting Board adoption. The Designated Safeguarding Lead role it depends on has been formally framed (see the Designated Safeguarding Lead Framework) but not yet filled — appointing one is the highest-priority action tied to this policy.

Conduct & Discipline

Introduction

A caring, safe, friendly environment is necessary for effective teaching and learning. SULTAN aims to provide such an environment where learners develop in a relaxed, secure atmosphere.

Purpose

Bullying is intentional, repetitive hurting of one person by another, often involving an imbalance of power. At SULTAN, expectations of good behaviour are defined by our Islamic creed. Aims include:

  • Demonstrating bullying is taken seriously and won't be tolerated.
  • Preventing all forms of bullying, in school and off-site.
  • Supporting identification and protection of those bullied.
  • Encouraging learners to speak up rather than suffer in silence.
  • Increasing online safety awareness.
  • Helping bullies understand why they must change.
Scope

Applies to all members of the SULTAN community — learners, staff, visitors, parents, and guardians — in school and at off-school activities.

Forms of Bullying
  • Emotional: exclusion, tormenting, threatening gestures, property damage/theft.
  • Physical: pushing, kicking, hitting, punching, spitting, or any physical violence.
  • Cultural/Racial: racial taunts, gestures, graffiti, stereotyping.
  • Religious: anti-religious/sectarian taunts, gestures, graffiti.
  • Sexual/Sexist: unwanted contact, abusive comments/gestures, rumours.
  • Verbal: name-calling, teasing, mocking, rumours, offensive comments.
  • Ability: targeting special needs or disability.
  • Cyber: misuse of internet/email/chat/mobile/camera to torment or spread rumours.
Disciplinary Consequences
  • Prohibiting derogatory name-calling; requiring genuine apology.
  • Prohibiting adult humiliation of a learner.
  • Immediately correcting behaviour; keeping evidence on record.
  • Official warning; informing parents/guardians; conference meeting if needed.
  • Special monitoring on school premises.
Conclusion

Final interpretation is the sole responsibility of the Principal, VP Administration, and School Guidance Counsellor.

Introduction & Purpose

This Code states, in one place, what SULTAN expects of every student's conduct — in class, around campus, online, and off-site when representing the school — building on the Anti-Bullying & Disciplinary Policy rather than duplicating it.

Expected Conduct
  • Punctual attendance and preparedness for class.
  • Respectful conduct toward staff, peers, and visitors, consistent with our Islamic creed expectations.
  • Honesty in academic work.
  • Appropriate use of personal devices and school digital systems.
  • Care for school property and the shared environment.
  • Off-site conduct at any school-organised activity, or online conduct affecting another student, is treated as on-campus conduct.
A Graded Response
  • Minor misconduct is addressed directly by the class teacher, informally.
  • Repeated or moderate misconduct is referred to VP Administration, logged, and guardians informed.
  • Serious misconduct is referred to the relevant Principal; guardians informed the same day.
  • A restorative conversation is used alongside or instead of a purely punitive consequence, wherever appropriate.
  • Before any suspension is confirmed, the student is given an opportunity to explain their account of events.
Safeguards on Discipline

Searching a student's belongings and any use of physical intervention are both deliberately narrow in scope — used only as a last resort, always logged, and always reported to the Principal the same day.

Appeals

A guardian may appeal a suspension or more serious consequence to the Principal's immediate superior, decided by someone who was not part of the original decision.

Purpose

To establish a standard dress policy across staff categories. Appearance reflects on the school before students, parents, and visitors, and must be clean, decent, and professional, mindful of safety and role.

Scope

Applies to all SULTAN staff — permanent, contract, or otherwise employed.

Policy Elements
  • Male staff: casual corporate wear (chinos acceptable, no jeans), caftan and cap to fit. Female staff: comfortable clothing covering all parts of the body — suits or shirt-and-trousers with a matching tie.
  • Drivers and sanitary officers wear uniforms Monday–Friday.
  • Staff must be clean and well-groomed; religious grooming styles not restricted but worn appropriately.
  • Casual wear (jeans, t-shirts, flip-flops) not permitted Monday–Friday.
  • Clothes must be professional, clean, and free of rips, tears, or offensive stamps.
Responsibilities & Consequences
  • Formal or casual dress permitted Fridays only; formal required other days.
  • Non-compliance: verbal warning, then written warning, then suspension without pay; may lead to termination in severe cases.
Conclusion

Final interpretation is the sole responsibility of the Principal/VP Administration.

Introduction

SULTAN is responsible for equality of opportunity in access and outcome throughout school life, so present and future life chances are widened, not impeded — though equal access does not necessarily mean equality of outcome.

Purpose
  • Communicating commitment to equal opportunity.
  • Eliminating discrimination, harassment, victimisation.
  • Advancing equality between persons who share protected characteristics and those who don't.
  • Ensuring all staff and learners feel valued and reach their potential.
Protected Characteristics
  • Gender · Marital status · Age · Disability · Pregnancy and maternity · Race · Religion or belief
Provision
  • Equal access to educational activities and resources for boys and girls.
  • Raising staff awareness of gender-role stereotyping.
  • Monitoring equipment and teacher-time access across genders.

Introduction

SULTAN works closely with parents and guardians in a partnership that benefits every child. Complaints are handled fairly, openly, and honestly, per national educational guidance and global best practice.

Top Tips for Complaints Handling
  • Be transparent — an accessible procedure increases confidence.
  • Demonstrates commitment to service and readiness to handle complaints.
  • Resolving every complaint through dialogue and mutual understanding.
  • Parents made aware of their right to complain and their options.
Scope

Routine concerns are dealt with through class teacher discussion. Occasionally a concern develops into a formal complaint. Anonymous complaints are discouraged.

Arrangements for Managing Complaints
  • Parents may request a copy of this policy at any time.
  • Principal/VP responds within 24 hours, normally in full within 5 school days.
  • All complaints handled in strict confidence; no effect on how a pupil is treated.
  • Equal rights for both parties; pupils not directly involved in the process.
Role of Principal & Governing Board
  • Principal holds overall responsibility, or delegates while remaining informed.
  • Governing body ensures the procedure is in place and reviewed regularly.
  • A Complaints Committee of three governors considers formal written complaints.
  • Chair of governing body investigates if the Principal is the subject.
Conclusion

Final interpretation is the sole responsibility of the governing board and school management.
