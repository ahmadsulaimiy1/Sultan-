A Word From Our Founder

The Conviction Behind the School

Founder & Director

Dr. Zakariya Olanrewaju Anofi

B.Sc. Applied Accounting (Oxford Brookes University) · M.Sc. Financial Management (Edinburgh Business School, Heriot-Watt University) · FCCA (UK) · FCA (ICAN) · Doctoral Candidate, Edgewood University, Madison, Wisconsin

Governance is what keeps a good intention alive after the person who had it has gone.

On why the Board can overrule him

It is my privilege to welcome you to Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools, a hybrid Islamic and secular institution built in Ikorodu, Lagos State, on a single conviction: that faith and scholarship are one inheritance, not two, and that a child raised well in both will want for neither. As Director, I hold our staff and our institution to that standard in a school that is secure, orderly and genuinely inclusive.

My father, Anofi Aliu Akano, was a clerk at the Nigerian Ports Authority. He did not live to see this school. It was built after he died, in the year my younger brother and I were serving the country under the NYSC, which is to say it was built by two young men with very little, out of an obligation neither of us had to discuss with the other.

The two decades I then spent in banking, insurance, oil and gas, and consulting taught me the unglamorous half of building an institution. Governance is what keeps a good intention alive after the person who had it has gone. An organisation without accounts, minutes and published policy has good intentions and nothing else. That is the discipline I have tried to put underneath this school: a Board of Governors that can overrule me, policies any parent may read in full, and a register in which every result and every certificate we issue can be checked long after all of us are gone.

Our doors are open to Muslims and non-Muslims, to sons and daughters alike. Every educator here is expected to send a child out able to sit a national examination, hold a Qur'anic text in memory, argue a position honestly, and earn a living, and to be judged on whether that happens. You are welcome here: the family enquiring for the first time, the family already with us, and the graduate who has come back to look.

Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools was founded by Dr. Zakariya Olanrewaju Anofi — a chartered accountant — in July 2016.

The school takes its name from the founder's late father, Anofi Aliu Akano — a clerk at the Nigerian Ports Authority whose own education was limited, but who was determined that his children would have the opportunities he didn't. Building the school became a way to honour that legacy after his father's passing, during a period when the founder and his younger brother were serving the nation during their NYSC year.

The idea took shape years earlier, during Lagos Governor Babatunde Fashola's administration, and was carried through with the encouragement of the founder's mentor, Governor Seyi Makinde, his brother Lukman Olajide Anofi, and others. Rooted in the Imowonla community in Ikorodu, the school has grown into a byword for what focused, values-led education can do for an underserved area.

Our Crest

Every Quadrant Tells Part of the Story

A shield is a promise made in shorthand. Ours is quartered, and each quarter names something the school has agreed to be held to.

Crescent & Star

Faith, set at the head of the shield — because everything taught beneath it answers to that first. Islam here is not a subject on the timetable; it is the ground the timetable stands on.

The Open Book

Knowledge, held open. A closed book guards; an open one gives. The device is a standing undertaking that what is known here is taught here — to Muslims and non-Muslims, to sons and daughters, without reservation.

Three Stars

Discipline, character and excellence — three, because not one of them survives alone. A disciplined child without character is merely obedient, and character without excellence asks too little of a life.

The Tree

Growth measured in decades. The tree is the one device on this shield that cannot be hurried — and the school was built the same way: rooted in Imowonla, grown season by season, and intended to outlast the people who planted it.

The shield draws itself as the band is reached. Rest on a quarter — or on its card — and the other lights with it.

Our Foundation

Mission, Vision & the CLEVER Standard

Our Mission

“To provide holistic education by imparting both Islamic and secular knowledge through rigorous research and effective teaching methods — promoting ethical behaviour and instilling strong values in pursuit of a secure, informed, and progressive society.”

Adopted by the Board of Governors, and carried here in the words in which it was adopted.

What that means in practice is a single academic standard, applied without exception. Every subject this school teaches — Mathematics, the Sciences, Information and Communication Technology, the Humanities, Languages, Arabic and the Islamic sciences alike — is a department with named staff, a written scheme of work, and examinations set and marked on the same terms. Nothing is taught as a supplement to anything else, and no subject is graded more gently than another. That parity is the whole of the school’s method, and it is what makes rigorous research and effective teaching a claim the institution can be held to rather than a phrase in a paragraph.

Our Vision

“To be recognised as a leading institution excelling in knowledge dissemination and character building — creating a positive impact wherever our presence is felt.”

Adopted by the Board of Governors, and carried here in the words in which it was adopted.

The institution asks to be measured rather than described, and offers four measures. The first is academic: pupils sit the BECE, WAEC and NECO under the same conditions as every other candidate in Nigeria, on papers set and marked by the same national bodies. The second is what a graduate can carry away and have checked — a national qualification, and, where it is earned, an Ijāzah examined by named scholars, each entered in the register under a reference a stranger can verify twenty years later. The third is how the school teaches: a Digital Campus that puts a pupil’s whole record in their family’s hands, and a governance library published entire. The fourth is what the Imowonla community can do that it could not do before this school stood here.

Pastoral Care & Safeguarding

What Happens on the Bad Day

A school is best judged on the ordinary day something goes wrong. Here is what is in place, and where a parent can read it in full before enrolling.

A Certified First Aider on Duty

A currently certified First Aider is on duty on the campus through the school day, and certification is renewed on a defined schedule — so that “certified” means certified now rather than certified once. A First Aider who recognises an incident beyond their training escalates it rather than exceeding it.

The Sick Bay

Anything beyond minor first aid is escalated at once to the school’s nurse. The campus keeps a sick bay — a room a child can be taken out of class to and watched in, rather than sent home by default — with a first-aid cabinet held to the standard set out in SW-06.

Told the Same Day

Any safety incident, any illness beyond minor first aid, and any substantive disciplinary matter is communicated to the guardian the same day. A parent does not learn about a difficult week at the end of term.

A Policy You Can Read First

The Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy is published in full, alongside the rest of the policy library, so a family can read the standard we hold ourselves to before they trust us with a child.

The Numbers We Are Not Publishing Yet

Royal College opened in 2021, and a pupil entering at JSS 1 spends six years reaching SSS 3. Before You Ask

Questions Families Ask

The seven the admissions desk answers most often — answered here in the same words the school uses everywhere else.

Do you take children who are not Muslim?

Yes. Our doors are open to Muslims and non-Muslims, to sons and daughters alike. The Islamic formation runs through the school day and is not optional, but it has never been a condition of admission.

What does it cost?

The fee schedule is issued by the Bursary for each academic year and given to you in writing for the class you are applying to, together with anything available by way of scholarship or financial support. It is given in writing rather than posted here because a figure left on a website goes stale and a family plans around it.

The admission form itself carries a charge, confirmed by the admissions office. Registering for the Parent Portal is free, and tuition is paid once the offer is in your hands — not before.

What examinations will my child actually sit?

The BECE at the end of junior secondary, then WAEC and NECO at the end of senior secondary. Those are the same papers, set and marked by the same national bodies, as every other candidate in Nigeria. There is no internal certificate standing in for them, and no separate, gentler track for a student carrying a heavy Arabic and Islamic Sciences load.

What do I need to apply?

Three things open your child's admissions file: the birth certificate, two recent passport photographs, and a report or testimonial from any previous school. The application itself is made from a free Parent Portal account, and every stage of it is recorded there — including the decision, whether that decision is an offer, a waitlist place, or a decline.

The seven steps, in full

Can an adult study here?

Yes. The School of Islamic and Arabic Studies is open to the wider Muslim Ummah at any age, adults included. It runs on weekdays and at weekends, teaches to the Saudi Arabian curriculum, and follows the same seven-step digital admission process as the school's other enrolled institutions — see Admission for how it works.

Do you board, and who is awake at night?

Qur'an College takes day students and boarders aged 9 to 16. Full boarding runs Monday to Sunday; half boarding, Monday to Friday. At least one warden is awake and present in each boarding house overnight, with a second reachable within the site, and rooms are checked at lights-out and at least once more during the night.

Boarding, in full — governed by SD-04

How would anyone check a certificate we issue is genuine?

On the public verification portal, with no account and no fee. Any certificate, graduation document, identity card or fee receipt is confirmed against the school's own register. An Ijāzah is signed by the examining scholars and entered permanently under a unique reference number — which is why a graduate can have it verified twenty years later.
