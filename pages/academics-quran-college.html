IV — Day & Boarding · 24–36 Month Programme

Qur'an College

Established to provide a platform where students from all walks of life spend an average of 24 to 36 months completing memorisation of the Qur'an, alongside the science of the Qur'an, Arabiyyah language, and other Islamic knowledge and good morals — culminating in standard Ijazaat (licences/certifications) upon completion, under Principal Imam Ahmad Sulaimiy.

Most Qur'an College students board on campus for the duration of the programme — see the Boarding Facility chapter for eligibility and arrangements.

The Class Architecture

The Hifz Journey, set out class by class: how the published Hifz Regulations' five-stage process maps onto the progression a family can place their child on, from a first correct reading of the Qur'an to an Ijazah.

Foundation
1

Qur'an Reading Foundation Nāẓirah

Correct reading of the Qur'an from the Mus'haf and clear letter articulation (Makhraj), before memorisation begins.

2

Tajwīd Foundation

The rules of precise articulation, length and pausing — inseparable from the memorisation that follows, not a separate subject.

The Ḥifẓ Journey · 24–36 Months
3

Ḥifẓ — Juz' 1 to 10

New memorisation paired with scheduled Murāja'ah; a weekly check verifies what has been memorised is still held.

4

Ḥifẓ — Juz' 11 to 20

The same pace-based method continues; retention of earlier Juz' is checked alongside new memorisation.

5

Ḥifẓ — Juz' 21 to 30

All thirty Juz' completed; the Tajwīd standard is confirmed across the whole memorised Qur'an.

Certification
6

Qirā'āt Advanced · Where Pursued

For students who continue, study of the canonical recitations beyond the standard riwāyah.

✓

Ijāzah

Examination by named scholars, then a licence signed and entered permanently in the register.

The Foundation stage's naming (Nāẓirah) and the Qirā'āt elective are set out here for the first time on this page, alongside the already-published Hifz Regulations facts (24–36 months, pace-based progression, weekly retention checks, named-scholar examination, permanent register). Governed by the Hifz Regulations and Ijazah Governance Framework.

Subjects & Textbooks

Each institution's own chapter above sets out the class levels it teaches and the subjects taught in them, and the syllabus for any year group is issued to its families in full at the start of term. See also our Faculty Directory for the educators teaching them.

Islamic & Qur'anic Studies

Our Islamiyyah School and Qur'an College adopt the Saudi Arabian educational curriculum, with Saudi-approved textbooks. Extra-curricular activities span sports & games, debate, and Islamic arts & culture.

A Mixed Curriculum Practice

The Royal College and Basic School adopt the Nigerian National Curriculum for the secular programme, infused with entrepreneurial skills, financial intelligence, leadership, broad-based technology, personal development, career planning, and work-ready elective subjects.

Qur'an Recitation

Students present what they have memorised in a formal recitation setting — the visible, public-facing counterpart to the daily memorisation and Muraja'ah described in the Hifz Regulations.

The Graduation

The cohort at the close of the programme, with the faculty and the visiting scholars among them. Every student here has completed the memorisation, sat the examination before named scholars, and been entered permanently in the register — Stages 4 and 5 of the journey set out above, arrived at.

The Ijazah and the Prize

The Ijazah is a licence to teach what has been learnt, granted by scholars who have heard the student recite. It is handed over in front of the family, the faculty and the guests, because a thing certified privately is only a document — and what is being marked here took a child between two and three years.
