# The Letterhead — an editorial bible

The standard the school's stationery is held to, and the reasoning
behind each rule. Written because "make it more premium" is not a brief
anyone can execute twice the same way, and because the previous three
attempts each failed in a way that could have been predicted from a
standard and was not.

---

## I. What a letterhead is for

A letterhead is not decoration around a letter. It is the school
**asserting, before a word is read, that this document is authentic and
that an institution stands behind it.** Everything below serves that
assertion or it does not belong on the page.

Three consequences follow, and they settle most arguments:

1. **The letter is the hero; the sheet is the frame.** A letterhead that
   competes with its own text is a failure however beautiful. The eye
   should land on the crest, take the name, and go to the salutation.
2. **Authority is shown by restraint and precision, not by quantity.**
   A passport, a banknote, a university degree: none of them are busy.
   They are exact. Cheap documents are busy *because* they cannot afford
   to be exact.
3. **Every mark must be true.** A letterhead is evidence. An ornament
   may be invented; a fact, a seal, an endorsement or an approval may
   never be.

## II. The three registers, and why plain failed

The first draft was clean, symmetrical, thin-ruled — and it read as a
consultancy, not a school with a charter. That was not a matter of
taste; it was a category error. Stationery sits in one of three
registers and the school must choose deliberately:

| Register | Reads as | Devices |
|---|---|---|
| **Corporate** | competent, current, disposable | hairlines, sans caps, white space, one accent colour |
| **Institutional** | permanent, governed, accountable | crest, engraved rules, small caps, a foot of record |
| **Ceremonial** | commemorative, singular | seals, gilt, borders, script, heavy ornament |

Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools is **Institutional, tending Ceremonial** —
a chartered body that issues certificates, holds children's records, and
answers to a Board. Pure Corporate under-claims it. Pure Ceremonial
turns a routine memo into a diploma, which is ridiculous the second time
you send one. **The target is Institutional with ceremonial density in
the masthead and foot, and complete calm in between.**

## II-b. The palette is chosen, not assumed

Brown-and-gold was never a requirement. It was the *coffee* livery, one
of six the design system already ships — **coffee, royal, garnet,
emerald, sapphire, obsidian** — and defaulting to it made the sheet look
like a cafe menu with a crest on it.

The stationery takes **garnet**: deep oxblood `#3B1420 → #14060A` with
gold `#C9A45E` and an ivory ground. The reasoning is not taste:

- Oxblood is the register of charters, chambers and university presses.
  It reads as *record*, where brown reads as *rustic*.
- It holds gold better than brown does. Brown and gold are neighbours on
  the wheel, so gold on brown goes muddy; gold on oxblood separates and
  reads as metal.
- It is unmistakably not the website's everyday chrome. Stationery
  should be recognisably of the same house and not identical to a web
  page — a letter is a different object.

Sapphire (deep navy) is the legitimate alternative and would read more
newspaper-editorial, less charter. Emerald is available and carries both
Islamic and Nigerian resonance, but sits closer to the crest's own gold
and competes with it.

**The rule this settles:** the palette is a decision with a reason
attached, taken from the school's own livery system, never a default
inherited from whatever was open in another file.

## II-c. The masthead is a band, not a half-page

The single worst fault of the earlier sheets: the identity block —
band, medallion, motto, institutions, microtext — stacked into roughly a
third of the sheet, so the letter began below the fold of its own page.

**A masthead occupies no more than 15% of the sheet.** Measured, on
every version. Everything in it is merged into *one horizontal band*:
crest at the left, wordmark and Arabic name beside it, the place-of-
issue ranged right. One row, not five stacked ones.

What that displaces has to go somewhere honest: the **motto moves to the
foot**, immediately above the record. It is a statement the school makes
about itself, and it belongs with the campus, the telephones and the
verification line rather than in the reader's way.

## III. Materials

The page must read as *stock*, not as screen. Four properties do this:

- **A warm ground, not white.** `#FDFAF3` → `#EFE5D0` on a soft radial.
  Paper is never one flat value; it lightens where light falls.
- **Grain.** A fractal-noise tile at ~50% opacity, multiplied. Below the
  threshold of conscious notice, above the threshold of feeling.
- **Vignette.** A whisper of warmth at the extremes, as a sheet gathers
  shadow at its edges. Never a visible ring.
- **Ink that sits in the paper.** Deep espresso `#1C1207`, never pure
  black. Black is toner; espresso is ink.

**Gold is never flat.** A single hex gold is the surest sign of an
amateur letterhead. Gold is a *gradient through the letterform* —
highlight, body, shadow, return highlight — because foil catches light
along its length. `#FBF0D2 → #E4C88C → #B98F45 → #8A6A2E → #F2DFAF`.

## IV. Ornament vocabulary

Ornament is admitted only if it is **generated by rule**, not drawn by
hand and not licensed clipart. Rule-generated ornament is why banknotes
look like banknotes.

- **Guilloche** — two summed sine waves, phase-shifted 13 times. This is
  how security printing actually rules a lathe pattern. It must be
  *computed*, never a texture image faked over the top.
- **Rosette** — three superimposed rose curves at k = 5, 7, 11. The
  medallion behind the crest.
- **Microtext** — the school's name set at ~2pt, legible only under
  inspection. It is both an ornament and a genuine authenticity mark.
- **Corner filigree** — three nested quarter-arcs with terminal dots.
  Fine enough to read as engraving, never as a frame from a template.
- **The lozenge ◆** — the school's separator. One mark, used everywhere,
  never mixed with bullets, dashes or pipes.

**Forbidden:** drop shadows on text, bevels, glows, stock flourishes,
more than one script face, any ornament whose provenance cannot be
stated.

## V. Structure — the three-band sheet

```
┌──────────────────────────────┐
│  MASTHEAD BAND  (espresso)   │  ceremonial density
│  crown · wordmark · nation   │
├────────── medallion ─────────┤  the crest straddles the edge
│  motto · institutions        │
│  microtext rule              │
│                              │
│         BODY (calm)          │  ← the letter. Nothing decorates it
│                              │     but a single gold drop initial
│                              │
├──────────────────────────────┤
│  FOOT BAND (espresso)        │  the record: campus, telephone,
│  contacts · seal · cipher    │  correspondence, governance, verify
└──────────────────────────────┘
```

**The medallion straddling the band's lower edge is the single most
important move on the sheet.** It breaks the rectangle, gives the crest
physical presence, and reads as an applied seal rather than a printed
logo. Without it the masthead is a coloured box.

**The foot band is not a footer.** It is the *record* — where an
institution states where it can be found and how its documents may be
verified. It carries equal weight to the masthead, because a document
without provenance is a leaflet.

## V-b. Composition — the part the earlier drafts had no answer for

Principles alone produced three symmetrical, centred, static sheets.
Symmetry is the default a machine reaches for; it is *balance without
tension*, and tension is what makes a page look designed rather than
assembled. This section is the missing system.

### The grid

Twelve columns across 210mm, 12mm gutter, with a **7mm side rail** taken
off the left edge before the grid starts. Nothing but the rail lives in
that 7mm. The body sits on columns 2–12.

### Five devices that put movement into a still page

1. **The side rail.** A garnet strip the full height of the sheet
   carrying the issuing office in 5pt gold caps, rotated. It costs
   nothing — that margin was dead — and it turns a rectangle of text
   into a page with an edge and a spine.
2. **Asymmetry.** The masthead panel spans roughly two-thirds, not the
   full width. The remaining third is paper carrying the place of issue
   in dark type. Full-width bands are stable and dull; a panel that
   stops has direction.
3. **The angled edge.** The masthead's lower boundary is cut on a
   shallow diagonal (about 1.4°), mirrored inverted at the foot. Almost
   subliminal, and the single cheapest way to stop a layout reading as
   a stack of boxes.
4. **Overlap.** The crest disc straddles the panel's edge — half on
   garnet, half on paper. Overlap creates depth; depth reads as physical.
5. **One focal accent.** Exactly one element may be the brightest thing
   on the sheet. Here it is the crest disc. Everything else defers.

### Icons — required, and specified

A modern flagship letterhead marks its contact details. Absence of icons
is not restraint; it is an unmade decision.

- **Inline SVG only.** No icon font, no image, nothing licensed.
- **Line, not solid.** 1.1px stroke on a 24px box, round caps and joins.
- **Gold `#C9A45E` on the bands, garnet `#7A2E3E` on paper.**
- **4.4mm square**, optically centred to the cap-height of the text
  beside them, never to its bounding box.
- **Five, and only five**: pin (campus), telephone, envelope
  (correspondence), globe (web), seal (verification). A sixth icon means
  an unnecessary line of foot copy.

Icons never appear in the body of a letter. A letter is prose.

### Spatial rhythm

One unit = 3.2mm. Every vertical gap is a whole multiple: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8.
Arbitrary gaps are what make a page feel assembled. If a gap needs to be
4.7mm, the composition is wrong, not the number.

### What the sheet must never do

- Begin the letter on the second page. **A letter starts on page one.**
  The blank sheet is a separate artefact for a separate purpose and is
  delivered as its own file — it is not the cover of a letter.
- Repeat the ceremonial masthead on continuation sheets.
- Centre everything. Centring is the absence of a decision.

## VI. Typography

| Role | Face | Size | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wordmark | Cinzel 800 | 19pt | 0.086em, gold gradient, **never wraps** |
| Arabic name | Amiri 400 | 11.6pt | RTL, beneath the wordmark always |
| Crown / nation | Inter 400 | 6–6.1pt | 0.27–0.42em caps — extreme tracking is the ceremonial signal |
| Motto | Cormorant Garamond 500 italic | 12.8pt | the one voice where the school speaks of itself |
| Institutions | Inter 400 | 6.4pt | 0.15em caps, lozenge-separated |
| Subject | Cinzel 700 | 10.2pt | caps, ruled beneath |
| Body | Inter 400 | 9.7pt / 1.68 | justified, hyphenated |
| Drop initial | Cinzel 700 | 23pt | gold, first paragraph only, once per letter |

**Tracking carries the ceremony.** 0.27em on the nation line does more
for authority than any ornament, and costs nothing.

## VII. Colour — garnet

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | `#1A1116` | body |
| Band | `#3B1420 → #14060A` | masthead and foot |
| Gold | `#C9A45E` | rules, terminals, foil |
| Garnet | `#7A2E3E` | small caps, drop initial, rules on paper |
| Cream | `#EDDCC0` | text on the bands |
| Ground | `#FFFEFC → #F1EADD` | the sheet |

CMYK-safe by construction: no saturated RGB gold, which prints green.

## VIII. Rules that are not negotiable

0. **The Arabic name is مدارس السلطان حنفي الملكية** — plural, with the
   article, exactly as the school's own Arabic pages set it 49 times.
   The singular مدرسة names one school; this house has five.
1. **The wordmark never wraps.** Assert it — measure `scrollWidth`
   against `clientWidth`.
2. **Nothing overlaps the nation line.** The medallion's top edge must
   sit below its baseline. Assert it.
3. **Every footer sits inside its page.** Assert it, on every sheet.
4. **The letter is never condensed to fit.** If it runs to seven sheets
   it runs to seven sheets. Re-paginate at a change of subject; never
   trim a fact to save a line.
5. **Continuation sheets carry a lighter masthead** — crest, name,
   office — never the full ceremonial band. Repeating the full masthead
   on page four is what a template does, not an institution.
6. **No placeholder that a person must fill by hand** where the system
   already holds the value. If the number exists in the database, the
   document fetches it.

## IX. What may never appear on this letterhead

Stated because they were asked for, and because a letterhead is
evidence:

- **The Coat of Arms of the Federal Republic of Nigeria.** It is the
  mark of the Federal Government, protected under the Flags and Coat of
  Arms Act. On a private school's stationery it asserts that this
  document issues from the State. The school's own crest is the correct
  and sufficient mark of authority.
- **"Approved by the Lagos State Ministry of Education", or any
  ministry, board or examination-body mark**, unless the school holds
  that approval and supplies the certificate number, which is then
  printed *with* its number. This project's own audit records
  accreditation as "the sharpest real gap — no visible
  inspection/accreditation history". A claim of approval on a letterhead
  is a claim made to every parent who receives one.
- **Any award, ranking, affiliation or partner mark** not evidenced.

What *is* legitimate, and already present: **Ikorodu ◆ Lagos State ◆
Federal Republic of Nigeria** — a statement of where the school is,
which is true, carries the geographic dignity, and claims nothing.

If the school holds a Ministry approval number, give it and it goes on
the foot band the same day, set in the record where it belongs.

## X. Acceptance tests

A sheet ships only when all ten pass, measured — never eyeballed:

1. Wordmark on one line at 210mm.
2. Nation line clear of the medallion.
3. Every footer inside its page, every sheet.
4. All faces confirmed loaded at render (7+ faces).
5. Page measures exactly 794 × 1123 px (A4 at 96 dpi).
6. Body ink ≥ 12:1 against the paper at its lightest point.
7. Cream on band ≥ 7:1.
8. No console errors.
9. No page-break inside a signature block.
10. No placeholder remaining that the system could have filled.
11. Masthead ≤ 15% of sheet height.
12. Drop initial on the opening sentence of the letter — never on the
    address block. `:first-of-type` catches the addressee; use a class.
13. The letter begins on sheet one. Blank stationery ships as its own file.
14. Five contact icons present, inline SVG, optically aligned to cap-height.
15. Every vertical gap is a whole multiple of 3.2mm.
