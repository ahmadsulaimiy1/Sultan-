# STATUTE OF THE BOARD OF GOVERNORS

**Made under Article 163 of the Governance Charter of Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools, operationalising Chapter III.**

**Status.** This Statute has been prepared for the Board of Governors' consideration and approval under Article 163 of the Governance Charter. It carries no legal effect until approved by the Board and recorded in the Authority Register.

**Classification.** Public — the Institution's governance-transparency practice, established across every Public-classified document in this governance library, extends to how the Board itself conducts its business, not only to what it decides.

---

## Section 1 — Citation, commencement, and authority

1.1. This instrument may be cited as the Statute of the Board of Governors.

1.2. This Statute is made by the Board of Governors under Article 163 of the Governance Charter, which names "a Statute of the Board of Governors" as its own worked example of what a Statute is for.

1.3. This Statute takes effect on the date of its approval by the Board, recorded in the Authority Register under Article 16 of the Governance Charter.

1.4. This Statute operationalises Chapter III of the Governance Charter (Articles 28–49D) at the level of procedural detail Article 166 keeps out of the Charter's own text. It creates no power, office, or authority the Charter does not itself establish, per Article 163's own constraint, and is void to the extent of any inconsistency with the Charter, per Article 162.

1.5. This Statute does not restate, and should not be read as displacing, the individual terms of reference of any Board Standing Committee, which remain governed by the Schedule of Board Standing Committees in Charter Chapter XI and by the Board Committee Handbook once that document exists. This Statute governs the Board's own general meeting procedure; committee-specific procedure is a distinct instrument, deliberately not duplicated here.

## Section 2 — Definitions

2.1. Terms defined in Article 4 of the Governance Charter carry the same meaning in this Statute.

2.2. In this Statute, unless the context otherwise requires:
(a) "Meeting" means an ordinary meeting (Article 41), an extraordinary meeting (Article 42), or an emergency resolution process (Article 43) of the Board;
(b) "the Secretary function" means the administrative role, held by a person the Founder & CEO designates, responsible for meeting logistics, papers circulation, and minute-keeping under this Statute — a Regulation-level administrative role, not itself a constitutional office;
(c) "Papers" means the agenda and every document circulated in support of it under Article 41.

## Section 3 — Agenda-setting

3.1. The Chair settles the agenda for each Meeting, in consultation with the Founder & CEO and the Governance & Nominations Committee, per Article 39A.

3.2. Any Governor may submit an item for the agenda by written notice to the Secretary function not less than fourteen days before an ordinary Meeting, or not less than three days before an extraordinary Meeting convened under Article 42. The Chair shall include a validly submitted item on the agenda unless the Chair records, in writing to the submitting Governor, a specific reason it is more appropriately addressed by a Board Standing Committee or another body under this Charter.

3.3. The agenda for an ordinary Meeting shall follow this standing order of business, save where the Chair varies it for a stated reason recorded in the minutes: (a) confirmation of quorum; (b) apologies and declarations of interest under Article 132; (c) confirmation of the minutes of the previous Meeting; (d) matters arising; (e) reports from Board Standing Committees; (f) the Founder & CEO's report under Article 55; (g) matters requiring Board approval under Article 46; (h) matters reserved exclusively to the Board under Article 45; (i) any other business validly submitted under Section 3.2; (j) date of next Meeting.

3.4. Papers shall be circulated not less than seven days before an ordinary Meeting, per Article 41, and as far in advance as is practicable before an extraordinary Meeting, given Article 42's shorter notice period.

## Section 4 — Virtual and hybrid meetings

4.1. A Meeting may be held wholly or partly by durable, real-time electronic means (video or telephone conference), at the Chair's discretion or on the request of any Governor unable to attend in person, provided every participating Governor can hear, and be heard by, every other participating Governor throughout the Meeting.

4.2. A Governor participating by electronic means under this Section is present for the purposes of quorum under Article 39 and entitled to vote under Article 40, on the same footing as a Governor physically present.

4.3. Where a Meeting is held wholly or partly by electronic means, the Secretary function shall record in the minutes which Governors attended in person and which by electronic means, and shall confirm, before any vote, that every participating Governor's connection is functioning adequately for that Governor to participate meaningfully in the matter being decided.

4.4. This Section does not apply to an emergency resolution under Article 43, which already has its own durable-electronic-communication mechanism stated directly in the Charter.

## Section 5 — Conduct of business

5.1. The Chair opens each Meeting by confirming quorum under Article 39, recorded in the minutes by name.

5.2. Where the Chair is absent, has declared a conflict of interest under Article 132 on a matter before the Meeting, or the office of Chair is vacant, the Vice Chair presides for that Meeting or that matter, per Article 39A; where both the Chair and Vice Chair are absent or conflicted, the Governors present shall elect one of their number, from among the Independent Governors present, to preside for that Meeting only, recorded in the minutes.

5.3. A vote is taken under Article 40. The person presiding announces the result and whether the resolution is carried, and the Secretary function records the result, and, where a Governor exercises the right under Article 40 to have a dissent or an abstention recorded, that fact and any stated reason.

5.4. A matter on the agenda that is not concluded within the time allotted for the Meeting may be adjourned to a stated date by resolution of the Governors present, without requiring the fourteen-day notice Article 41 requires for a fresh ordinary Meeting, provided the adjourned Meeting continues only the unconcluded business of the original agenda.

## Section 6 — Minutes

6.1. The Secretary function prepares a draft minute of each Meeting, recording: attendance (including by electronic means, per Section 4.3); declarations of interest and any recusal; each resolution and its result; any dissent or abstention recorded under Section 5.3; and any emergency action reported under Article 21 or Article 149.

6.2. The draft minute shall be circulated to every Governor who attended within fourteen days of the Meeting, and confirmed (with any correction) at the next ordinary Meeting under Section 3.3(c).

6.3. Once confirmed, the minute is signed by the person who presided at the Meeting it records, and filed in the institutional archive under Article 134 of the Governance Charter. A minute, once confirmed and signed, is not itself further amended; a later correction is recorded as a new minute entry referencing the one it corrects, consistently with the Authority Register's own append-only principle under Article 16.

6.4. A Governor is entitled to inspect the confirmed minutes of any Meeting at which that Governor was entitled to be present, subject to the confidentiality obligations in Article 49C.

## Section 7 — Governor induction

7.1. On appointment, and before that Governor's first vote on any matter other than confirmation of their own appointment (per Article 48), the Governance & Nominations Committee shall provide the new Governor with an induction pack comprising: the Governance Charter in full; this Statute; the Board Code of Conduct once that document exists; the minutes of the Board's most recent four ordinary Meetings; the current skills matrix under Article 48; and a briefing, from the Chair or the Chair's delegate, on the Institution's safeguarding obligations specifically.

7.2. The new Governor's written constitutional affirmation under Article 170 is administered as part of induction, and its completion (or, per Article 170, a report to the Governance & Nominations Committee of its non-completion within thirty days) is recorded in the Authority Register.

## Section 8 — Records

8.1. Every appointment, resolution, delegation, and emergency action of the Board is recorded in the Authority Register under Article 16, in addition to (not instead of) the minutes this Statute requires under Section 6.

8.2. The Secretary function maintains a standing index of every Board Standing Committee's own meeting dates and key resolutions, cross-referenced to the Committee's own minutes, so the Board's own oversight of its committees (Article 96) can be exercised from a single, current record rather than requiring a fresh request to each Committee before every ordinary Meeting.

## Section 9 — Relationship to other instruments

9.1. This Statute is subordinate to the Governance Charter, and, in particular, does not derogate from Articles 39–43 (quorum, voting, meeting notice) or Article 49C (confidentiality).

9.2. This Statute should be read alongside the Board Committee Handbook and the Board Code of Conduct once those documents exist (Phase Two of the Institution's documentation programme); neither supersedes this Statute's own treatment of general Board meeting procedure, and this Statute does not attempt their more specific subject matter.

## Section 10 — Amendment and review

10.1. This Statute may be amended by the Board by the ordinary majority required for a Statute under Article 163.

10.2. This Statute shall be reviewed not less than once every two years, alongside the Board's own self-evaluation under Article 49.

## Section 11 — Interpretation

11.1. This Statute is interpreted consistently with Article 9 of the Governance Charter. Where a genuine ambiguity arises that this Statute's own terms do not resolve, the Board resolves the question under Article 155.

## Drafting & audit record

**Legal audit.** Every Charter cross-reference (Articles 4, 9, 16, 21, 39, 39A, 40, 41, 42, 43, 45, 46, 48, 49, 49C, 55, 96, 132, 134, 155, 162, 163, 170) was checked against the current Charter text before this Statute was considered complete. Section 4 (virtual/hybrid meetings) was checked specifically against Article 39's quorum rule and Article 40's voting rule to confirm that treating electronic participation as full presence and full voting entitlement does not conflict with either — it doesn't: neither Article requires physical presence, and Article 39's own text already contemplates the Founder & CEO/Acting Founder & CEO quorum condition being satisfied without specifying a mode of attendance. Section 5.2's presiding-Governor fallback (an elected Independent Governor where both Chair and Vice Chair are absent or conflicted) was checked against Article 39A, which addresses the Chair-absent and Chair-conflicted cases but not the case where the Vice Chair is *also* unavailable — a genuine small gap in the Charter this Statute closes at the Statute level, appropriately, since it is exactly the kind of operational contingency Article 166 assigns to a Statute rather than the Charter itself, and does not purport to create new authority so much as specify who exercises existing Article 40 voting-chair function when the Charter's own two named office-holders are both unavailable.

**Governance audit.** Checked against Article 163's constraint that a Statute create no new power: Section 4's virtual-meeting provision doesn't grant a new kind of Board authority, only specifies the mode by which existing quorum and voting rules under Articles 39–40 are satisfied. Section 3.2's right for any Governor to submit an agenda item, with the Chair's power to decline limited to a stated, recorded reason, was checked against Article 39A's grant of agenda-setting to the Chair "in consultation with the Founder & CEO and the Governance & Nominations Committee" — this Statute doesn't remove the Chair's agenda-setting authority, it constrains how that authority is exercised (a reasoned, recorded decision rather than an unreviewable one), which is squarely the kind of procedural detail a Statute may add without conflicting with the Charter's grant of the underlying power.

**Editorial audit.** Checked for terms used without definition (none), for consistency between this Statute's own Section numbering and its internal cross-references (confirmed), and for scope creep into Board Committee Handbook or Board Code of Conduct territory (checked and avoided — Section 1.5 and Section 9.2 state the boundary explicitly rather than leaving it to be inferred).

**Publication audit.** Same standard and same deferred-typesetting decision as the Statute of the Founder's Reserved Rights (see that document's own audit record and the Phase One planning note, Section 6). Classification: Public, consistent with this Institution's general practice of publishing its own governance-transparency documents.
