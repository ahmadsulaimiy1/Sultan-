# PHASE ONE — CONSTITUTIONAL FOUNDATION: COMPLETION NOTE

*Prepared per the Staged Drafting Directive, on completion of the three documents identified as Phase One's real work in `phase-one-constitutional-foundation-plan.md`. This is a status note, not part of the Charter or any Statute, and carries no legal effect.*

---

## 1. What was planned, and what was actually drafted

The directive proposed five items for Phase One. The planning note (`phase-one-constitutional-foundation-plan.md`, Section 1) checked each against the Charter's actual text before drafting began, rather than executing the list mechanically, and found:

| Proposed item | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Governance Charter (refine and finalise) | Already complete — full Constitutional Review conducted in the preceding round of this engagement; nothing further open except Board adoption |
| Constitution of SHRS | Correctly deferred — premature per Article 178 and prior analysis |
| Statutes | **Drafted — this phase's real work, all three complete** |
| Schedule of Reserved Powers | Not drafted — already exists in full as Articles 14 and 45 |
| Interpretation & Definitions Schedule | Not drafted — already exists in full as Articles 4 and 9 |

Three Statutes were drafted, in the sequence the planning note set out, each audited before the next began:

1. **Statute of the Founder's Reserved Rights** (`statute-founders-reserved-rights.md`) — operationalises Article 27D. Flagship quality. Classification: Confidential.
2. **Statute of the Board of Governors** (`statute-board-of-governors.md`) — operationalises Chapter III. Flagship quality. Classification: Public.
3. **Statute of the Executive Management Team** (`statute-executive-management-team.md`) — operationalises Chapter IV. Standard quality, shorter, per the planning note's own reasoning that Chapter IV's simpler constitutional design (no committees, no supermajority thresholds, no succession machinery of its own) gives it genuinely less procedural ground to cover. Classification: Internal.

## 2. What the audits actually found

Each Statute carries its own "Drafting & audit record" section (Legal / Governance / Editorial / Publication) at the end of the document itself, rather than in a separate file, so the record travels with the instrument it concerns. The one substantive finding worth restating here, because it was a real error and not a formality: the first draft of the Statute of the Founder's Reserved Rights, Section 3.3, incorrectly stated that the Founder's Article 27D entitlement survives an Article 23A removal from the office of Chief Executive Officer untouched. Checking Article 27C(c)'s actual text against that claim — during the Statute's own mandated legal audit, before it was presented as complete — showed the opposite: Article 27C(c) expressly lists "the entitlements reserved under Article 27D" among the powers Article 27C reserves to the Founder, and Article 27C's suspension clause applies to every power so listed without exception. Section 3.3 and the related Section 6.1 were corrected before delivery. No other substantive error was found in any of the three Statutes; the remaining audit findings across all three documents concerned scope boundaries (what a Statute may and may not do under Article 163) and internal consistency, and confirmed the drafts rather than changing them.

## 3. What was deliberately not built

Per the planning note's Section 6, no bespoke HTML/PDF publication pipeline was built for these three Statutes individually. "Flagship publication standard" for a Statute in this phase meant drafting rigour and a full internal audit, not the Charter's own ceremonial typesetting apparatus — that decision was stated explicitly in the planning note before drafting began, and is restated here because it bears on what "complete" means for this phase: these three documents are legally and editorially complete and ready for Board consideration; they are not yet typeset for ceremonial publication, and building that typesetting is deferred to a future shared Policy Compendium / Statute Register pipeline, when one exists to serve the whole Statute and Policy library at once rather than one document at a time.

## 4. Outstanding gap not resolved by this phase

The planning note (Section 2) flagged that neither the Board nor the EMT had a procedural Statute before this phase began — both bodies were operating on constitutional principle alone. That gap is now closed for both bodies. No further gap of this kind was identified during drafting.

## 5. Status

Phase One is complete: three Statutes drafted, each individually audited, none outstanding from the phase's real (post-verification) scope. Consistently with the directive's own staged-process instruction — Phase Two does not begin until Phase One has been internally audited, which it now has been — these three documents are ready for delivery and for the Board's own consideration under Article 163, and Phase Two (Board of Governors Standing Orders, Board Committee Handbook, Executive Management Team Charter, Shariah Advisory Council Charter, Quality Assurance Charter, Internal Audit Charter, Risk Management Framework) has not been started.
