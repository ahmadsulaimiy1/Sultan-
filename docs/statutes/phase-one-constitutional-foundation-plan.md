# PHASE ONE — CONSTITUTIONAL FOUNDATION: PLANNING NOTE

*Prepared per the Staged Drafting Directive, before any Phase One document is drafted. This is a planning document, not part of the Charter or any Statute, and carries no legal effect.*

---

## 1. Every document proposed for this phase, and whether it actually belongs here

The directive proposed five items. Checked one by one against what already exists in this governance library — not accepted as a list to execute mechanically:

| # | Proposed item | Belongs in Phase One? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Governance Charter (refine and finalise) | **Already done — no further drafting action open** | GV-01 went through a full independent Constitutional Review in the immediately preceding round of this engagement: 10 Critical and 4 Important findings fixed directly in the text (broken cross-references, a real quorum deadlock, an entrenchment workaround, a safeguarding-committee quorum gap, and others — see `docs/shrs-governance-charter-constitutional-review.md`). What remains is Board adoption, which is a Board action this drafting process cannot perform, not a drafting gap. |
| 2 | Constitution of Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools | **Not recommended for this phase — genuinely premature** | The Charter's own Article 178 states plainly that a future Constitution is a broader instrument (governing academic life, students, community relationship — not governance alone), that nothing creates an obligation to adopt one by any particular date, and that the Charter remains supreme "until superseded." This was already checked once, in the Institutional Documentation Framework round, and reached the same conclusion; nothing about SHRS's circumstances has changed since then. Drafting a Constitution now, with no broader academic-life instrument actually needed yet, would be exactly the "unearned institutional structure" this project's own drafting notes have repeatedly declined to create elsewhere (Note 2 of the Charter's Editorial Record: inventing an office or instrument with no real function behind it). |
| 3 | Statutes | **Yes — this is the phase's real, substantive work** | Charter Article 163 already establishes Statutes as instruments beneath the Charter, made by Board approval, "governing the detailed constitution and operation of a single organ established by this Charter." None has yet been made. This is a genuine, checkable gap — see Section 3 below for which ones and in what order. |
| 4 | Schedule of Reserved Powers | **Not recommended as a separate document — already exists in substance** | Charter Article 14 names the Founder & CEO's reserved powers exhaustively; Article 45 names the Board's reserved-and-non-delegable matters exhaustively. A standalone "Schedule of Reserved Powers" would either restate that text (creating a second copy that drifts the moment either is amended without the other — exactly the two-sources-of-truth problem the Constitutional Review round worked to eliminate elsewhere in the Charter) or attempt to state something new, which Article 163 itself forbids a Statute from doing ("shall not create... a power this Charter does not itself establish"). This was flagged once already, in the Master Documentation Blueprint, and is reaffirmed here rather than quietly reversed now that drafting is actually starting. |
| 5 | Interpretation & Definitions Schedule | **Not recommended as a separate document — already exists in substance** | Charter Article 4 is already a full, twenty-eight-item defined-terms article; Article 9 is already the Charter's own interpretive-principles article. A second schedule restating either would create the same drift risk as item 4 above, for the same reason. |

**Net effect:** of the five proposed items, one is complete, one is correctly deferred, one is genuinely still needed and is this phase's actual work, and two are not needed as separate documents because they already exist, in full, inside the Charter itself. This is not a smaller phase for convenience — it is what checking the proposed list against the real document found.

## 2. Constitutional or governance gaps identified before drafting begins

Beyond the five items above, two things are worth naming before Phase One's drafting starts, because they bear directly on what gets drafted and in what order:

- **Article 27D of the Charter already names the precondition this phase must resolve first.** The Founder's revenue entitlement "is fixed from time to time by Statute under Chapter XX" — the Charter's own text states that this specific power cannot lawfully be exercised until that Statute exists. This makes the Statute of the Founder's Reserved Rights the single most constitutionally necessary document in this entire phase, not a discretionary addition — it was already identified as such in the Master Documentation Blueprint, and this plan confirms and acts on that finding rather than restating it as background colour.
- **No Statute yet exists for either constitutional body the Charter itself expects one for.** Chapter III (the Board) and Chapter IV (the EMT) are both fully constituted in the Charter's own text, but both explicitly rely on Article 166's principle that operational procedure belongs in a Statute, not the Charter — meaning both bodies are currently operating on constitutional principle alone, with no procedural instrument beneath either of them. This is a real, if lower-urgency, gap.

## 3. Dependency order

```
Governance Charter (GV-01) — in force once adopted
        │
        ├──▶ Statute of the Founder's Reserved Rights
        │    (operationalises Art. 27D directly; depends on nothing else in this
        │    phase — it can be made the moment the Charter itself is in force)
        │
        ├──▶ Statute of the Board of Governors
        │    (operationalises Charter Ch. III; independent of the EMT Statute)
        │
        └──▶ Statute of the Executive Management Team
             (operationalises Charter Ch. IV; independent of the Board Statute,
             but references the Board Statute's meeting-papers convention for
             consistency, so is sequenced after it)
```

None of the three Statutes depends on either of the other two for its own validity — each derives its authority directly from the Charter (Article 163), not from a sibling Statute. The sequencing below is about drafting priority, not legal dependency.

## 4. Recommended drafting sequence

1. **Statute of the Founder's Reserved Rights** — drafted first, because it is the one Article 27D itself names as a precondition for a power already granted; every day it doesn't exist is a day that power sits un-exercisable by the Charter's own design.
2. **Statute of the Board of Governors** — drafted second, because Phase Two's own first item (Board Standing Orders) sits beneath this Statute, and Phase Two should not begin on top of an unwritten foundation.
3. **Statute of the Executive Management Team** — drafted third, for the equivalent reason relative to Phase Two's Executive Management Team Charter.

## 5. Flagship-quality designation

- **Statute of the Founder's Reserved Rights** — flagship quality. It touches the Founder's own personal entitlement directly, interacts with real financial governance (Charter Ch. XV, the drafted Financial Controls Policy FN-01), and is the phase's single highest-stakes document.
- **Statute of the Board of Governors** — flagship quality. It is the operational foundation every Board Standing Committee, every Board Statute that follows it, and Phase Two's Board Standing Orders will all sit on.
- **Statute of the Executive Management Team** — standard quality, shorter. The EMT's constitutional design (Chapter IV) is already simpler than the Board's (no committees, no supermajority thresholds, no succession machinery of its own), so its Statute has genuinely less ground to cover; drafting it at the same length as the other two would be padding, not rigour.

## 6. What "flagship publication standard" means for a Statute, stated plainly

The Charter's own flagship HTML/PDF/DOCX pipeline (covers, ceremony pages, dynamic running headers, QR-coded institutional information) is purpose-built for the Charter as a singular, ceremonial constitutional instrument — not a template meant to be re-run, unmodified, for every one of the dozens of Statutes and Policies this governance library will eventually contain. Building a bespoke publication pipeline for each individual Statute now, before the Statute even exists in text, would be exactly the kind of premature, unrequested infrastructure this project has declined to build elsewhere. "Flagship standard" for a Statute in this phase means: the same drafting rigour as the Charter itself (numbered Articles, precise cross-references, no undefined terms, no invented authority), a full internal audit before being considered complete, and clean, well-structured markdown ready to be typeset the moment the Institution's own Policy Compendium (identified as a real future document in the Master Documentation Blueprint, Phase XVIII/Publications) makes a shared publication pipeline worth building for the whole Statute/Policy library at once, rather than one Statute at a time. This is a scope decision, stated here rather than left implicit.
