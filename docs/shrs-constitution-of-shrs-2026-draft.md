# THE CONSTITUTION OF SULTAN HANAFI ROYAL SCHOOLS

**Made under Article 178 of the Governance Charter of Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools.**

**Status.** This Constitution has been prepared for the Board of Governors' consideration. It carries no legal effect until adopted by the Board under the procedure Article 178 of the Governance Charter requires — the same procedure as a constitutional amendment under Chapter XVIII of the Governance Charter, and, to the extent any future amendment of this Constitution would alter the substance of Article 4 below, the entrenched procedure Article 157 of the Governance Charter requires. Until adoption, the Governance Charter of Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools remains, on its own terms, the supreme governing instrument of the Institution.

**Classification.** Public.

---

## Article 1 — Citation and nature of this instrument

1.1. This instrument may be cited as the Constitution of Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools.

1.2. This Constitution is made under Article 178 of the Governance Charter, which anticipates it as "a broader instrument governing the Institution as a whole — its academic life, its students, its Constituent Institutions, and its relationship to the community it serves."

1.3. On adoption, this Constitution becomes the outermost instrument of the Institution's constitutional order. The Governance Charter continues in force, unamended by the act of adopting this Constitution, as this Constitution's own governance component, exactly as Article 178 of the Governance Charter itself anticipates. Nothing in this Constitution repeals, restates, or requires re-adoption of the Governance Charter's own text.

1.4. This Constitution does not restate the operational content of the Institution's existing Policies and Handbooks — the Student Handbook, the Parent Handbook, the Academic Regulations, the Admissions Policy, the Boarding Regulations, the Student Code of Conduct, the Hifz Regulations, the Ijazah Governance Framework, the Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy, and the rest of the governance library. Each continues in force on its own terms. This Constitution states the principles those instruments already give effect to; where this Constitution and any of them appear to differ in a matter of operational detail, the more specific instrument governs that detail, and this Article records that expressly so the relationship is not left to be inferred.

## Article 2 — Supremacy and scope

2.1. Subject to Article 1.3, this Constitution is the supreme instrument of the Institution's constitutional order. Every instrument made under it — the Governance Charter, every Statute, Regulation, Policy, Handbook, and Operational Manual — derives its ultimate authority from it, and none may lawfully conflict with it.

2.2. This Constitution governs the Institution as a whole: its identity and objects, the standing of its Constituent Institutions as one system, and the constitutional relationship of its principal stakeholders — Students, Parents and Guardians, Staff, the Founder, the Board of Governors, and the wider community the Institution serves — to the Institution and to one another. It does not itself govern the day-to-day conduct of any of them; that remains the office of the Policy, Handbook, or Regulation that already does so.

## Article 3 — Identity and objects

3.1. ***Amended [2026-08-04].*** The Institution is Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools, founded in December 2017 in Ikorodu, Lagos State, Nigeria, comprising, as at 2026-08-04, five Constituent Institutions: Sultan Hanafi Royal College, Sultan Hanafi Qur'an College, Sultan Hanafi School of Islamic and Arabic Studies, Sultan Hanafi Nursery and Primary School, and Sultan Hanafi Online & Distance Learning School, each a Constituent Institution within the meaning given in Article 1 of the Governance Charter. The fifth, Sultan Hanafi Online & Distance Learning School, was established by the Board-approved governance restructuring amendment of 2026-08-04 and, as at that date, has no appointed Head, enrolled student body, or settled curriculum.

3.2. The Institution's vision, mission, motto, core values, and objects are as adopted from time to time by the Board on the recommendation of the Head of Schools / Administrator, per Article 7 of the Governance Charter, and are incorporated into this Constitution by reference on the same footing as they are incorporated into the Governance Charter. This Constitution does not itself restate them, for the same reason Article 7 of the Governance Charter gives: a text that could change by Board resolution should not be frozen into an instrument whose own amendment procedure is deliberately heavier.

3.3. Nothing in this Constitution determines the Institution's external legal corporate form, per Article 3 of the Governance Charter, which this Constitution does not disturb.

## Article 4 — Islamic character

4.1. The Institution is, and shall at all times remain, an Islamic educational institution. This Constitution adopts, in full and without modification, the institutional character Article 8 of the Governance Charter establishes — that character is foundational to every provision of this Constitution, exactly as it is foundational to every provision of the Governance Charter, and is not restated here in different words that could be read as narrowing or altering it.

4.2. This Article may be amended only under the entrenched procedure Article 157 of the Governance Charter requires, applied to this Constitution on the same terms it applies to Article 8 of the Governance Charter itself.

## Article 5 — The Constituent Institutions as one system

5.1. The Institution is one system, not a federation of separate schools sharing a name. Each Constituent Institution is administered as Chapters V–VIII of the Governance Charter provide, but none is constitutionally separate from the Institution, and none may be established, closed, or restructured except as Articles 146A, 146B, and 147 of the Governance Charter provide.

5.2. A person's enrolment, employment, or appointment at a particular Constituent Institution does not create a constitutional relationship with that Constituent Institution distinct from the person's relationship with the Institution as a whole; the rights and standing this Constitution recognises in Articles 6 through 9 below attach to the Institution, exercised in practice at the Constituent Institution where the person is enrolled, employed, or appointed.

## Article 6 — Constitutional standing of Students

6.1. Every Student is entitled to be treated in accordance with the Institution's Islamic character under Article 4, with the safeguarding standard the Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy and the Designated Safeguarding Lead Framework establish, and with the rights and expectations the Student Handbook and the Student Code of Conduct set out in full operational detail. This Constitution recognises those entitlements as a matter of constitutional principle; it does not restate their operational detail, which remains governed, and may be amended, under those instruments' own terms.

6.2. Nothing in this Article creates a right, entitlement, or standard beyond what the instruments named in Article 6.1 already establish, and this Article is void to the extent it is read to do so.

## Article 7 — Constitutional standing of Parents and Guardians

7.1. Every Parent and Guardian, within the meaning Article 4(p) of the Governance Charter gives those terms, stands in the relationship with the Institution the Parent Handbook describes, and is entitled to the standard of communication, partnership, and account the Parent Handbook sets out. This Constitution recognises that standing as a matter of constitutional principle, on the same non-duplicating basis as Article 6 above.

## Article 8 — Constitutional standing of Staff

8.1. Every member of Staff stands in the relationship with the Institution the Staff Handbook and the Staff Conduct Policy describe, is entitled to the standard of treatment those instruments set out, and is bound by the obligations they impose. This Constitution recognises that standing as a matter of constitutional principle, on the same non-duplicating basis as Articles 6 and 7 above.

## Article 9 — Constitutional standing of the Founder

9.1. This Constitution adopts, in full and without modification, the personal and permanent status of the Founder that Article 27A of the Governance Charter establishes, the reserved powers Article 27C of the Governance Charter reserves, and the Founder's ceremonial and constitutional entitlements under Articles 27D through 27F of the Governance Charter. Nothing in this Constitution adds to, diminishes, or restates in different terms any of those provisions; this Article exists so the Founder's status is recognised at the level of this Constitution as well as at the level of the Governance Charter, not to create a second, independent source of the same status.

## Article 10 — The Board of Governors and executive governance

10.1. ***Amended [2026-08-04].*** The Institution's governance — the Board of Governors (comprising the Chairman, the Secretary, and three Other Members, per Article 29 of the Governance Charter), the office of Head of Schools / Administrator, the Management Team, and every constitutional office, council, and committee the Governance Charter establishes — is, and remains, governed exclusively by the Governance Charter and the instruments made under it. There is no office of "Chief Executive Officer" under the Governance Charter, following its Board-approved governance restructuring amendment of 2026-08-04; the Founder holds the office of Chairman of the Board of Governors and the office of Head of Schools / Administrator, and exercises the Founder's constitutional authority solely through those two offices and the Founder's personal reserved rights under Article 27C of the Governance Charter. This Constitution does not create, alter, or duplicate any part of that architecture; Article 178 of the Governance Charter itself describes the Governance Charter's governance provisions as forming a part of this Constitution once adopted, and this Article gives effect to that description rather than restating what the Governance Charter already governs completely.

## Article 11 — The wider community

11.1. The Institution recognises its relationship with the wider community it serves — including alumni, the Sultan Hanafi Foundation, and the Institution's advancement and community-facing functions under Chapter XI of the Governance Charter — as part of the Institution's constitutional character, exercised in practice through the Institutional Advancement Council and the offices the Governance Charter establishes for that purpose. This Article recognises that relationship as a matter of principle; it does not itself govern the Institution's fundraising, alumni, or community-engagement practice, which remains governed by the instruments made for those purposes.

## Article 12 — Hierarchy of instruments

12.1. Authority under this Constitution is exercised, in descending order, through: this Constitution; the Governance Charter, as this Constitution's governance component; a Statute made under Article 163 of the Governance Charter; a Regulation made under Article 164 of the Governance Charter; a Policy made under Article 165 of the Governance Charter; and a Handbook, Operational Manual, Form, Template, Register, or Standard Operating Procedure made under or in support of any of the foregoing.

12.2. No instrument in that hierarchy may lawfully conflict with an instrument above it in the hierarchy, and is void to the extent of any such conflict. This Article restates, at the level of this Constitution, the same principle Article 162 of the Governance Charter already states for instruments made under the Governance Charter; the two are read together, not as competing statements of the same rule.

## Article 13 — Amendment

13.1. This Constitution may be amended only by the same procedure Article 178 of the Governance Charter requires for its own adoption: the procedure for a constitutional amendment under Chapter XVIII of the Governance Charter, and, where the amendment would alter the substance of Article 4 above, the entrenched procedure under Article 157 of the Governance Charter.

13.2. An amendment to the Governance Charter made under its own Chapter XVIII does not, of itself, amend this Constitution, and an amendment to this Constitution does not, of itself, amend the Governance Charter; each is amended under its own procedure, consistently with Article 1.3 above.

## Article 14 — Interpretation

14.1. This Constitution is interpreted consistently with Article 9 of the Governance Charter, read together with this Constitution's own Article 4. Where a genuine ambiguity arises in this Constitution that its own terms do not resolve, and the ambiguity does not arise under the Governance Charter itself, the Board resolves the question under Article 155 of the Governance Charter, applied to this Constitution on the same terms.

## Article 15 — Commencement and saving

15.1. This Constitution takes effect on the date of its adoption by the Board, recorded in the Authority Register under Article 16 of the Governance Charter.

15.2. Every instrument in force immediately before that date — the Governance Charter and every Statute, Regulation, Policy, and Handbook made under it — continues in force, unaffected by the adoption of this Constitution, to the extent not inconsistent with it, exactly as Article 5 of the Governance Charter already provides for instruments predating the Governance Charter itself.

## Drafting & audit record

**Legal audit.** Every Governance Charter cross-reference in this Constitution (Articles 1, 3, 4(p), 5, 7, 8, 9, 16, 27A, 27C, 27D–27F, 146A, 146B, 147, 155, 157, 162, 163, 164, 165, 178) was checked against the actual current text of `docs/shrs-constitution-2026-draft.md` before this Constitution was considered complete. Article 7 of the Governance Charter was checked specifically because it states that the Institution's vision, mission, motto, and core values are "incorporated into this Charter by reference" and not restated in the Charter's own text — this Constitution follows the identical pattern in Article 3.2, rather than inventing vision/mission language this drafting process has no authority to adopt and no real text to draw from.

**Governance audit.** Checked that this Constitution does not, anywhere, restate or duplicate the Governance Charter's own governance architecture (Article 10), the operational content of any existing Policy or Handbook (Articles 6–8), or invent a new instrument-hierarchy rule inconsistent with Article 162 of the Governance Charter (Article 12, checked and found consistent rather than competing). Checked that every Article recognising a stakeholder's constitutional standing (6, 7, 8, 9) contains an explicit non-duplication clause, so this Constitution cannot later be read as a second, competing source of a right or standard already governed elsewhere.

**Editorial audit.** Checked for terms used without definition (none — every term either carries the meaning Article 4 of the Governance Charter gives it, per this Constitution's own interpretation Article, or is defined by cross-reference to the specific instrument that governs it). Checked for internal numbering consistency (confirmed) and for the deliberate absence of any Article restating operational detail this document's own scoping note (`docs/shrs-constitution-phase1b-scoping-note.md`) identified as already existing elsewhere.

**Publication audit.** Same standard as the Governance Charter's Phase 1A freeze: drafting rigour and a full internal audit, not yet a bespoke typeset publication pipeline. Classification: Public, consistent with the Governance Charter's own classification and with this Constitution's nature as a foundational, family-facing instrument rather than an internal operating document.

**Status.** Phase 1B — complete and audited. Prepared for the Board's consideration under Article 178 of the Governance Charter.

**Amendment record [2026-08-04].** Articles 3.1, 3.2, and 10.1 were amended to reflect the Board-approved governance restructuring amendment of 2026-08-04 to the Governance Charter: the abolition of the office of "Chief Executive Officer" and its replacement by the office of Head of Schools / Administrator (held, together with the office of Chairman of the Board of Governors, by the Founder); the Board of Governors' restated composition (Chairman, Secretary, and three Other Members); the renaming of Sultan Hanafi Royal College to Sultan Hanafi Royal College, Sultan Hanafi School of Islamic and Arabic Studies to Sultan Hanafi School of Islamic and Arabic Studies, and Sultan Hanafi Nursery and Primary School to Sultan Hanafi Nursery and Primary School; and the establishment of a fifth Constituent Institution, Sultan Hanafi Online & Distance Learning School. This amendment follows the same procedure as this Constitution's own adoption, per Article 13.1.
