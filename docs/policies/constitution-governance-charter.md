# Policy GV-01 — SHRS Constitution & Governance Charter

*Retrofitted to the full 13-section governance architecture and
deepened, per the Tier 3 retrofit directive. The highest document in
the SHRS governance ecosystem — every other document in
`docs/policies/` should align beneath it, not the reverse. Every
principle from v0.1 is preserved unchanged; none weakened or removed.
New in v2.0: a governance-gap escalation procedure (naming how an
unfilled role like the Designated Safeguarding Lead stays visible to
the Board until filled, rather than quietly persisting), a
succession/vacancy principle for named authority roles, and a Records
and Documentation section — genuinely new, since v0.1 never stated
what records of Board resolutions and Charter amendments are kept.*

## 2. Purpose

To state, in one place, what kind of institution SHRS is, who governs
it, what each level of authority may and may not decide alone, and how
that authority is delegated down to four institutions, an Executive
Management Team, and — increasingly — digital systems (the Parent
Portal, a future Student Portal, and an AI assistant) acting on the
institution's behalf. Every governance document drafted after this one
should be traceable to an authority this Charter establishes.

## 3. Scope

Applies to the Board of Trustees, the Executive Management Team, all
four institutions (Royal College, Qur'an College, School of Arabic &
Islamic Studies, Nursery & Primary), and — by extension through the
delegation principle in Section 7.1 — every digital system acting under
the SHRS name.

## 4. Definitions

- **Board of Trustees** — the institution's ultimate governing body.
- **Executive Management Team** — the CEO and the Principals/Head
  Teacher of each institution, plus named functional heads (VP
  Administration, Registrar, ICT Head, Head of Research & Development),
  per `about-governance.html`.
- **Institution** — one of the four schools operating under SHRS:
  Royal College, Qur'an College, School of Islamic & Arabic Studies,
  Nursery & Primary.
- **Delegated authority** — a decision the Board has authorised a named
  role to make without seeking Board approval each time, within stated
  limits.
- **Governance gap** — a role this Charter or a document beneath it
  assumes is filled, but which currently has no named person holding it
  (Section 7.5).
- **Vacancy** — the temporary absence of a named authority role between
  one holder leaving and a successor being confirmed (Section 7.6).

## 5. Policy Statement

Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools is a single institution operating four
distinct schools under one Board of Trustees and one Executive
Management Team — not four independently governed schools sharing a
brand. A policy adopted by the Board (e.g. the Child Protection &
Safeguarding Policy) applies across all four institutions identically,
while institution-specific documents (e.g. Hifz Regulations, which
applies only to Qur'an College) exist precisely because that
institution's operating reality differs, not because its governance is
separate. Authority flows from the Board downward through clearly
delegated channels — no institution, functional head, or digital system
holds authority the Board has not knowingly delegated. Islamic
scholarship and secular educational leadership sit together in the
Executive Management Team by design (the CEO's finance background, the
Royal College Principal's education background, and the Qur'an College
and School of Islamic & Arabic Studies Principals' Islamic scholarship
background, per the real roster) — this Charter treats that as a
structural strength to preserve, not something to flatten into a single
generic "Principal" role. Accountability runs both ways: the Board
holds the Executive Management Team accountable for institutional
performance, and the Executive Management Team is entitled to clear,
written delegation rather than ambiguous authority it must guess at.
Family-facing and student-facing governance (handbooks, codes of
conduct, academic regulations) must be internally consistent with each
other — this is why the Phase A/B/C/D/E/F dependency reviews and
consistency audits exist, and this Charter formally endorses that
discipline as an ongoing practice, not a one-time exercise.

## 6. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Board of Trustees | Approves this Charter and any amendment to it (Section 13); approves all Tier 1 governance documents; appoints and holds accountable the CEO; receives an annual report from the Designated Safeguarding Lead (once appointed) and from the ICT Head/Data Protection Owner on Parent Portal security and privacy matters; resolves a genuine governance gap (Section 7.5) once escalated. |
| CEO | Day-to-day institutional operation across all four institutions; approves Tier 2–4 governance documents on the Board's behalf unless the Board reserves a specific document to itself; resources and empowers the Designated Safeguarding Lead role to act without institutional pressure. |
| Principal / Head Teacher (per institution) | Holds academic and disciplinary decision authority within their institution, per the Academic Regulations and Student Code of Conduct; may not unilaterally adopt a policy that conflicts with a Board-approved Tier 1 document. |
| Any named authority-role holder | Escalates a vacancy in their own or an adjacent role (Section 7.6) rather than allowing a silent gap in accountability. |

## 7. Procedures

### 7.1 Delegation principles
Delegated authority is written down, not assumed — this is the same
discipline the Delegation of Authority Policy (Governance Master
Register, Governance, Tier 2, not yet drafted) will formalise in more
operational detail; this Charter states the principle it will operate
under. Delegation extends to digital systems acting under the SHRS name
(the Parent Portal's automated notifications, the website's AI
assistant) — but only within the scope the Board has approved via the
relevant policy (Data Protection & Privacy Policy, AI Usage Policy). A
digital system's designers do not hold authority beyond what those
approved policies grant.

### 7.2 Relationship to other documents
This Charter sits above every document in `docs/policies/`. Where a
conflict is found between this Charter and any other document, this
Charter governs, and the conflicting document must be corrected — the
same principle the Phase A/B consistency audit already applied in
practice (no document was allowed to quietly contradict another) is
formalised here as a standing rule, not just an audit habit.

### 7.3 Amendment procedure
Amended only by Board resolution, following a defined notice period
(recommend 30 days' notice to the Executive Management Team before a
vote, to be confirmed by the Board) — this Charter does not set its own
amendment procedure unilaterally beyond proposing that default.

### 7.4 Emergency delegation
During a declared emergency (Emergency Response Plan, SW-09), the
Incident Commander holds operational authority to direct evacuation,
lockdown, or immediate safety decisions that would ordinarily sit with
a Principal or the CEO — a deliberate, temporary, and narrowly scoped
exception to Section 6's authority structure, ending the moment the
institutional lead named in the Emergency Response Plan takes over or
the emergency is declared over. **This is a new provision, not
previously stated in v0.1** — added because the Emergency Response Plan
already names an Incident Commander role but this Charter never stated
where that authority comes from or how it relates to the normal
academic/executive authority structure.

### 7.5 Governance gap escalation
Where a role this Charter or a document beneath it assumes is filled
has no named holder (the clearest current example: the Designated
Safeguarding Lead), that gap is tracked in the Governance Resolution
Register and reported to the Board at least annually until resolved —
it is not left as a silent assumption in the documents that depend on
it. A governance gap is a standing Board agenda item, not a drafting
footnote, for as long as it remains open.

### 7.6 Succession and vacancy
When a named authority-role holder (CEO, a Principal/Head Teacher, a
functional head) leaves the role, their authority passes to whoever the
Board or CEO names as successor or interim holder — it does not lapse,
transfer informally, or default to the most senior person present
without an explicit confirmation. Until a successor is confirmed, the
outgoing holder's immediate superior (the Board for the CEO; the CEO
for a Principal/Head Teacher or functional head) holds that authority
directly rather than leaving it unexercised. **This is a new provision,
not previously stated in v0.1** — added because the continuity test
this project's own Institutional Readiness Report raised ("would SHRS
run consistently if the CEO, Principal, or Registrar changed
tomorrow?") had no explicit answer in this Charter itself until now.

## 8. Monitoring and Compliance

This Charter does not certify SHRS's compliance with Nigerian
corporate/educational-institution law regarding board governance (e.g.
Companies and Allied Matters Act requirements if SHRS operates as a
company limited by guarantee, or equivalent requirements under whatever
legal form actually applies) — that confirmation requires the school's
own legal counsel and corporate records, not this drafting pass. The
Board reviews adherence to Sections 6 and 7's authority and delegation
principles at each biennial review, and immediately if a governance gap
(Section 7.5) or an unconfirmed succession (Section 7.6) is reported.

## 9. Records and Documentation

**New in v2.0.** Every Board resolution adopting or amending this
Charter, every governance gap reported under Section 7.5, and every
succession/vacancy confirmed under Section 7.6 is recorded and
retained by the Board Secretary (or equivalent role, to be confirmed)
— **this is a proposed record-keeping practice, not a confirmation that
SHRS currently maintains a formal Board minute book**; adopting it is
itself a Board decision.

## 10. Related Policies

Every document in `docs/policies/` sits beneath this Charter by design
— see the Governance Master Register for the full list. Most directly:
the Delegation of Authority Policy (Governance Master Register,
Governance, Tier 2, not yet drafted, which Section 7.1 anticipates),
the Emergency Response Plan (SW-09, for Section 7.4's emergency
delegation), the Designated Safeguarding Lead Framework (SW-02, the
running example in Section 7.5), and the Governance Resolution
Register (where a governance gap is formally tracked).

## 11. Exceptions

Section 7.4's emergency delegation is the one deliberate, narrowly
scoped exception to the normal authority structure in Section 6 — named
explicitly here, not an oversight, and it lapses automatically once the
emergency ends.

## 12. Appeals and Complaints

Not generally applicable to a constitutional document; a genuine
dispute over this Charter's interpretation is resolved by Board
resolution, following the same convention as any other governance
document in this ecosystem — a defined decision-maker, not an open-
ended dispute.

## 13. Review and Amendment

Biennial, or immediately upon any material change to the Board's
composition or the Executive Management Team's structure, or upon any
governance gap (Section 7.5) being resolved or newly identified.
