# SHRS Constitution & Governance Charter

**Status:** DRAFT v0.1 — not yet adopted. The highest document in the
SHRS governance ecosystem; every other document in `docs/policies/`
should align beneath it, not the reverse.
**Owner:** Board of Trustees.
**Review cycle:** Biennial.
**Depends on:** none — foundational. Everything else depends on this,
not the other way around.

> **Before this governs a real decision:** this Charter describes a
> governance structure consistent with what `about-governance.html`
> already publishes (a Board of Trustees above an Executive Management
> Team) — it does not establish SHRS's legal corporate form (e.g.
> whether it is registered as a company limited by guarantee, a trust,
> or another structure under Nigerian law), which requires confirmation
> from the school's own legal/corporate records, not an assumption
> made here. Anywhere this Charter states something as a governance
> *principle* going forward, that is a proposal for Board adoption, not
> a description of a rule that has always been formally in force.

---

## 1. Purpose

To state, in one place, what kind of institution SHRS is, who governs
it, what each level of authority may and may not decide alone, and how
that authority is delegated down to four institutions, an Executive
Management Team, and — increasingly — digital systems (the Parent
Portal, a future Student Portal, and an AI assistant) acting on the
institution's behalf. Every governance document drafted after this one
should be traceable to an authority this Charter establishes.

## 2. Scope

Applies to the Board of Trustees, the Executive Management Team, all
four institutions (Royal College, Qur'an College, School of Arabic &
Islamic Studies, Nursery & Primary), and — by extension through the
delegation principle in Section 6 — every digital system acting under
the SHRS name.

## 3. Definitions

- **Board of Trustees** — the institution's ultimate governing body.
- **Executive Management Team** — the CEO and the Principals/Head
  Teacher of each institution, plus named functional heads (VP
  Administration, Registrar, ICT Head, Head of Research & Development),
  per `about-governance.html`.
- **Institution** — one of the four schools operating under SHRS:
  Royal College, Qur'an College, School of Arabic & Islamic Studies,
  Nursery & Primary.
- **Delegated authority** — a decision the Board has authorised a named
  role to make without seeking Board approval each time, within stated
  limits.

## 4. Institutional identity

Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools is a single institution operating four
distinct schools under one Board of Trustees and one Executive
Management Team — not four independently governed schools sharing a
brand. This matters concretely: a policy adopted by the Board (e.g. the
Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy) applies across all four
institutions identically, while institution-specific documents (e.g.
Hifz Regulations, which applies only to Qur'an College) exist
precisely because that institution's operating reality differs, not
because its governance is separate.

## 5. Governance philosophy

1. Authority flows from the Board downward through clearly delegated
   channels — no institution, functional head, or digital system holds
   authority the Board has not knowingly delegated.
2. Islamic scholarship and secular educational leadership sit together
   in the Executive Management Team by design (the CEO's finance
   background, the Royal College Principal's education background, and
   the Qur'an College and School of Arabic & Islamic Studies
   Principals' Islamic scholarship background, per the real roster) —
   this Charter treats that as a structural strength to preserve, not
   something to flatten into a single generic "Principal" role.
3. Accountability runs both ways: the Board holds the Executive
   Management Team accountable for institutional performance, and the
   Executive Management Team is entitled to clear, written delegation
   rather than ambiguous authority it must guess at.
4. Family-facing and student-facing governance (handbooks, codes of
   conduct, academic regulations) must be internally consistent with
   each other — this is why `phase-b-dependency-review.md` and the
   consistency audits exist, and this Charter formally endorses that
   discipline as an ongoing practice, not a one-time exercise.

## 6. Authority structure

### 6.1 Board powers
- Approves this Charter and any amendment to it.
- Approves all Tier 1 governance documents (per the Governance Master
  Register): Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy, Data Protection &
  Privacy Policy, Emergency Response Plan, Boarding Regulations,
  Student Code of Conduct, Ijazah Governance Framework, Academic
  Regulations, Parent Handbook, and this Charter itself.
- Appoints and holds accountable the CEO.
- Receives an annual report from the Designated Safeguarding Lead
  (once appointed) and from the ICT Head/Data Protection Owner on
  Parent Portal security and privacy matters.

### 6.2 Executive powers (CEO)
- Day-to-day institutional operation across all four institutions.
- Approves Tier 2–4 governance documents on the Board's behalf, unless
  the Board reserves a specific document to itself.
- Resources and empowers the Designated Safeguarding Lead role to act
  without institutional pressure (Child Protection & Safeguarding
  Policy §5).

### 6.3 Academic powers (Principals / Head Teacher)
- Each institution's Principal (or, for Nursery & Primary, Head
  Teacher) holds academic and disciplinary decision authority within
  their institution, per the Academic Regulations and Student Code of
  Conduct — this Charter does not re-grant that authority in different
  terms; it confirms it flows from here.
- No institution's Principal may unilaterally adopt a policy that
  conflicts with a Board-approved Tier 1 document across all four
  institutions (e.g. a Principal cannot adopt a lighter safeguarding
  standard for their own institution).

### 6.4 Delegation principles
- Delegated authority is written down, not assumed — this is the same
  discipline the Delegation of Authority Policy (Governance Master
  Register, Tier 2, not yet drafted) will formalise in more operational
  detail; this Charter states the principle it will operate under.
- Delegation extends to digital systems acting under the SHRS name
  (the Parent Portal's automated notifications, the website's AI
  assistant) — but only within the scope the Board has approved via
  the relevant policy (Data Protection & Privacy Policy, AI Usage
  Policy). A digital system's designers do not hold authority beyond
  what those approved policies grant.

## 7. Accountability principles

- Every Tier 1 document names a real, accountable owner (per the
  Governance Master Register) — never "the school" as an anonymous
  actor.
- Where an owning role does not yet exist (the Designated Safeguarding
  Lead is the clearest example), this Charter treats that as an open
  governance gap requiring a Board decision, not something to leave
  ambiguous indefinitely.

## 8. Relationship to other documents

This Charter sits above every document in `docs/policies/`. Where a
conflict is found between this Charter and any other document, this
Charter governs, and the conflicting document must be corrected — the
same principle the Phase A/B consistency audit already applied in
practice (no document was allowed to quietly contradict another) is
formalised here as a standing rule, not just an audit habit.

## 9. Amendment procedure

Amended only by Board resolution, following a defined notice period
(recommend 30 days' notice to the Executive Management Team before a
vote, to be confirmed by the Board) — this Charter does not set its own
amendment procedure unilaterally beyond proposing that default.

## 10. Compliance

This Charter does not certify SHRS's compliance with Nigerian
corporate/educational-institution law regarding board governance
(e.g. Companies and Allied Matters Act requirements if SHRS operates as
a company limited by guarantee, or equivalent requirements under
whatever legal form actually applies) — that confirmation requires the
school's own legal counsel and corporate records, not this drafting
pass.

## 11. Appeals

Not generally applicable to a constitutional document; a genuine
dispute over this Charter's interpretation is resolved by Board
resolution.

## 12. Exceptions

None defined.

## 13. Review cycle

Biennial, or immediately upon any material change to the Board's
composition or the Executive Management Team's structure.
