# SHRS Designated Safeguarding Lead Framework

**Status:** DRAFT v0.1 — not yet adopted. This framework defines the
DSL role in full; **it does not itself appoint anyone** — that remains
a Board decision this framework cannot make on its own. Everywhere else
in this document ecosystem that says "the DSL role does not yet exist,"
the accurate statement after this framework is: *the role is now fully
defined; nobody has been appointed to it yet.* Those are different
claims, and Task #61 of this project updates the highest-impact
references accordingly.
**Owner:** Board of Trustees (the appointment decision); this framework
document itself is maintained by the CEO.
**Review cycle:** Annual, or immediately upon appointment of the first
DSL, or upon any campus-expansion decision.
**Depends on:** Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy (Phase A, which
this framework operationalises); Constitution & Governance Charter
(Phase C, for the Board's appointment authority).

> **Before this governs a real decision:** the campus-scaling ratios
> below are proposed defaults reflecting common child-protection
> practice (one DSL cannot realistically hold direct knowledge of every
> child across a large, multi-site institution), not a standard
> verified against Nigerian or Lagos State safeguarding regulation. They
> need confirmation from a child-protection professional, not just
> Board judgement, given how much weight this specific ratio carries.

---

## 1. Purpose

To answer, concretely, the question the Child Protection & Safeguarding
Policy raised but could not resolve on its own: who is the DSL, what do
they actually do day to day, how does the Deputy DSL structure work
across four institutions today, and — the gap the Phase C Scalability
Stress Test named explicitly — how does this scale if SHRS grows to
multiple campuses, where one DSL per institution stops being enough.

## 2. Scope

The DSL role, Deputy DSL roles per institution (and, at scale, per
campus), and their reporting lines to the Board and CEO.

## 3. Definitions

Reuses "Designated Safeguarding Lead," "Deputy DSL," "disclosure," and
"referral" exactly as defined in the Child Protection & Safeguarding
Policy §4 — this framework does not redefine them, only operationalises
the DSL role those definitions already named.

## 4. Principles

1. The DSL role sits at Executive Management level, not delegated to a
   single institution's staff — because a safeguarding concern doesn't
   respect institutional boundaries (a Qur'an College boarding concern
   may have implications the DSL needs to weigh against, say, a Royal
   College sibling's circumstances).
2. Every institution has a named Deputy DSL today; every **campus**
   will need one once SHRS operates more than one site per institution
   — the two are not the same unit, and this framework treats them
   accordingly rather than assuming institution-level coverage
   automatically scales.
3. The DSL's independence from day-to-day management pressure (Child
   Protection & Safeguarding Policy §6, CEO's responsibility to
   "resource and empower" the role) is a structural requirement, not
   an aspiration — this framework states what that requires in
   practice (Section 7).
4. A DSL appointment is never informal — it is a named Board decision,
   documented, with a clear start date and successor plan.

## 5. DSL responsibilities

- First and final point of contact for every safeguarding disclosure
  or concern reported anywhere across SHRS (Child Protection &
  Safeguarding Policy §7.2–7.5).
- Decides on internal handling versus external statutory referral for
  every case (§7.4–7.5).
- Maintains the confidential safeguarding log across all institutions
  (§7.3) — a single log, not four institution-siloed ones, so a
  pattern spanning institutions (e.g. siblings at different schools)
  isn't missed.
- Handles or directly oversees any allegation against a staff member
  (§7.6), in consultation with the CEO.
- Reports to the Board annually (Constitution & Governance Charter
  §6.1) on safeguarding activity in aggregate — case volumes and
  systemic themes, never individual case detail that would breach a
  child's confidentiality.
- Reviews the Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy, Boarding
  Regulations, and this framework annually, proposing amendments where
  practice has revealed a gap.

## 6. Deputy DSL responsibilities (institution level, today)

One Deputy DSL per institution — proposed: the Principal of Royal
College, Qur'an College, and School of Arabic & Islamic Studies, and
the Head Teacher of Nursery & Primary (matching the Child Protection &
Safeguarding Policy §6's existing proposal, not a new assignment).

- First point of contact within their institution for a concern,
  escalating to the DSL the same day (§7.2).
- Maintains institution-level awareness of any student flagged in the
  central log, without needing to see the DSL's full cross-institution
  picture.
- Ensures every staff member in their institution knows who the Deputy
  DSL and DSL are — a basic awareness check, not assumed automatically
  true.

## 7. Reporting lines

```
Board of Trustees
      |
   (appoints, holds accountable, receives annual aggregate report)
      |
Designated Safeguarding Lead (Executive Management level)
      |
   (delegates institution-level first response to)
      |
Deputy DSL — Royal College   Deputy DSL — Qur'an College
Deputy DSL — School of Arabic & Islamic Studies
Deputy DSL — Nursery & Primary
      |
   (all staff report a concern to their institution's Deputy DSL,
    or directly to the DSL, whichever is faster)
```

The CEO sits alongside this chain, not inside it, for one specific
reason: the DSL's independence (Principle 3) means the DSL does not
report safeguarding case decisions to the CEO for approval — only in
aggregate to the Board. The CEO's role is resourcing and protection of
that independence (Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy §6), not
oversight of individual case handling.

## 8. Campus scaling model (the Phase C stress-test gap, resolved)

The Scalability Stress Test in the Governance Master Register named
this exact gap: at 5,000 students across multiple campuses, one Deputy
DSL per **institution** is very likely insufficient if an institution
operates several physical campuses. This framework proposes:

1. **Trigger for a per-campus Deputy DSL:** once any single institution
   operates more than one physical campus, that institution gets one
   Deputy DSL per campus, not one for the whole institution — the
   original per-institution model was sized for a single-site
   institution and should not silently stretch to cover multiple sites.
2. **Ratio guidance (proposed, needs professional confirmation):** no
   Deputy DSL should hold direct safeguarding awareness responsibility
   for more students than they could plausibly know by name and
   circumstance — a common informal benchmark is in the low hundreds,
   not thousands; the Board should set an explicit number once real
   campus sizes are known, rather than this framework guessing one.
3. **The DSL role itself does not multiply** — even at large scale,
   one DSL holds the cross-institution/cross-campus picture and final
   referral decision authority, supported by more Deputy DSLs beneath
   them. Splitting the DSL role itself would recreate the exact
   "siblings at different schools" blind spot Principle 1 above exists
   to prevent.
4. **International students and boarding-heavy campuses** warrant a
   Deputy DSL with specific training in cross-border safeguarding
   considerations (e.g. a student's guardian being outside Nigeria) —
   named here as a future requirement once international admissions
   exist (Admissions Policy §9, still blocked), not something to build
   out prematurely.

## 9. Appointment process

1. The Board identifies a candidate — internal (an existing Executive
   Management Team member, most plausibly VP Administration or a
   Principal, given the Executive-level principle in Section 4) or
   external.
2. The candidate completes safeguarding-lead training appropriate to
   Nigerian/Lagos State practice — **specific accredited training
   providers need to be identified by the Board**, not assumed by this
   framework.
3. The Board formally appoints, documents the appointment (name, start
   date, scope), and the appointment is communicated to all staff and,
   via the Parent Handbook update, to all guardians.
4. Deputy DSLs are appointed by the DSL in consultation with each
   institution's Principal/Head Teacher, not unilaterally by either.

## 10. Compliance

This framework does not certify that its proposed structure satisfies
any specific Nigerian or Lagos State safeguarding-lead requirement
(if one exists in a form more specific than general child-protection
law) — that verification is outside this drafting pass.

## 11. Appeals

Not generally applicable to a role-definition framework.

## 12. Exceptions

None defined.

## 13. Review cycle

Annual, or immediately upon the first DSL appointment (this framework
should be revisited once a real appointee's actual working pattern is
known, rather than assumed to be correct as drafted) or any
multi-campus expansion.
