# Policy SW-02 — Designated Safeguarding Lead Framework

*Retrofitted to the full 13-section governance architecture and
deepened, per the Tier 3 retrofit directive. This framework defines the
DSL role in full; **it does not itself appoint anyone** — that remains
a Board decision this framework cannot make on its own. Everywhere else
in this document ecosystem that says "the DSL role does not yet
exist," the accurate statement after this framework is: *the role is
now fully defined; nobody has been appointed to it yet.* Those are
different claims. Every principle and procedure from v0.1 is preserved
— none weakened or removed. New in v2.0: an interim-coverage procedure
naming who actually handles a safeguarding concern today while the role
sits vacant, and a handover/succession procedure for the day a DSL
eventually leaves the role, tying directly into the Constitution &
Governance Charter's (GV-01) new succession principle.*

## 2. Purpose

To answer, concretely, the question the Child Protection & Safeguarding
Policy (SW-01) raised but could not resolve on its own: who is the DSL,
what do they actually do day to day, how does the Deputy DSL structure
work across four institutions today, and — the gap the Phase C
Scalability Stress Test named explicitly — how does this scale if SHRS
grows to multiple campuses, where one DSL per institution stops being
enough.

## 3. Scope

The DSL role, Deputy DSL roles per institution (and, at scale, per
campus), and their reporting lines to the Board and CEO.

## 4. Definitions

Reuses "Designated Safeguarding Lead," "Deputy DSL," "disclosure," and
"referral" exactly as defined in the Child Protection & Safeguarding
Policy (SW-01) §4 — this framework does not redefine them, only
operationalises the DSL role those definitions already named.

- **Interim coverage** — the arrangement that applies while the DSL
  role sits vacant, distinct from the DSL role itself (Section 7.7).
- **Handover** — the structured transfer of the DSL's live caseload
  knowledge and safeguarding log access from an outgoing DSL to their
  successor (Section 7.6).

## 5. Policy Statement

The DSL role sits at Executive Management level, not delegated to a
single institution's staff — because a safeguarding concern doesn't
respect institutional boundaries (a Qur'an College boarding concern may
have implications the DSL needs to weigh against, say, a Royal College
sibling's circumstances). Every institution has a named Deputy DSL
today; every **campus** will need one once SHRS operates more than one
site per institution — the two are not the same unit, and this
framework treats them accordingly rather than assuming institution-
level coverage automatically scales. The DSL's independence from
day-to-day management pressure (Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy
§6, CEO's responsibility to "resource and empower" the role) is a
structural requirement, not an aspiration. A DSL appointment is never
informal — it is a named Board decision, documented, with a clear
start date and successor plan, consistent with the Constitution &
Governance Charter's (GV-01) succession principle (§7.6).

## 6. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Board of Trustees | Identifies and formally appoints the DSL (Section 7.5); receives the DSL's annual aggregate report (Constitution & Governance Charter, GV-01, §6); tracks the current vacancy as a governance gap (GV-01 §7.5) until filled. |
| CEO | Resources and protects the DSL's independence; maintains this framework document; sits alongside the reporting chain (Section 7.1) rather than inside it. |
| Designated Safeguarding Lead *(role vacant — Section 7.7 names interim coverage)* | First and final point of contact for every safeguarding disclosure across SHRS; decides internal handling versus external referral; maintains the single cross-institution safeguarding log; oversees any allegation against staff; reports to the Board annually. |
| Deputy DSL (per institution today, per campus at scale) | First point of contact within their institution for a concern, escalating to the DSL the same day; maintains institution-level awareness without needing the DSL's full cross-institution picture. |

## 7. Procedures

### 7.1 Reporting lines
```
Board of Trustees
      |
   (appoints, holds accountable, receives annual aggregate report)
      |
Designated Safeguarding Lead (Executive Management level)
      |
   (delegates institution-level first response to)
      |
Deputy DSL — Royal College   Deputy DSL — Qur'an College
Deputy DSL — School of Arabic & Islamic Studies
Deputy DSL — Nursery & Primary
      |
   (all staff report a concern to their institution's Deputy DSL,
    or directly to the DSL, whichever is faster)
```
The CEO sits alongside this chain, not inside it, for one specific
reason: the DSL's independence means the DSL does not report
safeguarding case decisions to the CEO for approval — only in aggregate
to the Board.

### 7.2 DSL day-to-day responsibilities
First and final point of contact for every safeguarding disclosure or
concern reported anywhere across SHRS (Child Protection & Safeguarding
Policy §7.2–7.5); decides on internal handling versus external
statutory referral for every case (§7.4–7.5); maintains the
confidential safeguarding log across all institutions (§7.3) — a single
log, not four institution-siloed ones; handles or directly oversees any
allegation against a staff member (§7.6), in consultation with the CEO;
reviews the Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy, Boarding
Regulations, and this framework annually, proposing amendments where
practice has revealed a gap.

### 7.3 Deputy DSL day-to-day responsibilities
One Deputy DSL per institution — proposed: the Principal of Royal
College, Qur'an College, and School of Arabic & Islamic Studies, and
the Head Teacher of Nursery & Primary (matching the Child Protection &
Safeguarding Policy §6's existing proposal, not a new assignment).
First point of contact within their institution for a concern,
escalating to the DSL the same day (§7.2); maintains institution-level
awareness of any student flagged in the central log without needing
the DSL's full cross-institution picture; ensures every staff member in
their institution knows who the Deputy DSL and DSL are.

### 7.4 Campus scaling model
The Scalability Stress Test in the Governance Master Register named
this exact gap: at 5,000 students across multiple campuses, one Deputy
DSL per **institution** is very likely insufficient if an institution
operates several physical campuses.
1. **Trigger for a per-campus Deputy DSL:** once any single institution
   operates more than one physical campus, that institution gets one
   Deputy DSL per campus, not one for the whole institution.
2. **Ratio guidance (proposed, needs professional confirmation):** no
   Deputy DSL should hold direct safeguarding awareness responsibility
   for more students than they could plausibly know by name and
   circumstance — a common informal benchmark is in the low hundreds,
   not thousands; the Board should set an explicit number once real
   campus sizes are known.
3. **The DSL role itself does not multiply** — even at large scale, one
   DSL holds the cross-institution/cross-campus picture and final
   referral decision authority, supported by more Deputy DSLs beneath
   them.
4. **International students and boarding-heavy campuses** warrant a
   Deputy DSL with specific training in cross-border safeguarding
   considerations, named here as a future requirement once
   international admissions exist (Admissions Policy, PA-05, §7.5,
   still blocked), not something to build out prematurely.

### 7.5 Appointment process
1. The Board identifies a candidate — internal (an existing Executive
   Management Team member, most plausibly VP Administration or a
   Principal) or external.
2. The candidate completes safeguarding-lead training appropriate to
   Nigerian/Lagos State practice — specific accredited training
   providers need to be identified by the Board.
3. The Board formally appoints, documents the appointment (name, start
   date, scope), and the appointment is communicated to all staff and,
   via the Parent Handbook update, to all guardians.
4. Deputy DSLs are appointed by the DSL in consultation with each
   institution's Principal/Head Teacher, not unilaterally by either.

### 7.6 Handover and succession
When a DSL leaves the role, they hand over the live safeguarding log,
any open case's current status, and Deputy DSL relationships to their
successor directly — not through the CEO or Board as an intermediary,
since the log's content is safeguarding-confidential (Section 9). Until
a successor is confirmed and the handover complete, the Constitution &
Governance Charter's (GV-01) succession principle (§7.6) applies: the
CEO holds the DSL's authority directly rather than leaving it
unexercised, and this reverts to Section 7.7's interim-coverage
arrangement for open-case decisions specifically, given the CEO does
not sit inside the DSL's independent reporting chain (Section 7.1).
**This is a new provision, not previously stated in v0.1** — added
because v0.1 defined the DSL role fully but never stated what happens
operationally the day a serving DSL leaves it.

### 7.7 Interim coverage before first appointment
**As of this draft, no DSL has been appointed.** Until the Board
completes Section 7.5's appointment process, a safeguarding concern
reported anywhere across SHRS is handled jointly by the CEO and the
relevant institution's Principal/Head Teacher — consistent with what
the Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy (SW-01) §8 already states as
the interim monitoring arrangement. This interim arrangement does not
substitute for a real appointment: it lacks the single cross-
institution log (Section 7.2) and the structural independence
(Section 5) a real DSL provides, and the Governance Master Register
tracks the vacancy itself as an open governance gap (Constitution &
Governance Charter, GV-01, §7.5) requiring Board resolution, not an
acceptable steady state. **This is a new provision, not previously
stated in v0.1** — added because the framework fully defined the role
but never explicitly named who is accountable for a concern raised
before that role is filled.

## 8. Monitoring and Compliance

This framework does not certify that its proposed structure satisfies
any specific Nigerian or Lagos State safeguarding-lead requirement (if
one exists in a form more specific than general child-protection law)
— that verification is outside this drafting pass. The Board reviews
whether the interim-coverage arrangement (Section 7.7) is still in
effect at each meeting until a DSL is appointed, rather than letting the
vacancy go unmentioned.

## 9. Records and Documentation

The confidential safeguarding log (Section 7.2) is maintained by the
DSL (or, during interim coverage, jointly by the CEO and Principal/Head
Teacher handling a given concern) and retained per the Records
Retention Policy's (IT-04) safeguarding-record category. DSL and Deputy
DSL appointment records (name, start date, scope, training completion)
are maintained by the CEO and retained alongside the Constitution &
Governance Charter's (GV-01) succession records (§9) — **this is a
proposed record-keeping practice, not a confirmation of current
practice**, since no appointment has yet occurred to generate one.

## 10. Related Policies

Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy (SW-01, which this framework
operationalises), Constitution & Governance Charter (GV-01, for the
Board's appointment authority and the succession principle Section 7.6

international-student Deputy DSL consideration in Section 7.4).

## 11. Exceptions

The international/boarding-heavy Deputy DSL training requirement
(Section 7.4) is a named future exception to the standard Deputy DSL
model, not yet applicable since international admissions are not yet
defined.

## 12. Appeals and Complaints

Not generally applicable to a role-definition framework; a concern
about how a specific case was handled follows the Child Protection &
Safeguarding Policy's (SW-01) own procedures, not a separate appeal
process this framework invents.

## 13. Review and Amendment

Annual, or immediately upon the first DSL appointment (this framework
should be revisited once a real appointee's actual working pattern is
known), any DSL transition (Section 7.6), or any multi-campus
expansion.
