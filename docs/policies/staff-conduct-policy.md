## 1. Purpose

To state, for the first time, what SHRS expects of staff conduct beyond
appearance (already covered by the live Dress Code Policy) — covering
professional boundaries with students, safeguarding-adjacent behaviour,
off-duty conduct, and the disciplinary process for a staff conduct
breach — closing the exact gap the Staff Handbook named rather than
filled.

## 2. Scope

All staff across all four institutions, including boarding wardens,
contract and part-time staff, and volunteers with regular student
contact.

## 3. Definitions

Reuses "misconduct" (Student Code of Conduct, applied here to staff by
analogy, not by cross-application to students) and "Designated
Safeguarding Lead" (Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy) without
redefining them.

- **Staff conduct breach** — any failure to meet the standards in
  Section 5, distinct from a student disciplinary matter (Student Code
  of Conduct) or a performance matter (Staff Handbook §7, and the
  future Performance Management Policy).

## 4. Principles

1. Every staff member's conduct is held to a standard consistent with
   direct, regular contact with children — not a generic
   "professional workplace" standard alone.
2. Safeguarding-adjacent conduct rules apply identically regardless of
   how senior or long-tenured a staff member is.
3. A conduct concern is raised the same way a safeguarding concern is —
   promptly, in writing, to VP Administration or the Designated
   Safeguarding Lead where the concern has a safeguarding dimension —
   not left to informal handling because raising it feels awkward.
4. Off-duty conduct is in scope where it could reasonably affect a
   staff member's fitness to work with children, or the institution's
   reputation.

## 5. Professional conduct standards

- **Professional boundaries with students:** no one-on-one unsupervised
  contact outside official, logged channels; no private
  social-media contact with a student; no gift-giving or favouritism
  suggesting an inappropriate individual relationship. Boarding staff
  follow this identically to day-school staff — mirrors the Child
  Protection & Safeguarding Policy §7.8 and Boarding Regulations §6.5
  exactly, not a separately worded version.
- **Physical contact:** limited to what is necessary, appropriate, and
  consistent with the child's age (e.g. first-aid response, comforting
  a distressed young child) — never punitive or disciplinary physical
  contact of any kind.
- **Confidentiality:** staff do not discuss a specific student's
  welfare, academic, or disciplinary matter outside those with a
  legitimate need to know (mirrors the Data Protection & Privacy
  Policy's need-to-know principle, applied here to staff conduct
  specifically rather than system access).
- **Off-duty conduct:** a staff member's conduct outside school hours
  is a conduct matter where it involves a current student, suggests an
  unsafe attitude toward children, or seriously damages the
  institution's reputation — not a blanket monitoring of private life
  beyond that.
- **Representing the institution:** staff communicating with parents or
  media on SHRS's behalf follow the Communication Policy (Governance
  Master Register, Tier 3, not yet drafted) once it exists; until then,
  formal communications are routed through VP Administration or the
  CEO, not made independently.

## 6. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| VP Administration | Owns this policy; handles conduct breach investigations not involving a safeguarding dimension. |
| Designated Safeguarding Lead (Phase D Framework) | Handles any conduct concern with a safeguarding dimension, per that framework's reporting lines — not VP Administration alone. |
| Principals / Head Teacher | First point of contact for a conduct concern within their institution; escalate to VP Administration or the DSL as appropriate. |
| All staff | Model and uphold these standards; report a colleague's breach rather than staying silent. |

## 7. Procedure

1. A conduct concern is raised with the relevant Principal/Head Teacher
   or directly with VP Administration.
2. Where the concern has any safeguarding dimension, it is handled
   under the Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy §7.6 (allegations
   against a staff member) instead of this policy's general procedure —
   this policy does not create a second, competing process for the
   same situation.
3. For a non-safeguarding conduct breach: VP Administration
   investigates, the staff member is given an opportunity to respond,
   and an outcome (informal guidance, formal warning, or referral to
   the CEO for more serious action) is decided and recorded.
4. A serious breach may result in suspension pending investigation (a
   neutral protective measure, not a finding of guilt) or termination,
   decided by the CEO in consultation with VP Administration.

## 8. Compliance

No specific Nigerian labour-law citation is made regarding staff
discipline/termination procedure — that requires confirmation from
legal counsel familiar with Nigerian employment law, not an assumption
made here.

## 9. Appeals

A staff member may appeal a conduct-breach finding to the CEO, who was
not part of the original investigation — the same independent-reviewer
convention used throughout this document ecosystem
(`phase-b-dependency-review.md` §3), pending the more detailed Staff
Discipline Policy (Governance Master Register, HR, recommended next by
the HR Governance Framework).

## 10. Exceptions

None defined. Where this policy and the Child Protection & Safeguarding
Policy both apply to the same incident, the Safeguarding Policy's
procedure governs (Section 7, step 2 above) — a named exception, not
an oversight.

## 11. Review cycle

Annual, or immediately after any conduct-breach finding that reveals a
gap in this policy.
