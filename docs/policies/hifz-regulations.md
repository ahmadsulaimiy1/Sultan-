## 2. Purpose

To give Qur'an College's 24–36 month memorisation programme (already
publicly described at `pages/academics-quran-college.html`) a written
standard: how much a student memorises and by when, how retention is
verified (not just first-pass memorisation), how progress is assessed,
and how the whole framework connects to the Ijazah a student ultimately
earns.

## 3. Scope

All Qur'an College students in the Hifz (memorisation) programme,
whether boarding or day.

## 4. Definitions

- **Hifz** — the memorisation of the Qur'an.
- **Muraja'ah** — the structured revision of previously memorised
  material, without which memorisation decays; treated here as equal in
  importance to new memorisation, not an afterthought.
- **Juz'** — one of the Qur'an's 30 equal parts, the standard unit for
  measuring memorisation progress.
- **Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah** — as defined in the Ijazah Governance
  Framework (IQ-02); the teacher directly supervising a student's
  memorisation.
- **Tajweed** — the rules of correct Qur'anic recitation, assessed
  alongside memorisation accuracy, not as a separate track.

## 5. Policy Statement

Memorisation without retention is not progress — Muraja'ah is scheduled
and tracked with the same rigor as new memorisation, not left to a
student's own initiative. Progress is measured in verified, examined
Juz' completions, not in time elapsed. Tajweed accuracy is inseparable
from memorisation — a Juz' "completed" with poor Tajweed is not treated
as complete. The programme respects each student's individual pace
within the 24–36 month framework rather than forcing uniform weekly
output regardless of ability.

## 6. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah | Daily/weekly progress tracking, per-Juz' assessment, escalating a student who is falling behind to the Principal rather than silently carrying them forward. |
| Principal, Qur'an College | Overall programme standard; decides when a student is ready for Ijazah examination per the Ijazah Governance Framework (IQ-02). |
| Student | Daily memorisation and Muraja'ah effort; honest self-report of retention difficulty rather than concealing it. |
| Guardian | Supports home revision where the student boards part-time or is a day student; for boarding students, this responsibility shifts largely to the boarding structure (Boarding Regulations, SD-04). |

## 7. Procedures

### 7.1 Memorisation and Muraja'ah standards
- A daily new-memorisation target (recommend a defined number of lines
  or a fraction of a page, to be set by Qur'an College's own teaching
  staff based on real classroom experience — not invented here as an
  arbitrary figure) plus a daily Muraja'ah session covering previously
  memorised material, recorded by the assigned Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah in a
  per-student progress log (Section 9).
- A weekly consolidation check: the Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah verifies the
  week's new memorisation is retained without prompting, before moving
  to new material — a student who cannot retain the week's target is
  held at that point for additional Muraja'ah, not advanced regardless.
- A rotating revision schedule ensures every previously memorised Juz'
  is revisited on a defined cycle (the specific cycle length — e.g.
  weekly, fortnightly — is a decision for Qur'an College's teaching
  staff, informed by real cohort size and staffing, not fixed here),
  tracked separately from new-memorisation performance in the
  student's progress log, since strong new memorisation with weak
  Muraja'ah is a real, common failure mode this framework is designed
  to catch early.

### 7.2 Assessment framework
- Each Juz' is formally assessed on completion — covering memorisation
  accuracy, retention (via spot-check recitation of earlier sections,
  not just the newest material), and Tajweed — by the assigned
  Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah.
- A full programme-completion assessment (distinct from the per-Juz'
  checks) is conducted before a student is put forward for Ijazah
  examination — see Section 7.6.
- **The specific pass standard for a per-Juz' assessment is not set by
  this draft** — this is squarely a decision for Qur'an College's own
  qualified staff, per the same principle stated in the Ijazah
  Governance Framework.

### 7.3 Retention requirements
A student is not considered to have "completed" the programme, even
after memorising all 30 Juz', until sustained retention is verified
across a defined post-completion period (recommend a minimum window,
to be set by Qur'an College leadership) — preventing a scenario where a
student is certified immediately after finishing memorisation but
before retention is actually proven.

### 7.4 Progression framework
- Progression through the 30 Juz' is sequential and pace-based, not
  time-based — a student who needs the full 36 months is not treated
  as behind if their Muraja'ah retention is strong; a student who
  finishes memorisation faster does not skip the retention-
  verification period (Section 7.3).
- A student showing a sustained inability to progress (not a temporary
  difficulty) is reviewed by the Principal for a revised individual
  plan — not silently left to continue indefinitely without
  adjustment, and not summarily removed from the programme without
  that review.

### 7.5 Completion standards
Full programme completion requires: all 30 Juz' memorised, the
retention-verification period (Section 7.3) passed, and Tajweed
standard confirmed throughout — not memorisation alone. This is the
gate before a student is eligible for Ijazah examination.

### 7.6 Certification requirements and Ijazah relationship
This document governs the memorisation programme itself; **it does not
grant the Ijazah**. Once Section 7.5's completion standards are met,
the Principal puts the student forward for examination under the
Ijazah Governance Framework (IQ-02), which is examined by the named
Ijazah-granting scholar(s) — a distinct step, deliberately kept in a
separate document, so that day-to-day teaching supervision (this
document) and formal credentialing authority (the Ijazah framework) are
never exercised by the same undifferentiated process.

### 7.7 Special circumstances
- A student whose progress is interrupted by illness, extended family
  absence, or a documented hardship is not penalised against the
  standard progression pace (Section 7.4) — the Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah
  adjusts the individual plan to account for the interruption, rather
  than treating a documented absence the same as an ordinary lag.
- **This is a new provision, not previously stated in v1.0** — added
  because the progression framework already allowed for individual
  pace, but had never explicitly addressed a documented interruption
  distinct from ordinary variation in ability.

### 7.8 Progress recognition
- Juz' completions and full programme completion are marked with
  appropriate recognition (the specific form — a certificate, a
  ceremony — is a decision for Qur'an College leadership, not invented
  here) distinct from the formal Ijazah itself (Section 7.6), so a
  student's incremental progress is genuinely celebrated, not only the
  final credential.

## 8. Monitoring and Compliance

The Principal, Qur'an College reviews per-student progress logs
termly across the cohort, to confirm the standards in Section 7 are
being applied consistently by every Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah, not just
documented as policy.

## 9. Records and Documentation

Per-student progress logs (daily/weekly targets, Muraja'ah tracking,
per-Juz' assessment results) are maintained by the assigned
Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah, retained per the Records Retention Policy
(IT-04).

## 10. Related Policies

Ijazah Governance Framework (IQ-02, this document feeds directly into
it), Boarding Regulations (SD-04, most Hifz students board), Academic
Regulations (AC-02 §7.3, which explicitly exempts Qur'an College from
the secular promotion framework in favour of this document).

## 11. Exceptions

A student joining Qur'an College with significant prior memorisation
(e.g. transferring from another Hifz programme) may be placed at an
appropriate point in the sequence following an initial assessment,
rather than restarting from Juz' 1 — this exception should be confirmed
by Qur'an College leadership as standard practice, not assumed.

## 12. Appeals and Complaints

A student or guardian who disputes a Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah's retention or
Tajweed assessment may request review by the Principal — the same
independent-reviewer convention used throughout this document
ecosystem.

## 13. Review and Amendment

Annual, reviewed jointly with the Ijazah Governance Framework (IQ-02)
so the two stay aligned. No specific external Qur'anic-education
accreditation standard is cited here (e.g. any formal alignment with
Saudi curriculum certification bodies) because that alignment has not
been verified in enough detail to state as fact — a matter for Qur'an
College leadership to confirm.
