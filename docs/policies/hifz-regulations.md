# SHRS Hifz Regulations

**Status:** DRAFT v0.1 — not yet adopted. Intended as one of SHRS's
defining governance documents, not a generic memorisation guide.
**Owner (proposed):** Principal, Qur'an College (Shaykh Ahmad Ibrahim).
**Review cycle:** Annual.
**Related documents:** Ijazah Governance Framework (Phase A — this
document feeds directly into it, see Section 11 below), Boarding
Regulations (most Hifz students board — see this document's own
Section 8 for where that's addressed), Academic Regulations
(Section 6.2 of that document explicitly exempts Qur'an College from
the secular promotion framework in favour of this document).

> **Before this governs a real decision:** the daily/weekly memorisation
> targets, retention standards, and progression framework below are
> proposed structure grounded in common Hifz-programme practice, not a
> record of what SHRS's Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah staff currently apply.
> This must be reviewed and confirmed by Qur'an College's own scholarly
> and teaching staff before adoption — they hold the subject-matter
> authority this document formalises, not the other way around.

## 1. Purpose

To give Qur'an College's 24–36 month memorisation programme (already
publicly described at `pages/academics-quran-college.html`) a written
standard: how much a student memorises and by when, how retention is
verified (not just first-pass memorisation), how progress is assessed,
and how the whole framework connects to the Ijazah a student ultimately
earns.

## 2. Scope

All Qur'an College students in the Hifz (memorisation) programme,
whether boarding or day.

## 3. Definitions

- **Hifz** — the memorisation of the Qur'an.
- **Muraja'ah** — the structured revision of previously memorised
  material, without which memorisation decays; treated here as equal in
  importance to new memorisation, not an afterthought.
- **Juz'** — one of the Qur'an's 30 equal parts, the standard unit for
  measuring memorisation progress.
- **Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah** — as defined in the Ijazah Governance
  Framework; the teacher directly supervising a student's memorisation.
- **Tajweed** — the rules of correct Qur'anic recitation, assessed
  alongside memorisation accuracy, not as a separate track.

## 4. Guiding principles

1. Memorisation without retention is not progress — Muraja'ah is
   scheduled and tracked with the same rigor as new memorisation, not
   left to a student's own initiative.
2. Progress is measured in verified, examined Juz' completions, not in
   time elapsed — a student who has spent 20 months in the programme
   has not necessarily completed 20 months' worth of material.
3. Tajweed accuracy is inseparable from memorisation — a Juz'
   "completed" with poor Tajweed is not treated as complete.
4. The programme respects each student's individual pace within the
   24–36 month framework rather than forcing uniform weekly output
   regardless of ability.

## 5. Memorisation & Muraja'ah standards

### 5.1 Daily targets
- A daily new-memorisation target (recommend a defined number of lines
  or a fraction of a page, to be set by Qur'an College's own teaching
  staff based on real classroom experience — not invented here as an
  arbitrary figure) plus a daily Muraja'ah session covering previously
  memorised material.
- Daily targets are recorded by the assigned Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah in a
  per-student progress log.

### 5.2 Weekly targets
- A weekly consolidation check: the Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah verifies the
  week's new memorisation is retained without prompting, before moving
  to new material — a student who cannot retain the week's target is
  held at that point for additional Muraja'ah, not advanced regardless.

### 5.3 The Muraja'ah framework
- A rotating revision schedule ensures every previously memorised Juz'
  is revisited on a defined cycle (the specific cycle length — e.g.
  weekly, fortnightly — is a decision for Qur'an College's teaching
  staff, informed by real cohort size and staffing, not fixed here).
- Muraja'ah performance is tracked separately from new-memorisation
  performance in the student's progress log, since strong new
  memorisation with weak Muraja'ah is a real, common failure mode this
  framework is designed to catch early.

## 6. Assessment framework

- Each Juz' is formally assessed on completion — covering memorisation
  accuracy, retention (via spot-check recitation of earlier sections,
  not just the newest material), and Tajweed — by the assigned
  Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah.
- A full programme-completion assessment (distinct from the
  per-Juz' checks) is conducted before a student is put forward for
  Ijazah examination — see Section 11.
- **The specific pass standard for a per-Juz' assessment is not set by
  this draft** — this is squarely a decision for Qur'an College's own
  qualified staff, per the same principle stated in the Ijazah
  Governance Framework.

## 7. Retention requirements

A student is not considered to have "completed" the programme, even
after memorising all 30 Juz', until sustained retention is verified
across a defined post-completion period (recommend a minimum window,
to be set by Qur'an College leadership) — preventing a scenario where
a student is certified immediately after finishing memorisation but
before retention is actually proven.

## 8. Teacher, student & parent responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah | Daily/weekly progress tracking, per-Juz' assessment, escalating a student who is falling behind to the Principal rather than silently carrying them forward. |
| Principal, Qur'an College | Overall programme standard; decides when a student is ready for Ijazah examination per the Ijazah Governance Framework. |
| Student | Daily memorisation and Muraja'ah effort; honest self-report of retention difficulty rather than concealing it. |
| Guardian | Supports home revision where the student boards part-time or is a day student; for boarding students, this responsibility shifts largely to the boarding structure — see the Boarding Regulations, which this document does not duplicate. |

## 9. Progression framework

- Progression through the 30 Juz' is sequential and pace-based, not
  time-based — a student who needs the full 36 months is not treated
  as behind if their Muraja'ah retention is strong; a student who
  finishes memorisation faster does not skip the retention-verification
  period in Section 7.
- A student showing a sustained inability to progress (not a temporary
  difficulty) is reviewed by the Principal for a revised individual
  plan — not silently left to continue indefinitely without
  adjustment, and not summarily removed from the programme without
  that review.

## 10. Completion standards

Full programme completion requires: all 30 Juz' memorised, the
retention-verification period (Section 7) passed, and Tajweed standard
confirmed throughout — not memorisation alone. This is the gate before
a student is eligible for Ijazah examination.

## 11. Certification requirements & Ijazah relationship

This document governs the memorisation programme itself; **it does not
grant the Ijazah**. Once Section 10's completion standards are met,
the Principal puts the student forward for examination under the
Ijazah Governance Framework (Phase A), which is examined by the named
Ijazah-granting scholar(s) — a distinct step, deliberately kept in a
separate document, so that day-to-day teaching supervision (this
document) and formal credentialing authority (the Ijazah framework)
are never exercised by the same undifferentiated process.

## 12. Appeals & exceptions

- A student or guardian who disputes a Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah's
  retention or Tajweed assessment may request review by the Principal
  — the same independent-reviewer convention used throughout this
  document ecosystem.
- **Exception:** a student joining Qur'an College with significant
  prior memorisation (e.g. transferring from another Hifz programme)
  may be placed at an appropriate point in the sequence following an
  initial assessment, rather than restarting from Juz' 1 — this
  exception should be confirmed by Qur'an College leadership as
  standard practice, not assumed.

## 13. Compliance

No specific external Qur'anic-education accreditation standard is
cited here (e.g. any formal alignment with Saudi curriculum
certification bodies, referenced generally on the live Qur'an College
page) because that alignment has not been verified in enough detail to
state as fact — a matter for Qur'an College leadership to confirm.

## 14. Review cycle

Annual, reviewed jointly with the Ijazah Governance Framework so the
two stay aligned.
