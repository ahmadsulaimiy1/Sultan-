## 2. Purpose

To ensure that when SHRS awards an Ijazah, the credential means exactly
what it claims to mean — a verified, examined, properly transmitted
qualification — so that a graduate's Ijazah carries the same weight
wherever it is presented, and so that the institution can stand behind
every Ijazah it has ever issued.

## 3. Scope

All Qur'an College students in the 24–36 month programme, and any
Ijazah issued in Qur'an memorisation, Tajweed, or a related discipline
under the SHRS name.

## 4. Definitions

- **Ijazah** — a formal certification that a student has completed a
  defined course of study to a verified standard, under a qualified
  teacher's authority, within an unbroken chain of transmission.
- **Sanad** — the chain of transmission connecting a teacher's
  authority to grant an Ijazah back through their own teachers.
- **Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah** — a teacher qualified to supervise Qur'an
  memorisation.
- **Ijazah-granting authority** — the specific, named scholar(s) at
  SHRS recognised as holding the standing to grant an Ijazah in their
  own right (to be confirmed and listed by name, not assumed).
- **Revocation** — the rare act of withdrawing a previously granted
  Ijazah where it is later established the credential was granted in
  error or based on fraudulent examination conduct (Section 7.6).

## 5. Policy Statement

An Ijazah is earned through verified performance against a defined
standard (see the Tajweed Assessment Policy, IQ-04, once drafted) —
never awarded for attendance or duration alone. The chain of
transmission (*sanad*) for each Ijazah-granting teacher at SHRS should
be documented and available for verification, since the credential's
credibility rests on it. Every Ijazah issued under the SHRS name is
logged in a permanent institutional register — so a graduate, or a
third party, can verify it decades later. The standard is consistent
across cohorts — the same examination rigor applies whether a student
took 24 months or 36 months to reach it.

## 6. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Principal, Qur'an College | Overall accountability for Ijazah standards and the register. |
| Named Ijazah-granting scholar(s) *(to be confirmed and listed)* | Personally examine and certify each candidate before an Ijazah is issued; participate in a revocation review (Section 7.6) if ever needed. |
| Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah staff | Day-to-day memorisation supervision and progress tracking feeding into the final examination (see Hifz Regulations, IQ-01). |
| Registrar | Maintains the permanent Ijazah register (separate from, but linkable to, the Parent Portal's academic records); responds to third-party verification requests (Section 7.5). |

## 7. Procedures

### 7.1 Eligibility for examination
A student is put forward for Ijazah examination only once their
Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah confirms completion of the full memorisation and
Tajweed standard defined in the Hifz Regulations (IQ-01) and Tajweed
Assessment Policy (IQ-04, not yet drafted — until it exists, this
criterion is not yet fully operable and should not be treated as
settled).

### 7.2 Examination
- Conducted by the named Ijazah-granting scholar(s), not the student's
  own day-to-day Muhaffiz alone, to separate teaching from
  certification.
- Assessed against the Tajweed Assessment Policy's defined standard,
  covering memorisation accuracy, Tajweed application, and recitation
  fluency.
- A failed examination results in a defined remediation period, not an
  automatic re-sit without further study.

### 7.3 Granting
On successful examination, the Ijazah is granted and signed by the
examining scholar(s), stating explicitly what has been certified (e.g.
full Qur'an memorisation with Tajweed, or a partial/juz-level
certification if SHRS chooses to offer graduated credentials — a
decision for Qur'an College leadership, not assumed here).

### 7.4 The permanent register
- Every Ijazah issued is recorded permanently: student name, date,
  examining scholar(s), what was certified, and a unique reference
  number.
- The register is retained indefinitely (an Ijazah should be
  verifiable by a graduate or a third party years later) — this is a
  different retention standard from the Parent Portal's own data, and
  is named as an explicit exception in the Records Retention Policy
  (IT-04) §7.1.
- Whether the register is eventually digitised into the Parent/Student
  Portal, and if so with what public-verification mechanism, is a
  Phase 2/3 product decision (see `docs/parent-portal-phase2-3-
  roadmap.md`), not something this governance document builds.

### 7.5 Verification requests
- A third party (an employer, another institution) may request
  verification that a named individual holds an SHRS Ijazah. The
  Registrar confirms against the permanent register (Section 7.4) and
  responds with only what the register states (date, what was
  certified) — not additional academic or personal information about
  the graduate, consistent with the Data Protection & Privacy Policy's
  (IT-02) need-to-know principle.
- **This is a new provision, not previously stated in v1.0** — the
  register's whole purpose (Section 7.4) is future verification, but
  v1.0 never stated the procedure for actually handling a verification
  request when one arrives.

### 7.6 Revocation
- In the rare event it is established, after the fact, that an Ijazah
  was granted in error (a genuine assessment mistake) or through
  fraudulent examination conduct, the Principal, Qur'an College,
  convenes the named Ijazah-granting scholar(s) to review the
  original examination record before any revocation decision.
- A revocation is recorded in the permanent register (Section 7.4)
  alongside the original entry — the record is never deleted, only
  annotated, so the register's own history stays verifiable.
- **This is a new provision, not previously stated in v1.0.** No
  revocation has occurred; this section exists so a credible process
  exists before it's ever needed, not improvised under pressure if it
  is.

## 8. Monitoring and Compliance

The Principal, Qur'an College reviews the permanent register annually
for completeness (every examined student has an entry) and reviews any
verification request (Section 7.5) log for volume and pattern.

## 9. Records and Documentation

The permanent Ijazah register (Section 7.4) is retained indefinitely —
the one explicit exception to the Records Retention Policy's (IT-04)
general periods, named there directly. Verification-request logs
(Section 7.5) and any revocation record (Section 7.6) are retained
alongside it.

## 10. Related Policies

Hifz Regulations (IQ-01), Records Retention Policy (IT-04, for the
indefinite-retention exception this framework requires), Data
Protection & Privacy Policy (IT-02, for verification-request data
handling).

## 11. Exceptions

Any graduated/partial-credential offering (Section 7.3) should be added
explicitly once decided, not left ambiguous.

## 12. Appeals and Complaints

A student who disputes an examination outcome may request re-
examination by a different named Ijazah-granting scholar where more
than one exists, or a documented review of the original examination.

## 13. Review and Amendment

Biennial, or immediately if an Ijazah-granting scholar's status
changes (e.g. a new scholar joins, confirming their own chain of
transmission before they begin granting Ijazaat under the SHRS name).
This framework does not claim to establish religious authority — that
authority belongs to the qualified scholars themselves, per Islamic
scholarly tradition, and this document's role is purely to give that
existing authority a consistent institutional process and a permanent
record.
