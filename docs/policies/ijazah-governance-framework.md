# SHRS Ijazah Governance Framework

**Status:** DRAFT v0.1 — not yet adopted. This is the highest
credentialing-integrity gap on the Governance Master Register: the
site already publicly advertises Ijazaat as Qur'an College's programme
outcome (`pages/academics-quran-college.html`), but how that credential
is earned, verified, and awarded has never been written down.
**Owner (proposed):** Principal, Qur'an College (Shaykh Ahmad Ibrahim).
**Review cycle:** Biennial.
**Depends on:** Hifz Regulations and Tajweed Assessment Policy
(Governance Master Register, Phase B — this framework references them
but a full build-out should follow once those exist).

> **Before this governs a real decision:** an Ijazah in Qur'anic
> recitation/memorisation is a recognised credential within the
> Islamic scholarly tradition (an unbroken chain of transmission,
> *sanad*, from teacher to student). This document proposes an
> institutional governance layer around that tradition for SHRS — it
> does not itself establish religious authority to grant an Ijazah,
> which properly rests with the qualified Islamic scholars on staff
> (e.g. Shaykh Ahmad Ibrahim, Shaykh Abubakr Solah), not with an
> administrative document. This framework should be reviewed and
> approved by SHRS's own Qur'an College scholarly leadership before
> adoption, not imposed on them from outside their expertise.

---

## 1. Purpose

To ensure that when SHRS awards an Ijazah, the credential means exactly
what it claims to mean — a verified, examined, properly transmitted
qualification — so that a graduate's Ijazah carries the same weight
wherever it is presented, and so that the institution can stand behind
every Ijazah it has ever issued.

## 2. Scope

All Qur'an College students in the 24–36 month programme, and any
Ijazah issued in Qur'an memorisation, Tajweed, or a related discipline
under the SHRS name.

## 3. Definitions

- **Ijazah** — a formal certification that a student has completed a
  defined course of study to a verified standard, under a qualified
  teacher's authority, within an unbroken chain of transmission.
- **Sanad** — the chain of transmission connecting a teacher's
  authority to grant an Ijazah back through their own teachers.
- **Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah** — a teacher qualified to supervise Qur'an
  memorisation.
- **Ijazah-granting authority** — the specific, named scholar(s) at
  SHRS recognised as holding the standing to grant an Ijazah in their
  own right (to be confirmed and listed by name, not assumed).

## 4. Principles

1. An Ijazah is earned through verified performance against a defined
   standard (see the Tajweed Assessment Policy, Phase B) — never
   awarded for attendance or duration alone.
2. The chain of transmission (*sanad*) for each Ijazah-granting teacher
   at SHRS should be documented and available for verification, since
   the credential's credibility rests on it.
3. Every Ijazah issued under the SHRS name is logged in a permanent
   institutional register — so a graduate, or a third party, can
   verify it decades later.
4. The standard is consistent across cohorts — the same examination
   rigor applies whether a student took 24 months or 36 months to
   reach it.

## 5. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Principal, Qur'an College | Overall accountability for Ijazah standards and the register. |
| Named Ijazah-granting scholar(s) *(to be confirmed and listed)* | Personally examine and certify each candidate before an Ijazah is issued. |
| Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah staff | Day-to-day memorisation supervision and progress tracking feeding into the final examination (see Hifz Regulations, Phase B). |
| Registrar | Maintains the permanent Ijazah register (separate from, but linkable to, the Parent Portal's academic records). |

## 6. Procedures

### 6.1 Eligibility for examination
- A student is put forward for Ijazah examination only once their
  Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah confirms completion of the full memorisation and
  Tajweed standard defined in the Hifz Regulations and Tajweed
  Assessment Policy (both Phase B — until those exist, this criterion
  is not yet fully operable and should not be treated as settled).

### 6.2 Examination
- Conducted by the named Ijazah-granting scholar(s), not the student's
  own day-to-day Muhaffiz alone, to separate teaching from
  certification.
- Assessed against the Tajweed Assessment Policy's defined standard,
  covering memorisation accuracy, Tajweed application, and recitation
  fluency.
- A failed examination results in a defined remediation period, not an
  automatic re-sit without further study.

### 6.3 Granting
- On successful examination, the Ijazah is granted and signed by the
  examining scholar(s), stating explicitly what has been certified
  (e.g. full Qur'an memorisation with Tajweed, or a partial/juz-level
  certification if SHRS chooses to offer graduated credentials — a
  decision for Qur'an College leadership, not assumed here).

### 6.4 The permanent register
- Every Ijazah issued is recorded permanently: student name, date,
  examining scholar(s), what was certified, and a unique reference
  number.
- The register is retained indefinitely (an Ijazah should be
  verifiable by a graduate or a third party years later) — this is a
  different retention standard from the Parent Portal's own data, and
  should be flagged as an explicit exception in the Data Protection &
  Privacy Policy's retention section once that section is finalised.
- Whether the register is eventually digitised into the Parent/Student
  Portal, and if so with what public-verification mechanism, is a
  Phase 2/3 product decision (see `docs/parent-portal-phase2-3-roadmap.md`),
  not something this governance document builds.

## 7. Compliance

This framework does not claim to establish religious authority — that
authority belongs to the qualified scholars themselves, per Islamic
scholarly tradition, and this document's role is purely to give that
existing authority a consistent institutional process and a permanent
record. It should be reviewed by SHRS's own scholarly staff before
adoption to confirm it doesn't conflict with established practice in
their tradition.

## 8. Appeals

A student who disputes an examination outcome may request re-examination
by a different named Ijazah-granting scholar where more than one
exists, or a documented review of the original examination.

## 9. Exceptions

None defined; any graduated/partial-credential offering (see 6.3) should
be added explicitly once decided, not left ambiguous.

## 10. Review cycle

Biennial, or immediately if an Ijazah-granting scholar's status
changes (e.g. a new scholar joins, confirming their own chain of
transmission before they begin granting Ijazaat under the SHRS name).
