## 2. Purpose

To protect every child at Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools — from the
2-year-olds in Nursery through boarding students at Qur'an College and
Secular College — from abuse, neglect, exploitation, and harm, whether
the risk originates inside the school, at home, online, from a peer,
or through radicalisation, and to give every adult connected to SHRS
(staff, boarding wardens, visitors, contractors, and the Parent
Portal's digital systems) a clear and unambiguous duty to act, without
delay, on any concern.

## 3. Scope

Applies to all students across all five institutions (Basic School,
Secular College, Qur'an College, Islamiyyah College, Online & Distance
Learning School), whether day or boarding; to all staff, board members, and
volunteers; to all visitors and contractors on any SHRS site; and to
all digital touchpoints where a child's information or communication
passes through an SHRS system, including the Parent Portal and the
website's AI assistant.

## 4. Definitions

- **Child** — any student under 18, and any student of any age with a
  care or support need that increases vulnerability.
- **Safeguarding** — the broader duty to promote welfare and prevent
  harm before it occurs (supervision ratios, safer recruitment, site
  security, digital data handling).
- **Child protection** — the narrower duty to act once a specific child
  is, or may be, suffering harm.
- **Physical abuse** — hitting, shaking, or otherwise causing physical
  harm, including fabricated or induced illness.
- **Emotional abuse** — persistent emotional maltreatment causing
  severe adverse effects on a child's development, including
  age-inappropriate expectations, humiliation, or exposure to
  domestic conflict.
- **Sexual abuse** — forcing or enticing a child to take part in
  sexual activities, whether or not the child is aware of what is
  happening, including non-contact activities (e.g. exposure to
  sexual material, online grooming).
- **Neglect** — persistent failure to meet a child's basic physical or
  psychological needs, including supervision, nutrition, medical care,
  or protection from harm.
- **Exploitation** — a child being manipulated or coerced into an
  activity for another's advantage, including child trafficking, child
  labour, and (relevant to a boarding population specifically) any
  coercive relationship exploiting the reduced parental oversight
  boarding involves.
- **Peer-on-peer abuse** — abuse of one child by another, including
  bullying (governed in detail by the Student Code of Conduct and the
  live Anti-Bullying & Disciplinary Policy), but also including abuse
  more serious than the general behavioural definition of bullying —
  this policy treats peer-on-peer abuse as a distinct safeguarding
  category, not automatically a lesser concern than adult-on-child
  abuse.
- **Radicalisation** — the process by which a person comes to support
  extremist ideology, potentially leading to harm to themselves or
  others; awareness of this risk is a standard, internationally
  recognised safeguarding category, addressed here as a general
  awareness duty (Section 7.12), not because of any specific known risk
  at SHRS.
- **Disclosure** — a child telling an adult, directly or indirectly,
  that they are being harmed.
- **Designated Safeguarding Lead (DSL)** — as defined in full in the
  Designated Safeguarding Lead Framework (SW-02); the named individual
  with lead responsibility for child protection decisions.
- **Deputy DSL** — as defined in the DSL Framework (SW-02); the
  institution- (or, at scale, campus-) level first point of contact.
- **Referral** — the act of passing a safeguarding concern to an
  external statutory body for formal investigation or intervention.
- **Early help** — support offered before a concern escalates to the
  threshold requiring statutory referral — distinct from, and normally
  preceding, a full referral.

## 5. Policy Statement

The child's welfare is paramount at SHRS, above institutional
reputation, staff relationships, or parent relationships. Every adult
connected to SHRS has an individual duty to report a concern — no one
may assume "someone else will." A concern is reported the same day it
arises; nothing is held over a weekend, an exam period, or a holiday.
Confidentiality is need-to-know, never blanket — a child raising a
concern is never promised secrecy. Safer recruitment applies to every
adult with unsupervised access to children, including boarding house
staff, drivers, and contractors, not teaching staff alone. Boarding
students carry additional risk (reduced parental oversight, overnight
supervision, peer dynamics) and are addressed specifically, not folded
silently into day-school provisions. Digital systems that touch
children's data or communications (Parent Portal, AI assistant) are
safeguarding surfaces, not just IT systems. Peer-on-peer abuse is taken
as seriously as adult-on-child abuse. And no member of staff is ever
left to make a serious safeguarding judgement alone, without a named,
accountable escalation path.

## 7. Procedures

### 7.1 Recognising signs of abuse and risk categories
Staff are trained (Section 7.16) to recognise indicators across every
category in Section 4 — physical, emotional, sexual abuse, neglect,
exploitation, peer-on-peer abuse, and radicalisation risk. No category
is treated as more "serious" than another by default; the DSL's
judgement on a case-by-case basis (Section 7.4) determines response,
not a pre-set hierarchy of concern types.

### 7.2 Responding to a disclosure
- Listen. Do not promise confidentiality. Do not ask leading questions.
  Do not investigate or confront the alleged source of harm.
- As soon as possible afterward, record what was said in the child's
  own words, the date, time, location, and who was present — factually,
  without interpretation.
- Report to the DSL (or Deputy DSL) the same day, in person or by the
  fastest available channel.

### 7.3 Recording and the incident log
- The DSL maintains a single, confidential safeguarding log — physical
  or digital — separate from academic and disciplinary records, with
  access restricted to the DSL and Deputy DSLs.
- Every entry includes: date, child(ren) involved, nature of concern,
  action taken, and outcome.

### 7.4 Internal action
- The DSL decides, case by case, whether the matter is: (a) handled
  internally as an early-help matter, (b) requires a referral to an
  external statutory body, or (c) requires both.

### 7.5 External referral
- Where a concern indicates the child may be at risk of harm requiring
  statutory intervention, the DSL makes contact with the relevant
  external body without delay.
- **The specific agencies, current contact details, and referral
  procedure for Ikorodu/Lagos State must be confirmed by the appointed
  DSL and kept in a maintained appendix — not hard-coded into this
  public policy, since agency contacts change.** Bodies likely relevant
  (to be verified, not assumed current): the Lagos State Ministry of
  Youth and Social Development, the Nigeria Police Force's Child
  Protection Network, and — for suspected trafficking or exploitation —
  NAPTIP (the National Agency for the Prohibition of Trafficking in
  Persons).
- Parents are informed unless doing so would place the child at
  greater risk (a judgment call for the DSL, ideally made with external
  advice).

### 7.6 Allegations against a staff member
- Handled by the Head of Schools / Administrator in consultation with the DSL and, where the
  allegation is serious, external advice — never by the accused staff
  member's direct line manager alone if that creates a conflict.
- The staff member may be suspended as a neutral protective measure
  while the matter is investigated; suspension is not a finding of
  guilt.
- Coordinates with the Staff Conduct Policy (HR-01) §7, which handles
  the employment-conduct dimension of the same allegation in parallel,
  not sequentially, with this section's safeguarding investigation.

### 7.7 Peer-on-peer abuse
- A concern about one student's behaviour toward another is never
  dismissed as "just kids" or automatically downgraded to a Student
  Code of Conduct (SD-02) matter — the DSL assesses every peer-on-peer
  report against the full definition in Section 4 before deciding
  whether it is a safeguarding matter, a disciplinary matter, or both.
- Both the student who disclosed and the student who is the subject of
  the concern are treated with proportionate care — a safeguarding
  response does not preclude fair treatment of the second student
  under the Student Code of Conduct's own procedure.
- Boarding houses carry elevated peer-on-peer risk given reduced
  supervision relative to daytime — Boarding Regulations (SD-04) §6.5
  applies alongside this section, not instead of it.

### 7.8 Boarding-specific provisions
- Boarding houses maintain overnight supervision ratios and a room-check
  log (defined fully in the Boarding Regulations, SD-04).
- Staff-student contact outside supervised hours (private messaging,
  one-on-one unsupervised meetings) is prohibited except through
  official, logged channels.

### 7.9 Visitor and contractor safeguarding
- Extends the Visitors Policy (SW-08, redeveloped in full): no visitor
  or contractor is left unsupervised with children regardless of
  sign-in status.

### 7.10 Online and digital safeguarding (Parent Portal & AI assistant)
- The Parent Portal (`docs/parent-portal.md`, `docs/parent-portal-
  audit.md`) holds real student and guardian data. Access is restricted
  to a child's own linked guardians and authorised staff — this is
  already implemented in the Portal's access-control logic and should
  be referenced, not re-specified, here.
- The website's AI assistant (AI Usage Policy, IT-05) must not be
  relied on to receive or triage a safeguarding disclosure — it is
  configured to direct any indication of a safeguarding concern to a
  named human contact, never to attempt to handle one itself. **This
  requires a configuration check against the assistant's actual current
  behaviour, not just a policy statement.**
- Online grooming and peer-on-peer digital harassment are addressed
  under this section's general online-safeguarding principle, in
  coordination with the Acceptable Use Policy (IT-03).

### 7.11 Safer recruitment
- Every adult with unsupervised access to children — teaching staff,
  boarding wardens, drivers, contractors with recurring site access —
  is vetted before appointment, not teaching staff alone.
- Vetting includes: identity verification, reference checks from
  previous employers specifically addressing safeguarding conduct (not
  just competence), and, for teaching staff, TRCN (Teachers Registration
  Council of Nigeria) verification where applicable, consistent with
  credentials already listed on the real governance roster.
- **SHRS does not yet have a formal Recruitment Policy** (Governance
  Master Register, HR, Tier 3, evaluated but not drafted in the HR
  Governance Framework) — this section states the safeguarding
  principle that policy must eventually operationalise in full
  recruitment-process detail; it does not substitute for that policy.

### 7.12 Radicalisation and extremism awareness
- Staff safeguarding training (Section 7.16) includes general awareness
  of radicalisation and extremism risk indicators, consistent with
  standard international safeguarding practice — **this is a general
  awareness duty, not a claim that SHRS has identified any specific
  risk of this kind**, and nothing in this section should be read as
  suggesting otherwise.
- A concern of this nature is reported and handled through the same
  disclosure and referral procedure (Sections 7.2–7.5) as any other
  safeguarding concern — this policy does not create a separate,
  parallel process for it.

### 7.13 Missing education and non-attendance follow-up
- A student with an unexplained or concerning attendance pattern is
  followed up by the Registrar and the class teacher — coordinating
  with the future Attendance Policy (Governance Master Register,
  Student Affairs, Tier 2, not yet drafted) once it exists.
- A prolonged, unexplained absence with no guardian contact is escalated
  to the DSL as a potential safeguarding concern, not treated only as
  an administrative attendance matter.

### 7.14 Multi-agency working and information sharing
- Where a concern involves another agency (a hospital, another school
  a sibling attends, a statutory body already involved with a family),
  the DSL shares information on a need-to-know basis consistent with
  the safeguarding-overrides-confidentiality principle in Section 11.
- Information sharing with an external agency is logged in the same
  central safeguarding log (Section 7.3), not handled informally
  outside it.

### 7.15 Whistleblowing
- A staff member with a concern about how a colleague — including a
  senior colleague or the DSL — has handled or failed to handle a
  safeguarding matter may raise it directly with the Head of Schools / Administrator or the Board,
  bypassing the normal management chain where necessary.
- A dedicated Whistleblowing Policy (Governance Master Register,
  Governance, Tier 3, not yet drafted) will formalise this further;
  until then, this section states the principle that raising a
  concern this way is protected, not discouraged.

### 7.16 Training requirements
- Every staff member receives safeguarding induction training before
  unsupervised contact with students, covering recognition (Section
  7.1), reporting (Section 7.2), and who their Deputy DSL is.
- The DSL and Deputy DSLs receive additional, more advanced training
  appropriate to their role (per the DSL Framework, SW-02 §7.5).
- Training is refreshed at a defined interval — **the specific interval
  and accredited provider need to be confirmed by the Board**, not
  assumed by this policy.

## 8. Monitoring and Compliance

The DSL (once appointed) reviews the safeguarding log monthly for
patterns across institutions and reports an aggregate summary to the
Board annually, per the Constitution & Governance Charter (GV-01) §6.
Until a DSL is appointed, the Head of Schools / Administrator and each institution's Principal/Head
Teacher jointly perform this review.

## 9. Records and Documentation

- The central safeguarding log (Section 7.3) is the primary record,
  retained per the Records Retention Policy (IT-04) — that policy
  proposes a specific retention period for this category (until age 25
  or 7 years, whichever is longer) precisely because of this
  document's sensitivity; this policy does not set its own,
  potentially conflicting, period.
- External referral correspondence (Section 7.5) is retained alongside
  the log entry it relates to.

## 11. Exceptions

None. Where this policy conflicts with a data-protection or
confidentiality provision elsewhere (e.g. the Data Protection & Privacy
Policy), the child's safety takes precedence — that policy's own §11
names this same exception, so the two documents state it consistently
rather than one asserting it unilaterally.

## 12. Appeals and Complaints

A staff member subject to disciplinary action arising from a
safeguarding investigation may appeal to the Board of Governors, through
a process that does not require re-exposing the child to further
involvement. Details of this appeal process should be finalised
alongside a future Staff Discipline Policy (Governance Master Register,
HR, recommended next).

## 13. Review and Amendment

Annual, or immediately following any serious incident, external
referral, DSL appointment, or change in Nigerian/Lagos State
child-protection law. Amendments below the level of a full policy
change (e.g. updating the external referral appendix) may be made by
the DSL without a full Board review; a substantive change requires
Board approval per Section 1.
