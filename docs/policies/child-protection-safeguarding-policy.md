# SHRS Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy

**Status:** DRAFT v0.1 — not yet adopted. This is the highest-priority
document on the Governance Master Register.
**Owner (proposed):** Designated Safeguarding Lead — **this role does
not yet exist at SHRS and its creation is the first decision this
policy depends on.** Until appointed, responsibility sits with the
CEO and the Principal of each institution acting jointly.
**Review cycle:** Annual, and immediately after any serious incident
or safeguarding referral.
**Depends on:** none (foundational) — but the Digital Safeguarding
section below depends on the Data Protection & Privacy Policy (also
being drafted).

> **Before this governs a real decision:** this document must be
> reviewed by the Board of Trustees, and by a lawyer or child-protection
> professional familiar with Lagos State practice, before adoption. It
> references the Child's Rights Act 2003 and the Lagos State Child's
> Rights Law 2007 by name because both are real, publicly known
> Nigerian legislation — but this draft does **not** certify that SHRS
> complies with them, and the specific external referral contacts named
> in Section 8 are placeholders that must be confirmed and kept current
> by whoever is appointed Designated Safeguarding Lead (DSL). Nothing
> here is legal advice.

## 1. Purpose

To protect every child at Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools — from the
2-year-olds in Nursery through boarding students at Qur'an College and
Royal College — from abuse, neglect, exploitation, and harm, whether
the risk originates inside the school, at home, online, or elsewhere,
and to give every adult connected to SHRS (staff, boarding wardens,
visitors, contractors, and the Parent Portal's digital systems) a clear
and unambiguous duty to act, without delay, on any concern.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all students across all four institutions (Nursery &
Primary, Royal College, Qur'an College, School of Arabic & Islamic
Studies), whether day or boarding; to all staff, board members, and
volunteers; to all visitors and contractors on any SHRS site; and to
all digital touchpoints where a child's information or communication
passes through an SHRS system, including the Parent Portal and the
website's AI assistant.

## 3. Definitions

- **Child** — any student under 18, and any student of any age with a
  care or support need that increases vulnerability.
- **Safeguarding** — the broader duty to promote welfare and prevent
  harm before it occurs (supervision ratios, safer recruitment, site
  security, digital data handling).
- **Child protection** — the narrower duty to act once a specific child
  is, or may be, suffering harm.
- **Abuse** — physical, emotional, sexual abuse, neglect, or
  exploitation (including online exploitation and, in a boarding
  context, peer-on-peer abuse).
- **Disclosure** — a child telling an adult, directly or indirectly,
  that they are being harmed.
- **Designated Safeguarding Lead (DSL)** — the named individual with
  lead responsibility for child protection decisions. **Recommendation:
  this role should sit at Executive Management level (not delegated to
  a single institution's staff) given SHRS now spans four institutions
  and a boarding population.** A Deputy DSL should be named per
  institution.

## 4. Principles

1. The child's welfare is paramount, above institutional reputation,
   staff relationships, or parent relationships.
2. Every adult at SHRS has an individual duty to report a concern — no
   one may assume "someone else will."
3. A concern is reported the same day it arises. Nothing is held over a
   weekend, an exam period, or a holiday.
4. Confidentiality is need-to-know, never blanket. A child raising a
   concern is never promised secrecy.
5. Safer recruitment applies to every adult with unsupervised access to
   children, including boarding house staff, drivers, and contractors —
   not teaching staff alone.
6. Boarding students carry additional risk (reduced parental oversight,
   overnight supervision, peer dynamics) and are addressed specifically,
   not folded silently into day-school provisions.
7. Digital systems that touch children's data or communications
   (Parent Portal, AI assistant) are safeguarding surfaces, not just IT
   systems.

## 6. Procedures

### 6.1 Responding to a disclosure
- Listen. Do not promise confidentiality. Do not ask leading questions.
  Do not investigate or confront the alleged source of harm.
- As soon as possible afterward, record what was said in the child's
  own words, the date, time, location, and who was present — factually,
  without interpretation.
- Report to the DSL (or Deputy DSL) the same day, in person or by the
  fastest available channel.

### 6.2 Recording and the incident log
- The DSL maintains a single, confidential safeguarding log — physical
  or digital — separate from academic and disciplinary records, with
  access restricted to the DSL and Deputy DSLs.
- Every entry includes: date, child(ren) involved, nature of concern,
  action taken, and outcome.

### 6.3 Internal action
- The DSL decides, case by case, whether the matter is: (a) handled
  internally (e.g. a pastoral or disciplinary matter with no external
  harm indicated), (b) requires a referral to an external statutory
  body, or (c) requires both.

### 6.4 External referral
- Where a concern indicates the child may be at risk of harm requiring
  statutory intervention, the DSL makes contact with the relevant
  external body without delay.
- **The specific agencies, current contact details, and referral
  procedure for Ikorodu/Lagos State must be confirmed by the appointed
  DSL and kept in a maintained appendix — not hard-coded into this
  public policy, since agency contacts change.** Bodies likely relevant
  (to be verified, not assumed current): the Lagos State Ministry of
  Youth and Social Development, the Nigeria Police Force's Child
  Protection Network, and — for suspected trafficking or exploitation —
  NAPTIP (the National Agency for the Prohibition of Trafficking in
  Persons).
- Parents are informed unless doing so would place the child at
  greater risk (a judgment call for the DSL, ideally made with external
  advice).

### 6.5 Allegations against a staff member
- Handled by the CEO in consultation with the DSL and, where the
  allegation is serious, external advice — never by the accused staff
  member's direct line manager alone if that creates a conflict.
- The staff member may be suspended as a neutral protective measure
  while the matter is investigated; suspension is not a finding of guilt.

### 6.6 Boarding-specific provisions
- Boarding houses maintain overnight supervision ratios and a room-check
  log (defined fully in the separate Boarding Regulations document).
- Staff-student contact outside supervised hours (private messaging,
  one-on-one unsupervised meetings) is prohibited except through
  official, logged channels.

### 6.7 Visitor and contractor safeguarding
- Extends the existing Visitors Policy (Policy VIII): no visitor or
  contractor is left unsupervised with children regardless of sign-in
  status.

## 7. Digital safeguarding (Parent Portal & AI assistant)

- The Parent Portal (`docs/parent-portal.md`, `docs/parent-portal-audit.md`)
  holds real student and guardian data. Access is restricted to a
  child's own linked guardians and authorised staff — this is already
  implemented in the Portal's access-control logic and should be
  referenced, not re-specified, here.
- The website's AI assistant (`docs/digital-assistant.md`) must not be
  relied on to receive or triage a safeguarding disclosure — it should
  be configured to direct any indication of a safeguarding concern to a
  named human contact, never to attempt to handle one itself. **This
  requires a configuration check against the assistant's actual current
  behaviour, not just a policy statement.**
- Any future AI Usage Policy (see Governance Master Register, Tier 2)
  should cross-reference this section rather than duplicate it.

## 8. Compliance

This policy is written with awareness of the Child's Rights Act 2003
(federal) and the Lagos State Child's Rights Law 2007, and of Nigeria's
broader child-protection institutional landscape (including NAPTIP for
trafficking-related concerns). **This document does not certify SHRS's
compliance with any of these instruments** — that determination requires
review by legal counsel or a child-protection professional with current
Lagos State expertise, which has not yet occurred.

## 9. Appeals

A staff member subject to disciplinary action arising from a
safeguarding investigation may appeal to the Board of Trustees, through
a process that does not require re-exposing the child to further
involvement. Details of this appeal process should be finalised
alongside the Staff Handbook (Governance Master Register, Tier 2).

## 10. Exceptions

None. Where this policy conflicts with a data-protection or
confidentiality provision elsewhere (e.g. the Data Protection & Privacy
Policy), the child's safety takes precedence — the Data Protection
Policy should include a matching exception clause for this reason.

## 11. Review cycle

Annual, or immediately following any serious incident, external
referral, or change in Nigerian/Lagos State child-protection law.
