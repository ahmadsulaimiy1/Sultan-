# Phase A + B Cross-Document Consistency Audit

*Performed after all eleven documents were drafted (six Phase A, five
Phase B), per the Phase B Master Directive's closing requirement. This
audit checks the documents against each other and against
`phase-b-dependency-review.md`'s canonical reference sheet — it does
not re-evaluate legal or regulatory soundness, which remains outside
what any drafting pass here can certify (see each document's own
"before this governs a real decision" note).*

**Documents in scope:** Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy, Data
Protection & Privacy Policy, Emergency Response Plan, Boarding
Regulations, Student Code of Conduct, Ijazah Governance Framework
(Phase A); Parent Handbook, Student Handbook, Academic Regulations,
Admissions Policy, Hifz Regulations (Phase B).

---

## 1. Terminology consistency — PASS, two defects found and fixed during this audit

Every term defined once (in one canonical document) and referenced,
not redefined, everywhere else — verified against the map in
`phase-b-dependency-review.md` §2. Two internal cross-reference errors
were found and corrected in the course of this audit, not left for a
future pass:

- **Hifz Regulations** pointed readers to "Section 12" twice (in the
  header's Related Documents line, and in Section 6) for the
  Ijazah-relationship content — Section 12 is actually "Appeals &
  exceptions"; the Ijazah relationship is Section 11. Both references
  corrected to Section 11.
- **Data Protection & Privacy Policy** §5 referred to "the retention
  section below," but no dedicated retention section exists in that
  document — the retention gap is named in §5 itself and picked up
  again in §10 (Compliance). The dangling forward-reference was
  removed and replaced with a direct pointer to §10.

No other dangling or mismatched section references were found in a
full pass over every `see Section X` / `§X` cross-reference in all
eleven documents.

## 2. Governance consistency — PASS, with one long-standing conflict correctly *not* re-resolved here

All eleven documents use the same governance structure: a Board of
Trustees above a CEO, four institution-level Principals/Head Teacher
(Royal College, Qur'an College, School of Arabic & Islamic Studies,
Nursery & Primary — explicitly, consistently, **not** a single overall
Principal), and named functional owners (VP Administration, Registrar,
ICT Head) drawn from the real governance roster. No document invents a
title that doesn't exist in `about-governance.html`.

**Carried forward, not resolved:** the conflict already flagged in the
Information Architecture Blueprint — the live policies page signs
Mr. Oladele Abdulwasiu Adebayo as "Principal," while the governance
roster lists him as "Head, Research & Development." All eleven new
documents use his roster title consistently and do not adopt
"Principal" for him. This audit does not resolve the conflict (only
the school can confirm his actual title) but confirms no new document
introduced a third variant.

## 3. Authority consistency — PASS, one new conflict surfaced (not previously flagged anywhere)

Every document assigns decision authority to a real, named role, and no
two documents assign the same decision to two different authorities.
One genuinely new conflict was found during this audit, not previously
documented anywhere in this project:

- The live **Assessment Policy (Policy I)** states that "correct and
  final interpretation is the sole responsibility of the Principal and
  Vice-Principal Academics" — a title, "Vice-Principal Academics," that
  does not exist anywhere in the governance roster. **Academic
  Regulations (Phase B) does not invent a person to fill that title.**
  Its appeals procedure (§11) names the conflict explicitly and
  substitutes the real, existing authority (the Principal/Head Teacher
  of the student's own institution) rather than silently assuming the
  phantom title resolves itself. This is now recorded in
  `phase-b-dependency-review.md` §1 alongside the Adebayo conflict, so
  future documents inherit the same awareness.

## 4. Appeals consistency — PASS

Confirmed: no two documents describe competing procedures for the same
kind of decision. Four genuinely distinct pathways exist, deliberately,
per `phase-b-dependency-review.md` §3:

| Decision type | Pathway | Defined in |
|---|---|---|
| Disciplinary (suspension or worse) | Escalate to CEO / Board committee | Student Code of Conduct §9 |
| Ijazah examination outcome | Re-examination by a different scholar | Ijazah Governance Framework §8 |
| Grade / promotion / academic standing | Class teacher → Registrar → Principal/Head Teacher | Academic Regulations §11 (new in Phase B) |
| General complaint not otherwise covered | — | Complaint Policy (IX, live) |

Every Phase B document that mentions an appeal (Parent Handbook §14,
Student Handbook §10, Boarding Regulations §8, Admissions Policy §14)
points to exactly one of these four rather than restating any of them,
and all four share the same underlying convention (independent
decision-maker, defined response window, written outcome) without
being merged into a single false "one appeals process" document.

## 5. Safeguarding consistency — PASS

The full disclosure/reporting/referral procedure exists in exactly one
place — the Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy. Every other
document that touches safeguarding (Boarding Regulations §§4, 5, 6.5;
Parent Handbook §10; Student Handbook §13; Emergency Response Plan's
cross-reference) summarises in one or two sentences and points back,
rather than restating the procedure with slightly different wording —
the exact drift risk the Master Directive named. The still-vacant
Designated Safeguarding Lead role is referenced identically (and
identically flagged as unfilled) everywhere it comes up, rather than
being silently assumed to exist in some documents and flagged as
missing in others.

## 6. Privacy consistency — PASS, with open items correctly *not* independently re-decided per document

Retention period, data-subject rights, and breach-notification timing
are named as open items in exactly one place (Data Protection & Privacy
Policy §§5, 7, 8, 10). Academic Regulations §10 and Admissions Policy
§13 both need a retention answer for their own record types, and both
correctly defer to that single open item rather than proposing their
own, potentially conflicting, retention period. No document
independently invents a retention number.

## 7. Student welfare consistency — PASS

Boarding-specific welfare provisions (supervision, room checks,
overnight medical response) live only in the Boarding Regulations and
are referenced, not duplicated, by the Parent Handbook, Student
Handbook, and Hifz Regulations. General (non-boarding) welfare and
discipline live only in the Student Code of Conduct and are referenced
the same way by the Student Handbook. No document creates a third,
slightly different welfare or discipline standard for a subset of
students.

---

## Summary

Eleven documents, seven consistency dimensions checked, two internal
defects found and corrected (both citation errors, not substantive
contradictions), one new authority conflict surfaced and recorded
(Vice-Principal Academics) alongside the one already known (Adebayo's
title). No competing procedures, no duplicated definitions, no
conflicting authorities were found. The ecosystem holds together as
one integrated framework expressed through eleven documents, not
eleven independent ones — with the explicit exception of the
governance-roster conflicts, which remain the school's decision to
resolve, not a drafting decision.

**Still true, and still binding, from Phase A:** nothing in these
eleven documents — individually or as a set — should be treated as
legal advice or a compliance confirmation. The open legal/regulatory
review items named in each document (Nigerian child-protection law,
the NDPA 2023, curriculum accreditation, boarding-specific regulation,
academic due-process standards) still require qualified external
review before Board adoption.
