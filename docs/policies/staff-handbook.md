**Status:** DRAFT v0.1 — not yet adopted.
**Owner (proposed):** VP Administration (Mrs. Anofi-Badmus Fatimat
Omolola).
**Review cycle:** Annual.
**Depends on:** the **Staff Conduct Policy** (drafted, Phase D — this
Handbook's Section 5 below is updated to point to it rather than the

Safeguarding Policy; Data Protection & Privacy Policy; Constitution &

> **Update, Phase D:** the Staff Conduct Policy this Handbook originally

---

## 1. Purpose

To be the one document a staff member reads to understand what SHRS
expects of them professionally, safeguarding-wise, and administratively
— across all four institutions — without needing to separately locate
nine live policies and the growing set of governance documents.

## 2. Scope

All staff across all four institutions, including boarding wardens and
any staff member with regular student contact.

## 3. Definitions

Reuses definitions already established elsewhere (Designated
Safeguarding Lead, personal data, misconduct) rather than restating
them — see `phase-b-dependency-review.md` §2 and
`phase-c-dependency-review.md`.

## 4. Roles & responsibilities (staff-facing summary)

A short table pointing to the Constitution & Governance Charter's full
authority structure (Section 6) rather than restating it:

| If you are... | Your first point of contact for a concern is... |
|---|---|
| A class teacher | Your institution's Principal / Head Teacher |
| A boarding warden | The Boarding Regulations' institutional lead (Principal, Qur'an College, or as confirmed) |
| Any staff member with a safeguarding concern | The Designated Safeguarding Lead (once appointed) — until then, any senior staff member, who must escalate the same day |

## 5. Professional conduct

**Governed in full by the Staff Conduct Policy (Phase D)** — this
Handbook does not restate its professional-boundary, physical-contact,
confidentiality, or off-duty-conduct standards; staff should read that
policy directly. Two things worth flagging here rather than there:
- Appearance and presentation are governed separately by the live
  Dress Code Policy (Policy IV) — neither document duplicates the
  other.
- A conduct concern is raised the same way as before this policy
  existed (with the relevant Principal/Head Teacher or VP
  Administration), now formalised in the Staff Conduct Policy §7 rather
  than handled ad hoc under general Charter authority.

## 6. Safeguarding responsibilities

A staff-facing summary only, pointing to the Child Protection &
Safeguarding Policy for the full procedure:
- Every staff member has an individual duty to report a concern the
  same day it arises (Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy §5).
- Every staff member must know who their institution's Deputy DSL is
  (Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy §6) — this Handbook does not
  restate the full responding-to-a-disclosure procedure (§7.2); staff
  should read that document directly, not rely on a summary for an
  actual disclosure.

## 7. Performance expectations

A slot for SHRS's actual performance-review structure. **A dedicated
Performance Management Policy is evaluated, not fully drafted, in the
HR Governance Framework (Phase C)** — until it exists in full, this
section states only the general expectation (punctuality, professional
development engagement, adherence to this Handbook and the policies it
references), not a detailed appraisal procedure this Handbook doesn't
own.

## 8. Professional development

Points to the Head of Research & Development (Mr. Oladele Abdulwasiu
Adebayo) as the real, existing role for professional development
coordination — a dedicated Professional Development Policy is
evaluated in the HR Governance Framework (Tier 4, lower urgency) rather
than drafted here.

## 9. Communication standards

Staff are expected to use the same controlled channels described in
the Parent Handbook (Portal, WhatsApp, email) when communicating with
guardians, and to escalate rather than independently promise outcomes
outside their authority (e.g. a class teacher should not independently
commit to an academic exception reserved for the Registrar under the
Academic Regulations §7.6).

## 10. Digital conduct

- Staff access to the Parent Portal's admin functions is role-scoped
  (per the Data Protection & Privacy Policy §6) — staff must not share
  admin tokens or credentials.
- Any staff use of the AI assistant or future AI systems is governed by
  the AI Usage Policy (Phase C) — this Handbook signposts rather than
  restates it.

## 11. Student interaction standards

Mirrors the Student Code of Conduct's expectations from the staff side
— respectful, non-discriminatory, safeguarding-conscious interaction —
without creating a second, competing description of what "respectful
conduct" means. Boarding-specific staff-student contact rules are in
the Boarding Regulations §7.5 and the Child Protection & Safeguarding
Policy §7.8 alone.

## 12. Grievance & discipline (staff)

The Staff Conduct Policy (Phase D) §7 now defines the conduct-breach
procedure and §9 its appeal pathway — this Handbook signposts rather
than restates them. A **Grievance Policy** and a more detailed **Staff
Discipline Policy** covering non-conduct disciplinary matters (e.g.
performance-related dismissal) remain evaluated, not drafted, in the
HR Governance Framework — recommended as the next two HR documents now
that the Staff Conduct Policy itself is done. Until they exist, a
non-conduct staff grievance is raised with VP Administration, escalating
to the CEO if unresolved, under the general authority structure in the
Constitution & Governance Charter §6.2.

## 13. Monitoring & compliance

VP Administration reviews this Handbook annually and immediately once
the Staff Conduct Policy, Performance Management Policy, or Grievance/
Discipline policies are actually drafted, replacing the placeholder
sections above with real cross-references rather than leaving them as
permanent stand-ins.

## 14. Review cycle

Annual.
