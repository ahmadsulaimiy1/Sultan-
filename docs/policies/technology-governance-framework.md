# SHRS Technology Governance Framework

**Status:** DRAFT v0.1 — not yet adopted. The umbrella document tying
together the technology policies already drafted in full and
evaluating the remaining named ones, rather than drafting everything
in one undifferentiated pass.
**Owner:** ICT Head (Mr. Oguntade Adebola Aliu).
**Review cycle:** Annual.
**Depends on:** Data Protection & Privacy Policy, Information Security
Policy, Acceptable Use Policy, Records Retention Policy, AI Usage
Policy (all Phase A/C, already drafted in full).

---

## 1. Purpose

To confirm the technology governance documents already drafted in full
cover SHRS's real current systems (the Parent Portal, the AI
assistant), and to evaluate the remaining named technology policies
honestly rather than draft all of them regardless of urgency.

## 3. Evaluation of remaining named items

| Policy | Needed now? | Reasoning | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cybersecurity Framework** | Not urgent | The technical substance already exists at implementation level (`docs/parent-portal-audit.md`) and at policy level (Information Security Policy). A standalone Cybersecurity Framework would mostly restate both as a higher-level governance document — valuable once SHRS's systems are more numerous/complex, not blocking anything today. | 3 |
| **Digital Learning Policy** | Not yet applicable | SHRS does not currently run a learning-management system or digital classroom platform — this project's digital footprint today is the marketing site, Parent Portal, and AI assistant, not classroom EdTech. Drafting this now would be governance for a system that doesn't exist. Revisit if/when a Student Portal or LMS is actually built (see `docs/parent-portal-phase2-3-roadmap.md`). | 4 |
| **Incident Response Policy** | Genuinely useful soon, not yet drafted | The Information Security Policy §7 already names "no formal incident-response runbook exists yet" as an open gap. This is the most concrete unmet need in this table — recommend drafting next, once Phase C's current batch is reviewed. | 2 |

## 4. Recommendation

The five full documents already cover SHRS's real, live technology
surface. Of the three remaining named items, only the **Incident
Response Policy** addresses a concrete, already-named gap (Information
Security Policy §7) rather than a hypothetical future system — recommend
it as the next technology document, ahead of the Cybersecurity
Framework or Digital Learning Policy, both of which are better
deferred until they'd govern something that actually exists.

## 5. Review cycle

Annual, or immediately if SHRS adds a Student Portal, LMS, or any new
system this framework's "not yet applicable" reasoning depends on.
