# SHRS AI Usage Policy

**Status:** DRAFT v0.1 — not yet adopted.
**Owner (proposed):** ICT Head (Mr. Oguntade Adebola Aliu), the same
proposed owner as the Data Protection & Privacy Policy.
**Review cycle:** Annual, or immediately on any material change to what
an SHRS AI system is allowed to do.
**Depends on:** Data Protection & Privacy Policy (Phase A), Child
Protection & Safeguarding Policy §7.10 (digital safeguarding),
`docs/digital-assistant.md` (the real, live system this policy
primarily governs today).

> **Before this governs a real decision:** SHRS today operates exactly
> one live AI system — the website's chat assistant
> (`docs/digital-assistant.md`), which is honestly self-disclosed as
> AI and answers only from published site content, saying plainly when
> something (fees, academic calendar, scholarships) isn't published
> yet. **Parent AI, Student AI, and Admissions AI systems do not exist
> today** — they are named in this policy as anticipated future
> touchpoints this policy is written to also cover, not as systems
> already running. Do not read any section below as a description of
> something currently live unless it explicitly says so.

---

## 1. Purpose

To state what any AI system operating under the SHRS name may and may
not do, how it must identify itself, what data it may access, when a
human must be involved instead, and how this scales if SHRS adds more
AI touchpoints (a Parent Portal assistant, a Student Portal assistant,
an admissions-screening assistant) beyond the one that exists today.

## 2. Scope

The live website chat assistant today; any future AI system operating
under the SHRS name, across the website, Parent Portal, a future
Student Portal, or admissions process.

## 3. Definitions

- **AI system** — any automated conversational or decision-support tool
  operating under the SHRS name, whether public-facing (the website
  assistant) or internal.
- **Hallucination** — a plausible-sounding but false or unverifiable
  statement generated by an AI system.
- **Human oversight** — a named staff member's ability to review,
  override, or escalate what an AI system says or does.

## 4. Principles

1. Every SHRS AI system honestly discloses that it is AI on first
   interaction — never impersonates a staff member or office. This is
   already true of the live assistant and is now a standing requirement
   for any future one.
2. An AI system states only what is actually published or verifiable —
   it does not guess at fees, academic calendar dates, scholarship
   criteria, or international-admission arrangements, and says plainly
   when something isn't published yet, pointing to a real contact
   instead. This is already the live assistant's behaviour (per
   `docs/digital-assistant.md`) and is now a policy requirement, not
   just an implementation choice that could quietly change.
3. Safeguarding overrides normal AI operation: an AI system must not
   attempt to handle a safeguarding disclosure itself — it must direct
   the user to a named human contact, per the Child Protection &
   Safeguarding Policy §7.10. **This requires a configuration check against
   the assistant's actual current behaviour** (as that section already
   notes), not just a policy statement.
4. An AI system does not make an admission, disciplinary, academic
   grading, or Ijazah-certification decision on its own — those remain
   human decisions under the authorities established in the Academic
   Regulations, Student Code of Conduct, Admissions Policy, and Ijazah
   Governance Framework respectively. An admissions-screening AI, if
   ever built, could support that process (e.g. summarising
   documentation) but the actual decision stays with the Registrar/
   Principal per the Admissions Policy — this policy does not grant an
   AI system decision authority those documents reserve for named human
   roles.

## 5. Appropriate use

- Answering published, verifiable questions about SHRS (the four
  institutions, admission steps, boarding terms, governance, contact
  details) — the live assistant's actual current scope.
- General academic help (writing, homework explanation, exam prep)
  using the AI's general knowledge, clearly distinguished from
  school-specific claims — again, the live assistant's actual current
  behaviour, now stated as policy rather than only as implementation.
- A future Parent/Student Portal assistant, if built, answering
  questions about a guardian's own linked child's data — but only
  within the same access boundaries the Parent Portal itself already
  enforces (a guardian sees only their own linked children — Data
  Protection & Privacy Policy §5), not a broader data access an AI
  interface might otherwise make tempting to add.

## 6. Human oversight

- A named staff member (ICT Head, or a future Digital Campus role) is
  accountable for reviewing the AI system's actual behaviour against
  this policy, not just its intended configuration — since an AI
  system's real output can drift from what its system prompt intends.
- Any AI system that could plausibly encounter a safeguarding
  disclosure, a distressed user, or a question it cannot safely answer
  must have a clear escalation path to a human — the live assistant
  already does this via WhatsApp escalation links; any future AI
  touchpoint must have an equivalent, not a lesser one.

## 7. Privacy & data handling

- The live assistant does not currently authenticate users or persist
  conversation history server-side (per `docs/digital-assistant.md`) —
  **this must be re-verified at each review**, since that could change
  with a future feature and this policy's privacy claims would need
  updating accordingly, not left stale.
- A future AI system with access to Parent Portal data must comply with
  the Data Protection & Privacy Policy in full — this policy does not
  create a separate, weaker data standard for AI-mediated access to the
  same data.

## 8. Hallucination risk

- Any AI system's tendency to generate a plausible but false answer is
  treated as a known, ongoing risk requiring the guardrails in Section
  4 (Principle 2), not a solved problem — the live assistant's
  restriction to published content is the current mitigation, and any
  future AI system must have an equivalent restriction appropriate to
  its scope, confirmed at each review rather than assumed to still
  hold.

## 9. Staff responsibilities

Staff are not required to treat an AI system's output as authoritative
without verification, particularly for anything touching a safeguarding,
academic, or admissions decision — the human authorities named in the
relevant policy remain accountable, not the AI system.

## 10. Educational use

Where an AI system (the live assistant, or a future classroom tool) is
used directly with students for academic help, its use should be
consistent with the Academic Integrity Policy once drafted (Governance
Master Register, Tier 1, not yet written) — this policy does not
attempt to set academic-integrity rules for AI-assisted work itself,
only notes that dependency.

## 11. Compliance

Written with awareness that the Data Protection & Privacy Policy's open
items (retention, breach notification) apply equally to any data an AI
system touches — this policy does not resolve those items independently
and risk contradicting that document.

## 12. Appeals

A guardian or student who believes an AI system gave a harmful,
incorrect, or inappropriate response may raise it through the existing
Complaint Policy (Policy IX) — the same signpost used throughout this
document ecosystem, not a new AI-specific complaints channel.

## 13. Exceptions

None defined; any AI system granted broader authority than Section 4
describes (e.g. an admissions-screening tool making an actual decision)
would require an explicit Board-approved amendment to this policy, not
a quiet expansion in practice.

## 14. Review cycle

Annual, or immediately when SHRS adds a new AI touchpoint (Parent AI,
Student AI, Admissions AI) — at which point this policy's Section 5
(Appropriate use) must be updated to reflect what that new system
actually does, not left describing only the original website assistant.
