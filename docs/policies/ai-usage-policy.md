# Policy IT-05 — AI Usage Policy

*Retrofitted to the full 13-section governance architecture and
substantially deepened, per the Tier 1 retrofit directive. Every
principle and scope boundary from v1.0 is preserved — none weakened,
and the core discipline holds: this policy still does not claim Parent
AI, Student AI, or Admissions AI exist today. New in v2.0: vendor/model
change management and a pre-launch review requirement for any new AI
touchpoint, tied directly to the Data Protection & Privacy Policy's
Data Protection Impact Assessment requirement.*

## 2. Purpose

To state what any AI system operating under the SHRS name may and may
not do, how it must identify itself, what data it may access, when a
human must be involved instead, and how this scales if SHRS adds more
AI touchpoints (a Parent Portal assistant, a Student Portal assistant,
an admissions-screening assistant) beyond the one that exists today.

## 3. Scope

The live website chat assistant today; any future AI system operating
under the SHRS name, across the website, Parent Portal, a future
Student Portal, or admissions process.

## 4. Definitions

- **AI system** — any automated conversational or decision-support tool
  operating under the SHRS name, whether public-facing (the website
  assistant) or internal.
- **Hallucination** — a plausible-sounding but false or unverifiable
  statement generated by an AI system.
- **Human oversight** — a named staff member's ability to review,
  override, or escalate what an AI system says or does.
- **Model change** — a change to the underlying AI provider or model
  version an SHRS AI system uses, which can alter its behaviour even if
  no SHRS-side code changes.

## 5. Policy Statement

Every SHRS AI system honestly discloses that it is AI on first
interaction — never impersonates a staff member or office. This is
already true of the live assistant and is now a standing requirement
for any future one. An AI system states only what is actually
published or verifiable — it does not guess at fees, academic calendar
dates, scholarship criteria, or international-admission arrangements,
and says plainly when something isn't published yet, pointing to a
real contact instead. Safeguarding overrides normal AI operation: an AI
system must not attempt to handle a safeguarding disclosure itself —
it must direct the user to a named human contact, per the Child
Protection & Safeguarding Policy (SW-01) §7.10. An AI system does not
make an admission, disciplinary, academic grading, or Ijazah-
certification decision on its own — those remain human decisions under
the authorities established in the Academic Regulations (AC-02),
Student Code of Conduct (SD-02), Admissions Policy (PA-05), and Ijazah
Governance Framework (IQ-02) respectively.

## 6. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| ICT Head | Owns this policy; reviews AI system behaviour against it (Section 8); maintains the model-change log (Section 7.7). |
| Any staff member deploying or configuring an AI system | Conducts the pre-launch review (Section 7.8) before any new AI touchpoint goes live. |
| Designated Safeguarding Lead *(SW-02, once appointed)* | Consulted on the safeguarding-escalation configuration (Section 7.2) for any AI system that could plausibly encounter a disclosure. |

## 7. Procedures

### 7.1 Appropriate use
- Answering published, verifiable questions about SHRS (the four
  institutions, admission steps, boarding terms, governance, contact
  details) — the live assistant's actual current scope.
- General academic help (writing, homework explanation, exam prep)
  using the AI's general knowledge, clearly distinguished from
  school-specific claims — again, the live assistant's actual current
  behaviour, now stated as policy rather than only as implementation.
- A future Parent/Student Portal assistant, if built, answering
  questions about a guardian's own linked child's data — but only
  within the same access boundaries the Parent Portal itself already
  enforces (a guardian sees only their own linked children — Data
  Protection & Privacy Policy, IT-02, §5), not a broader data access an
  AI interface might otherwise make tempting to add.

### 7.2 Human oversight
- A named staff member (ICT Head, or a future Digital Campus role) is
  accountable for reviewing the AI system's actual behaviour against
  this policy, not just its intended configuration — since an AI
  system's real output can drift from what its system prompt intends.
- Any AI system that could plausibly encounter a safeguarding
  disclosure, a distressed user, or a question it cannot safely answer
  must have a clear escalation path to a human — the live assistant
  already does this via WhatsApp escalation links; any future AI
  touchpoint must have an equivalent, not a lesser one.

### 7.3 Privacy and data handling
- The live assistant does not currently authenticate users or persist
  conversation history server-side (per `docs/digital-assistant.md`) —
  **this must be re-verified at each review**, since that could change
  with a future feature and this policy's privacy claims would need
  updating accordingly, not left stale.
- A future AI system with access to Parent Portal data must comply with
  the Data Protection & Privacy Policy (IT-02) in full — this policy
  does not create a separate, weaker data standard for AI-mediated
  access to the same data.

### 7.4 Hallucination risk mitigation
Any AI system's tendency to generate a plausible but false answer is
treated as a known, ongoing risk requiring the guardrails in Section 5,
not a solved problem — the live assistant's restriction to published
content is the current mitigation, and any future AI system must have
an equivalent restriction appropriate to its scope, confirmed at each
review rather than assumed to still hold.

### 7.5 Staff responsibilities
Staff are not required to treat an AI system's output as authoritative
without verification, particularly for anything touching a safeguarding,
academic, or admissions decision — the human authorities named in the
relevant policy remain accountable, not the AI system.

### 7.6 Educational use
Where an AI system (the live assistant, or a future classroom tool) is
used directly with students for academic help, its use should be
consistent with the Academic Integrity Policy once drafted (Governance
Master Register, Academic Affairs, Tier 1, not yet written) — this
policy does not attempt to set academic-integrity rules for AI-assisted
work itself, only notes that dependency.

### 7.7 Vendor and model change management
- A change to the AI provider or model version an SHRS system uses
  (Section 4's "model change") is logged by the ICT Head, with a note
  on whether the change was tested against Section 7.1's scope
  boundaries before or immediately after the change took effect.
- **This policy does not assume model behaviour is static** — a
  provider-side model update can change how an AI system responds
  without any SHRS-side code change, which is exactly why Section 8's
  monitoring requirement checks actual behaviour, not just
  configuration.

### 7.8 Pre-launch review for new AI touchpoints
- Before any new AI system (a Parent Portal assistant, a Student Portal
  assistant, an admissions-screening assistant) goes live, it is
  reviewed against this policy's scope boundaries (Section 7.1) and
  against the Data Protection & Privacy Policy's (IT-02) §7.5 Data
  Protection Impact Assessment requirement — the two reviews happen
  together, not as separate, potentially inconsistent processes.
- Section 5's core principles (honest AI disclosure, no unverified
  claims, safeguarding escalation, no autonomous high-stakes decisions)
  apply to every new touchpoint by default — a new AI system does not
  start from a blank policy slate.

## 8. Monitoring and Compliance

The ICT Head reviews the live assistant's actual behaviour (not just
its system prompt) against Sections 5 and 7.1–7.4 at least annually,
and immediately after any vendor/model change (Section 7.7) or any
guardian/student complaint (Section 12) suggesting a drift from
policy.

## 9. Records and Documentation

The model-change log (Section 7.7) and any pre-launch review record
(Section 7.8) are maintained by the ICT Head, retained per the Records
Retention Policy (IT-04).

## 10. Related Policies

Data Protection & Privacy Policy (IT-02), Child Protection &
Safeguarding Policy (SW-01), Information Security Policy (IT-01),
Academic Regulations (AC-02), Student Code of Conduct (SD-02),
Admissions Policy (PA-05), Ijazah Governance Framework (IQ-02).

## 11. Exceptions

None defined; any AI system granted broader authority than Section 5
describes (e.g. an admissions-screening tool making an actual decision)
would require an explicit Board-approved amendment to this policy, not
a quiet expansion in practice.

## 12. Appeals and Complaints

A guardian or student who believes an AI system gave a harmful,
incorrect, or inappropriate response may raise it through the existing
Complaint Policy (PA-01) — the same signpost used throughout this
document ecosystem, not a new AI-specific complaints channel.

## 13. Review and Amendment

Annual, or immediately when SHRS adds a new AI touchpoint (Parent AI,
Student AI, Admissions AI) — at which point this policy's Section 7.1
(Appropriate use) must be updated to reflect what that new system
actually does, not left describing only the original website
assistant.
