# Phase D Governance Consistency Audit

*A full-ecosystem audit, per the Phase D directive's Priority 4 —
across the Constitution, all policies, regulations, handbooks, and
frameworks (23 documents in `docs/policies/` as of this audit, plus
the two living registers at `docs/`). Builds on, rather than repeats,
the Phase A/B audit (`phase-a-b-consistency-audit.md`) and Phase C's
review work (`phase-c-dependency-review.md`) — this pass focuses on
what changed in Phase D and re-verifies the whole ecosystem still
holds together, not just the two new documents in isolation.*

---

## 1. Citation/cross-reference check — PASS, no defects found this round

Every `§X` / `Section X` cross-reference in the Staff Conduct Policy
and Designated Safeguarding Lead Framework was checked against the
actual section numbering of the document it points to (Child
Protection & Safeguarding Policy, Boarding Regulations, Constitution &
Governance Charter, Staff Handbook, Admissions Policy). All resolved
correctly. One internal citation was caught and fixed *before* this
audit, during drafting itself (Staff Conduct Policy §10 originally
said "Section 7.2," corrected to "Section 7, step 2" since that
section is a plain numbered procedure, not lettered subsections) —
recorded here so the fix isn't lost, not because it's still open.

This is a genuine improvement over Phase B, where two citation defects
were only caught *after* drafting, during the dedicated audit pass —
worth naming as a real trend, not just a clean bill of health.

## 2. Authority consistency — PASS, with one new gap surfaced

Both new documents assign every decision to a real role from the
governance roster or the Constitution's authority structure. No
invented titles. One genuine, previously-unnoticed gap was found while
checking this:

- **Boarding Regulations' owner is "Principal, Qur'an College," but its
  own Scope (§2) says "All boarding students"** — not "all Qur'an
  College boarding students." Qur'an College is where boarding is
  concentrated today (per the live Qur'an College page: "most Qur'an
  College students board"), but the document doesn't actually restrict
  itself to that institution, and neither Royal College nor the
  Constitution assigns a second authority for a hypothetical Royal
  College boarder. This is a real, if currently low-impact, authority
  gap — not urgent while boarding is Qur'an-College-concentrated, but
  it should be resolved (either narrow the Scope explicitly to Qur'an
  College, or name a joint-authority arrangement) before it matters.
  **Added to the Governance Resolution Register is the natural next
  step; it is not added there in this pass because it's a documentation
  fix, not a pending external decision** — recommend VP Administration
  resolve it directly at the next Boarding Regulations review.

## 3. Duplication check — PASS

Staff Conduct Policy's professional-boundary language explicitly
mirrors (cross-references) rather than re-words the Child Protection &
Safeguarding Policy §6.6 and Boarding Regulations §6.5 contact rules —
verified by direct comparison, not just by the "mirrors" claim in the
text. The Designated Safeguarding Lead Framework operationalises the
Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy's existing role definitions
(§3, §5) without redefining any term — verified the same way.

## 4. Missing-procedure check — one true gap, already tracked correctly

The only missing-procedure question worth raising is: what happens if
a Board member has a conflict of interest in a Governance Resolution
Register item (e.g. the fee-schedule decision)? No Conflict of Interest
Policy exists yet to answer this. This is not a new finding — it was
already on the Governance Master Register (Governance, Tier 2, MISSING)
before this audit — but it's worth naming here because the Governance
Resolution Register's very first category (fee schedule) is exactly
the kind of decision where this gap could matter in practice, not just
in theory.

## 5. The Governance Resolution Register itself — checked for a
category error, not just content

Verified the register does what it claims: it aggregates existing open
items rather than re-deciding them or inventing new ones. Spot-checked
five of its 23 entries against their source document and confirmed each
one is a faithful restatement, not a new claim:
- Fee schedule → matches Admissions Policy §9–10 and Parent Handbook §13.
- Numeric promotion threshold → matches Academic Regulations §5.
- Adebayo title conflict → matches `phase-b-dependency-review.md` §1.
- Safeguarding-log retention → matches Records Retention Policy §5.
- DSL appointment → matches Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy
  owner line (as updated this phase) and the DSL Framework itself.

## 6. Institutional Readiness Report — not separately audited here

The Readiness Report (Priority 5) is a synthesis document built *from*
this audit's findings and the Governance Resolution Register, not a
new source of claims — it is checked for consistency against both by
construction (written after them, from them), not as a separate
cross-reference exercise.

---

## Summary

Two new documents integrated cleanly into a 23-document ecosystem: zero
citation defects (an improvement on Phase B's process, where two were
found only after drafting), one small but real authority-scope gap
found (Boarding Regulations' owner vs. its stated scope), zero
duplication, and one already-tracked missing-procedure gap (Conflict of
Interest Policy) reconfirmed as relevant rather than newly discovered.
The ecosystem holds together as one system, not 23 independent
documents — the standard this project set for itself in Phase B and has
now held across two further phases.

**Still true, and still binding:** nothing in any of these 23 documents
should be treated as legal advice or a compliance confirmation. The
Governance Resolution Register's Section 6 (Legal & Regulatory
Verification) is the current complete list of what still needs
external confirmation before Board adoption.
