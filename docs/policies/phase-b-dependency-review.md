## 3. Appeals — one convention, several distinct pathways (not one pathway pretending to cover everything)

There is no single "appeals" document, and there shouldn't be one — a
grade dispute, a disciplinary suspension, an Ijazah re-examination
request, and a general parent complaint are genuinely different
processes with different decision-makers. What is shared, and must stay
shared, is the **convention**:

- The appeal is decided by someone who was not part of the original
  decision.
- A response window is set and communicated (Phase A used 5 school
  days as a proposed default; Phase B reuses the same default rather
  than inventing a different number for the same kind of decision).
- The outcome is communicated in writing with the reason.

Distinct pathways already defined, which Phase B must point to rather
than re-describe:
- **Disciplinary appeal** (suspension or worse) — Student Code of
  Conduct §9: escalate to the Principal's immediate superior (CEO or a
  Board-designated committee).
- **Ijazah examination appeal** — Ijazah Governance Framework §8:
  re-examination by a different named scholar.
- **General parent complaint** — the existing, live Complaint Policy
  (Policy IX): the front door for anything not already covered by a
  more specific pathway.
- **New in Phase B:** Academic Regulations needs its own appeal
  pathway for grade, promotion, and academic-standing disputes — this
  is genuinely a new pathway (Policy IX and the Student Code of Conduct
  don't cover it), not a duplicate of either.

## 4. Real content already live, that Phase B must build on rather than re-invent

- **Assessment Policy (Policy I):** CA 40 + Exam 60 = 100 marks/term;
  cumulative 200 at 2nd term, 300 at 3rd term; grading scale A+ through
  F; report cards issued at term end; Open Day broad-sheet feedback
  each half term. Academic Regulations reuses this exactly.
- **Admission page (`pages/admission.html`):** a real, live 12-stage
  admission process (Enquiries → Settling In) and a real required-
  documents list (birth certificate, 2 passport photographs, prior
  school report/testimonial). The page already states, honestly, that
  fees, scholarship criteria, and international-student arrangements
  are not yet published. The Admissions Policy expands the governance
  around the 12 stages and preserves that same honesty rather than
  filling the gap with invented figures.
- **Qur'an College page:** Ijazaat awarded after a 24–36 month
  memorisation programme, Saudi curriculum for Islamic/Qur'anic
  studies, boarding is the norm for this cohort. Hifz Regulations
  builds on this without contradicting the programme-length framing.
- **The nine live policies (I–IX):** Assessment, Anti-Bullying &
  Disciplinary, Career, Dress Code, Equal Opportunity, First Aid,
  Health & Safety, Visitors, Complaint. None of the five Phase B
  documents duplicates any of these — each links out to the relevant
  one instead.
