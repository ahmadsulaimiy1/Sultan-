# Policy SD-02 — Student Code of Conduct

*Retrofitted to the full 13-section governance architecture and
substantially deepened, per the Tier 1 retrofit directive. Every
principle, role, and procedure from v1.0 is preserved; none is
weakened or removed. New in v2.0: a graded sanctions approach,
restorative-practice provisions, explicit (and deliberately limited)
searching/confiscation and reasonable-force sections, a dedicated
safeguarding-interface subsection, and formal record-keeping and
guardian-communication procedures.*

## 2. Purpose

To state, in one place, what SHRS expects of every student's conduct —
in class, around campus, online, and off-site when representing the
school — building on the existing Anti-Bullying & Disciplinary Policy
(SD-01) rather than duplicating it, and closing the gap that, before
v1.0, there was no single document a family could be pointed to for
"what is expected of my child."

## 3. Scope

All students, across all five institutions, on any SHRS site, during
any SHRS-organised off-site activity, and online where conduct affects
another SHRS student or the school's reputation.

## 4. Definitions

- **Misconduct** — any breach of this Code, ranging from minor
  (addressed by a class teacher) to serious (requiring VP Administration
  or Principal involvement).
- **Serious misconduct** — misconduct involving a safety risk, serious
  dishonesty, serious disrespect, or a bullying finding under the
  Anti-Bullying & Disciplinary Policy (SD-01) — the threshold that
  triggers Section 7.3's procedure rather than a lesser response.
- **Bullying** — as defined in the existing Anti-Bullying &
  Disciplinary Policy (SD-01) — not redefined here, to avoid two
  competing definitions existing in parallel.
- **Islamic creed expectations** — the behavioural standard SD-01
  already grounds good conduct in; this Code operationalises it into
  day-to-day expectations rather than restating the principle alone.
- **Off-site conduct** — a student's conduct at any SHRS-organised
  activity away from the main site, or online conduct affecting
  another SHRS student or the school's reputation — treated identically
  to on-campus conduct for disciplinary purposes (Section 7.9).
- **Restorative approach** — a response to misconduct focused on
  repairing harm and restoring a relationship (e.g. a facilitated
  conversation between students), used alongside or instead of a purely
  punitive consequence where appropriate to the student's age and the
  nature of the breach.
- **Reasonable force** — the minimum physical intervention necessary to
  prevent a student from harming themselves, another person, or
  property, used only as a last resort — see Section 7.8's deliberately
  narrow scope.
- **Search and confiscation** — the act of examining a student's
  belongings or removing an item, used only under the deliberately
  limited circumstances in Section 7.7.

## 5. Policy Statement

Every student is treated with the same standard regardless of
institution, age, or day/boarding status (boarding-specific additions
live in the Boarding Regulations, SD-04, not here). Discipline is
proportionate and educational in intent, not purely punitive — a
graded response is used before a severe one, and a restorative approach
is considered wherever it fits the situation. A student's dignity is
preserved even during a disciplinary process — no public shaming as a
disciplinary tool. Every serious disciplinary decision is recorded,
with the student and guardian informed of the reason and the outcome.
Expected conduct includes: punctual attendance and preparedness for
class (already tracked via the Parent Portal's attendance summaries —
this Code gives that data a standard to be measured against);
respectful conduct toward staff, peers, and visitors, consistent with
the Islamic creed expectations already established in SD-01; honesty
in academic work (see the future Academic Integrity Policy for
exam/coursework-specific rules this Code doesn't itself define);
appropriate use of personal devices and school digital systems (see
the Acceptable Use Policy, IT-03); and care for school property and the
shared environment.

## 6. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| VP Administration | Owns this Code; handles serious disciplinary matters; maintains the disciplinary record. |
| Class teachers | First point of response for minor misconduct; refer upward when a matter exceeds classroom-level resolution. |
| Principals (per institution) | Involved for matters serious enough to consider suspension; decide on reasonable-force or search/confiscation matters escalated under Sections 7.7–7.8. |
| Designated Safeguarding Lead *(SW-02, once appointed)* | Consulted whenever a disciplinary matter also raises a safeguarding concern — the two processes run in parallel, not sequentially, when both apply (Section 7.10). |
| Boarding wardens | Apply this Code's standards to boarding students alongside the Boarding Regulations (SD-04) — this Code does not create a lesser standard for boarding conduct. |
| Students | Expected to know and follow this Code; entitled to be heard before a serious consequence is applied (Section 7.5). |
| Guardians | Informed of any serious disciplinary matter the same day; entitled to appeal (Section 12). |

## 7. Procedures

### 7.1 Minor misconduct
Addressed directly by the class teacher; informal, not formally logged
unless repeated. A restorative conversation is used where appropriate
rather than an automatic formal consequence.

### 7.2 Repeated or moderate misconduct
Referred to VP Administration; logged; guardian informed. A graded
consequence proportionate to the pattern (e.g. a reflection task, a
restricted privilege) may be applied — **the specific consequence
options available are a decision for VP Administration to formalise
against real current practice, not invented here as a fixed list.**

### 7.3 Serious misconduct
Serious misconduct (Section 4's definition) is referred to the relevant
Principal; guardian informed the same day; may result in suspension.

### 7.4 Suspension and potential expulsion
Decided by the Principal in consultation with VP Administration and,
where a safeguarding dimension exists, the DSL; guardian formally
notified in writing with the reason and the right of appeal (Section
12).

### 7.5 Right to be heard
Before any suspension is confirmed, the student is given an
opportunity to explain their account of events, proportionate to their
age — this happens in every case, without exception, regardless of how
clear-cut the incident appears.

### 7.6 Restorative approaches
Where appropriate to the students' ages and the nature of the breach, a
restorative conversation or facilitated mediation is used alongside, or
instead of, a purely punitive consequence — particularly for
peer-conflict matters that don't rise to the level of a safeguarding
concern under the Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy (SW-01) §7.7
(peer-on-peer abuse).

### 7.7 Searching and confiscation
- Deliberately narrow in scope: a staff member may confiscate an item
  that is unsafe, disruptive to learning, or breaches the Acceptable
  Use Policy (IT-03) — a search of a student's person or bag beyond
  what the student voluntarily produces is escalated to the Principal,
  not conducted unilaterally by a class teacher.
- **What a Nigerian private school may lawfully do regarding a
  student search has not been verified by legal counsel** — this
  section states a conservative, escalation-first practice rather than
  asserting a specific legal search power.
- A confiscated item is logged and returned to the guardian, not
  retained indefinitely without a stated reason.

### 7.8 Use of reasonable force
- Used only as an absolute last resort, only to prevent a student
  harming themselves, another person, or property — never as a
  disciplinary consequence in itself.
- Any use of reasonable force is reported the same day to the
  Principal and logged, with the guardian informed, mirroring the
  same-day reporting standard used throughout this document ecosystem.
- **This section deliberately does not describe specific physical
  techniques** — any staff member who may need to use reasonable force
  should be trained by a qualified provider, not rely on this policy's
  text alone.

### 7.9 Off-site and online conduct
Off-site conduct at any SHRS-organised activity, or online conduct
affecting another SHRS student, is treated as on-campus conduct for
disciplinary purposes — the same procedure (Sections 7.1–7.4) applies
regardless of where the conduct occurred.

### 7.10 Safeguarding interface
Where a disciplinary matter also raises a safeguarding concern, the
Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy (SW-01) §7.7 (peer-on-peer
abuse) or §7.6 (allegations against a staff member, where relevant)
governs in parallel with this Code's disciplinary procedure — the two
processes run alongside each other, neither substituting for the
other.

### 7.11 Record-keeping
Every serious disciplinary decision (Section 7.3 onward) is logged by
VP Administration with the date, students involved, nature of the
matter, and outcome — retained per the Records Retention Policy
(IT-04).

### 7.12 Communication with guardians
A guardian is informed the same day of any serious misconduct finding
(Section 7.3 onward), in writing for a suspension or more serious
consequence (Section 7.4), consistent with the same-day communication
standard the Boarding Regulations and Child Protection & Safeguarding
Policy also use.

## 8. Monitoring and Compliance

VP Administration reviews the disciplinary log termly for patterns
(repeated matters involving the same student, or a pattern suggesting
an unaddressed underlying issue), and reports a summary to the Head of Schools / Administrator
annually.

## 9. Records and Documentation

The disciplinary log (Section 7.11) is the primary record, retained per
the Records Retention Policy (IT-04) — that policy's proposed period
for this category (duration of enrolment + 3 years) applies here; this
policy does not set an independent, potentially conflicting period.

## 10. Related Policies

Anti-Bullying & Disciplinary Policy (SD-01, live), Child Protection &
Safeguarding Policy (SW-01), Boarding Regulations (SD-04), Acceptable
Use Policy (IT-03), Academic Regulations (AC-02, for academic-integrity
matters specifically), Records Retention Policy (IT-04).

## 11. Exceptions

None defined; any institution-specific variation (e.g. Qur'an College's
additional Islamic conduct expectations, see the Islamic Conduct
Framework, Governance Master Register Islamic & Qur'an Education
category) is additive to this Code, not a replacement for it.

## 12. Appeals and Complaints

A guardian may appeal a suspension or more serious consequence to the
Principal's immediate superior — in practice, the Head of Schools / Administrator or the Board's
designated committee — within a fixed window (recommend 5 school days,
to be confirmed) of being notified. The appeal is decided by someone
who was not part of the original decision.

## 13. Review and Amendment

Annual, or immediately after any incident revealing a gap in this
procedure, particularly in the searching/confiscation (7.7) or
reasonable-force (7.8) sections, both of which need legal confirmation
before being treated as settled.
