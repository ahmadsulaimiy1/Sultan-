# SHRS Student Code of Conduct

**Status:** DRAFT v0.1 — not yet adopted.
**Owner (proposed):** VP Administration (Mrs. Anofi-Badmus Fatimat
Omolola, per the governance roster).
**Review cycle:** Annual.
**Depends on:** Anti-Bullying & Disciplinary Policy (Policy II,
exists); Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy; Boarding Regulations
(for boarding-specific conduct, which stays in that document rather
than being duplicated here).

> **Before this governs a real decision:** the disciplinary
> consequences and appeal timelines below are proposed structure, not
> a record of SHRS's actual current disciplinary practice. Whoever
> adopts this should confirm it matches, or deliberately update, how
> discipline is actually handled today.

---

## 1. Purpose

To state, in one place, what SHRS expects of every student's conduct —
in class, around campus, online, and off-site when representing the
school — building on the existing Anti-Bullying & Disciplinary Policy
(Policy II) rather than duplicating it, and closing the gap that today
there is no single document a family can be pointed to for "what is
expected of my child."

## 2. Scope

All students, across all four institutions, on any SHRS site, during
any SHRS-organised off-site activity, and online where conduct affects
another SHRS student or the school's reputation.

## 3. Definitions

- **Misconduct** — any breach of this Code, ranging from minor
  (addressed by a class teacher) to serious (requiring VP Administration
  or Principal involvement).
- **Bullying** — as defined in the existing Anti-Bullying &
  Disciplinary Policy (Policy II) — not redefined here, to avoid two
  competing definitions existing in parallel.
- **Islamic creed expectations** — the behavioural standard Policy II
  already grounds good conduct in; this Code operationalises it into
  day-to-day expectations rather than restating the principle alone.

## 4. Principles

1. Every student is treated with the same standard regardless of
   institution, age, or day/boarding status (boarding-specific
   additions live in the Boarding Regulations, not here).
2. Discipline is proportionate and educational in intent, not purely
   punitive.
3. A student's dignity is preserved even during a disciplinary process
   — no public shaming as a disciplinary tool.
4. Every serious disciplinary decision is recorded, with the student
   and guardian informed of the reason and the outcome.

## 5. Expected conduct

- Punctual attendance and preparedness for class (already tracked via
  the Parent Portal's attendance summaries — this Code gives that data
  a standard to be measured against).
- Respectful conduct toward staff, peers, and visitors, consistent with
  the Islamic creed expectations already established in Policy II.
- Honesty in academic work (see Academic Integrity Policy, Governance
  Master Register Phase B, for the exam/coursework-specific rules this
  Code doesn't itself define).
- Appropriate use of personal devices and school digital systems —
  cross-references the future Acceptable Use Policy (Governance Master
  Register, Tier 2) rather than restating device rules here.
- Care for school property and the shared environment.
- Off-site conduct at any SHRS-organised activity, or online conduct
  affecting another SHRS student, is treated as on-campus conduct for
  disciplinary purposes.

## 6. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| VP Administration | Owns this Code; handles serious disciplinary matters; maintains the disciplinary record. |
| Class teachers | First point of response for minor misconduct; refer upward when a matter exceeds classroom-level resolution. |
| Principals (per institution) | Involved for matters serious enough to consider suspension. |
| Designated Safeguarding Lead | Consulted whenever a disciplinary matter also raises a safeguarding concern — the two processes run in parallel, not sequentially, when both apply. |
| Students | Expected to know and follow this Code; entitled to be heard before a serious consequence is applied. |

## 7. Disciplinary procedure

1. **Minor misconduct** — addressed directly by the class teacher;
   informal, not formally logged unless repeated.
2. **Repeated or moderate misconduct** — referred to VP Administration;
   logged; guardian informed.
3. **Serious misconduct** (safety risk, serious dishonesty, serious
   disrespect, a bullying finding under Policy II) — referred to the
   relevant Principal; guardian informed the same day; may result in
   suspension.
4. **Suspension or a matter that could lead to expulsion** — decided by
   the Principal in consultation with VP Administration and, where a
   safeguarding dimension exists, the DSL; guardian formally notified in
   writing with the reason and the right of appeal (Section 9).
5. Before any suspension is confirmed, the student is given an
   opportunity to explain their account of events, proportionate to
   their age.

## 8. Compliance

No specific Nigerian education-law citation is made here regarding
student discipline/expulsion procedure, because doing so accurately
requires legal confirmation of what due-process standard applies to a
private Nigerian school — this is a gap to close with counsel, not an
assumption to make silently.

## 9. Appeals

A guardian may appeal a suspension or more serious consequence to the
Principal's immediate superior — in practice, the CEO or the Board's
designated committee — within a fixed window (recommend 5 school days,
to be confirmed) of being notified. The appeal is decided by someone
who was not part of the original decision.

## 10. Exceptions

None defined; any institution-specific variation (e.g. Qur'an College's
additional Islamic conduct expectations, see the Islamic Conduct
Framework, Governance Master Register Phase C) is additive to this
Code, not a replacement for it.

## 11. Review cycle

Annual.
