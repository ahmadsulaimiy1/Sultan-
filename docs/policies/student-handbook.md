# SHRS Student Handbook

**Status:** DRAFT v0.1 — not yet adopted.
**Owner (proposed):** VP Administration (Mrs. Anofi-Badmus Fatimat
Omolola), the same owner as the Student Code of Conduct, so the two
stay in step.
**Review cycle:** Annual.
**Related documents:** Student Code of Conduct, Child Protection &
Safeguarding Policy, Boarding Regulations, Data Protection & Privacy
Policy, Academic Regulations, Assessment Policy (I, live), Anti-
Bullying & Disciplinary Policy (II, live).

> **Before this is handed to a student:** this Handbook is written to
> be read and understood by the student themselves (age-appropriate
> language for the youngest readers is a separate, later editorial
> pass — this draft establishes structure and content, not final
> reading-level tuning). It restates nothing from the Student Code of
> Conduct's disciplinary procedure — see Section 9 below.

---

## 1. Purpose

To teach every SHRS student — from Nursery through Qur'an College —
what is expected of them and what they can expect from the school in
return, in language addressed to the student, not to a parent or
policy committee.

## 2. Scope

All enrolled students, all four institutions, day and boarding.

## 3. Definitions

Reuses the definitions already established elsewhere rather than
restating them: misconduct and bullying (Student Code of Conduct /
Policy II), boarding terms (Boarding Regulations), Ijazah-related terms
(Ijazah Governance Framework, for Qur'an College students).

## 4. Student rights

- To be treated with respect and dignity by staff and peers.
- To be heard before a serious disciplinary consequence is applied
  (Student Code of Conduct §7.5).
- To have a safeguarding concern taken seriously and acted on the same
  day (Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy §6.1).
- To see their own attendance and academic records via the Parent
  Portal (through their guardian's account today — a direct student
  login is a Phase 2/3 product question, see
  `docs/parent-portal-phase2-3-roadmap.md`, not something this
  Handbook can promise ahead of that decision).

## 5. Student responsibilities

- Attend punctually and come prepared.
- Follow the Student Code of Conduct — this Handbook does not restate
  its expected-conduct list; see that document directly.
- Be honest in academic work (see Section 12, Academic Integrity).
- Treat school property, staff, and peers with care and respect,
  consistent with the Islamic creed expectations already set out in
  the Anti-Bullying & Disciplinary Policy (Policy II).

## 6. Academic expectations

Report cards each term; Continuous Assessment (40) + Examination (60)
= 100 marks; grading scale A+ through F — exactly as published in the
Assessment Policy (Policy I). Promotion and academic-standing criteria
are in the Academic Regulations (Phase B) — this Handbook signposts,
not duplicates.

## 7. Conduct expectations

Governed in full by the Student Code of Conduct. This Handbook's role
is to explain, in student-facing language, *why* the Code exists and
how it's applied day to day (minor issue → class teacher; repeated or
serious issue → VP Administration or Principal) — not to create a
second, competing version of the disciplinary procedure.

## 8. Digital behaviour

- Personal devices and school digital systems are used per the future
  Acceptable Use Policy (Governance Master Register, Tier 2) — not yet
  drafted, so this section states the plain expectation (respectful,
  honest, safe use; no sharing of another student's information
  without consent) rather than a detailed device-by-device rule set
  that doesn't exist yet.
- Any online conduct affecting another SHRS student is treated exactly
  like on-campus conduct, per the Student Code of Conduct §5.

## 9. Discipline procedures

**Not restated here.** The full disciplinary procedure — minor,
moderate, and serious misconduct, suspension, and the appeal process —
lives in the Student Code of Conduct alone. Duplicating it in two
documents is exactly the risk the Phase B Master Directive asked to be
avoided; a student reading this Handbook is pointed there directly.

## 10. Appeals procedures

Also not restated — see the Student Code of Conduct §9 for a
disciplinary appeal, and the Academic Regulations (Phase B) for an
academic (grade/promotion) appeal. Two different situations, two
different documents, one shared convention (an independent decision-
maker, a defined response window) as set out in
`phase-b-dependency-review.md` §3.

## 11. Boarding expectations

For boarding students: a short pointer to the Boarding Regulations for
the daily routine, room checks, and conduct expectations specific to
boarding life — this Handbook does not restate lights-out times or
supervision arrangements, both of which belong in that document.

## 12. Academic integrity

Honesty in coursework and examinations is a Student Code of Conduct
expectation (§5) with its own future Academic Integrity Policy
(Governance Master Register, Tier 1, not yet drafted) to define
specific consequences for cheating/plagiarism in more academic detail
than the general Code covers. Until that policy exists, a suspected
integrity breach is handled under the Student Code of Conduct's general
disciplinary procedure.

## 13. Welfare & support

A student with a welfare concern (about themselves or a peer) can raise
it with any staff member, who is required to act the same day per the
Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy. This Handbook states that
plainly, in first-person student-facing language, without reproducing
the staff-side procedure.

## 14. Safety guidance

A short, age-appropriate summary of what to do in an emergency (know
your assembly point; follow your teacher; do not go back for
belongings) — pointing to the Emergency Response Plan for the full
procedure staff and wardens follow.

## 15. Leadership opportunities

A slot for real, existing student leadership structures (prefects,
class representatives, boarding house leadership, Qur'an College
peer-mentoring roles if they exist). **Populate with what SHRS
actually runs today** rather than inventing a leadership structure
that doesn't exist.

## 16. Spiritual & character development

Reflects the Islamic creed expectations already grounding good conduct
across SHRS (Anti-Bullying & Disciplinary Policy, Policy II) and, for
Qur'an College students, the memorisation and character formation
described in the Hifz Regulations (Phase B) — this section signposts
both rather than restating either.

## 17. Monitoring & compliance

VP Administration reviews this Handbook annually alongside the Student
Code of Conduct, so the two are always updated together rather than
drifting apart.

## 18. Review cycle

Annual, in step with the Student Code of Conduct.
