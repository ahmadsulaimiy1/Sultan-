## 2. Purpose

To teach every SHRS student — from Nursery through Qur'an College —
what is expected of them and what they can expect from the school in
return, in language addressed to the student, not to a parent or
policy committee.

## 3. Scope

All enrolled students, all five institutions, day and boarding.

## 4. Definitions

Reuses the definitions already established elsewhere rather than
restating them: misconduct and bullying (Student Code of Conduct,
SD-02), boarding terms (Boarding Regulations, SD-04), Ijazah-related
terms (Ijazah Governance Framework, IQ-02, for Qur'an College
students).

## 5. Policy Statement

Every SHRS student has rights (Section 7.1) and responsibilities
(Section 7.2) that apply consistently regardless of institution or
age. This Handbook's role is to explain, in student-facing language,
why the Student Code of Conduct exists and how it applies day to day —
never to create a second, competing version of its disciplinary
procedure.

## 6. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| VP Administration | Owns this Handbook alongside the Student Code of Conduct; reviews both together (Section 8). |
| Class teachers | First point of contact for a student's question about this Handbook's expectations. |
| Students | Expected to read and follow this Handbook; entitled to the rights in Section 7.1. |

## 7. Procedures

### 7.1 Student rights
- To be treated with respect and dignity by staff and peers.
- To be heard before a serious disciplinary consequence is applied
  (Student Code of Conduct, SD-02, §7.5).
- To have a safeguarding concern taken seriously and acted on the same
  day (Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy, SW-01, §7.2).
- To see their own attendance and academic records via the Parent
  Portal (through their guardian's account today — a direct student
  login is a Phase 2/3 product question, see
  `docs/parent-portal-phase2-3-roadmap.md`, not something this
  Handbook can promise ahead of that decision).

### 7.2 Student responsibilities
- Attend punctually and come prepared.
- Follow the Student Code of Conduct (SD-02) — this Handbook does not
  restate its expected-conduct list; see that document directly.
- Be honest in academic work (Section 7.8, Academic integrity).
- Treat school property, staff, and peers with care and respect,
  consistent with the Islamic creed expectations already set out in
  the Anti-Bullying & Disciplinary Policy (SD-01, live).

### 7.3 Academic expectations
Report cards each term; Continuous Assessment (40) + Examination (60)
= 100 marks; grading scale A+ through F — exactly as published in the
Assessment Policy (AC-01). Promotion and academic-standing criteria are
in the Academic Regulations (AC-02) — this Handbook signposts, not
duplicates.

### 7.4 Conduct expectations
Governed in full by the Student Code of Conduct (SD-02). This
Handbook's role is to explain, in student-facing language, why the
Code exists and how it's applied day to day (minor issue → class
teacher; repeated or serious issue → VP Administration or Principal).

### 7.5 Digital behaviour
- Personal devices and school digital systems are used per the
  Acceptable Use Policy (IT-03) — this section states the plain
  expectation (respectful, honest, safe use; no sharing of another
  student's information without consent) rather than restating that
  policy's full device rules.
- Any online conduct affecting another SHRS student is treated exactly
  like on-campus conduct, per the Student Code of Conduct (SD-02)
  §7.9.

### 7.6 Discipline procedures
**Not restated here.** The full disciplinary procedure — minor,
moderate, and serious misconduct, suspension, and the appeal process —
lives in the Student Code of Conduct (SD-02) alone. A student reading
this Handbook is pointed there directly.

### 7.7 Boarding expectations
For boarding students: a short pointer to the Boarding Regulations
(SD-04) for the daily routine, room checks, and conduct expectations
specific to boarding life.

### 7.8 Academic integrity
Honesty in coursework and examinations is a Student Code of Conduct
(SD-02) expectation with its own future Academic Integrity Policy
(Governance Master Register, Academic Affairs, Tier 1, not yet
drafted) to define specific consequences for cheating/plagiarism in
more academic detail than the general Code covers. Until that policy
exists, a suspected integrity breach is handled under the Student Code
of Conduct's general disciplinary procedure (SD-02 §7.5's academic
integrity section).

### 7.9 Welfare and support
A student with a welfare concern (about themselves or a peer) can raise
it with any staff member, who is required to act the same day per the
Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy (SW-01).

### 7.10 Safety guidance
A short, age-appropriate summary of what to do in an emergency (know
your assembly point; follow your teacher; do not go back for
belongings) — pointing to the Emergency Response Plan (SW-09) for the
full procedure staff and wardens follow.

### 7.11 Leadership opportunities
A slot for real, existing student leadership structures (prefects,
class representatives, boarding house leadership, Qur'an College
peer-mentoring roles if they exist). **Populate with what SHRS
actually runs today** rather than inventing a leadership structure
that doesn't exist.

### 7.12 Spiritual and character development
Reflects the Islamic creed expectations already grounding good conduct
across SHRS (Anti-Bullying & Disciplinary Policy, SD-01) and, for
Qur'an College students, the memorisation and character formation
described in the Hifz Regulations (IQ-01).

### 7.13 Uniform and presentation
Governed by the live Dress Code Policy (SW-03) — this Handbook does not
restate its requirements, only confirms it applies to every student
equally regardless of institution.

### 7.14 Extra-curricular and off-site activities
A student representing SHRS at any off-site activity (sports fixture,
competition, excursion) follows this Handbook's conduct expectations
(Section 7.4) identically to on-campus conduct, consistent with the
Student Code of Conduct's (SD-02) off-site conduct provision (§7.9).
**This is a new provision, not previously stated in v1.0** — added
because off-site activities were only addressed from the Code's own
disciplinary-scope angle, not from the student-facing expectation
angle this Handbook provides.

## 8. Monitoring and Compliance

VP Administration reviews this Handbook annually alongside the Student
Code of Conduct, so the two are always updated together rather than
drifting apart.

## 9. Records and Documentation

Where a student holds a leadership role (Section 7.11), it is recorded
by VP Administration as part of the student's record — **a proposed
addition, not a confirmation this is currently tracked.**

## 11. Exceptions

None defined.

## 12. Appeals and Complaints

Not restated — see the Student Code of Conduct (SD-02) §12 for a
disciplinary appeal, and the Academic Regulations (AC-02) §12 for an
academic (grade/promotion) appeal. Two different situations, two
different documents, one shared convention (an independent decision-
maker, a defined response window) as set out in
`phase-b-dependency-review.md` §3.

## 13. Review and Amendment

Annual, in step with the Student Code of Conduct.
