Directive, Phase E. Supersedes the live site's compressed Health &
Safety Policy (formerly "Policy VII") as the authoritative operational
instrument. The live policy's real hazard list, fire/security/
environmental provisions, and management-staff accountability are
preserved exactly — this redevelopment adds the risk-assessment,
area-specific, and incident-reporting depth the live version compressed
into single bullet points.*

## 2. Purpose

To provide every child, staff member, and visitor a comfortable, safe
environment — the live policy's own stated objective, preserved
unchanged — fostering safety, minimising injury potential, and
preparing for and managing hazards, now with the operational detail
(who assesses risk, how often, what gets documented) the live version's
single-paragraph objectives statement didn't include.

## 3. Scope

All SHRS sites, all students, staff, contractors, and visitors, across
all four institutions, day and boarding.

## 4. Definitions

- **Hazard** — any source of potential harm, per the live policy's own
  list: tripping/slipping/falling, unprotected wiring, aggression from
  visitors/intruders, machinery noise, pests, sunburn, dangerous play,
  fire, and psychological hazards (bullying, harassment) — preserved
  exactly, not altered.
- **Risk assessment** — a documented evaluation of a hazard's
  likelihood and severity, and the control measure applied.
- **Site inspection** — a scheduled physical walk-around checking for
  hazards, distinct from a one-off risk assessment of a specific
  activity or space.

## 5. Policy Statement

SHRS identifies, assesses, and controls the hazards named in Section 4
across every site, through regular inspection, documented risk
assessment for higher-risk activities (laboratories, sports), and a
same-day incident reporting requirement — extending the live policy's
existing fire-safety, security, and environmental provisions (all
preserved below) with the area-specific and documentation depth this
redevelopment adds.

## 7. Procedures

### 7.1 Risk assessment
A documented risk assessment is completed for any activity or space
carrying elevated risk (laboratories, sports, boarding overnight
supervision) — **the current existence and format of such assessments
at SHRS needs confirmation**; this policy states the requirement, not
a claim that it's already fully in place.

### 7.2 Hazard identification
Every hazard on the live policy's existing list (Section 4) is a
standing item on every site inspection checklist, plus any
site-specific hazard identified through the risk-assessment process
above.

### 7.3 Site inspections
The Health and Safety Committee's existing weekly walk-around (live
policy) produces a written checklist result, not just an informal
walk-through — logged, with any identified hazard assigned an owner and
a resolution deadline, reported to management staff (or the named
Policy Owner once designated).

### 7.4 Classroom safety
Electrical outlets, wiring, and furniture are checked as part of the
standing site inspection (Section 7.3); a class teacher observing a
hazard mid-lesson reports it immediately rather than waiting for the
next scheduled walk-around.

### 7.5 Laboratory safety
Laboratories carry elevated hazard status (per the live policy's
hazard list referencing them implicitly through general safety
provisions) — **specific laboratory safety procedures (chemical
storage, protective equipment, supervision ratios during practical
work) need to be documented by the relevant HoD Science** in
consultation with this policy's owner; this redevelopment names the
requirement rather than inventing laboratory-specific technical detail
this project has no basis to specify.

### 7.6 Sports safety
Sports and physical activity carry their own injury-prevention
requirements (appropriate supervision, equipment condition checks,
weather-appropriate scheduling given Lagos's climate) — coordinated
with the First Aid Policy's (SW-06) sports-hall/field first aid kit
location.

### 7.7 Boarding safety
Boarding-specific safety (fire evacuation from boarding houses
specifically, overnight hazard reporting) is governed jointly by this
policy and the Boarding Regulations (SD-04) — this policy does not
duplicate that document's supervision-ratio and room-check provisions,
only confirms the same hazard-reporting standard applies overnight as
during the day.

### 7.8 Fire safety
Preserved from the live policy: fire-danger education, occasional fire
drills and evacuation procedures, fire-prevention equipment placed and
regularly inspected, clearly indicated fire escapes and exits. This
redevelopment adds one cross-reference the live policy didn't have:
the full drill and evacuation procedure now lives in the Emergency
Response Plan (SW-09) — this policy states that the commitment exists,
that document states how it's executed.

### 7.9 Electrical safety
Electrical wiring and equipment are inspected as part of the standing
site inspection (Section 7.3) — the live policy's "unprotected wiring"
hazard is addressed through this recurring inspection rather than a
one-time fix-and-forget response.

### 7.10 Food safety
High hygiene standards in food-service areas, per the live policy's
existing environmental-measures provision — **specific food-safety
certification or inspection practice (e.g. a food handler's permit
requirement) needs confirmation** from whoever manages the dining hall;
not invented here.

### 7.11 Contractor safety
PPE and minimal-risk practice for workers/contractors, preserved
exactly from the live policy, extended with the Visitors Policy's
(SW-08) contractor-access provisions for site-entry control specifically
— the two documents address different aspects of the same population
(who gets in, versus what safety standard applies once they're in) and
deliberately don't duplicate each other's content.

### 7.12 Emergency procedures
Deferred in full to the Emergency Response Plan (SW-09) — this policy
does not restate fire, medical, security, or severe-weather emergency
procedures; it establishes the preventive/inspection layer that
document's procedures respond to when prevention wasn't enough.

### 7.13 Incident reporting
Any hazard-related incident (an injury caused by an unaddressed hazard,
a near-miss) is reported the same day to the Health and Safety
Committee and logged — extending the First Aid Policy's (SW-06)
same-day accident-report requirement to near-misses and property/
environment incidents that don't involve an injury but reveal a hazard.

## 8. Monitoring and Compliance

The Health and Safety Committee's weekly walk-around results and any
incident reports are reviewed monthly by management staff (or the named
Policy Owner), with an annual summary to the Board as part of the wider
safeguarding/safety reporting the Constitution & Governance Charter
already establishes (§6.1).

## 9. Records and Documentation

Site inspection checklists, risk assessments, and incident reports are
the primary records — retained per the Records Retention Policy
(IT-04), which does not yet name a specific period for this category;
another open item for that policy's next review, alongside the
equivalent gap named in the First Aid Policy (SW-06) and Visitors
Policy (SW-08).

## 10. Related Policies

First Aid Policy (SW-06), Visitors Policy (SW-08, security/access
provisions this policy's Section 7.11 cross-references rather than
duplicates), Emergency Response Plan (SW-09, full emergency procedures
this policy defers to), Boarding Regulations (SD-04).

## 11. Exceptions

None defined.

## 12. Appeals and Complaints

A staff member or parent with a health and safety concern not resolved
through direct reporting (Section 7.13) may raise it with management
staff, escalating through the existing Complaint Policy (PA-01, live
Policy IX) if unresolved.

## 13. Review and Amendment

Annual, or immediately after any incident revealing a gap. **This
review is already overdue** (the live policy's last stated review
date, July 2025, has passed) — the same finding as the First Aid
Policy (SW-06); both should be completed together given how much
content they cross-reference.
