# SHRS Emergency Response Plan

**Status:** DRAFT v0.1 — not yet adopted.
**Owner (proposed):** Principal — but SHRS has three Principals (Royal
College, Qur'an College, School of Arabic & Islamic Studies) plus a
Head Teacher (Nursery & Primary) and no single overall Principal. **This
policy assumes a site-wide emergency response needs one accountable
lead across all four institutions — the Board should decide who that
is, or confirm a rotating/joint arrangement.**
**Review cycle:** Annual, plus after every drill and every real
incident.
**Depends on:** Health & Safety Policy (Policy VII, exists — this plan
is the standalone crisis document that policy references but doesn't
itself contain).

> **Before this governs a real decision:** SHRS's site(s) in Ikorodu,
> Lagos have not been assessed by a fire-safety or emergency-planning
> professional as part of producing this draft — the procedures below
> are structural best practice, not a site-specific risk assessment.
> Evacuation routes, assembly points, and headcount procedures must be
> walked and confirmed against the actual physical campus before this
> plan is treated as operative.

---

## 1. Purpose

To give every student, staff member, and boarding house parent a single,
rehearsed answer to "what do I do right now" for the emergencies most
relevant to a Lagos day-and-boarding school: fire, medical emergency,
security threat/intruder, and severe weather — rather than leaving each
incident to be improvised.

## 2. Scope

All SHRS sites, all students (day and boarding), all staff, at all
times including outside normal school hours for boarding students.

## 3. Definitions

- **Emergency** — any event posing immediate risk to life or safety,
  requiring an immediate, practiced response rather than normal
  procedure.
- **Assembly point** — a pre-designated safe location for headcount
  after evacuation. **Actual locations must be confirmed on-site — not
  invented here.**
- **Incident Commander** — the senior staff member present who takes
  charge until the named institutional lead (see Owner, above) arrives.

## 4. Principles

1. Every drill produces an assembly-point headcount discrepancy report
   — "everyone accounted for" must be affirmatively confirmed, not
   assumed by the absence of a report.
2. Boarding students overnight are a distinct scenario from a daytime
   incident — supervision is lighter and response time from senior
   staff is longer; this plan addresses both explicitly.
3. Every classroom and boarding room displays its evacuation route.
4. Communication to parents follows a single controlled channel
   (WhatsApp broadcast / Parent Portal notification) so no
   contradictory information spreads.

## 5. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Institutional lead *(to be confirmed — see Owner above)* | Overall command decision during a real emergency; approves this plan; commissions the site-specific risk assessment this draft cannot substitute for. |
| Incident Commander (senior staff member present) | Takes charge until the institutional lead arrives; directs evacuation or lockdown. |
| Class teachers / boarding wardens | Account for every student in their charge at the assembly point or designated safe room; report headcount to the Incident Commander. |
| ICT Head | Sends the parent communication once the Incident Commander confirms it is safe to do so. |
| Security unit (referenced in the existing Visitors Policy) | First response to a security/intruder scenario; liaises with Nigeria Police Force if required. |

## 6. Procedures

### 6.1 Fire
- Immediate evacuation via the nearest marked route to the assembly
  point — no re-entry for belongings.
- Class teachers/wardens take the attendance register (or, for
  boarding, the room list) with them.
- Headcount reported to the Incident Commander within a fixed target
  time (to be set once assembly-point distances are actually measured
  on-site).
- Fire drills held at a regular interval (recommend termly, to be
  confirmed by the Board) with results logged.

### 6.2 Medical emergency
- First response per the existing First Aid Policy (Policy VI).
- For anything beyond first-aid capability: immediate call to
  emergency medical services and to the student's guardian via the
  fastest available channel.
- A designated first-aid point and trained first-aider per institution
  — confirm current coverage as part of adopting this plan.

### 6.3 Security threat / intruder
- Lockdown procedure: doors secured, students moved away from windows
  and sightlines, silence maintained, no one leaves a secured room
  until the Incident Commander gives the all-clear.
- Security unit and, if warranted, Nigeria Police Force contacted
  immediately — contact protocol to be confirmed and kept current by
  the Incident Commander/Security unit, not hard-coded here.

### 6.4 Severe weather
- Given Lagos's rainy-season flooding and storm risk: students moved
  indoors away from windows; boarding students' overnight movement
  restricted if conditions are hazardous; day-school dismissal delayed
  if travel conditions are unsafe, communicated via the controlled
  parent channel.

### 6.5 Boarding-specific: overnight emergencies
- Boarding wardens hold a current room list at all times, not just
  during drills.
- A named on-call senior staff member is reachable by phone overnight
  for any boarding emergency — this on-call rotation does not yet
  exist and needs to be established, not assumed.

### 6.6 Parent communication
- One controlled message, sent once the Incident Commander confirms
  facts, via WhatsApp broadcast and/or Parent Portal notification —
  never multiple staff members independently messaging parents.

## 7. Compliance

No specific Nigerian fire-code or emergency-planning regulation is
cited here because none has been verified against SHRS's actual site —
that verification (a proper site risk assessment, likely involving the
Lagos State Fire Service for code compliance) is a prerequisite to
treating this plan as complete, not an optional add-on.

## 8. Appeals

Not generally applicable to an emergency plan; any staff concern about
the plan's adequacy should be raised with the institutional lead ahead
of the next annual review, not litigated during an actual incident.

## 9. Exceptions

None defined. Any site-specific variation (e.g. a boarding house with a
different layout) should be added as a named appendix once the
site-specific risk assessment exists.

## 10. Review cycle

Annual, after every drill, and immediately after any real incident
(post-incident review feeds directly back into this document).
