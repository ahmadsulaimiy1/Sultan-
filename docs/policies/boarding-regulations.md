## 2. Purpose

To give SHRS's boarding population — families entrust significantly
more to the school than a day-school family does — a clear, written
standard for supervision, safety, conduct, and welfare while resident,
closing the gap between the marketing description of boarding life at
`/boarding/` (which stays as-is; this document doesn't replace it) and
an actual governing document.

## 3. Scope

All boarding students (day students are out of scope for this
document; see the Student Code of Conduct, SD-02, for provisions
applying to all students), all boarding house staff/wardens, and any
staff member with a supervisory role overnight.

## 4. Definitions

- **Boarding student** — a student resident at an SHRS boarding house
  outside normal school hours.
- **Boarding warden / house parent** — staff responsible for overnight
  supervision.
- **Room check** — a scheduled confirmation that every boarding student
  is present and accounted for.
- **Lights-out** — the fixed time after which students are expected to
  be in their rooms; specific time to be set by the institutional lead,
  not assumed here.
- **Leave-out** — a period during which a boarding student leaves the
  premises with guardian authorisation (a weekend visit home, a
  family event).
- **Settling-in period** — the initial weeks of a new boarding
  student's residence, during which homesickness and adjustment support
  is most likely to be needed.

## 5. Policy Statement

Boarding supervision is continuous — there is no unsupervised overnight
period, even during sleeping hours (this is what room checks exist
for). Boarding staff-student contact rules from the Child Protection &
Safeguarding Policy (SW-01) §7.8 apply in full; boarding does not get a
lighter version of safeguarding because it's "just how boarding works."
A boarding student's guardian is informed the same day of any safety
incident, illness beyond minor first aid, or disciplinary matter of
substance — not held until a routine update. Religious and academic
routine (prayer times, Qur'an memorisation/Muraja'ah schedule for
Qur'an College boarders) is a scheduled, structured part of boarding
life, not left informal. A new boarding student's settling-in period is
treated as a distinct phase warranting extra pastoral attention, not
assumed to be identical to an established boarder's routine.

## 6. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Institutional lead (Principal, Qur'an College, or as confirmed) | Overall accountability for boarding welfare and safety. |
| Boarding wardens / house parents | Overnight supervision, room checks, first response to any overnight incident, escalation per the Emergency Response Plan (SW-09). |
| Designated Safeguarding Lead / Deputy DSL *(SW-02, once appointed)* | Boarding-specific safeguarding oversight per the Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy (SW-01). |
| Guardians | Provide accurate emergency contact information and any medical information relevant to overnight care; authorise any leave-out (Section 7.8). |
| Guidance Counsellor *(if this post exists or is created)* | Supports the settling-in period (Section 7.10) for new boarding students. |

## 7. Procedures

### 7.1 Supervision ratios
- A minimum warden-to-student overnight ratio should be set and
  published (a common starting benchmark is in the range of 1:15–1:25
  for older boarding students, tighter for younger ones — **this is a
  general boarding-sector reference point, not a Nigerian regulatory
  requirement, and the actual ratio SHRS commits to must be a Board
  decision based on real house capacity**).
- At least one warden is awake and present in each boarding house
  overnight; a second is reachable within the site.

### 7.2 Room checks
- A room check is conducted at lights-out and at least once more
  during the night, logged by name and time.
- Any student absent from a room check is located immediately and the
  incident logged, regardless of how it resolves.

### 7.3 Daily routine
- Structured schedule covering: wake time, prayer times, meals, study
  hours, Qur'an memorisation/Muraja'ah sessions (Qur'an College
  boarders — see the Hifz Regulations, IQ-01), free time, and
  lights-out.
- Exact times are set by the institutional lead and published to
  guardians, not left to vary informally by warden.

### 7.4 Health and medical care
- Per the existing First Aid Policy (SW-06) for immediate incidents;
  any condition requiring ongoing management (medication, chronic
  condition) is recorded with the boarding house and the guardian
  informed of exactly who administers it and when.
- Illness beyond first-aid scope: guardian contacted the same day,
  medical care arranged per the Emergency Response Plan (SW-09).

### 7.5 Staff-student contact
- No one-on-one unsupervised contact between a warden/staff member and
  a boarding student outside official, logged channels — mirrors the
  Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy (SW-01) §7.8 directly, not a
  separate rule.

### 7.6 Communication with guardians
- Guardians are entitled to request a call with the boarding warden
  within a reasonable response window (to be set, e.g. same working
  day) — should tie into the future Communication Policy (Governance
  Master Register, Parent Affairs, Tier 3).
- Any safety incident, illness beyond first aid, or substantive
  disciplinary matter is communicated to the guardian the same day.

### 7.7 Discipline
- Boarding-specific conduct expectations (lights-out compliance, room
  cleanliness, respect for shared living space) sit alongside, not
  instead of, the general Student Code of Conduct (SD-02).
- Serious breaches follow the same process as the Student Code of
  Conduct's disciplinary procedure (SD-02 §7.3–7.4) — boarding does not
  create a separate disciplinary track.

### 7.8 Weekend and leave-out procedures
- A boarding student leaves the premises outside a scheduled activity
  only with prior guardian authorisation, logged by the boarding warden
  (who is collecting/authorised, expected return time).
- A student not returned by the expected time triggers guardian contact
  immediately — not held until the next scheduled check.
- **The specific weekend/leave-out schedule (which weekends students
  may go home, standing authorisation vs. per-instance) is not set by
  this draft** — a decision for the institutional lead based on real
  boarding-house practice.

### 7.9 Personal belongings and money
- Boarding students' valuables and any money held are logged if kept
  with the boarding warden for safekeeping — **SHRS's current practice
  for this needs confirmation**; this section states the requirement
  for a logged system, not a specific storage arrangement assumed to
  already exist.
- Lost or damaged property is reported to the boarding warden the same
  day it's noticed, logged, and the guardian informed where the item is
  of significant value.

### 7.10 Homesickness and settling-in support
- A new boarding student's first weeks (the settling-in period, Section
  4) receive additional pastoral check-ins from the boarding warden,
  beyond the standard daily routine.
- A student showing persistent distress beyond ordinary homesickness is
  referred to the Guidance Counsellor (if the post exists) or the
  Designated Safeguarding Lead where the distress suggests a welfare
  concern beyond normal adjustment.

### 7.11 Fire and emergency procedures (boarding-specific)
- Boarding houses follow the Emergency Response Plan's (SW-09) general
  fire and emergency procedures, with the addition that a current room
  list is held by the warden at all times, not only during drills —
  this section does not duplicate that plan's procedure, only confirms
  its boarding-specific application.
- A boarding-house fire drill is held at the same interval as the
  general site drill (Emergency Response Plan §7.1), conducted
  overnight at least once per year in addition to the standard daytime
  drill, given the distinct overnight-evacuation challenge boarding
  presents.

### 7.12 End of term and departure procedures
- A boarding student's departure at end of term is confirmed against
  the guardian collecting them or an authorised alternative — logged by
  the boarding warden, mirroring the same verification standard as the
  Visitors Policy's (SW-08) parent-collection provision.
- Belongings and any medication held by the boarding house (Section
  7.9) are returned and confirmed received at departure.

## 8. Monitoring and Compliance

The institutional lead reviews the room-check log, leave-out log, and
any boarding-specific incident report termly, reporting a summary to
the DSL (once appointed) and Head of Schools / Administrator annually.

## 9. Records and Documentation

Room-check logs, leave-out authorisations, and boarding-specific
incident reports are retained per the Records Retention Policy
(IT-04); the specific period for these boarding-specific record types
is not separately set by that policy today and should be added at its
next review.

## 11. Exceptions

None defined at this draft stage; any house-specific variation should
be added as a named appendix, not assumed silently.

## 12. Appeals and Complaints

A guardian who disputes a boarding-related disciplinary decision may
appeal through the same process as the Student Code of Conduct (SD-02
§12).

## 13. Review and Amendment

Annual, or immediately after any boarding safety incident.
