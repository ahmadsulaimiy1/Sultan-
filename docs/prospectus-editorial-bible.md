# SHRS Flagship Prospectus — Editorial Bible v1.0

*The constitutional, editorial, and design authority for the Sultan
Hanafi Royal Schools Flagship Prospectus, and the framework future
institutional publications (annual reports, handbooks, admission
guides) should derive from. Approved before any layout, spread, or
production work begins, per the governing directive.*

## How this document was built

The directive asks this to be produced by a CEO, a Chief Brand/
Experience/Marketing/Communications/Admissions/Design/Publishing/
Information/Advancement Officer, a Prestige & Reputation Strategist,
three Directors, six boards, and thirteen professors. This document
does not literally stage thirteen invented personas exchanging
memos — that would be theatre, not editorial governance, and every
"no ceiling" directive this engagement has received has been honoured
by taking the ambition seriously and answering it with real,
disciplined work, not by performing scale. What it does instead:
every part below is written to the standard of rigor those roles
represent, and — the more important discipline — **every fact,
statistic, name, colour, typeface, and story in this document is
drawn from what SHRS has actually published**, not invented to sound
more impressive. Where the directive assumes something exists that
doesn't yet (a "Vision 2035" target, a Chairman distinct from the
Head of Schools / Administrator, a finalised page count), that is named as an open decision, the
same way every other governance document in this project has treated
a real gap — not silently invented and not silently dropped.

## PART I — Editorial Philosophy

### What SHRS represents
A hybrid Islamic-and-secular institution — the founder's own words for
it, used consistently across the live site — built on the premise that
faith and academic rigour are not in tension. Five institutions under
one foundation (Basic School, Royal College, Islamiyyah
College, Qur'an College, and Online & Distance Learning School) serve different ages and pathways but share
one mission, one set of values, and one crest. SHRS represents a school
built from a specific act of filial honour — named for the founder's
late father, Anofi Aliu Akano — grown from one community (Imowonla,
Ikorodu) outward, not a franchise or an import of a foreign model
wearing local branding.

### What SHRS does not represent
Not a purely secular international school that treats Islamic identity
as an add-on programme. Not a purely religious institution that treats
secular academic rigour as secondary. Not an exclusive institution —
the founder's own welcome message states the schools are "open to
Muslims and non-Muslims, males and females." Not a school that
claims a heritage it doesn't have: SHRS is young (registered December
2017, Royal College established 2021) and its prestige case rests on
what it has actually built in that time — a real Digital Campus, a
published governance architecture, a specific five-stage Hifz
methodology — not on manufactured antiquity.

### Institutional character
Values-led, community-rooted, professionally governed. The founder's
own background (FCCA, FCA, two decades in banking/insurance/oil & gas/
consulting before founding SHRS) gives the institution a operational
and financial seriousness uncommon in a school this young — the
prospectus should let that show through governance and structure, not
just photography.

### Educational identity
Holistic by design: the Mission statement already published
(`pages/about.html`) commits to "both Islamic and secular knowledge
through rigorous research and effective teaching methods." The
prospectus's academic sections should honour that word "rigorous" —
this is not a soft, values-only offering.

### Islamic identity
Expressed through the crest itself (Crescent & Star: "faith as the
compass of everything taught here" — already-published symbolism, not
invented here) and through the Qur'an College's real, specific,
five-stage Hifz Journey and Ijazah credentialing framework — the one
part of SHRS's offering with genuine, publishable methodological depth
distinct from any competitor.

### International identity
Real, not aspirational-only: the founder holds degrees from Oxford
Brookes University and Edinburgh Business School (Heriot-Watt
University), and the school documents real scholarly visits abroad
(`gallery/scholarly-visit-1.jpg`, `-2.jpg`). The prospectus's
international register should be built from these facts, not from
comparison to Oxford/Cambridge/Harvard as if SHRS claims their
lineage — see Part IX for how this project handles that distinction.

### Nigerian identity
Explicitly located, not placeless. Ikorodu, Lagos State, the Imowonla
community specifically — named, not obscured. The founder's community
investment (free lectures, COVID-19 relief, road maintenance,
electrification support) is real local-impact material a flagship
prospectus should carry, not hide behind aspirational imagery.

### Future vision identity
**Open finding, not invented here**: no "Vision 2035" or similarly
dated strategic target exists anywhere in this project's published
material. The only published Vision statement is: "To be recognised
as a leading institution excelling in knowledge dissemination and
character building — creating a positive impact wherever our presence
is felt." The prospectus's forward-looking section should be built
from this real statement, expanded with real, already-shipped evidence
(the Digital Campus, the governance architecture) as proof of
trajectory — not a fabricated dated roadmap. If SHRS leadership wants
a dated strategic vision for the prospectus, that is a real content
decision for them to supply, not one this document invents.

### One coherent voice
Every section of the prospectus should sound like it was written by
the same institution that wrote the Mission/Vision/Values already on
the live site and the "CLEVER" values acronym (Creativity, Leadership,
Engagement, Versatility, Ethics, Reliability) — warm but precise,
proud but not boastful, exactly the register Part X formalises below.

## PART II — Brand Narrative Framework

### The SHRS story, in the shape the prospectus should tell it

**Past** — Founded December 2017 by Zakariya Olanrewaju Anofi, named
for his late father Anofi Aliu Akano, a Nigerian Ports Authority clerk
whose own education was limited but who was determined his children
would have opportunities he didn't. The idea took shape during Lagos
Governor Babatunde Fashola's administration and was carried through
with the encouragement of the founder's mentor, Governor Seyi Makinde,
and his brother Lukman Olajide Anofi. Rooted in Imowonla, Ikorodu.

**Present** — Five institutions, one crest, one governance architecture
spanning 20+ published policies, a real Digital Campus (Parent Portal,
Student Portal, Staff Identity Platform, Registrar's Office, Hifz &
Ijazah tracking) — genuinely unusual depth for a school of this age,
and a real, differentiating prospectus asset: most competitor
prospectuses show buildings and uniforms; SHRS can show a governed,
audited, permission-controlled institutional operating system.

**Future** — Built from the published Vision statement (see Part I),
not a fabricated roadmap. Where SHRS leadership supplies real
near-term plans (a Teacher Portal, an Examinations Office, physical
campus expansion), those belong here once confirmed — not before.

**Transformation / Impact / Legacy** — The community-impact record
(free lectures, COVID-19 relief, road maintenance, electrification) is
real transformation evidence, not marketing filler — it should carry
real weight in the narrative, likely as its own feature panel (Part
VIII), not a footnote.

**Global aspirations** — Grounded in the founder's own international
academic credentials and the documented scholarly visits abroad, not
asserted as an already-achieved international footprint SHRS doesn't
have. The honest claim is "internationally-informed, locally-rooted,"
not "international school" in the sense of an overseas campus network.

**Educational mission** — The published Mission statement, verbatim,
is the anchor line of the entire narrative arc — every section should
trace back to it.

### The reader's journey through the story
The prospectus should read as one continuous narrative arc — Heritage
→ Doctrine (Mission/Vision/Values) → the Five Institutions → the
Digital Campus (SHRS's genuine point of difference) → Governance &
Safeguarding (trust) → Admissions (action) — mirroring the "Chapter"
structure the live site's `about.html` already uses (`Chapter II —
Heritage`, `Chapter III — Doctrine`). Reusing that chaptered framing in
print, rather than inventing a new structural metaphor, keeps the
prospectus and the website speaking as one institution rather than two.

## PART III — Publication Architecture

### Total page philosophy
A flagship prospectus at this level typically runs 32–64 pages (16–32
spreads) in the benchmark category the directive names (university
prospectuses, luxury annual reports). **This document does not fix an
exact count** — that is Deliverable #2 (Prospectus Master Architecture)'s
job, once content volume per section is actually planned. What this
Bible fixes is the section *hierarchy* below, so Master Architecture
allocates pages against a settled structure, not an improvised one.

### Section hierarchy (maps directly to the Content Structure the
directive named, sequenced per Part II's narrative arc)

1. **Cover** — see Part VIII
2. **Welcome Message** (Director's Message — the real, already-published
   voice; see the "Chairman's Message" note below)
3. **About SHRS** — the Heritage chapter (Part II)
4. **Mission, Vision & Values** — the Doctrine chapter, including the
   CLEVER standard
5. **Educational Philosophy / Why Choose SHRS**
6. **The Five Institutions** — one feature spread each:
   Basic School, Royal College, School of Islamic and Arabic Studies,
   Qur'an College, and Online & Distance Learning School — presented as **equally-weighted pillars**,
   not Royal College as primary with supplements
7. **Student Life** — pastoral care, community, the values in practice
8. **Facilities**
9. **Technology & Digital Campus** — SHRS's genuine differentiator;
   given real editorial weight, not a single token page
10. **Admissions** — the pathway, process, what to expect
11. **Governance & Safeguarding** — the trust chapter; this is where a
    school this young earns credibility a legacy institution gets for
    free, so it deserves real design investment, not a compliance
    afterthought
12. **Parent Partnership**
13. **Achievements**
14. **Future Vision** — built from the real Vision statement (Part I)
15. **Contact Information**

**Open point, named not resolved**: the directive asks for both a
"Chairman's Message" and a "Head of Schools / Administrator's Message" as distinct sections. No
Chairman distinct from the founder is publicly named anywhere in this
project — governance documentation records a five-member Board of
Governors whose composition is "not individually published." Two
options exist: (a) treat the founder's existing, real Director's
Message as the sole leadership voice, or (b) SHRS supplies a named
Board Chairman and a distinct message. This Bible does not invent a
Chairman to fill the directive's template — that decision belongs to
SHRS leadership, flagged here for Master Architecture to resolve
before spread planning.

### Reader journeys
- **Parent journey**: Welcome → Mission/Values → the specific
  institution their child would join → Facilities/Student Life →
  Admissions → Contact. The four-institution spread should let a
  parent self-select their child's pathway within one page-turn.
- **Student-facing journey** *(where the prospectus addresses an older
  applicant directly, e.g. Royal College senior secondary or Qur'an
  College)*: Student Life → the relevant institution → Achievements →
  Admissions.
- **International/diaspora visitor journey**: Heritage (credibility of
  founding story) → Governance (institutional seriousness) → the
  Digital Campus (proof of operational sophistication) → Admissions.
  This reader is the least likely to visit in person before deciding,
  so the prospectus carries more evidentiary weight for them than for
  a local parent who can simply visit campus.

## PART IV — Typography Constitution

*Every typeface named below is already in production use across the
live site (`css/brand.css`), verified directly rather than proposed
fresh — the prospectus should extend the site's existing type system,
not invent a parallel one. That consistency is itself a prestige
signal: an institution whose print and digital identity visibly agree
reads as more disciplined than one where they don't.*

| Role | Typeface pairing | Where it's already proven |
|---|---|---|
| **Cover / Display** | Cinzel (Latin) + Amiri (Arabic) | Section eyebrows, crest labelling, the seal's own heading (`.seal-inner h2`) — an engraved, authoritative register |
| **Headings** | Cinzel + Amiri, uppercase, wide letter-spacing (0.06–0.2em depending on weight) | Every section head across the site |
| **Editorial / pull quotes / lede paragraphs** | Cormorant Garamond (Latin) + Amiri (Arabic), often italic | The footer's institutional statement, the seal quadrants, map introductions — SHRS's established "elegant aside" voice |
| **Body text** | Inter (Latin) + Cairo (Arabic) | All running text, forms, captions site-wide |
| **Statistics / data labels** | Cinzel, small caps, tight tracking | Matches the existing "eyebrow" label convention |
| **Captions** | Inter, smaller size, `--ink-soft` colour | Matches existing caption treatment |

### Arabic typography
Amiri is the established Arabic serif pairing for display/heading
contexts; Cairo for Arabic body text — both already selected and
proven at scale across the bilingual site, not new choices for print.

### Bilingual typography rules
The live site already runs a full parallel Arabic build (`ar/`
directory, RTL-aware CSS) — the prospectus should decide explicitly
(a Master Architecture question, not this Bible's) whether it ships
as an English edition with Arabic pull-quotes/crest text (the site's
current print-adjacent convention, e.g. the seal quadrant Arabic
labelling) or as a genuine dual English/Arabic edition mirroring the
website's full bilingual build. **Recommendation, not yet a decision**:
given the scale of a full Arabic edition, start with an English
edition carrying Arabic in display/ceremonial contexts (crest, seal,
pull-quotes) exactly as the website already does, with a full Arabic
edition as a named future phase — consistent with this project's
established "ship the real thing now, name the rest honestly" pattern
rather than committing to double production scope up front.

### No generic fonts, no accidental typography
Every typeface above is justified by existing production use, not
picked for this document. Any new typeface introduced at the Visual
Design System stage (Deliverable #4) needs the same justification.

## PART V — Colour Constitution

*Grounded entirely in `css/brand.css`'s actual, already-refined token
system (expanded specifically for "prestige" purposes in an earlier
phase of this project) — not a new palette invented for print. Mapping
the directive's requested palette onto what already exists:*

| Directive's term | Maps to existing token | Hex | Where it already lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Royal Coffee Brown | `--navy` / `--navy-deep` | `#3B2A1D` / `#221709` | Primary dark ground colour site-wide — despite the CSS variable name "navy," the actual hue is a deep coffee-brown, matching the directive's intent exactly |
| Royal Gold | `--gold` / `--gold-bright` | `#C6A15B` / `#E9CE8A` | The institution's signature accent — crest, headings, CTAs |
| Premium Cream | `--ivory` / `--cream` | `#F7EEDF` / `#F1E4C8` | Light-mode surface colour |
| Deep Charcoal | `--charcoal` / `--charcoal-deep` | `#2A2621` / `#1C1914` | Already an established supporting dark tone |
| Ivory White | `--milk` | `#FCFAF6` | The lightest established surface tone |
| Rich Bronze | `--bronze` / `--bronze-bright` | `#7C5430` / `#A47843` | Already an established accent, distinct from gold |
| Elegant Navy | `--oxford-navy` | `#41618C` | A genuine blue-navy already in the palette, distinct from the brown "navy" token above — this resolves a naming collision the directive's own list would otherwise create |
| Soft Sandstone | `--parchment` | `#EAE0C0` | An established warm neutral, sits between ivory and gold |

### Where each colour belongs
- **Coffee brown (`navy`/`navy-deep`)** — dominant ground colour for
  cover, chapter dividers, and any full-bleed "seal" moment. This is
  SHRS's most identity-bearing colour; it should never be used as a
  minor accent.
- **Gold/gold-bright** — accents, rules, small-caps labels, the crest
  itself. Never as a large background field (gold-on-gold text
  readability fails, and it reads as gaudy rather than prestigious at
  scale — the live site already avoids this).
  Photography-safe: gold overlays on imagery should use the existing
  low-alpha tint conventions already proven in the site's card/overlay
  system, not flat colour blocks.
- **Ivory/cream/milk** — body-copy backgrounds; the "reading" surface
  of the publication, mirroring the site's existing light-mode surface
  logic.
- **Charcoal** — an alternative dark ground for sections that need
  separation from the coffee-brown "identity" sections without reading
  as a completely different publication (e.g. a data-heavy Governance
  spread).
- **Bronze** — a secondary accent for iconography and diagrams, so not
  everything visual reads as "gold," which would flatten the palette's
  hierarchy.
- **Oxford-navy (Elegant Navy)** — reserved, used sparingly for
  specific institutional contexts (this token is already used
  elsewhere on the site for a distinct accent identity) — likely best
  suited to a Governance/Academic-Excellence data-visualisation context
  rather than the emotional/heritage sections.
- **Parchment (Soft Sandstone)** — texture and warmth in
  photography-adjacent panels, an alternative to flat ivory where a
  softer, aged-paper feel suits the content (Heritage chapter).

### Where each colour must never appear
Coffee-brown/charcoal text on gold backgrounds below body-text size
(fails contrast); gold text on cream/ivory below a certain weight
(low contrast, already avoided site-wide via the `--ink-soft` pattern
for secondary text instead); more than two "identity" colours
(coffee-brown, gold) competing for dominance on a single spread — the
site's own discipline is one dominant + one accent + neutrals, and
print should hold that same restraint.

### Accessibility and printing considerations
The live site already carries documented light/dark-mode contrast
logic (`--on-surface`, `--on-surface-soft` tokens) — the print palette
should be checked against WCAG-equivalent contrast ratios even though
print has no literal "mode," because the same coffee-brown/gold/ivory
combinations will likely be reused digitally (a PDF edition, web
excerpts). CMYK conversion of the exact hexes above needs a proofing
pass before commercial print — flagged here as a production
requirement for Deliverable #5, not resolved in this document.

## PART VI — Photography Constitution

### Photography style
Documentary-authentic, not staged-stock. The live site's existing
photo integration work (campus gallery, workshop photos, a competition
photo, building hero imagery, recitation photos, the college hall, the
scholarly-visit-abroad pair) already establishes the house style: real
students, real staff, real spaces, natural light where possible,
warm-toned grading that harmonises with the coffee-brown/gold palette
rather than a cool corrective grade. The prospectus should draw from
this existing, Director-approved photo library first, and commission
new photography only where a genuine gap exists (this Bible does not
inventory that gap — a Deliverable #2/#3 task).

### Category-specific direction
- **Student photography** — candid over posed; genuine classroom and
  co-curricular moments, consistent with the "documentary-authentic"
  standard above, not a stock-agency "diverse smiling students" grid.
- **Classroom photography** — should show real teaching in progress,
  not empty staged rooms.
- **Qur'an College photography** — reverent, calm register; the
  existing recitation photos already establish this tone.
- **Leadership photography** — the founder's photo already published
  on the Governance page is the anchor image; formal but warm, not
  corporate-generic.
- **Facilities photography** — the building hero and college hall
  images already integrated set the standard: architectural pride
  without over-processing.
- **Technology photography** — genuinely hard to source authentically
  (a "Digital Campus" is mostly interface, not a physical scene) —
  recommend UI screenshots treated with the same premium framing
  language as physical photography (a "framed device" convention),
  rather than generic stock images of laptops.
- **Parent/community photography** — the founder's community-impact
  record (COVID-19 relief, infrastructure support) has real
  documentary value here if photographic evidence exists; if it
  doesn't, this should be named as a gap rather than filled with a
  generic "community" stock image that would undercut the specific,
  real story Part II tells.

### No stock-photo feel, no AI-photo feel
Every image used should be traceable to a real SHRS moment — the same
discipline the live site has followed (task history in this project
shows real photo integration replacing empty/placeholder slots, never
the reverse). Any AI-generated imagery would directly contradict Part
I's "what SHRS does not represent" — an institution asserting a
prestige it manufactures rather than earns.

## PART VII — Iconography Constitution

### Icon style, weight, size, placement
The live site's existing icon system (expanded in an earlier phase
specifically to move past "generic clip-art") establishes the
baseline: linear/outlined icons at a consistent stroke weight, gold or
bronze accent colour on a coffee-brown or ivory ground, sized large
enough to function as a genuine visual anchor at the top of a feature
panel (matching the directive's "large, professional, meaningful, not
generic clip-art" instruction directly). The prospectus should reuse
this exact icon language rather than introduce a second, print-specific
icon set — visual consistency between web and print is itself a
prestige signal, per Part V's closing note.

### Institutional diagram standards
Diagrams (academic progression pathways, the Hifz 5-stage journey, the
Digital Campus ecosystem, the admissions process) should use the same
restrained line-and-label language as icons — gold/bronze lines on
ivory or coffee-brown grounds, Cinzel labels — not a generic
management-consulting flowchart aesthetic (rounded rectangles,
drop-shadow arrows). The live site already has a real curriculum
diagram and a real timetable strip (built in an earlier phase) that
should inform this treatment directly rather than starting from
nothing.

### Infographic / data visualisation standards
Executive-report quality, per the directive — meaning restrained,
label-led, and never using more than the coffee-brown/gold/bronze/
oxford-navy accent set for a single chart, matching the "no more than
two identity colours competing" rule from Part V. The live site's
existing homepage counter/stat-band component (built specifically for
this kind of "institutional seriousness" signalling) is the direct
print precedent.

### No childish icons, no clip-art, no inconsistent systems
Explicitly ruled out: filled "flat design" app-icon style icons,
mismatched icon sets sourced from different libraries, and any icon
whose metaphor reads as playful rather than institutional (a cartoon
graduation cap, for instance) — the existing site icon system already
avoids all of these; the prospectus's job is fidelity to it, not
reinvention.

## PART VIII — Layout Constitution

### Cover system
Full-bleed premium photography (a real campus or ceremonial moment,
per Part VI) with a coffee-brown gradient overlay carrying the crest,
institution name in Cinzel display type, and one restrained prestige
line (drawn from the real Mission/Vision language, not an invented
tagline) — matching the directive's explicit instruction that the
cover must be "a genuine flagship publication cover," not a title
page. The crest (`crest-full.png`) already exists at production
quality and should anchor the composition.

### Opening spread system
Director's Welcome Message (the real, published voice) paired with a
single powerful portrait or campus image — mirroring the site's
existing `.director-card`/`.director-body` split-layout convention
directly, translated to print.

### Feature spread system (the Five Institutions)
Each institution gets a structurally identical template — one full
spread, one anchor image, one short narrative paragraph, one "at a
glance" data panel (ages, format, established date, distinguishing
feature) — enforcing Part III's "equally-weighted pillars"
requirement through layout discipline, not just editorial intent: if
the template is identical, no institution can visually read as more or
less important than another.

### Editorial spread system
Long-form narrative sections (Heritage, Student Life, Parent
Partnership) — generous margins, Cormorant Garamond pull-quotes
breaking up Inter body copy, one supporting image per spread rather
than a gallery grid, per the directive's "not image dumps" instruction.

### Statistics spread system
Reuses the Infographic standard from Part VII — large numerals in
Cinzel, gold or oxford-navy, minimal supporting chrome.

### Timeline spread system
For the Heritage chapter's Past→Present→Future arc (Part II) — a
single horizontal or vertical line in gold, marked with Cinzel-labelled
milestones, photography inset at key points rather than a dense
text-heavy timeline.

### Governance spread system
Given Part III's instruction that this chapter "deserves real design
investment, not a compliance afterthought" — this spread should use
the Statistics/Infographic language (Part VII) to visualise the
institution's actual governance depth (number of published policies,
the four-pillar office structure, the audit/permission architecture)
rather than a dense paragraph of policy prose. This is where SHRS's
genuine differentiator — a young school with an unusually mature
governance architecture — gets to look as impressive as it actually
is.

### Admissions spread system
Process-diagram-led (per Part VII's diagram standard), using the real
admissions journey (Enquiry → Application → Admission → Parent Portal)
already built and documented in this project's Digital Campus work —
a genuine, working process, not an invented illustrative one.

### Closing spread system
Contact information, crest, and a final pull-quote drawn from the
Mission or Vision statement — a quiet, confident close rather than a
final hard sales pitch, consistent with Part X's voice guide.

### Every spread intentional
No spread template above is generic — each is derived from a
component that already exists and works on the live site, translated
to the print medium rather than invented from a blank page.

## PART IX — Prestige Standards

*The directive asks this document to explain what makes the named
benchmark publications (Oxford, Cambridge, Harvard, Qatar Foundation,
NEOM, Aramco, Al-Qasimia, King Saud University) respected, and how
SHRS adopts those principles without copying them. This section does
that directly — it does not claim SHRS's history or scale, which would
undercut Part I's "what SHRS does not represent."*

### What makes those publications respected
1. **Restraint, not density** — they use white space as a prestige
   signal; a cheaper publication fills every inch to prove value, a
   confident one doesn't need to.
2. **Institutional voice, singular and consistent** — no publication
   in that tier sounds like five different departments wrote five
   different sections; one editorial hand governs the whole document
   (exactly what this Bible exists to guarantee for SHRS).
3. **Evidence over adjectives** — they show governance structures,
   real numbers, real named people and real outcomes rather than
   claiming "world-class" in prose. The word "excellence" is earned by
   the surrounding evidence, not asserted as a headline.
4. **Typographic discipline** — a small, deliberate type system used
   with total consistency, never more than two or three typefaces.
5. **Photography that documents rather than performs** — real people
   in real institutional moments, not stock imagery selling an
   aspiration.

### How SHRS adopts these principles without copying them
1. **Restraint** — Part VIII's layout constitution (one image per
   editorial spread, generous margins, restrained accent-colour use)
   applies the same discipline at SHRS's own scale.
2. **Singular voice** — this Bible itself, and Part X's voice guide
   specifically, is the mechanism; every contributor writes to one
   standard rather than one department per section.
3. **Evidence over adjectives** — SHRS's real evidence is different
   from Oxford's (it doesn't have 900 years of history) but is
   genuinely strong on its own terms: a published governance
   architecture most schools this size don't have, a real Digital
   Campus, a specific and rigorous Hifz methodology, a founder with
   real international credentials and a documented community-impact
   record. The Governance spread (Part VIII) exists specifically to
   let this evidence carry the "world-class" claim instead of the word
   itself doing it.
4. **Typographic discipline** — Part IV's type system, already proven
   in production, applied with total consistency.
5. **Documentary photography** — Part VI's standard, built from SHRS's
   own real photo library rather than a stock alternative.

### What SHRS should not attempt to copy
Scale claims (campus size, endowment, centuries of history), a
Western-institution register that would sit oddly against Part I's
Nigerian/Islamic identity commitments, or literal visual mimicry of
any named benchmark's crest, colour system, or layout — the goal is
publication-craft parity, not brand imitation.

## PART X — Editorial Voice Guide

### Tone
Warm but precise. Proud without exaggeration. The founder's own
published welcome message is the template: personal, credential-backed,
values-first, never using superlatives ("the best," "world-class,"
"unrivalled") without a specific, checkable fact immediately beside
them.

### Language and formality
Formal enough for an international reader unfamiliar with Nigerian
educational context (spell out qualifications, explain the Hifz
Journey's structure rather than assuming familiarity), warm enough that
it doesn't read as a legal or corporate document — the register
`about.html`'s existing prose already strikes.

### Aspirational messaging
Grounded in the real Vision statement and real evidence (Part IX), not
free-floating superlatives. Every aspirational claim should be
answerable with "because —" and a real fact.

### Parent communication style
Direct, reassuring, practical — the Admissions and Governance/
Safeguarding sections especially should read as answering a worried
parent's actual questions, not performing prestige at them.

### Student communication style
Where the prospectus speaks to an older applicant directly (Part III's
student-facing journey), the register can be slightly more energetic
and second-person, without dropping the institution's overall
formality — still not slang, still not "cool school" marketing voice.

### International/diaspora communication style
Should not assume prior familiarity with Nigerian schooling
conventions or Lagos geography — Ikorodu, NYSC, WAEC-equivalent
context, and Islamic educational terms (Hifz, Ijazah, Muhaffiz) should
be briefly glossed on first use, the same discipline the live site's
own multilingual/international-facing pages already apply.

### Never boastful, never exaggerated, never cheap-marketing-promotional

## PART XII — Flagship Quality Assurance Framework

Before any spread is approved (Deliverable #3 onward), it should pass
every check below. These translate the directive's "Would an Oxford
admissions officer respect this / would a Saudi parent trust this /
etc." questions into checks this project can actually verify, rather
than rhetorical questions no reviewer can objectively answer:

1. **Is every fact on this page traceable to something SHRS has
   actually published or confirmed?** (No invented statistics, no
   fabricated dated targets, no claimed history the institution
   doesn't have.)
2. **Does the typography match Part IV exactly** — no fonts outside the
   established Cinzel/Cormorant Garamond/Inter (+ Amiri/Cairo) system?
3. **Does the colour use match Part V** — no more than two identity
   colours dominant on one spread, correct contrast for body text?
4. **Is the photography documentary-authentic per Part VI** — no stock
   or AI-generated imagery?
5. **Do icons and diagrams match Part VII's established visual
   language** — no clip-art, no mismatched icon sets?
6. **Does the spread match its assigned template in Part VIII** — or,
   if it deliberately breaks the template, is that a considered
   editorial decision, not an inconsistency?
7. **Does the copy match Part X's voice** — no unearned superlatives,
   no register that would embarrass this project's own governance
   documents?
8. **Does the page respect Part XI's white-space rule** (≤~40% text
   coverage)?
9. **Would this page still look correct if reprinted in ten years** —
   i.e., does it avoid dated design trends in favour of the restrained,
   typographic, evidence-led approach Part IX describes as timeless?

A page that fails any check above returns to the relevant Part for
revision before advancing — the same escalation discipline this
project's governance documents already use for policy gaps, applied to
design.
