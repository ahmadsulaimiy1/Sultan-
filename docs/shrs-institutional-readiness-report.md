# SHRS Institutional Readiness Report

**Prepared:** Phase D, as the capstone synthesis of the Governance
Master Register, the Governance Resolution Register, and the Phase
A–D consistency audits.
**Audience:** Board of Trustees and Executive Management Team.

> **How to read this report.** Per the explicit instruction this phase
> was given: *do not measure success by the number of documents
> drafted — measure it by whether SHRS could operate consistently if
> leadership changed tomorrow.* Twenty-three governance documents now
> exist. That is not the finding of this report. The finding is what
> those documents do and do not yet make true about SHRS as an
> institution, independent of who currently holds each role — see the
> Institutional Continuity Test at the end, which is the real
> conclusion this report is building toward.

---

## Maturity scale used below

- **Not Started** — no document, no practice.
- **Foundational** — documented, not yet operational (nobody is doing
  the thing the document describes yet).
- **Developing** — partially operational, or fully documented with one
  concrete gap blocking full operation.
- **Established** — documented and consistent with real, current
  practice, and could be checked against real activity, not just paper.

No area below is scored as "Established" — this is an honest reflection
of where SHRS actually is: a strong documentation foundation has just
been built, and none of it has yet been tested against a full
operating cycle.

---

## 1. Governance

**Maturity: Developing.**
- **Strength:** the Constitution & Governance Charter now exists and
  names real people and real roles throughout, not generic titles.
- **Key risk:** the Charter has never been tested against an actual
  Board decision — it describes how authority *should* flow, not a
  verified record of how it *has* flowed historically.
- **Missing components:** Board Charter, Delegation of Authority
  Policy, Conflict of Interest Policy, Whistleblowing Policy, Risk
  Management Framework — all still MISSING (Governance Master
  Register, §1).
- **Recommended action:** the Board should formally adopt the
  Constitution first (nothing above it can be meaningfully adopted
  until the foundation is), then prioritise the Conflict of Interest
  Policy given its direct relevance to fee-schedule and vendor
  decisions already pending on the Governance Resolution Register.

## 2. Academic Affairs

**Maturity: Developing.**
- **Strength:** Academic Regulations now exist as a real document built
  on the live, already-functioning Assessment Policy grading scale —
  not invented from scratch.
- **Key risk:** the numeric promotion threshold and academic-integrity
  consequence scale are both still unset (Governance Resolution
  Register §2) — meaning two different class teachers could plausibly
  apply different informal standards today, invisibly, until a real
  number exists to check against.
- **Missing components:** Examination Policy, Academic Integrity
  Policy, Certification Policy — all still MISSING.
- **Recommended action:** set the promotion threshold before the next
  academic year's promotion decisions, not retroactively after a
  disputed one forces the question.

## 3. Student Affairs

**Maturity: Developing, closest to Established of any area.**
- **Strength:** Student Code of Conduct, Student Handbook, and Boarding
  Regulations form a genuinely complete, cross-referenced set — the
  strongest single cluster in this whole register.
- **Key risk:** the Boarding Regulations' owner (Principal, Qur'an
  College) doesn't quite match its stated scope (all boarding
  students) — a small gap surfaced in the Phase D consistency audit,
  low-impact today, worth closing before it matters.
- **Missing components:** Attendance Policy, Welfare Policy —
  standalone documents defining what's currently only implied by
  practice.
- **Recommended action:** narrow the Boarding Regulations' scope
  language or name a joint authority, at the next scheduled review —
  a documentation fix, not a new decision.

## 4. Safeguarding

**Maturity: Foundational — and this is the most important line in this
entire report.**
- **Strength:** the Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy, Boarding
  Regulations' safeguarding provisions, and the new Designated
  Safeguarding Lead Framework together are the single most thoroughly
  developed cluster of documents in the whole ecosystem.
- **Key risk, stated plainly: none of it is operational yet, because
  nobody has been appointed Designated Safeguarding Lead.** Every
  procedure in every safeguarding document assumes this role is filled.
  It is not. This is the one finding in this entire report that should
  reach the Board's attention before any other.
- **Missing components:** the DSL appointment itself; accredited
  training-provider identification; confirmed external referral-agency
  contacts.
- **Recommended action:** appoint a DSL. Not draft anything — appoint
  someone. This is a decision, not a document, and it is now the single
  highest-priority action on the Governance Resolution Register.

## 5. Technology

**Maturity: Developing.**
- **Strength:** Information Security Policy, Data Protection & Privacy
  Policy, Acceptable Use Policy, AI Usage Policy, and Records Retention
  Policy form a genuinely coherent set, grounded in what the Parent
  Portal actually implements today (verified against real code, not
  assumed).
- **Key risk:** no Incident Response Policy exists — if a real security
  incident happened today, response would be improvised, not
  procedural.
- **Missing components:** Incident Response Policy (recommended next),
  Cybersecurity Framework (lower urgency).
- **Recommended action:** draft the Incident Response Policy before the
  Parent Portal holds real production data — this is the one
  technology gap with a plausible near-term trigger (a real database is
  not yet provisioned, per `docs/parent-portal.md`, but will be soon).

## 6. Finance

**Maturity: Foundational.**
- **Strength:** the Financial Controls Policy establishes real
  approval and segregation-of-duties process, grounded in the fact that
  SHRS's own CEO and at least one Board member hold professional
  accounting qualifications.
- **Key risk:** three financial documents (Tuition & Fees, Refund,
  Scholarship Governance) are entirely blocked on a fee schedule that
  doesn't exist yet — this is the most concrete, actionable gap in the
  whole report, because closing it requires no drafting at all, only a
  decision the school can make independently of any of this governance
  work.
- **Missing components:** Tuition & Fees Policy, Refund Policy,
  Procurement Policy, Scholarship Governance Framework.
- **Recommended action:** this is squarely a "stop waiting on
  governance work and just decide the fee schedule" item — nothing
  about drafting more policy advances this.

## 7. Digital Campus

**Maturity: Foundational.**
- **Strength:** the Parent Portal is real, coded, and already
  security-audited (`docs/parent-portal-audit.md`); the AI assistant is
  real and honestly self-disclosing. Both now have governance layers
  (Data Protection & Privacy Policy, AI Usage Policy) written to match
  what they actually do, not what a generic policy template would
  assume.
- **Key risk:** neither system has real production data yet — no
  database provisioned, no Anthropic API key configured
  (`docs/parent-portal.md`, `docs/digital-assistant.md`). The
  governance is ready; the systems are not yet live with real users.
- **Missing components:** the actual production setup steps, which are
  outside this governance work's scope — they're the school's own
  infrastructure decision to make.
- **Recommended action:** complete the setup steps in both linked
  documents when ready; no further governance drafting is required
  first.

## 8. Compliance

**Maturity: Foundational.**
- **Strength:** every document in this ecosystem names, honestly, what
  it does and doesn't certify — no document claims legal compliance it
  hasn't verified.
- **Key risk:** the Governance Resolution Register's Section 6 lists
  six distinct items needing external legal/regulatory/professional
  confirmation (NDPA 2023, Child's Rights Act applicability, SHRS's
  corporate form, fire-safety assessment, Ijazah *sanad* documentation,
  Nigerian labour law) — none resolved yet.
- **Missing components:** the external legal engagement itself.
- **Recommended action:** a single legal-review engagement covering all
  six items at once would likely be more efficient than resolving them
  one at a time as each policy comes up for adoption.

## Institutional Continuity Test

*The actual question this report was asked to answer: if every current
named leader — the CEO, every Principal, the VP Administration, the
Registrar, the ICT Head — left tomorrow, would SHRS continue to
function on these documents alone?*

**Partially — and the gap is not where document count would suggest.**

The good news first: this governance ecosystem is genuinely
**role-based, not person-based**, and that was a deliberate design
choice held consistently across all four phases. Every document names
a role (CEO, VP Administration, Principal, Registrar) as the
accountable owner, not "Zakaria" or "Fatimah" as individuals — a new
CEO could read the Constitution & Governance Charter and know exactly
what authority they hold, without needing the previous CEO to explain
it. That is a real, structural continuity strength, and it is the
correct way to have built this from the start.

**The real continuity risk is different, and more specific than "we
need more policies":** several operational parameters this governance
ecosystem correctly deferred to "the school's own qualified staff"
currently exist only as tacit knowledge inside specific people's heads,
not written down anywhere yet —

- The Hifz Regulations' daily/weekly memorisation targets and per-Juz'
  pass standard exist today as whatever SHRS's current
  Muhaffiz/Muhaffizah staff actually apply — if that staff member left
  before it's written down, the standard leaves with them, not because
  no policy exists, but because the policy correctly deferred the
  *number* to expertise that hasn't been captured yet.
- The same is true of the Muraja'ah revision-cycle length, the named
  Ijazah-granting scholars' documented chain of transmission (*sanad*),
  and the specific external safeguarding-referral agency contacts a
  DSL would need on day one.
- None of these are failures of this governance work — they were
  correctly left to real subject-matter authority rather than invented
  by a drafting pass. But **an institution is only as continuity-proof
  as its least-written-down critical knowledge**, and right now that
  least-written-down knowledge is concentrated in exactly the roles
  (Qur'an College teaching staff, a not-yet-appointed DSL) where
  turnover would hurt the most.

**The honest answer to the continuity question:** SHRS's governance
*structure* would survive a full leadership change tomorrow — the
documents are role-based and would hand over cleanly. SHRS's
*safeguarding and Hifz-programme operation* would not yet survive it
cleanly, because the DSL role is unfilled and several Qur'an College
operational specifics haven't been captured from the people who
currently hold them. **That gap — not the number of documents drafted
— is the actual measure of institutional maturity this report is
reporting against, and it is where the next real effort should go:**
appoint the DSL, and have Qur'an College's teaching staff write down
their actual current practice against the Hifz Regulations' open
placeholders, before treating this governance ecosystem as complete.

## Review cycle

This report should be re-issued after the next Governance Resolution
Register quarterly review, not on a fixed annual cycle — its value is
tracking whether the continuity gap above is closing, and that's best
checked against real decisions, not a calendar date.
