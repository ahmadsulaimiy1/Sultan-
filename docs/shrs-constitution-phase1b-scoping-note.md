# PHASE 1B — THE CONSTITUTION: SCOPING NOTE

*Prepared before drafting, per the Staged Drafting Directive's own refinement: "Draft the Constitution of Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools. Cross-check every article against the Governance Charter. Ensure there are no contradictions." This is a planning document, not part of the Constitution or the Charter, and carries no legal effect.*

---

## 1. What Article 178 of the Charter actually commits this document to

The Charter's own Article 178 is the only source of authority for this document, and states plainly what a future Constitution is:

> "a broader instrument governing the Institution as a whole — its academic life, its students, its Constituent Institutions, and its relationship to the community it serves — of which this Charter's governance provisions would then form a part or a subordinate instrument."

Two things follow directly from that text, checked rather than assumed:

- **It is broader than the Charter, not a duplicate of it.** The Charter already governs the Institution's governance apparatus exhaustively (206 Articles, 22 Chapters). A Constitution that re-derives Board procedure, executive authority, or committee structure would conflict with Article 178's own description of itself as *containing* the Charter's governance provisions, not competing with them.
- **Adoption is a real constitutional act, not a drafting formality.** Article 178 requires the same procedure as a Chapter XVIII amendment (and the Article 157 entrenched procedure to the extent it would touch Article 8's substance). This document is prepared, like the Charter itself, as a Board submission draft — it has no effect until the Board adopts it.

## 2. The honest tension, stated plainly (flagged to the client before drafting began)

Article 178 says the Constitution governs "academic life, students, ... and its relationship to the community." Checking that against what already exists in this governance library, rather than assuming a gap:

| Subject Article 178 names | Already exists, in real operational depth | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Academic life | Yes — 241 lines | Academic Regulations |
| Students | Yes — 218 + 255 lines | Student Handbook, Student Code of Conduct |
| Admissions | Yes — 235 lines | Admissions Policy |
| Boarding life | Yes — 242 lines | Boarding Regulations |
| Relationship to community/parents | Yes — 254 lines | Parent Handbook |
| Islamic/Qur'anic education specifically | Yes | Hifz Regulations, Ijazah Governance Framework |
| Safeguarding | Yes | Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy, DSL Framework |

**This means the operational substance Article 178 lists is not missing — it exists, in real depth, as Policies and Handbooks.** A Constitution that tried to restate any of that at Article-level would either duplicate text that already exists (creating the same two-sources-of-truth drift risk flagged repeatedly throughout this engagement) or thin it out to something less rigorous than what a family or student already has in the Handbook they actually read. Neither is a service to the Institution.

**What genuinely does not exist yet, and is the real gap a Constitution can close honestly:** a single foundational instrument that states, at the level of principle rather than procedure, *what the Institution is, why it exists, who belongs to it, and how its governance, academic, and community life relate to one another as one system* — the "as a whole" Article 178 asks for. That is a real gap. It is a *narrower* gap than "draft a Constitution covering academic life, students, and community" might suggest at first reading, and this document is honest about that rather than padding itself to look more comprehensive than the actual gap warrants.

## 3. What this Constitution will do

A foundational compact, in the tradition of a university or school's charter-of-charters: identity and objects, founding narrative, the Institution as one system of Constituent Institutions, the constitutional status of its principal stakeholders (Students, Parents/Guardians, Staff, the Founder, the Board, the wider community) stated as *rights and standing*, not operational procedure, the Islamic character and mission restated at the level a founding compact states it (cross-referencing, not duplicating, Article 8's entrenched text), and the instrument hierarchy that places itself, the Governance Charter, and every subordinate instrument in relation to one another.

## 4. What this Constitution will not do

It will not restate the Governance Charter's governance mechanics (Board procedure, executive authority, succession, committees — all Charter Chapter III–XVII territory). It will not restate the Student Handbook's, Parent Handbook's, Academic Regulations', Admissions Policy's, or Boarding Regulations' operational detail — each is cross-referenced by name, not reproduced. It will not invent a new safeguarding, disciplinary, or admissions standard that conflicts with what those documents already say.

## 5. Relationship to the Charter and to Policies

Per Article 178 itself: on adoption, this Constitution becomes the outermost instrument, with the Governance Charter continuing in force as its governance component (Article 178's own wording: "this Charter's governance provisions would then form a part or a subordinate instrument"). Every existing Policy and Handbook continues in force unchanged; nothing in this Constitution alters their text or their own amendment procedures.

## 6. Drafting standard

Same rigour as the Charter and the Phase One Statutes: numbered Articles, precise cross-references (checked against the actual current text of the Charter and the Policies it cites, not assumed from memory), a legal/governance/editorial/publication audit before this document is considered complete, and an honest "Status" note that it is a Board submission draft with no present legal effect.
