# SHRS — ten flagship systems

Stage two. Ten identity **systems**, not ten headers. Each is one
structural grammar carried across six artefacts, because the previous
round's failure was precise: those were header treatments, and a header
treatment cannot be an identity. The test each must pass is the
fifteen-per-cent test — **show any fragment and the school is
recognisable**.

Rendered: `brand/systems.html` → the contact sheet.

---

## What changed in the thinking

Four corrections carried from the critique, each of which changed the
work rather than the vocabulary describing it:

**Hierarchy was weak because nothing had mass.** A hairline is not
hierarchy; it is punctuation. Prestige in print comes from *ink weight
in the right place* — a solid coffee field that the eye reads as
substance before it reads as decoration. Six of the ten below now carry
real mass. The four that do not (B, E, G, I) earn their restraint by
being the ones that reduce best to a phone screen and an ID card, and
they are shortlisted on that basis alone.

**Coffee was being used as ink, not as material.** Brown text on cream
paper is a colour choice. Coffee as a *field* — a solid mass the seal
sits on or breaks — is a material choice. That is the difference between
a school that prints in brown and a school whose documents are coffee
and gold objects.

**Arabic was a caption.** In every system below the Arabic sits inside
the same structural element as the English, at 1.15× the Latin point
size for equal optical presence, aligned on optical centre rather than
baseline. In B it is *load-bearing*: the gold rule is dimensioned by
whichever language runs longer, so the lock-up cannot be built without
both.

**There was no signature.** Each system now names exactly one, and it is
structural — a position, a proportion, a mass — never an ornament.

## The ten

### A · The Column
**Signature:** a coffee measure-column on the left edge, seal at its head.

The column is a *dimension*, not a decoration: one grid module wide on
every artefact, so it scales without redrawing. On a letterhead it is a
spine; on an ID card it becomes the photo field; on signage it is the
post the lettering hangs from; on the website it is the nav rail. It is
the most transferable idea here.

**Thirty-year argument:** vertical division is the oldest stable device
in book design and it has never dated, because it derives from how pages
are bound rather than from how a decade looked. **Risk:** it costs
measure on dense documents, and on landscape formats the column has to
become a header or the grammar breaks.

### B · The Lock
**Signature:** a bilingual title lock — English above, Arabic below,
joined by a gold rule the exact width of the longer line.

The only system where bilingualism is *structural*. The rule is
dimensioned by the language, so the identity cannot be assembled in one
language alone — which is a true statement about this school made into a
construction rule.

**Thirty-year argument:** it encodes a fact about the institution rather
than a style. Facts do not date. **Risk:** the least visually assertive;
in a stack of mail it will not shout.

### C · The Threshold
**Signature:** one coffee band whose *depth* encodes the document's
weight — correspondence shallow, certificate deep, ID card entire.

A single variable produces a whole family. Nothing is redesigned per
artefact; one number changes, and the reader learns to read gravity from
the ink.

**Thirty-year argument:** parametric systems age better than drawn ones,
because the rule survives even when a proportion is retuned. **Risk:**
the most familiar of the ten — a top band is what most institutions do,
so it wins on system logic and loses on originality.

### D · The Aperture
**Signature:** a gold aperture framing the seal, placed by grid, never
centred.

The aperture makes the seal *deliberate*. It also solves a real problem:
where a crest is placed by eye it drifts between documents, and drift is
what makes a set look assembled. Here the seal's position is a rule.

**Thirty-year argument:** it is a placement system rather than a shape,
so the crest itself could be redrawn in 2050 and the identity would
survive. **Risk:** reads as a picture frame if the aperture is not
substantially larger than the seal.

### E · The Spine
**Signature:** a full-height gold hairline at column one, with a single
perpendicular tick where content begins.

The tick is the whole idea: it marks the entry point of every document
so the eye always lands in the same place. Cheapest to print of the ten
— one foil pass, no coverage.

**Thirty-year argument:** it is pure grid, made visible. **Risk:** at 15%
it may read as a rule, not as SHRS.

### F · The Cartouche
**Signature:** the seal occupies a fixed three-module cartouche at
top-left; all content hangs from its baseline.

The strongest at establishing *where the institution speaks from*. It
also gives the crest a dark ground, which is where it looks best.

**Thirty-year argument:** corner-anchored identity is how passports,
charters and academic presses have worked for a century. **Risk:** with
the mass at top-left, the page can feel weighted to one corner unless
the foot answers it.

### G · The Straddle
**Signature:** two gold hairlines at fixed separation, wordmark
straddling them.

Pure typographic authority — the masthead device of serious newspapers
and university presses. Nothing to draw, everything to set.

**Thirty-year argument:** it is a *typographic* signature, and
typographic signatures outlive graphic ones. **Risk:** demands faultless
letterspacing; competent typography ruins it.

### H · Weight Below
**Signature:** ivory above, coffee mass at the foot. Hierarchy inverted.

The most original composition here. Almost every institution puts its
weight at the top; putting it at the foot says the *record* — provenance,
verification, governance — is what the document rests on. That is true
of this school specifically.

**Thirty-year argument:** inversion of a universal convention is
memorable precisely because the convention does not change. **Risk:**
on a one-line memo the foot mass can look heavier than the message.

### I · The Quadrant
**Signature:** the crest's four quadrants abstracted to a 2×2 gold mark,
used as the system's punctuation and its page division.

The only system that derives its signature *from the school's own crest*
rather than importing a device. The mark can be a bullet, a folio
ornament, a favicon, a signage plate, a debossed blind-stamp.

**Thirty-year argument:** it is heraldically derived, so it is defensible
and cannot be accused of fashion. **Risk:** an abstract 2×2 is close to
several corporate marks; it must be tested for collision.

### J · The Bisected Seal
**Signature:** the seal is cut by the horizon rule of every document —
half in coffee, half in ivory.

The most arresting at a glance and the most unmistakable at 15%. It
gives the crest genuine physical presence and makes the horizon rule
load-bearing rather than decorative.

**Thirty-year argument:** the device is a *relationship* between two
elements, not a shape, so it survives a redrawn crest and a retuned
palette. **Risk:** centred, which the grid work has argued against; and
it demands print registration accuracy that cheap printing will not hold.

## The shortlist, and the honest argument

**F + H** is the strongest combination on the brief as written. The
cartouche establishes where the institution speaks from; the inverted
foot mass makes the record carry the weight. Together they use coffee as
*material* at both ends of the sheet with a calm ivory field between —
which is the "carefully engineered luxury palette" the critique asked
for, rather than brown ink on cream.

**J** is the strongest single signature and the best answer to the
fifteen-per-cent test.

**B** should be folded into whichever wins, because bilingual parity
should be structural in the final system regardless of which composition
carries it.

**What I would build:** F's cartouche and H's foot, with B's lock as the
title element inside the cartouche, and I's quadrant as the system's
punctuation mark. That is one grammar with four consistent parts, all
derived from either the crest or the school's own facts.

## What stage three delivers, once a direction is chosen

Full artefact set at production size — letterhead, continuation sheet,
certificate, transcript, examination paper, envelope, folder, business
card, staff and student ID, email signature, website and portal header,
signage plate — plus the grid specification, the type scale, the foil
and deboss schedule, and the acceptance tests.

## What I cannot judge for you

**Originality against what you have seen.** If any of these ten reminds
you of an institution you know, name it and it is withdrawn — that
judgement is yours, not mine.
