## 2. Purpose and Status of This Handbook

This Handbook exists to answer one practical question as completely as
possible: **how does governance at Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools actually
work, end to end?** It walks through the full governance hierarchy the
Board's governance restructuring amendment of 2026-08-04 established —
tiers A through J, from the Board of Governors down to Student Clubs —
explaining, for each tier, who or what sits there, how that tier is
populated, how it relates to the tier above and below it, and its real
current status as at the date of this Handbook.

**This Handbook is explicitly an implementation document, not a
constitutional instrument.** It creates no power, office, committee, or
rule that the Governance Charter and Policy GV-01 do not already
establish. Where a genuine ambiguity exists, or a decision has not yet
been made, this Handbook says so plainly and points to the Governance
Resolution Register rather than inventing an answer — the same
no-fabrication discipline every Statute in this governance library
holds to: a vacant office stays vacant, an unnamed committee stays
unnamed, and a tier the Charter deliberately leaves unpopulated with
named individuals (Student Representatives, School Prefects, Class
Captains, Student Clubs) is described structurally, exactly as the live
public governance page (`pages/about-governance.html`) already does.

If a reader needs the Board's own detailed meeting procedure, that
belongs in the Board Handbook, not here. If a reader needs a visual
organogram, that belongs in the Organisational Structure Manual. This
Handbook cross-references both rather than duplicating them.

## 3. Scope

This Handbook covers the Institution's full governance hierarchy: the
Board of Governors and its Board-Level Committees; the Management Team
and its Management-Level Committees; Heads of Departments; Educators;
and the four tiers of student governance (Student Representatives,
School Prefects, Class Captains, Student Clubs). It applies across all
five Constituent Institutions — Sultan Hanafi Royal College, Sultan
Hanafi Qur'an College, Sultan Hanafi School of Islamic and Arabic Studies, Sultan Hanafi
Basic School, and Sultan Hanafi Online & Distance Learning School — on
the same footing the Governance Charter applies to each: one governance
system, not five separately governed schools.

This Handbook does not restate the Founder's personal, permanent
constitutional status or reserved rights in detail; those are governed
exclusively by Charter Articles 27A–27F and the Statute of the
Founder's Reserved Rights, and are described here only to the extent
needed to show how they interact with the A–J hierarchy (Section 4
below).

## 4. A Note Before the Hierarchy: The Founder's Status Sits Outside It

Before walking through tiers A–J, one structural point needs to be
stated clearly: **the Founder's personal status is not tier A, and is
not part of the A–J hierarchy at all.** Zakariya Olanrewaju Anofi holds
two constitutional offices — Chairman of the Board of Governors (tier A)
and Head of Schools / Administrator (tier C) — and, separately, the
personal and permanent status of "the Founder" under Charter Article
27A, which is not an office, is not part of any tier, and carries only
the specific reserved rights Article 27C lists (approval of changes to
the Institution's name/emblem/motto/objects; a right to be consulted,
not a veto, before establishing or closing a Constituent Institution;
first ceremonial precedence; and the revenue entitlement governed by
the Statute of the Founder's Reserved Rights). There is no office of
Chief Executive Officer: the Founder exercises constitutional authority
solely through the two offices named above (Policy GV-01 §5; Charter
Article 10). Schedule A of the Charter places the Founder's status above
the governing tier in its organogram precisely because it is a distinct,
personal category, not a governance tier this Handbook numbers alongside
A–J.

## 6. Tier A — Board of Governors

governing body (Charter Article 28). Its core constitutional
composition, restated by the 2026-08-04 amendment, is five Governors:
the Chairman; the Secretary to the Board; and three further Governors
("the Other Members"). The Board may extend to not more than eleven
Governors in total through co-opted Governors (Charter Article 29).

Governance Resolution Register, Category 8.

**How it is populated.** Founding Governors are nominated by the Head
of Schools / Administrator and confirmed by simple majority of Governors
then in office; Appointed Governors — including the three Other
Members — are appointed by Board resolution, typically on recommendation
of the Governance & Nominations Committee; co-opted Governors address a
specific expertise gap, for renewable two-year terms (Charter Articles
30, 32, 33). A Governor holds office for four years, renewable once
(Articles 34–35).

**Relationship above and below.** The Board governs; it does not manage
day-to-day operations, which is the Management Team's function (Article
28). It appoints and holds accountable the Head of Schools /
Administrator, approves Tier 1 governance documents, and reserves to
itself the matters Article 45 lists (constitutional amendment, budget
approval, external auditor appointment/removal, merger/dissolution/
restructuring, and removal of the Head of Schools / Administrator from
the Board). Everything else not reserved, or requiring its approval
under Article 46, is delegated to the Head of Schools / Administrator
under Article 47.

The Board's own meeting procedure — agenda setting, quorum, virtual
meetings, minutes, Governor induction — is governed in operational
detail by the Statute of the Board of Governors and described fully in
the Board Handbook, not duplicated here.

## 7. Tier B — Board-Level Committees

**Composition per Policy GV-01 and the 2026-08-04 amendment.** The
Board's governance restructuring amendment named the **Educational
Technical Committee** and the **Finance Committee** directly as
Board-Level Committees, and left **one further standing committee** to
be determined by the Board — not yet named as at the date of this
Handbook.

**A fuller picture from the Charter's own committee architecture.** The
Charter's Schedule of Board Standing Committees (Article 97) is broader
than the three committees GV-01's tier-B language names: it also
continues in being the **Governance & Nominations Committee** and the
**Disciplinary & Ethics Committee** (both pre-existing, not abolished by
the amendment), and separately establishes the **Safeguarding
Committee** under its own dedicated chapter (Chapter XII), chaired by
the Designated Safeguarding Lead rather than a Governor. GV-01's tier-B
language is the headline description of what the 2026-08-04 amendment
specifically changed; the Charter Schedule (Article 97) is the complete,
authoritative list of every Board-level standing body, and controls
where the two descriptions differ in scope.

| Committee | Mandate (summary) | Chair |
|---|---|---|
| Educational Technical Committee | Board-level oversight of academic and educational technical standards across all Constituent Institutions | A Governor appointed by the Board |
| Finance Committee | Budget, financial oversight, internal audit, procurement oversight, risk management, the institutional risk register | A Governor appointed by the Board, none holding a Management Team role |
| Governance & Nominations Committee | Governor recruitment/induction, the Board skills matrix, Board self-evaluation, succession-readiness monitoring, Board seat vacancies | A Governor appointed by the Board |
| Disciplinary & Ethics Committee | Governor and Management Team conduct, the ethics/conflicts register, final constitutional-or-procedural appeal | A Governor appointed by the Board |
| Safeguarding Committee | Child protection, student welfare, staff safeguarding, safety policy compliance (Chapter XII, in full) | Designated Safeguarding Lead |
| *(one further committee)* | To be fixed by Board resolution | To be fixed |

**How it is populated.** Board-Level Committee members are Governors
(save the Safeguarding Committee, chaired by the DSL with per-institution
Deputy DSLs and the Head of Student Affairs as further members). Chairs
are appointed by the Board. No further Board-Level Committee beyond
this Schedule may be created except by constitutional amendment — save
the one further committee the 2026-08-04 amendment already anticipates,
which the Board may name by ordinary resolution (Charter Article 44).

**Relationship above and below.** Board-Level Committees exercise
fiduciary and constitutional oversight on the Board's behalf — they
recommend, they do not independently spend or decide academic matters
within the Academic Council's own remit (Educational Technical
Committee) or bind the Institution to expenditure (Finance Committee).
They report to the Board, per the Schedule's own "Reports to" column,
and sit above no operational tier of their own — operational governance
runs through the Management Team and its committees (tier D) instead,
precisely so the Board's committees are never asked to manage business
that belongs to Management (Charter Article 28).

each Principal/Head Teacher for the Board's confirmation (Article 46A);
the Board appoints and holds accountable the Head of Schools /

Chair holds authority over another — membership is a peer relationship
(Article 51).

**Relationship above and below.** The Management Team is accountable to
the Board through the Head of Schools / Administrator; no individual
Governor may instruct a Management Team member directly (Charter
Article 56). It reports to the Board not less than once each ordinary
Board meeting on institutional performance, risk, and any matter
requiring Board approval (Charter Article 55). Below it sit the
Management-Level Committees (tier D) it may establish, vary, or
dissolve by its own resolution (Charter Article 58), and the Heads of
Departments (tier E) who report to it per the Schedule of Offices
(Charter Article 81).

Academic Committee, and the Sports Committee explicitly, plus "others as
Management establishes."

100–101) fixes seven Management-Level Committees as a constitutional

|---|---|---|

| Academic Committee | Day-to-day academic policy, curriculum implementation, internal examinations administration | Vice Principal (Academics) |

| Communications & Public Affairs Committee | Branding, public relations, media, publications, the website, social media | Head of Communications |
| Student Life Committee | Student welfare, non-sporting competitions, community service, school social activities | Head of Student Affairs |
| Admissions & Enrolment Committee | Admissions policy administration, enrolment processing oversight | Registrar |
| Health, Safety & Facilities Committee | Institution-wide health and safety at operational level, facilities/estates coordination | Head of Facilities & Estates |

**How it is populated.** Chairs are ex officio, tied to the office
stated in the Schedule above, not separately elected. A committee is
created, varied, or dissolved by Management Team resolution recorded in
the Authority Register (Charter Article 58; Statute of the Management
Team, Section 8) — no constitutional amendment is required, which is
precisely why this tier can grow ("others as Management establishes")
without touching the Charter itself.

**Relationship above and below.** Management-Level Committees are
operational bodies of the Management Team (Charter Article 95(b)) —
they report to Management, and, where a matter is strategic academic
policy referred upward, the Academic Committee additionally reports to
the Board (Charter Article 101, "Reports to" column). They sit above no
tier of their own within the A–J hierarchy; their work is carried out
through the office holders who chair them and the staff those offices
direct.

Permission Matrix §4.3), not a governance-hierarchy matter.

**How it is populated.** A Constituent Institution Head appoints
Educators through Human Resources (Charter Article 126); the Role &
Permission Matrix records this as an area where the Institution's Staff
Identity system is still being built out — the operational workforce
this tier assumes (Class Teacher, Subject Teacher, Muhaffiz, Arabic/
Islamic Studies Instructor) exists in job description and in the

ongoing foundational work (Role & Permission Matrix §6, Finding 9).

**Relationship above and below.** Educators report to the relevant HOD
and, through the academic structure, to their Constituent Institution's
Principal or Head Teacher (tier C). Educators do not sit above any
further governance tier in the A–J sense — their relationship to
Student Representatives, Prefects, Class Captains, and Clubs (tiers
G–J) is one of supervision and pastoral responsibility, not a
governance reporting line those tiers report "up" through in the same
sense HODs report to Management.

## 12. Tiers G–J — Student Governance

Tiers G through J share a single constitutional mechanism (Charter
Chapter X), so this Handbook treats them together rather than repeating
the same description four times. All four are **structural only in
this Handbook**: no named office holders are recorded here, consistent
with `pages/about-governance.html`'s own stated practice — "Named
office-holders in each of these bodies will be published here as the
relevant school year's appointments are confirmed."

| Tier | Body | Composition (Charter Article 91) |
|---|---|---|
| G | Student Representatives | The Student Representative Council |
| H | School Prefects | Head Prefect and Deputy Head Prefect; Senior Prefects and School Prefects, at each Constituent Institution |
| I | Class Captains | Class Captains and Assistant Class Captains per class; House Captains and Assistant House Captains |
| J | Student Clubs | Recognised clubs/societies, with Club Presidents where a Constituent Institution recognises them |

**How each is populated.** Every office in this Chapter is filled by
nomination and confirmation under Chapter XIII, supervised by the
Student Affairs Office and the Head of the relevant Constituent
Institution (Charter Article 93). A Constituent Institution Head may,
by written instrument recorded in the Authority Register, recognise a
further student office or club consistent with Chapter X; none carries
constitutional standing until so recognised (Article 91).

**Relationship above and below, for all four tiers.** No office
established under Chapter X forms part of the Institution's governance,
executive, academic, administrative, or religious authority under
Chapters II–IX (Charter Article 92). A student holding any of these
offices exercises representation and delegated school leadership, not
constitutional governance authority, and does not exercise authority
over another student except pursuant to a specific responsibility a
staff member delegates in writing. A matter any of these bodies raises
may be escalated, through the Student Affairs Office, to the Student
Life Committee (tier D) for consideration — this creates no independent
decision-making authority for any student office (Article 94).

## 13. Reporting Lines and Escalation Paths

Read narratively, the reporting chain runs: **Educators (F) → Heads of
Departments (E) → Management Team (C) → Head of Schools / Administrator,
on the Management Team's behalf → Board of Governors (A)**, with
Board-Level Committees (B) and Management-Level Committees (D) sitting
alongside — not above or below — the tier whose business they oversee,
feeding recommendations upward rather than receiving reports from a
lower operational tier of their own. Student governance (G–J) escalates
differently: through Student Affairs and the relevant Constituent
Institution Head to the Student Life Committee, never directly to the
Board or Management, and never carrying constitutional governance
authority in its own right (Charter Article 92).

Two escalation paths run outside this ordinary chain by design:

- **Safeguarding.** The Safeguarding Committee receives and acts on
  every safeguarding disclosure raised anywhere in the Institution, and
  may suspend a person from contact with students immediately and
  without prior reference to any other body (Charter Article 120). It
  reports to the Board through the Head of Schools / Administrator —
  save that where a matter concerns the Head of Schools / Administrator
  personally, the report goes directly to the Chair of the Board
  instead (Charter Articles 120(d), 124). No office, including the Head
  of Schools / Administrator, may direct the Safeguarding Committee to
  withhold action (Charter Article 121).
- **Protected disclosure.** Any person — staff, Governor, student, or
  parent — may report suspected misconduct, financial irregularity,
  safeguarding concern, or Charter breach to the Governance &
  Nominations Committee, or directly to the Chair of the Board where the
  concern involves that Committee itself, without fear of retaliation
  (Charter Article 133).

| Tier | Reports to | Escalation route |
|---|---|---|
| A. Board of Governors | No internal tier — the Institution's final internal governing authority | External: applicable Nigerian law; internally, a Governor's own dissent is recorded in Board minutes (Charter Article 40) |
| B. Board-Level Committees | Board of Governors | Recommends to the Board; no independent spending or override authority |
| C. Management Team | Board of Governors, through the Head of Schools / Administrator | Head of Schools / Administrator reports not less than once each ordinary Board meeting (Charter Article 55) |
| D. Management-Level Committees | Management Team (Academic Committee additionally to the Board on strategic academic policy referred to it) | Committee chair reports to Management Team |
| E. Heads of Departments | Management Team (directly, or through a named Management Team member per the Schedule of Offices) | Through the relevant Constituent Institution Head or Management Team member |
| F. Educators | Relevant Head of Department and Constituent Institution Head | Through the academic structure |
| G. Student Representatives | Student Affairs Office and relevant Constituent Institution Head | Via Student Affairs to the Student Life Committee (tier D) |
| H. School Prefects | Student Affairs Office and relevant Constituent Institution Head | Via Student Affairs to the Student Life Committee (tier D) |
| I. Class Captains | Student Affairs Office and relevant Constituent Institution Head | Via Student Affairs to the Student Life Committee (tier D) |
| J. Student Clubs | Student Affairs Office and relevant Constituent Institution Head | Via Student Affairs to the Student Life Committee (tier D) |

## 14. How Authority Is Delegated Top-Down

Authority under the Governance Charter attaches to office, not to the
person holding it (Charter Article 6), and flows downward through four
clearly delegated stages:

1. **Board → Head of Schools / Administrator.** The Board reserves
   certain matters exclusively to itself (Charter Article 45:
   constitutional amendment, budget approval, external auditor
   appointment/removal, merger/dissolution/restructuring, and removal of
   the Head of Schools / Administrator from the Board) and requires its
   own approval for a further list (Article 46: transactions above a
   fixed threshold, changes to the Institution's objects, Appointed
   Governor appointments, institution-wide policy adoption). Everything
   else — all executive, academic, administrative, and religious
   governance not reserved or requiring approval — is delegated to the
   Head of Schools / Administrator under Charter Article 47.
2. **Head of Schools / Administrator → Management Team.** The Head of
   Schools / Administrator chairs and directs the Management Team,
   exercising executive authority through it (Charter Article 13), and
   may overrule any Management Team position other than a matter within
   the Academic Council's or a religious office's exclusive
   constitutional remit (Article 57).
3. **Management Team → Heads of Departments.** The Management Team
   directs the offices under Chapter VIII (the Schedule of Offices) and
   the academic departments under Chapter IX, within each office's own
   stated mandate (Article 82) — no office exercises authority beyond
   that mandate.
4. **Heads of Departments → Educators.** Within a department, an HOD
   directs Educators' day-to-day work in that department, and a
   Constituent Institution Head appoints Educators through Human
   Resources (Article 126).

Every delegation of a specific power — as distinct from this
Charter-level structural delegation — must, under Charter Article 15,
name a specific delegate, state a specific and limited scope, carry a
mandatory expiry date, and be recorded in the Authority Register. Silence
is never construed as a grant of authority; a delegate holds only the
scope expressly stated. This is the discipline Policy GV-01 §7.1
describes as "delegated authority is written down, not assumed," and it
governs every stage of the chain above, not only the Board's own
delegations.

## 15. How Information and Accountability Flow Bottom-Up

The upward flow mirrors the downward one, but is built on reporting and
accountability rather than command:

1. **Educators → Heads of Departments.** Day-to-day academic and
   pastoral performance, results, and concerns move up through the
   department structure.
2. **Heads of Departments → Management Team.** HODs report through the
   Schedule of Offices' own lines; academically, department heads also
   sit on the Academic Council (Chapter VI), which reports on academic
   quality, curriculum, and examination standards to the Academic
   Committee (a Management-Level Committee) and, on strategic academic
   policy, to the Board.
3. **Management Team → Board, through the Head of Schools /
   Administrator.** Not less than once each ordinary Board meeting, on
   institutional performance, risk, and any matter requiring Board
   approval (Charter Article 55).
4. **Board-Level Committees' independent oversight.** The Finance
   Committee independently reviews internal controls and the
   institutional risk register, and may require a written Management
   Team response within thirty days (Charter Article 97 Schedule) — an
   accountability channel that does not wait for the ordinary Management
   → Head of Schools / Administrator → Board reporting cycle.
5. **Safeguarding and protected disclosure**, described in Section 13
   above, are the two channels this Charter deliberately allows to
   bypass the ordinary chain in a genuine emergency or a concern about
   someone within the chain itself.

Accountability, per Policy GV-01 §5, runs both ways: the Board holds the
Management Team accountable for institutional performance, and the
Management Team is entitled to clear, written delegation rather than
ambiguous authority it must guess at. This Handbook's description of
each tier's "relationship above and below" is written in that spirit —
naming both directions, not only the downward one.

## 17. Bodies Outside the A–J Line

Not every governance body at SHRS sits inside the ten-tier hierarchy,
and conflating them with it would misstate the Charter's own design.
The following exist alongside the A–J tiers, each with its own distinct
constitutional character (Charter Article 95):

- **The Academic Council** (Chapter VI) — membership includes every
  Constituent Institution Head, the Vice Principal (Academics), the
  Registrar, and every Head of Department — is not itself a numbered
  tier; it is where tier E's academic members convene collectively.
- **The Religious Affairs Coordination Council** and the **Shariah
  Advisory Council** (Articles 78, 78A) coordinate and advise on
  religious matters. The Shariah Advisory Council holds exclusive and
  final authority only within its narrow religious-doctrine remit
  (Article 80D) and does not report through, or receive reports
  through, any tier of the ordinary hierarchy.
- **The Institutional Advancement Council** (Article 84) coordinates
  the Alumni Office and the Sultan Hanafi Foundation, reporting to
  Management and, on fundraising policy, to the Finance Committee.
- **Temporary bodies** — Ad-hoc Committees, Working Groups, Task
  Forces, Panels, Advisory Councils, Review Boards (Articles 105–110) —
  attach to whichever tier convened them and do not appear as a
  permanent branch of the hierarchy.

A reader who cannot find a specific body in Sections 6–12 above should
check this section before assuming this Handbook has omitted it.

## 19. Reading This Handbook Alongside the Board Handbook and the Organisational Structure Manual

This Handbook is one of three documents produced from the same
2026-08-04 governance restructuring amendment, each with a distinct job:

- **This Governance Handbook** answers how the whole A–J hierarchy
  works and how authority and accountability move through it — the
  practical, prose-and-table reference for any tier, any reader.
- **The Board Handbook** (`docs/board-handbook.md`) answers how the
  Board of Governors itself runs its business — agenda setting, quorum,
  virtual and hybrid meetings, minutes, Governor induction, and
  committee procedure, at the level of detail the Statute of the Board
  of Governors operationalises. Where this Handbook's Section 6 (Tier A)
  needs that detail, it points there rather than restating it.
- **The Organisational Structure Manual** (`docs/organisational-structure-manual.md`)
  answers what the structure looks like — the visual organograms and
  reporting-line diagrams that complement, rather than duplicate, this
  Handbook's prose and tables.

None of the three attempts to be the other two; a reader should expect
to move between them, not find everything in one place. That separation
is deliberate, so each document can be maintained on its own terms
without growing unmanageably large.

## 20. Review and Amendment Procedure for This Handbook

This Handbook is an implementation document, not a constitutional
instrument, and its amendment procedure reflects that: it does not
require the Charter's constitutional-amendment procedure (Chapter
XVIII), because amending this Handbook changes no power, office, or rule
the Charter or Policy GV-01 does not already establish.

- **Ordinary review.** Annual, on the same cycle Policy GV-01
  recommends for governance documents generally, or immediately upon any
  material change to the hierarchy it describes — most obviously, when a
  currently vacant seat (Section 18) is filled, the one further
  Board-Level Committee is named, or student office holders (tiers G–J)
  are confirmed for a school year, consistent with the practice
  `pages/about-governance.html` already follows.
- **Approval authority.** Because this Handbook restates rather than
  creates authority, day-to-day maintenance (correcting a name, updating
  a vacancy once filled) is carried out by the Head of Schools /
  Administrator's office, with the Board of Governors retaining approval
  authority over the document as a whole, consistent with Policy GV-01
  §6's rule that the Management Team approves Tier 2–4 documents on the
  Board's behalf unless the Board reserves a specific document to
  itself. This Handbook is treated as Board-owned, given its
  governance-wide scope.
- **Triggered review.** A revision of the Governance Charter, Policy
  GV-01, or any of the three Statutes this Handbook relies on triggers a
  review of this Handbook, to confirm no cross-reference has gone stale.
- **What this Handbook cannot do.** No revision may create a power,
  office, committee, or rule the Charter and its Statutes do not
  themselves establish. A proposed change that would do so is not an
  amendment to this Handbook but a proposal for constitutional or
  Statute amendment through the Charter's own Chapter XVIII or Article
  163 procedure — this Handbook is then updated afterward to reflect
  what was adopted, never in advance of it.
