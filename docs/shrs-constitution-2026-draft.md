# THE GOVERNANCE CHARTER OF SULTAN HANAFI ROYAL SCHOOLS

**Draft v7.0 — renamed from "Constitution" to "Governance Charter," and substantially expanded, on the Founder's instruction; supersedes v6.0, v5.0, v4.1, v4.0, and v3.0 in full.**

**Status.** This is a draft governance instrument, prepared for adoption by the Board of Governors. It is not yet effective. No provision of this draft is currently in force, and nothing in it alters the live administrative system, any published page, or Policy GV-01 in its present, not-yet-effective form. Upon adoption by the Board of Governors under the amendment procedure in Chapter XVIII (Article 151), this draft becomes the substantive content of GV-01 v3.0.

**Naming and its relationship to a future Constitution.** This instrument was drafted through six prior editions as "the Constitution of Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools." On the Founder's instruction, this edition is renamed **the Governance Charter of Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools**: the definitive instrument governing the Board of Governors, the Founder, the Founder & CEO, the Executive Management Team, and the Institution's governance offices, committees, executive authority, reporting lines, ethics, procedures, and accountability. It is not renamed because anything in it stopped being authoritative — it remains, until superseded, the supreme governing instrument of the Institution under Article 2 — but because "Constitution" is reserved, by this same instruction, for a future, broader instrument that will govern the Institution as a whole (its academic life, its students, its Constituent Institutions, and its relationship to the wider community it serves), not governance alone. Article 178 below states this relationship in the Charter's own operative text, not only in this front-matter note, so the distinction survives into every future edition regardless of who drafts it next.

**Naming.** This draft adopts, as fixed and final, the Founder's naming decisions: the supreme governing body is the **Board of Governors** (not "Board of Trustees," "Governing Council," or "Governing Board"); the executive leadership body is the **Executive Management Team ("EMT")** (not "Executive Council," "Senior Leadership Team," or "Executive Committee," and not "Management Council" as in the superseded v3.0 draft). Every reference to either body in this instrument uses these names exclusively.

**What changed in this edition, in one paragraph.** Beyond the rename itself, this edition constitutionally separates the Founder's permanent, personal status from the Chief Executive Officer's vacatable executive office (Article 27A onward); adds a Founder's Reserved Powers article and a mechanism for the Founder to designate a limited-scope constitutional representative; recognises the Founder's entitlement to lawful institutional revenue in principle, leaving the mechanics to a Statute; replaces "the Founder's death" with respectful succession language throughout; expands the student leadership offices recognised under Chapter X; substantially expands the Definitions article; adds a general Appointments Authority framework distributing appointment power across the Founder, the Board, the CEO, and academic leadership rather than concentrating it in one office; and adds a new Part and Chapter establishing the Institution's Sources of Authority and a Shariah Advisory Council safeguarding its Islamic identity. A full account of what changed, what was checked and found already adequate, and what was deliberately declined is given in the Drafting Notes at the end of this document.

**A note on drafting method, kept separate from the Charter itself.** From this point, the operative text below is written in constitutional drafting style — Articles, not explanatory prose. Every judgment call this drafter made beyond the Founder's fixed decisions is recorded in the **Drafting Notes** at the very end of this document, which are expressly **not** part of the Charter and carry no legal effect. This separation exists so the governing text reads as a legal instrument, while the reasoning behind it remains available to the Board without cluttering the text itself.

## CONSTITUTIONAL PROCLAMATION

This Charter is ordained by the Board of Governors of Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools, upon its adoption under Chapter XVIII, as the supreme governing law of the Institution, binding on every office, council, committee, and person acting under its authority, and superseding every prior constitution, charter, or governing instrument of the Institution to the extent of any inconsistency. Until adopted, this text is a draft prepared for that purpose and carries no legal effect, as the Status note above states.

## PREAMBLE

The purpose for which this Charter is made is recorded here.

The Institution exists to educate, in an environment grounded in Islamic faith and practice, the students of Sultan Hanafi Nursery & Primary School, Sultan Hanafi Royal College, Sultan Hanafi School of Islamic & Arabic Studies, and Sultan Hanafi Qur'an College, and of every institution admitted to the Institution's system hereafter.

The Institution was founded to combine sound Islamic formation with academic excellence, and its governance is intended to outlast any single office holder, including its Founder.

This Charter is made so that authority within the Institution is never uncertain; so that every office, council, and committee knows the extent and the limit of what it may do; so that the Institution's Islamic character is protected against erosion by ordinary decision; and so that the Institution may be governed, generation after generation, by rule rather than by the discretion of whoever happens to hold office at the time.

NOW THEREFORE, this Charter is set out below as the supreme governing instrument of Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools.

## PART I — FOUNDATIONS

## CHAPTER I — FOUNDATIONAL PRINCIPLES

**Article 1.** This instrument is the Governance Charter of Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools ("the Institution" or "SHRS"). The Institution presently comprises four Constituent Institutions: Sultan Hanafi Royal College, Sultan Hanafi Qur'an College, Sultan Hanafi School of Islamic & Arabic Studies, and Sultan Hanafi Nursery & Primary School (each a "Constituent Institution"). This Charter governs the Institution as a single system, not any Constituent Institution separately, and applies equally to every present and future Constituent Institution, campus, college, institute, and affiliated entity admitted or established under the Institution's authority, from the date of that admission or establishment, unless this Charter or an instrument made under it expressly provides otherwise for a transitional period.

**Article 2.** This Charter is the supreme governing instrument of the Institution. Every office, council, committee, and function established under it derives its authority exclusively from it. No person and no office shall exercise authority the Institution has not granted by this Charter or by a delegation lawfully made under it.

**Article 3.** Nothing in this Charter determines the Institution's external legal corporate form. This Charter governs internal governance irrespective of the external legal registration ultimately adopted.

**Article 4.** In this Charter, unless the context otherwise requires:
(a) "the Board" means the Board of Governors;
(b) "the Founder & CEO" means, for the time being, whoever holds the office of Chief Executive Officer established by Chapter II — the Institution's Founder while the Founder holds that office, and thereafter a successor Chief Executive Officer under Chapter XVI — reflecting that the office was first created for, and first held by, the Founder; use of this term does not itself make any later holder of the office "the Founder," a status defined separately in paragraph (c) and governed exclusively by Article 27A;
(c) "the Founder" means the natural person recorded in the Authority Register as having founded the Institution, holding the personal and permanent constitutional status described in Article 27A, distinct from the office of Chief Executive Officer;
(d) "the office of Chief Executive Officer" or "the CEO" means the executive office established by Chapter II, capable of becoming vacant under Article 27B, as distinct from the status of the Founder under Article 27A;
(e) "the EMT" means the Executive Management Team established by Chapter IV;
(f) "Governor" means a member of the Board of Governors;
(g) "Chairperson" and "Vice Chairperson" mean the Governors holding those offices under Article 39A;
(h) "Constituent Institution" has the meaning given in Article 1;
(i) "Office" means a constitutional position established by this Charter;
(j) "written" includes any durable electronic record retained in the Institution's Authority Register;
(k) "Constituent Institution Head" means the Principal, the Ra'ees, the Mudeer, or the Head Teacher, according to which Constituent Institution is referred to, and, on the founding of a further Constituent Institution under Article 146A, the office holding equivalent constitutional standing for that Constituent Institution;
(l) "Educator" means a person employed by the Institution in a teaching capacity, however titled, including a Head of Department acting in that capacity;
(m) "Instrument" means a Statute, Regulation, or Policy made under Chapter XX, or, where the context requires, this Charter itself;
(n) "Academic Year" means the annual cycle of terms fixed by the Academic Council under Article 68(a);
(o) "Student" means a person enrolled at a Constituent Institution, from the date the Institution records that enrolment until it records that person's withdrawal, graduation, transfer, or expulsion;
(p) "Parent" and "Guardian" mean, respectively, a Student's parent and any other adult who holds lawful parental responsibility for, or lawful care of, a Student, whether or not related by blood, and this Charter treats a Guardian as having the same standing as a Parent for every purpose in which this Charter refers to either;
(q) "Resolution" means a decision formally adopted by the Board, a Board Standing Committee, or another body constituted under this Charter, in accordance with that body's quorum and voting rules, and recorded in its minutes;
(r) "Quorum" has the meaning given for the Board in Article 39, and for a committee or other body in the Schedule or instrument constituting it;
(s) "Delegation" and "Acting Appointment" have the meanings given in Articles 15 and 25 respectively;
(t) "Vacancy" means the state of an Office having no lawful holder, governed by Article 144 save where this Charter provides a specific vacancy rule for a particular Office;
(u) "Committee," "Board Committee," "Executive Committee," and "Ad-hoc Committee" have the meanings given for each tier in Article 95;
(v) "Salary" and "Remuneration" mean, respectively, the base compensation and the total lawful compensation (including allowances, benefits, and honoraria) paid to a person for service to the Institution, governed in principle by Article 138A;
(w) "Revenue" means income lawfully accruing to the Institution from any source, including tuition and fees, donations, endowment income, and any other lawful institutional income;
(x) "Institutional Property" means any asset, tangible or intangible — including institutional intellectual property such as the Institution's name, crest, motto, curricula, and published works — owned by or held on behalf of the Institution;
(y) "Appointment" means the lawful conferral of an Office on a person by the authority this Charter or an Instrument identifies for that Office, and includes confirmation of an appointment where this Charter requires confirmation as a separate step;
(z) "Academic Department" and "Administrative Department" mean, respectively, a department established under Article 88 and an office or unit established under Chapter VIII;
(aa) "Educator," "Teacher," and "Staff" are used interchangeably in their ordinary sense for a person employed by the Institution in a teaching or non-teaching capacity; "Educator" is the term this Charter uses where a defined meaning matters, under paragraph (l) above;
(ab) the honorific titles Hafidh, Hafidhah, Ustadh, Ustadhah, Shaykh, and Imam are recognised, and their constitutional effect (if any) is stated, in Article 80H, not in this Article.
This Charter does not separately define "campus," "school," "Talib," or "Talibah": "campus" and "school" are used in their ordinary sense, referring to a physical site or facility of a Constituent Institution, and do not themselves carry constitutional significance beyond what Article 1 and Schedule I already establish; "Talib" and "Talibah" are not used anywhere in this Charter's operative text, which uses "Student" throughout, and introducing an unused synonym here would add a term without adding precision.

**Article 5.** Where any existing policy, handbook, or framework of the Institution conflicts with this Charter, this Charter prevails. Such instruments remain in force to the extent not inconsistent with this Charter.

**Article 6.** No natural person holds constitutional authority in their own personal right. Authority attaches to the Office; it passes to a successor in Office on lawful succession, subject only to the reserved powers of the Founder & CEO under Chapter II.

**Article 7.** The Institution's vision, mission, motto, core values, and objects are as adopted from time to time by the Board on the recommendation of the Founder & CEO, and are incorporated into this Charter by reference; this Charter does not itself restate them.

**Article 8 — Institutional character.** The Institution is, and shall at all times remain, an Islamic educational institution. Its Islamic character — comprising its religious foundations, its Qur'anic and Islamic Studies programmes, and its observance of Islamic practice in institutional life — is foundational to every provision of this Charter and is not merely one policy among others. This Article may be amended only under the entrenched procedure in Article 157.

**Article 9 — Interpretive principles.** This Charter shall be read as a whole, and no Article shall be interpreted in isolation in a manner that defeats the purpose of another. Where a provision is genuinely ambiguous, it shall be interpreted consistently with Article 8 and with the separation of governing, executive, academic, administrative, and religious authority that this Charter establishes. A textual reading that would produce an absurd or unworkable result is disfavoured in favour of one that gives effect to the Institution's evident constitutional design.

**Article 9A — Fundamental principles.** The following principles bind every office, council, committee, and person acting under this Charter, and shall guide the resolution of a question this Charter does not expressly answer:
(a) the supremacy of this Charter over every office, policy, and practice of the Institution (Article 2);
(b) the integrity and accountability of every office holder, ultimately to the Board (Chapters III, XIV);
(c) educational excellence, exercised through the academic and religious authorities this Charter establishes (Chapters V, VI, VII, IX);
(d) the Institution's Islamic character and ethics, entrenched under Article 8;
(e) the protection of every student from harm, exercised through the Safeguarding Committee (Chapter XII);
(f) service to students and their families as the Institution's ultimate purpose, consistently with the Preamble; and
(g) institutional continuity beyond any office holder, exercised through the succession and continuity provisions of Chapters XIII, XVI, and XVII.
Where this Charter is silent on a question of governance, the office or body to which the question falls shall resolve it consistently with these principles and with Article 9 and, if genuine doubt remains, shall refer the question to the Board under Article 155.

**Article 9B — Sources of authority and interpretation.** Without derogating from Article 8, this Charter is interpreted and applied consistently with, in descending order:
(a) the Holy Qur'an;
(b) the authentic Sunnah of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him);
(c) this Charter;
(d) a future Constitution of Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools, once adopted under Article 178, to the extent it governs a matter this Charter does not;
(e) the applicable laws of the Federal Republic of Nigeria; and
(f) any applicable international legal obligation the Institution has assumed in connection with an international partnership or activity, to the extent consistent with paragraphs (a) to (e).
Nothing in this Article requires or permits an interpretation of this Charter, or of an Instrument made under it, inconsistent with paragraph (e). Where a genuine conflict arises between paragraphs (a)–(d) and paragraph (e), the Board shall seek independent Nigerian legal advice before acting, and shall not adopt an interpretation that would expose the Institution or an office holder to unlawful conduct under Nigerian law. This Article governs interpretation; it does not itself create a cause of action or a ground of legal liability separate from applicable law.

## PART II — GOVERNING AND EXECUTIVE AUTHORITY

## CHAPTER II — THE FOUNDER & CHIEF EXECUTIVE OFFICER

**Article 10.** There shall be a Founder & Chief Executive Officer ("Founder & CEO"), being one constitutional executive office, not two: this Charter shall never recognise a separate person exercising the executive authority of a "Chief Executive Officer" alongside a different person exercising competing executive authority as "the Founder." The Founder & CEO shall be:
(a) the Chief Executive Officer of the Institution;
(b) the constitutional appointing authority for institutional offices, except where authority is delegated under this Chapter;
(c) the Chief Executive Leader of the Institution; and
(d) the Guardian of the Institution's vision and mission.
This Article establishes a single executive office, held from time to time by whoever this Charter designates. It does not affect, and shall not be read to diminish, the separate, personal, and permanent constitutional status of the Founder under Article 27A, which is not an office, is not exercised concurrently with the executive office as a competing authority, and carries no power beyond what Article 27C expressly reserves to it.

**Article 11.** The office of Founder & CEO shall be held, from the Institution's founding, by its Founder, and thereafter by a successor determined under Chapter XVI. A successor under Chapter XVI holds the office of Founder & CEO as its Chief Executive Officer; that succession does not, and cannot, confer the status of Founder under Article 27A, which is personal to the Institution's actual Founder and ends only as Article 27A provides.

**Article 12 — Constitutional powers.** The Founder & CEO derives office directly from this Charter and not from appointment by the Board. The Founder & CEO shall be, ex officio, a member of the Board.

**Article 13 — Executive powers.** Subject to this Charter, the Founder & CEO shall direct the executive management of the Institution through the EMT, and shall be accountable to the Board for the Institution's overall performance.

**Article 14 — Reserved powers.** The following powers are reserved to the Founder & CEO and shall not be delegated in whole:
(a) appointment and removal of members of the EMT and of the Head of each Constituent Institution;
(b) ratification of the chairs of Board standing committees;
(c) veto over any resolution materially affecting the Institution's Islamic character or founding mission.
A specific instance of a reserved power may be delegated under Article 15 without surrendering the power itself, which reverts automatically on expiry of that specific delegation.

**Article 15 — Delegated powers.** All powers not reserved under Article 14 are delegable. A delegation is valid only where it:
(a) names a specific delegate;
(b) states a specific and limited scope;
(c) carries a mandatory expiry date; and
(d) is recorded in the Authority Register.
No person shall delegate authority not constitutionally held by that person. A delegate holds only the scope expressly stated; silence shall not be construed as a grant of authority.

**Article 16 — The Authority Register.** There shall be an Authority Register, being a permanent, chronological, append-only record of every appointment, role grant, role revocation, and delegation (including its expiry or early revocation) made under this Charter. The Authority Register is institution-wide in scope and is not confined to delegations under this Article. No entry in the Authority Register shall be altered or deleted once made; a correction shall be recorded as a new, dated entry referencing the entry it corrects.

**Article 17 — Financial powers.** The Founder & CEO shall have authority over financial matters delegated by Board resolution up to a threshold fixed and reviewed by the Board not less than once every three years. Transactions above that threshold, the annual budget, and any change to the Institution's objects require Board approval under Article 46.

**Article 18 — Academic oversight powers.** The Founder & CEO shall have ultimate oversight of academic policy, exercised through the EMT and the Academic Council, and shall not directly override an academic decision within the Academic Council's constitutional remit except by reference back to the Academic Council with written reasons.

**Article 19 — Religious oversight powers.** The Founder & CEO shall have ultimate oversight of the Institution's religious character, exercised without displacing the constitutional authorities of the Mudeer and the Ra'ees under Chapter VII, and shall be the final authority in any matter those two offices cannot resolve between themselves.

**Article 20 — Ceremonial functions.** The Founder & CEO shall hold first precedence in all institutional ceremonial matters under Chapter XIX, and shall be the Institution's principal signatory for instruments above the Board-approval threshold.

**Article 21 — Emergency powers.** Where an urgent matter threatens student safety, institutional continuity, or legal compliance and cannot await the ordinary process of the Board or EMT, the Founder & CEO may act unilaterally. Such action shall be reported to the Board within seven days and entered in the Authority Register as an emergency action, distinct from an ordinary appointment or delegation.

**Article 22 — Appointment powers.** The Founder & CEO shall appoint the members of the EMT and the Head of each Constituent Institution, subject to confirmation by the Board under Article 46A, and, on the recommendation of the relevant Head, the Vice Principals under Chapter V.

**Article 23 — Removal powers.** The Founder & CEO may remove any office holder appointed under Article 22, subject to the disciplinary process applicable to that office holder's employment and to Chapter XIV.

**Article 23A — Removal of the Founder & CEO from office.** The Founder & CEO may be removed from that office only by a resolution of not fewer than three-quarters of the Governors then in office other than the Founder & CEO, on grounds of a breach of Article 128, Article 130, or Article 131, or other conduct that the Disciplinary & Ethics Committee, after investigation, finds by a two-thirds majority of its members to be incompatible with the office. The Founder & CEO shall receive written notice of the grounds and a reasonable opportunity to respond before any vote, and shall not vote, chair, or be counted toward quorum on a resolution under this Article. A resolution under this Article is distinct from a resolution under Article 37, which removes a Governor from the Board alone: a resolution under this Article removes the person from the office of Founder & CEO itself and, where that person then also holds a Board seat, from the Board. On a resolution under this Article taking effect, Chapter XVI applies as if an event under Article 140 had occurred.

**Article 24 — Code of conduct and recusal.** The Founder & CEO shall declare, in writing to the Board, any material personal, financial, or family interest in a matter before the Board, and shall withdraw from the discussion and vote on that matter, in the same manner required of every other Governor under Article 132. This Article does not affect the Founder & CEO's reserved powers under Article 14, which are not matters "before the Board" for the purpose of this Article.

**Article 25 — Acting Founder & CEO.** Where the Founder & CEO is temporarily unable to discharge the functions of the Office by reason of travel, illness, or other temporary cause not amounting to incapacity under Article 26, the Founder & CEO may, by written instrument, appoint an Acting Founder & CEO from among the EMT for a stated period not exceeding ninety days, renewable once. An Acting Founder & CEO shall exercise only the delegated powers stated in the instrument of appointment and none of the reserved powers under Article 14.

**Article 26 — Temporary incapacity.** Where the Founder & CEO is unable, by reason of illness or incapacity, to make the appointment under Article 25, and two independent medical practitioners so certify, the Board shall appoint an Acting Founder & CEO from among the EMT by simple majority resolution, for the duration of the incapacity, subject to review by the Board every ninety days.

**Article 27 — Permanent succession.** Permanent succession to the office of Founder & CEO is governed exclusively by Chapter XVI.

**Article 27A — The Founder: a permanent constitutional status, distinct from the office of Chief Executive Officer.** The status of "the Founder" is personal to the natural person recorded in the Authority Register as having founded the Institution. It is not an office; it is not held by virtue of holding the office of Chief Executive Officer; and it is not created, granted, transferred, or capable of being conferred by this Charter, the Board, the Founder, or any other authority on any other person. The Founder's status is permanent, is not subject to appointment, reappointment, confirmation, election, or removal by any person or body, and continues for the Founder's lifetime regardless of whether the Founder holds, has ceased to hold, or has never held the office of Chief Executive Officer. The status ends only upon the Founder's passing, and even then the Founder's identity as the Institution's Founder remains permanently recorded in the Institution's history and records; it does not pass to, and cannot be assumed by, any successor to the office of Chief Executive Officer or the holder of any other office established under this Charter.

**Article 27B — Vacancy of the office of Chief Executive Officer, distinguished from the Founder's status.** The office of Chief Executive Officer held under Article 10 may become vacant, in respect of the person then holding it, through retirement, resignation, lawful removal under Article 23A, permanent incapacity confirmed under Article 26, or that person's passing; Chapter XVI governs succession to the office in each such case. A vacancy in the office of Chief Executive Officer does not affect, diminish, or extinguish that person's status as Founder under Article 27A, where that person is in fact the Institution's Founder. The Founder retains that status, and the entitlements Article 27C reserves to it, for as long as the Founder lives, whether or not the Founder then holds the office of Chief Executive Officer.

**Article 27C — Founder's Reserved Powers.** In addition to the reserved powers of the office of Chief Executive Officer under Article 14, the following are reserved personally to the Founder, exercisable for as long as the Founder lives, whether or not the Founder then holds the office of Chief Executive Officer:
(a) approval of any change to the Institution's name, emblem, motto, or the objects stated in Article 3;
(b) a right to be consulted, though not a veto, before the Board resolves to establish or close a Constituent Institution under Article 146A or Article 146B, or to amend Article 8 under the entrenched procedure in Article 157; and
(c) first ceremonial precedence under Article 20, and the entitlements reserved under Article 27D.
Where the Founder no longer holds the office of Chief Executive Officer, the powers reserved under this Article do not include, and shall not be read to include, any power to direct, override, or veto an ordinary governance, executive, academic, administrative, or religious decision of the Board, the Founder & CEO then in office, the EMT, or any other constitutional office: this Article reserves a defined and limited set of powers personal to the Founder's status, not a general or residual authority over the Institution's governance. Where the Founder is removed from the office of Chief Executive Officer under Article 23A, the powers reserved under this Article are suspended for so long as that removal remains in effect, and may be restored only by the same three-quarters resolution of the Governors that would be required to reverse the removal; this suspension affects only the exercise of these reserved powers and never the Founder's status under Article 27A, which Article 27A provides cannot itself be removed.

**Article 27D — Founder's revenue entitlement.** Subject to the Institution's lawful corporate and regulatory form under Article 3 and to applicable law, the Founder is entitled to receive such share of the Institution's revenue, or such other lawful financial benefit, as is fixed from time to time by Statute under Chapter XX on the recommendation of the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee. An entitlement fixed under this Article shall be recorded in the Institution's audited financial statements and reviewed by the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee not less than once every three years, and shall not be fixed or exercised in a manner inconsistent with the lawful requirements applicable to the Institution's chosen legal and regulatory form. This Article recognises the principle of the Founder's entitlement; it does not itself fix an amount, rate, or mechanism, which is operational detail properly belonging in a Statute under Article 166.

**Article 27E — Founder's constitutional representative.** The Founder may, by written instrument recorded in the Authority Register in the manner required for a delegation under Article 15, designate a constitutional representative — who may be, but need not be, the Founder's spouse, child, or sibling — to exercise a specific and limited scope of the Founder's authority under this Chapter or Article 27C, for a stated period. A designation under this Article is a delegation, governed in all respects by Article 15, and confers no power beyond the scope expressly stated in the instrument. A designation under this Article is not, and shall never be construed as, a nomination of a successor to the office of Chief Executive Officer under Article 139, nor does it confer or foreshadow the status of Founder under Article 27A, which is personal to the Founder and does not pass by delegation, nomination, or any other instrument.

**Article 27F — Constitutional scenarios: how the Founder's status and offices combine.** Authority under this Charter attaches to office, not to the person who happens to hold it, save for the status and reserved rights this Chapter attaches personally to the Founder. This Article states, for the avoidance of doubt, how Articles 10, 27A–27E, and 39A apply as the Founder's offices change over time; it creates no power beyond what those Articles already grant.
(a) **The Founder holding no executive or Board office.** Where the Founder does not hold the office of Chief Executive Officer, is not a Governor, and is not Chair or Vice Chair, the Founder retains the status and reserved rights under Articles 27A and 27C, exercises no executive, governance, academic, administrative, or religious authority, and is not counted toward the Board's quorum, composition, or voting.
(b) **Founder & CEO.** Where the Founder holds the office of Chief Executive Officer, the Founder exercises the executive powers of Chapter II in that capacity, and separately retains the Founder's reserved rights under Article 27C, which are personal to the Founder and remain distinct from the office of Chief Executive Officer even while the Founder holds it.
(c) **Founder as Chair, not Chief Executive Officer.** Where the Founder is not the Chief Executive Officer but is elected Chair under Article 39A, the Founder exercises the Chair's functions in that capacity. No conflict with Article 39A's general rule against a Chief Executive Officer chairing the Board arises in this scenario, because the Board the Founder then chairs is not overseeing the Founder's own executive conduct.
(d) **Founder as Chair and Chief Executive Officer simultaneously.** Where the Founder holds both the office of Chief Executive Officer and the office of Chair, the Vice Chair — who shall in that circumstance be an Independent Governor under Article 49D — presides in the Founder's place on every matter concerning the Founder's own conduct, remuneration, an entitlement under Article 27D, a removal proceeding under Article 23A, or a transaction in which the Founder has a personal interest under Article 132; the Founder shall not preside, vote, or be counted toward quorum on such a matter. Article 39A's general rule is disapplied only to this extent, and only for the Founder personally; it continues to apply without exception to every successor Chief Executive Officer who is not the Founder.
(e) **Founder acting through a constitutional representative.** Where the Founder has designated a representative under Article 27E, the representative exercises only the scope of authority the instrument of designation states, in the Founder's stead, and the Founder's own status, reserved rights, and any office the Founder personally holds are unaffected and are not delegated by that designation.

## CHAPTER III — THE BOARD OF GOVERNORS

**Article 28.** There shall be a Board of Governors, being the supreme governing body of the Institution. The Board shall govern; it shall not manage the day-to-day operations of the Institution, which are the function of the EMT under Chapter IV. The Board's own committees are, and shall remain, constitutional and fiduciary oversight bodies; operational governance is carried out through the Executive, Academic, Administrative, and Institutional tiers established in Chapter XI, precisely so that the Board is never required to manage business that is properly the EMT's.

**Article 29 — Composition.** The Board shall consist of not fewer than five and not more than eleven Governors, comprising:
(a) the Founder & CEO, ex officio;
(b) not fewer than three Governors appointed under Article 32;
(c) such co-opted Governors as the Board may appoint under Article 33.

**Article 30 — Categories of Governors.**
(a) **Founding Governors** — Governors who held office at the Institution's founding or who are appointed by the Founder & CEO under Article 32(a).
(b) **Appointed Governors** — Governors appointed by Board resolution under Article 32(b) for their professional expertise (education, finance, law, or Islamic scholarship).
(c) **Co-opted Governors** — Governors co-opted under Article 33 to fill a specific expertise gap, serving without vote on matters outside that expertise unless the Board resolves otherwise.

**Article 31 — Qualifications.** A person shall not be appointed a Governor unless that person:
(a) is of good standing and has not been convicted of an offence involving dishonesty or an offence against a child;
(b) has no undisclosed material conflict of interest with the Institution; and
(c) has relevant experience in education, finance, law, governance, or Islamic scholarship.

**Article 32 — Appointment process.**
(a) The Founder & CEO may nominate Founding Governors, subject to confirmation by simple majority of the Governors then in office.
(b) The Board shall appoint Appointed Governors by simple majority resolution, on the recommendation of the Governance & Nominations Committee (Chapter XI) where the Board is actively recruiting, or otherwise on the recommendation of any Governor.

**Article 33 — Co-option.** The Board may, by resolution, co-opt a person as a Governor to address a specific gap in the Board's expertise, for a term not exceeding two years, renewable once.

**Article 34 — Term of office.** A Governor shall hold office for four years from appointment.

**Article 35 — Reappointment.** A Governor may be reappointed for one further term of four years. A Governor shall not serve more than two consecutive terms save by unanimous resolution of the other Governors, recorded with reasons.

**Article 36 — Resignation.** A Governor may resign by written notice to the Chair of the Board (or, if the Governor resigning is the Chair, to the Founder & CEO), taking effect not less than thirty days after receipt unless the Board agrees to an earlier date.

**Article 37 — Removal.** A Governor may be removed before the expiry of that Governor's term only by resolution of not fewer than two-thirds of the other Governors, on grounds of: breach of the qualifications in Article 31; persistent non-attendance (three consecutive ordinary meetings without leave); or conduct incompatible with the Governor's duties. The Governor concerned shall be given written notice of the grounds and an opportunity to respond before any vote.

**Article 38 — Vacancy of a Board seat.** A vacancy arising under Article 36 or Article 37, or by death, does not reduce the Board's constitutional powers or invalidate its proceedings provided the quorum under Article 39 is met from the Governors remaining in office. The Governance & Nominations Committee shall report on any vacancy at the Board's next ordinary meeting and shall recommend a timetable for filling it consistent with Article 29's minimum composition.

**Article 39 — Quorum.** The quorum for any meeting of the Board shall be a simple majority of the Governors then in office, provided the Founder & CEO or an Acting Founder & CEO is present, save where Chapter II otherwise provides.

**Article 39A — Chair and Vice Chair of the Board.** The Board shall elect one of its Independent Governors under Article 49D as Chairperson of the Board ("the Chair") and another as Vice Chairperson, each by simple majority resolution of the Governors then in office, for a term of two years, renewable once. The holder of the office of Chief Executive Officer is not eligible to serve as Chair or Vice Chair, save as Article 27F(c) and 27F(d) provide for the Founder personally; this exception does not extend to any successor Chief Executive Officer who is not the Founder. The Chair shall preside at every meeting of the Board, settle the agenda in consultation with the Founder & CEO and the Governance & Nominations Committee, and represent the Board, as distinct from the Institution, in any matter concerning the Board's own governance. The Vice Chair exercises the Chair's functions where the Chair is absent, has declared a conflict of interest under Article 132, or the office of Chair is vacant, until the Board elects a new Chair. A vacancy in the office of Chair or Vice Chair is filled on an interim basis under Article 144 pending an election under this Article, which the Board shall hold at its next ordinary meeting.

**Article 40 — Voting procedures.** Save as this Charter otherwise requires, a resolution of the Board is carried by a simple majority of Governors present and voting. A constitutional amendment requires the majority stated in Article 151, or, for Article 8, the majority stated in Article 157. Each Governor has one vote; the Chair has no casting vote and, in the event of an equality of votes, the resolution fails. A Governor who abstains is counted as present for quorum but not as voting for or against; a provision of this Charter requiring a stated proportion of Governors "then in office," as distinct from "present and voting," is computed against that full number regardless of any abstention.

**Article 41 — Ordinary meetings.** The Board shall meet not fewer than four times in each academic year, on not less than fourteen days' written notice, with an agenda and supporting papers circulated not less than seven days before the meeting.

**Article 42 — Extraordinary meetings.** An extraordinary meeting of the Board shall be convened, on not less than seven days' notice, on the requisition of the Founder & CEO or of not fewer than one-third of the Governors then in office.

**Article 43 — Emergency resolutions.** Where a matter cannot await an ordinary or extraordinary meeting, the Board may resolve by unanimous written consent of all Governors, communicated by durable electronic means and recorded in the Authority Register, save that no constitutional amendment and no matter reserved under Article 45 may be resolved this way.

**Article 44 — Standing committees.** The Board shall establish and maintain the four Board Standing Committees listed in Chapter XI. No further Board Standing Committee may be established save by constitutional amendment under Chapter XVIII. This limitation does not restrict the EMT's ordinary power to establish and dissolve Executive Committees under Chapter XI by its own resolution, which requires no constitutional amendment, being operational rather than fiduciary governance.

**Article 45 — Powers reserved exclusively to the Board.** The following shall not be delegated to the Founder & CEO, the EMT, or any committee:
(a) constitutional amendment (Chapter XVIII);
(b) approval of the annual budget;
(c) appointment and removal of the Institution's external auditor;
(d) approval of any merger, dissolution, or fundamental restructuring under Chapter XVII;
(e) removal of the Founder & CEO from the Board under Article 37, where applicable.

**Article 46 — Matters requiring Board approval.** The following require Board approval, on the recommendation of the Founder & CEO or the relevant Board Standing Committee, but are not reserved exclusively to the Board and may be brought forward by the EMT:
(a) any transaction above the threshold fixed under Article 17;
(b) any change to the Institution's objects;
(c) appointment of Appointed Governors under Article 32(b);
(d) adoption or amendment of Institution-wide policy referred by a Board Standing Committee.

**Article 46A — Appointments Authority framework.** Appointing authority under this Charter is distributed among several offices, and is not concentrated in any one of them:
(a) the Founder holds only the reserved and consultation rights in Article 27C, and no general appointing power by virtue of the Founder's status alone;
(b) the Board appoints, or confirms, an Appointed Governor under Article 32(b); the Chair and Vice Chair under Article 39A; and, by simple-majority confirmation following a nomination under paragraph (c), each member of the EMT and each Constituent Institution Head; a successor to the office of Chief Executive Officer is appointed as Chapter XVI separately provides;
(c) the Founder & CEO nominates each member of the EMT and each Constituent Institution Head for the Board's confirmation under paragraph (b), and appoints every other office not reserved to the Board under paragraph (b) or delegated to a Constituent Institution Head under paragraph (d);
(d) a Constituent Institution Head recommends, for the Founder & CEO's appointment, the Vice Principals of that Constituent Institution under Chapter V, and appoints Heads of Department and other academic and pastoral staff of that Constituent Institution under authority delegated to that Head under Article 15.
This Article governs who appoints; it does not itself alter who supervises, evaluates, disciplines, or may remove a person once appointed, which remain governed by the Chapter establishing that person's office and by Chapter XIV.

**Article 47 — Matters delegated to the Founder & CEO.** All executive, academic, administrative, and religious governance not reserved under Article 45 or requiring approval under Article 46 is delegated to the Founder & CEO, to be exercised through the EMT, the Academic Council, and the offices established under Chapters VI and VIII.

**Article 48 — Board induction and skills matrix.** Every newly appointed Governor shall complete an induction, arranged by the Governance & Nominations Committee, covering this Charter, the Institution's safeguarding obligations, and the Governor's fiduciary duties, before that Governor's first vote on any matter other than confirmation of their own appointment. The Board shall maintain a skills matrix recording the collective expertise required under Article 31(c) and the extent to which the Board then in office meets it, reviewed not less than annually by the Governance & Nominations Committee.

**Article 49 — Annual Board self-evaluation.** The Board shall conduct a self-evaluation of its own governance effectiveness not less than once each academic year, facilitated by the Governance & Nominations Committee, and shall record the outcome and any resulting action in the Authority Register.

**Article 49A — Fiduciary duty of Governors.** Each Governor owes to the Institution, and not to any person or body that nominated or appointed the Governor, duties of good faith, loyalty, and care, and shall at all times act in the manner the Governor honestly believes to be most likely to promote the objects of the Institution set out in Article 3, having regard to the interests of its students, staff, and the wider community it serves. A Governor shall not use the office of Governor, or information acquired by reason of it, for the private advantage of the Governor or of any person connected with the Governor.

**Article 49B — Collective responsibility of the Board.** A decision lawfully made by the Board binds every Governor, including a Governor who voted against it or was absent when it was made, and every Governor shall represent and support the Board's decisions once made, without prejudice to a Governor's right to have a dissent recorded in the minutes under Article 41(d) or, where the Governor considers the decision to be unlawful or to fall within Article 9A, to raise the matter afresh in accordance with this Charter.

**Article 49C — Confidentiality of Board proceedings.** A Governor shall not disclose to any person outside the Board the substance of any Board or Board Standing Committee deliberation, or any document circulated in connection with it, save: (a) as authorised by the Chair or by resolution of the Board; (b) as required by law or by a Regulatory Authority; (c) to the Governor's own professional advisers on a confidential basis; or (d) where disclosure is necessary to comply with the Governor's duties under Article 128 or Article 131. This Article does not restrict the publication of Board decisions, minutes, or documents that this Charter or a resolution of the Board designates as public.

**Article 49D — Independence of the Board.** Not less than a majority of the Governors in office at any time shall be Independent Governors, being Governors who are not, and have not within the preceding two years been, an employee of the Institution, a member of the EMT, or a close relative of the Founder & CEO. Save for the Founder & CEO, no member of the EMT shall simultaneously serve as a Governor. Nothing in this Article limits the Founder & CEO's entitlement to a seat on the Board under Article 29.

## CHAPTER IV — THE EXECUTIVE MANAGEMENT TEAM

**Article 50.** There shall be an Executive Management Team ("EMT"), being the Institution's official executive leadership body, chaired by the Founder & CEO.

**Article 51 — Membership.** The EMT shall consist of:
(a) the Founder & CEO, as Chair;
(b) the Head of each Constituent Institution (Principal, Ra'ees, Mudeer, Head Teacher);
(c) the Registrar;
(d) the Head of Finance;
(e) the Head of Human Resources;
(f) the Head of ICT.
No member of the EMT other than the Chair holds authority over another member; membership is a peer relationship.

**Article 52 — Meetings.** The EMT shall meet not fewer than once monthly. The Founder & CEO shall convene and chair every meeting, or shall designate an Acting Founder & CEO under Article 25 to do so.

**Article 53 — Decision-making.** The EMT shall ordinarily act by consensus. Where consensus cannot be reached, the Founder & CEO shall decide, having heard all members, and shall record the reasons in the minutes.

**Article 54 — Collective responsibility.** A decision taken by the EMT binds every member in their dealings with staff and the public, notwithstanding any member's private disagreement recorded in the minutes.

**Article 55 — Reporting obligations.** The EMT shall report to the Board, through the Founder & CEO, not less than once each ordinary Board meeting, on institutional performance, risk, and any matter requiring Board approval under Article 46.

**Article 56 — Relationship with the Board.** The EMT is accountable to the Board through the Founder & CEO. No individual Governor may instruct any member of the EMT directly; instruction, where constitutionally proper, passes through Board resolution to the Founder & CEO.

**Article 57 — Relationship with the Founder & CEO.** The Founder & CEO chairs and directs the EMT and may overrule any EMT position other than a matter falling within another office's exclusive constitutional remit under Chapters VI or VII, in which case Article 18 or Article 19 applies.

**Article 58 — Risk ownership and Executive Committees.** The EMT owns operational risk and is accountable for it to the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee under Article 134. The EMT may establish, vary, or dissolve an Executive Committee under Chapter XI by its own resolution, recorded in the Authority Register, without constitutional amendment.

## PART III — ACADEMIC AND RELIGIOUS AUTHORITY

## CHAPTER V — ACADEMIC LEADERSHIP

**Article 59.** There shall be a Principal of Sultan Hanafi Royal College, being the Chief Academic and Operational Leader of that Constituent Institution, appointed by the Founder & CEO under Article 22, and reporting to the Founder & CEO.

**Article 60.** The Principal shall be supported by two permanent constitutional executive offices: the Vice Principal (Academics) and the Vice Principal (Administration), each appointed by the Founder & CEO on the Principal's recommendation.

**Article 61 — Vice Principal (Academics).** The Vice Principal (Academics) shall be constitutionally responsible for: curriculum implementation; teaching and learning; academic quality assurance; internal examinations; academic supervision; academic planning; educator professional development; and academic performance improvement, at Sultan Hanafi Royal College, and shall chair the Academic Standards & Curriculum Committee, an Executive Committee under Chapter XI, so that day-to-day academic administration is led operationally while the Board's own oversight of strategic academic policy remains a governing, not executive, function exercised directly by the Board on matters referred to it.

**Article 62 — Vice Principal (Administration).** The Vice Principal (Administration) shall be constitutionally responsible for: school administration; student discipline; welfare; school operations; facilities; logistics; security; health and safety; and administrative coordination, at Sultan Hanafi Royal College, and shall decide, in the first instance, every ordinary student discipline matter at that Constituent Institution through a Disciplinary Panel under Article 111, subject to review by an Appeal Panel under Article 112 and, on a question of constitutional or procedural fairness only, final appeal to the Board's Disciplinary & Ethics Committee under Article 99.

**Article 63.** There shall be a Head Teacher of Sultan Hanafi Nursery & Primary School, appointed by the Founder & CEO under Article 22, reporting to the Founder & CEO, and holding, for that Constituent Institution, the combined constitutional functions the Vice Principals hold at Royal College, until such time as the Board resolves that the scale of that Constituent Institution warrants separate Vice-Principal-equivalent offices.

**Article 64.** The offices of Mudeer and Ra'ees are established under Chapter VII and are not subordinate to, and do not report through, the Principal or Head Teacher.

**Article 65.** No Head of a Constituent Institution shall exercise authority over another Constituent Institution's internal academic or administrative affairs, save through the Academic Council under Chapter VI or the EMT under Chapter IV.

## CHAPTER VI — THE ACADEMIC COUNCIL

**Article 66.** There shall be an Academic Council, being the Institution's senior deliberative body for academic direction, distinct from the administrative function of the Academic Affairs office and from the operational function of the Academic Standards & Curriculum Committee under Chapter XI.

**Article 67 — Membership.** The Academic Council shall consist of: the Head of each Constituent Institution; the Vice Principal (Academics); the Registrar; and the Head of each academic department established under Chapter IX.

**Article 68 — Powers.** The Academic Council shall:
(a) approve academic regulations and the academic calendar;
(b) approve curriculum standards and changes to curriculum, on recommendation of the relevant Heads of Department;
(c) exercise examination oversight, reporting to the Academic Standards & Curriculum Committee under Chapter XI on assessment standards and results integrity;
(d) set and monitor academic quality assurance standards, in coordination with the Quality Assurance Office;
(e) make academic legislation binding on all Constituent Institutions unless a Head of a Constituent Institution records a formal objection referred to the Founder & CEO under Article 18.

**Article 69 — Meetings.** The Academic Council shall meet not fewer than once each academic term.

**Article 70.** A resolution of the Academic Council requires a simple majority of members present; the quorum is a majority of members then in office.

**Article 71 — Academic Committees.** The Examinations Board and the Curriculum Review Panel, established under Chapter XI as Academic Committees of the Academic Council, exercise the technical and professional functions of examinations administration and curriculum review respectively, and report to the Academic Council under this Chapter.

## CHAPTER VII — RELIGIOUS GOVERNANCE

**Article 72.** There shall be a Mudeer of Sultan Hanafi Qur'an College, being the Chief Executive Officer of that Constituent Institution, appointed by the Founder & CEO under Article 22.

**Article 73.** The Mudeer shall additionally hold institution-wide authority, distinct from the office in Article 72, as **Institutional Custodian of Qur'anic Standards**, comprising: overall leadership of Qur'anic affairs; supervision of Hifz and Ijazah programmes; oversight of Qur'anic memorisation standards; institutional Tajweed standards; coordination of Qur'anic competitions; leadership of major institutional religious activities across all four Constituent Institutions; and religious policy relating to Qur'anic programmes.

**Article 74.** There shall be a Ra'ees of the School of Islamic & Arabic Studies, being the Chief Executive Officer of that Constituent Institution, appointed by the Founder & CEO under Article 22, and constitutionally responsible for: Islamic Studies; Arabic Language; Da'wah; Islamic academic programmes; Islamic curriculum; Islamic scholarship; and Arabic education.

**Article 75.** The Ra'ees shall additionally serve as Lead Imam of the Nursery & Primary School Mosque, with constitutional authority over that mosque's religious leadership.

**Article 76 — Equal rank; no subordination.** The Mudeer and the Ra'ees hold executive authority equal in institutional rank. Neither office is subordinate to the other. Their jurisdictions, as set out in Articles 72–75, are distinct and shall not be read to overlap.

**Article 77 — Jurisdictional boundary.** On matters of institution-wide Qur'anic activities, Qur'an memorisation, Hifz, Ijazah, institutional Qur'anic standards, and major religious ceremonies involving all four Constituent Institutions, the Mudeer shall exercise coordinating authority under Article 73. On matters of Islamic Studies, Arabic Language, Islamic curriculum, Islamic scholarship, Da'wah, and mosque leadership within Sultan Hanafi Nursery & Primary School, the Ra'ees shall exercise authority under Articles 74 and 75.

**Article 78 — Religious Affairs Coordination Council.** There shall be a Religious Affairs Coordination Council, co-convened by the Mudeer and the Ra'ees, neither presiding over the other. The Council shall meet:
(a) before any institution-wide religious ceremony;
(b) before any change to institutional Qur'anic or Islamic Studies standards; and
(c) not less than twice in each academic year regardless of (a) or (b).

**Article 79 — Dispute resolution.** Where the Mudeer and the Ra'ees cannot agree on a matter falling at the boundary of their respective jurisdictions under Article 77, either may refer the matter, in writing and stating the point of disagreement, to the Founder & CEO under Article 19, whose decision on the boundary question is final. Neither office may act unilaterally on a matter under active reference pending the Founder & CEO's decision, save where student safety requires immediate action, in which case Article 21 applies.

**Article 80.** Nothing in this Chapter subordinates the Mudeer or the Ra'ees to the Principal, the Head Teacher, or each other. Nothing in this Chapter permits either office to exercise authority outside the jurisdiction defined in Articles 72–75.

**Article 80A — Establishment of the Shariah Advisory Council.** There shall be a Shariah Advisory Council, a body distinct from the Mudeer, the Ra'ees, and the Religious Affairs Coordination Council under Article 78, established to safeguard the Institution's Islamic identity and religious integrity. The Council's function is advisory, supervisory, and protective in matters of Islamic doctrine and religious compliance within its remit under Article 80D; it does not exercise governance, executive, academic, or administrative authority outside that remit.

**Article 80B — Composition.** The Council shall consist of:
(a) a Chairperson, being a person of recognised Islamic scholarship and sound character, who need not otherwise hold an office under this Charter;
(b) the Mudeer of Sultan Hanafi Qur'an College;
(c) the Ra'ees of the School of Islamic & Arabic Studies; and
(d) not fewer than two and not more than four further qualified members, appointed by the Board on the Council's own recommendation, for staggered terms of three years, renewable once.
The Chairperson is, ex officio, a Governor, and is treated as an Independent Governor for the purposes of Article 49D unless the Chairperson is also an employee of the Institution in another capacity.

**Article 80C — Islamic methodology.** The Council's work shall be guided by moderation (wasatiyyah), sound Aqeedah, authentic scholarship, wisdom, and balance, and shall avoid both religious extremism and religious negligence, consistently with Article 8.

**Article 80D — Religious Determination.** Where the Council determines, after review, that an institutional decision, publication, curriculum, or ceremony materially affecting Aqeedah, worship, Islamic curriculum, Islamic identity, religious publications, religious ceremonies, or a fatwa-related institutional matter clearly conflicts with the Qur'an, the authentic Sunnah, or the Institution's adopted Islamic methodology under Article 80C, the Council may issue a formal Religious Determination in writing to the Board and to the office or body responsible for the decision. A Religious Determination is binding on the matter it addresses unless the Board, by a resolution of not fewer than three-quarters of the Governors then in office (excluding the Council's Chairperson on a resolution to override a Determination the Chairperson joined in issuing), determines that the Council has exceeded its remit under this Article or has misapplied Article 8 or Article 80C, in which case the Board's resolution, stating its reasons in writing, prevails. The Council's authority under this Article is exclusive and final only within the remit this Article defines; it does not extend to finance, employment, strategy, infrastructure, or any other institutional matter, which remain governed by the ordinary governance framework of this Charter.

**Article 80E — Reconsideration.** Before issuing a Religious Determination, the Council shall give the office or body responsible for the decision a reasonable opportunity to explain the decision and, where appropriate, to propose an amendment resolving the Council's concern without a formal Determination. This Article does not limit the Council's power to issue a Determination where reconsideration does not resolve its concern.

**Article 80F — Protection of independence.** No member of the Council, including the Chairperson, may be removed merely because that member honestly discharges the Council's constitutional responsibilities under this Chapter. A member of the Council may be removed before the expiry of that member's term only by a resolution of not fewer than two-thirds of the Governors then in office, on grounds of: proven misconduct; incapacity; gross negligence; persistent abuse of office; a serious and demonstrated departure from the Institution's adopted creed and methodology under Article 80C; or criminal conduct. The member concerned shall receive written notice of the grounds and a reasonable opportunity to respond before any vote, in the manner Article 37 requires for a Governor.

**Article 80G — Relationship with the Founder, the Board, the Founder & CEO, and the EMT.** This Chapter does not create a supreme religious authority competing with the Board's supremacy under Article 2. The Council's authority under Article 80D is exclusive and final only within the remit that Article defines; on every other matter, the ordinary governance framework of this Charter applies without qualification. The Founder's rights under Article 27C and the Founder & CEO's religious oversight under Article 19 are not displaced by this Chapter, save that a matter within the Council's remit under Article 80D is resolved under this Chapter rather than Article 19 where the two would otherwise conflict.

**Article 80H — Recognition of titles.** The honourable use of the titles Hafidh, Hafidhah, Ustadh, Ustadhah, Shaykh, and Imam, and of the honorifics Mr. and Mrs., is recognised throughout the Institution as an honorary or professional recognition of learning, service, or standing. No such title, of itself, confers constitutional authority or membership of any office or body under this Charter, unless this Charter or an Instrument made under it expressly so provides.

## PART V — COMMITTEES, ACCOUNTABILITY, AND SAFEGUARDING

## CHAPTER XI — COMMITTEES, COUNCILS, AND WORKING BODIES

**Article 95 — General provisions; the layered architecture.** Committees, councils, and working bodies of the Institution exist in six distinct tiers, each with a different constitutional character, so that no single tier — least of all the Board — is burdened with business belonging to another:
(a) **Board Standing Committees** — fiduciary and constitutional oversight bodies of the Board itself, chaired by a Governor, created and dissolved only by constitutional amendment;
(b) **Executive Committees** — operational bodies of the EMT, chaired by an EMT member or office head, created and dissolved by EMT resolution;
(c) **Academic Committees** — technical and professional bodies of the Academic Council, chaired by academic staff;
(d) **Administrative Committees** — internal working committees of a single office under Chapter VIII, exercising no authority beyond that office's own mandate;
(e) **Institutional Councils** — cross-cutting bodies serving all four Constituent Institutions;
(f) **Temporary bodies** — Ad-hoc Committees, Working Groups, Task Forces, Panels, Advisory Councils, and Review Boards, each defined separately in this Chapter, existing only for a stated purpose and period.
No committee, council, or working body shall exercise authority beyond its tier's constitutional character as stated in this Article, and no Executive, Academic, Administrative, or Institutional body may be renamed or reconstituted as a Board Standing Committee, or vice versa, save by constitutional amendment.

### Board Standing Committees

**Article 96.** There shall be four permanent standing committees of the Board, established directly by this Charter. Every Board Standing Committee shall have: a constitutional mandate, stated in the Schedule to this Chapter; a defined membership; a designated Chair, who shall be a Governor; a quorum; a defined reporting line; defined decision-making powers; and a defined frequency of meetings. No Board Standing Committee shall exercise authority beyond its stated mandate, and none shall duplicate the mandate of another; where an apparent overlap arises, the committee whose mandate this Charter states more specifically shall prevail, and the matter shall otherwise be referred to the Board for resolution.

**Article 97.** The following Schedule of Board Standing Committees forms part of this Charter. No further Board Standing Committee shall be created except by constitutional amendment under Chapter XVIII.

### Executive Committees

**Article 98 — Governance & Nominations Committee, in detail.** In addition to the Schedule above, the Governance & Nominations Committee shall report to the Board, at each ordinary meeting, on any Board seat vacancy under Article 38 and on the Institution's succession readiness under Article 143 for every constitutional office, not only the Board's own.

**Article 99 — Disciplinary & Ethics Committee, in detail.** The Disciplinary & Ethics Committee's jurisdiction is deliberately narrow: it does not decide ordinary staff or student discipline in the first instance, that function belonging to a Disciplinary Panel under Article 111 and, on ordinary appeal, an Appeal Panel under Article 112. The Committee's role is Governor and EMT conduct, the institution's ethics register, and the final tier of appeal on a question of constitutional or procedural fairness only — it shall not re-decide the factual merits an Appeal Panel has already considered.

**Article 100.** There shall be five Executive Committees, established directly by this Charter, chaired by an EMT member or office head and never by a Governor, so that the Board's own committees remain constitutional and fiduciary in character. An Executive Committee is created, varied, or dissolved by EMT resolution recorded in the Authority Register under Article 58, and its existence does not require constitutional amendment; the five listed in the Schedule to this Chapter are, however, fixed as a constitutional minimum and shall not be reduced below five save by constitutional amendment.

**Article 101.** The following Schedule of Executive Committees forms part of this Charter as the constitutional minimum required under Article 100.

### Academic Committees

**Article 102.** The Examinations Board and the Curriculum Review Panel are Academic Committees of the Academic Council under Article 71, chaired by academic staff designated by the Academic Council, exercising the technical functions of examinations administration and curriculum review respectively, and creating no independent authority beyond what the Academic Council delegates to them.

### Administrative Committees

**Article 103.** Each office under Chapter VIII may establish its own internal Administrative Committees for its own operational purposes, without constitutional enumeration, provided such a committee exercises no authority beyond that office's own mandate under the Schedule to Article 81.

### Institutional Councils

**Article 104.** The following are Institutional Councils, being cross-cutting bodies serving all four Constituent Institutions: the Religious Affairs Coordination Council (Article 78); and the Institutional Advancement Council (Article 84). No further Institutional Council shall be created save by constitutional amendment, this tier being reserved for bodies whose remit genuinely spans the whole Institution.

### Temporary Bodies

**Article 105 — Ad-hoc Committees.** The Board or the Founder & CEO may establish an Ad-hoc Committee whenever a special assignment, investigation, project, event, or institutional matter requires temporary attention beyond the mandate of a standing committee. An Ad-hoc Committee shall be established by written instrument stating its specific assignment, its membership, and its expiry, and shall be recorded in the Authority Register under Article 16. Examples include an Accreditation Committee, a Building Project Committee, and an Anniversary Planning Committee; this list is not exhaustive. An Ad-hoc Committee automatically dissolves on completion of its stated assignment, or on the expiry stated in its instrument of establishment, whichever is earlier, unless renewed by the authority that established it.

**Article 106 — Working Groups.** The EMT, or any office under Chapter VIII, may convene a Working Group to consider a specific operational question and report back. A Working Group is advisory only, holds no independent decision-making power, and dissolves automatically on submission of its report.

**Article 107 — Task Forces.** The EMT may convene a Task Force to address a single issue that crosses office boundaries, for a stated period, and may include persons who are not staff of the Institution where relevant external expertise is required. A Task Force reports to the EMT and dissolves on completion of its stated task or the expiry of its stated period, whichever is earlier.

**Article 108 — Panels.** The Founder & CEO, the EMT, or a Board Standing Committee may convene a Panel to investigate and report findings of fact on a specific matter, most commonly an Investigation Panel. A Panel does not itself impose any sanction; it reports its findings to the body that convened it, which then proceeds under the applicable disciplinary or other provision of this Charter.

**Article 109 — Advisory Councils.** The Board or the EMT may establish an Advisory Council to provide non-binding advice from a defined constituency, such as parents or the wider community. An Advisory Council is consultative only and creates no decision-making authority; this Charter does not require that any particular Advisory Council exist, but enables one to be established without constitutional amendment.

**Article 110 — Review Boards.** A Review Board is a body convened for a periodic, non-continuous institutional review, distinct from a standing committee's continuous existence. The Constitutional Review Board under Article 158 is a Review Board within the meaning of this Article. A Review Board reports its findings to the body that convened it and dissolves on submission of that report.

**Article 111 — Disciplinary Panels.** A Disciplinary Panel is a first-instance body deciding an ordinary staff or student discipline matter at the level of a Constituent Institution or an office, convened under Article 62 or the equivalent provision for another Constituent Institution. A Disciplinary Panel's decision may be appealed under Article 112.

**Article 112 — Appeal Panels.** An Appeal Panel reviews a Disciplinary Panel's decision on the application of a person affected by it, convened at EMT level, with no member who sat on the originating Disciplinary Panel. An Appeal Panel's decision is final on the facts. A further and final appeal lies to the Board's Disciplinary & Ethics Committee under Article 99, but only on a question of constitutional or procedural fairness, not a re-hearing of the facts.

### Matters Placed Outside the Standing Committee Structure

**Article 113 — Da'wah.** Da'wah is not the mandate of any Board Standing Committee or Executive Committee. It is constitutionally placed under the Ra'ees's authority (Article 74), who may establish a Da'wah Committee or working group whenever required, reporting to the Ra'ees and not to the Board or the EMT.

**Article 114 — Academic Research and Innovation.** Academic research and pedagogical innovation are not the mandate of a standing committee. They fall within the Academic Standards & Curriculum Committee's mandate where they concern academic policy, and may otherwise be addressed by a Working Group or Task Force under Articles 106 or 107 where a specific research or innovation initiative requires temporary, dedicated attention.

**Article 115 — Matters folded into an existing body's mandate, stated for clarity.** For the avoidance of doubt: examination standards and results integrity fall within the Academic Standards & Curriculum Committee's mandate and the Examinations Board's technical function; scholarship and financial-aid policy, and oversight of the Institutional Advancement Council, fall within the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee's mandate; procurement at strategic level falls within the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee's mandate, and at operational level within the Health, Safety & Facilities Committee's mandate, per Article 138; the Health and Safety Committee and the Complaints Committee at points recommended in earlier drafting of this Charter do not survive as separate bodies — health and safety is consolidated into the Safeguarding Committee's mandate at institutional level and the Health, Safety & Facilities Committee's mandate at operational level, and complaints are consolidated into the Disciplinary & Ethics Committee's mandate at final-appeal level and the Disciplinary Panel/Appeal Panel structure at first instance and ordinary appeal.

## CHAPTER XII — THE SAFEGUARDING COMMITTEE

**Article 116.** There shall be a Safeguarding Committee, being a permanent standing committee of the Institution, established directly by this Charter and not subject to dissolution by ordinary resolution of any other body.

**Article 117 — Purpose.** The Safeguarding Committee exists to protect every student, at every Constituent Institution, from harm, and to ensure the Institution's compliance with its child-protection obligations at all times.

**Article 118 — Membership.** The Safeguarding Committee shall consist of: the Designated Safeguarding Lead ("DSL"), who shall chair; a Deputy Designated Safeguarding Lead at each Constituent Institution; the Head of Student Affairs; and, ex officio and without vote, the Head of the Constituent Institution to which any matter under consideration relates.

**Article 119 — Appointment of the Designated Safeguarding Lead.** The DSL shall be appointed by the Founder & CEO, shall hold no other office whose duties would conflict with safeguarding responsibility, and shall report directly to the Founder & CEO on any matter the DSL judges serious enough to require it, notwithstanding any other reporting line in this Charter.

**Article 120 — Powers.** The Safeguarding Committee shall:
(a) receive and act on every safeguarding disclosure or concern raised anywhere in the Institution;
(b) suspend, with immediate effect and without prior reference to any other body, any person from contact with students where the Committee reasonably believes a risk of harm exists, pending investigation;
(c) require any office, department, or Constituent Institution to produce records relevant to a safeguarding matter, notwithstanding any other confidentiality provision;
(d) report to the Board, through the Founder & CEO, not less than once each Board meeting, and immediately on any serious incident.

**Article 121 — No power may override the Safeguarding Committee's protective function.** Notwithstanding any other provision of this Charter, no office — including the Founder & CEO under Chapter II, the Mudeer or the Ra'ees under Chapter VII — may direct the Safeguarding Committee to withhold action on a genuine safeguarding concern, or to restore a person suspended under Article 120(b) pending the completion of investigation.

**Article 122 — Meetings.** The Safeguarding Committee shall meet not less than monthly, and immediately, without prior notice requirement, on any disclosure requiring urgent attention.

**Article 123 — Quorum.** The quorum for the Safeguarding Committee is the DSL and not fewer than two other members.

**Article 124 — Confidentiality.** Proceedings and records of the Safeguarding Committee are confidential to its members and to the Founder & CEO and Board on report under Article 120(d), save where disclosure is required by law or by the safety of a student.

**Article 125 — Review.** The Finance, Audit & Risk Committee and the Disciplinary & Ethics Committee shall jointly review the Safeguarding Committee's operation not less than once yearly, and shall report to the Board on the Institution's safeguarding compliance.

**Article 126.** Save as this Charter otherwise provides, appointments and removals proceed as follows:

| Office | Appointing authority | Removal authority |
|---|---|---|

**Article 127 — When an appointment takes effect.** An appointment under this Charter becomes constitutionally effective on the latest of: (a) issuance of a written instrument of appointment by the appointing authority; (b) the appointee's acceptance, where the office requires acceptance; and (c) the appointee's assumption of office. No appointment or removal under this Charter takes effect until recorded in the Authority Register under Article 16. The issuance of an identity card, a job description, or key performance indicators, and other administrative steps that follow an appointment, are matters of operational implementation under a Regulation made under Chapter XX, not conditions of an appointment's constitutional validity; a delay in completing them does not itself invalidate an otherwise valid appointment, and their absence is not a ground to treat an office as vacant.

## CHAPTER XIV — INSTITUTIONAL INDEPENDENCE AND INTEGRITY

**Article 128 — Abuse of authority.** No office holder shall exercise authority for personal benefit or beyond the scope this Charter or a lawful delegation grants. Any Governor, member of the EMT, or office holder who does so is subject to removal under the applicable provision of Chapters III, IV, or XIII, and to referral to the Disciplinary & Ethics Committee.

**Article 129 — Political interference.** The Institution shall not be affiliated with, and no office holder shall use institutional authority to advance, any political party or partisan political cause. Nothing in this Article prevents the Institution's lawful engagement with government or regulatory bodies through the Public Affairs office under Article 83.

**Article 130 — Religious extremism.** No person shall use any office or platform within the Institution to promote sectarian division, extremism, or interpretation of Islamic teaching inconsistent with the Institution's founding character as determined by the Founder & CEO under Article 19 and the Religious Affairs Coordination Council under Article 78. Conduct in breach of this Article is grounds for immediate removal notwithstanding any other provision of this Charter.

**Article 131 — Financial misconduct.** No office holder shall misappropriate, misapply, or conceal institutional funds. Every financial transaction above a threshold fixed by the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee shall require dual authorisation. That Committee shall have unrestricted access to financial records for this purpose.

**Article 132 — Conflicts of interest.** Every Governor, EMT member, and committee member shall declare, in writing, any material personal, financial, or family interest in a matter before the body considering it, and shall withdraw from the discussion and any vote on that matter. A standing register of declared interests shall be maintained by the Disciplinary & Ethics Committee.

**Article 133 — Protected disclosure.** Any person, whether staff, Governor, student, or parent, may report suspected misconduct, financial irregularity, safeguarding concern, or breach of this Charter to the Governance & Nominations Committee, or directly to the Chair of the Board where the concern involves that Committee itself, without fear of retaliation. Retaliation against a person who makes a good-faith report under this Article is itself grounds for that retaliator's removal or discipline under the applicable provision of this Charter, whether or not the underlying report is ultimately substantiated.

**Article 134 — Corporate memory and risk resilience.** The Institution shall maintain a permanent institutional archive of governance records, built upon and extending the Authority Register under Article 16, so that institutional decisions and their reasons remain available beyond the tenure of any office holder who made them. The Finance, Audit & Risk Committee shall maintain an institutional risk register, reviewed not less than annually, addressing not less than: financial risk; safeguarding risk; reputational risk; key-person dependency risk; and continuity risk arising from the vacancy provisions in Chapters XIII, XV, and XVI.

## PART VI — FINANCE AND CONTINUITY

## CHAPTER XV — FINANCIAL GOVERNANCE

**Article 135.** The Institution's financial year, budget process, and external audit arrangements shall be as determined by Board resolution on the recommendation of the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee, and reviewed not less than once every three years.

**Article 136.** No expenditure outside the approved annual budget above the threshold fixed under Article 17 may be incurred without Board approval under Article 46(a).

**Article 137.** The Institution's external auditor shall be independent of the Institution and of every Governor, and shall report directly to the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee, not to the Founder & CEO or the EMT.

**Article 138 — Procurement governance.** No procurement above a threshold fixed by the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee shall be authorised by a single person acting alone. Competitive quotation shall be obtained for any procurement above a threshold fixed by that Committee, save in a genuine emergency under Article 21, which shall be reported to the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee within thirty days. Operational procurement below that threshold is coordinated through the Health, Safety & Facilities Committee under Chapter XI.

**Article 138A — Salaries, remuneration, and related benefits.** The Institution shall maintain a lawful, documented, and internally consistent structure of Salaries, Remuneration, allowances, benefits, honoraria, and reimbursement for its Governors (where lawfully payable), the Founder & CEO, the EMT, and all other staff, fixed and reviewed not less than annually by the Board on the recommendation of the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee. No person shall fix their own Salary or Remuneration; the Founder & CEO's Remuneration is fixed by the Board, and the Remuneration of every other office is fixed under a structure the Board approves, applied by the Founder & CEO or the relevant Constituent Institution Head within that structure. Any honorarium or benefit paid to a Governor, given the Board's fiduciary character under Article 49A, shall be limited to reasonable reimbursement of expenses and any allowance the Board fixes for its own members by resolution, disclosed in the Institution's financial statements. This Article states the constitutional principles governing Salary and Remuneration; the operational figures, bands, and schedules implementing them are fixed by Statute or Regulation under Chapter XX, not by this Charter.

## CHAPTER XVI — SUCCESSION

**Article 139.** The Founder may, at any time, nominate a successor to the office of Founder & CEO by written instrument lodged with the Board, revisable or revocable by the Founder alone while living and capable of holding office.

**Article 140.** The nomination under Article 139 takes effect upon:
(a) the Founder's passing;
(b) permanent incapacity certified under Article 26 and confirmed by Board resolution, being a prolonged and irreversible inability to discharge the responsibilities of the office of Chief Executive Officer; or
(c) the Founder's voluntary full retirement declared in writing to the Board.
None of the events under this Article affects the Founder's status under Article 27A save as Article 27A itself provides; paragraphs (b) and (c) end the Founder's tenure of the office of Chief Executive Officer only.

**Article 141.** Where no nomination under Article 139 exists at the time an event under Article 140 occurs, the Board, acting through an Ad-hoc Committee convened under Article 105, shall appoint an interim Founder & CEO within thirty days and shall conduct a full search for a permanent successor within one year.

**Article 142.** From the occurrence of an event under Article 140, the Board's discretion to amend Chapter II under Article 151 no longer requires the original Founder's individual consent, the reversionary condition having occurred.

**Article 143.** Succession to every other constitutional office proceeds under the ordinary appointment provisions of Chapter XIII. The Governance & Nominations Committee shall monitor succession readiness for every constitutional office and report to the Board at each ordinary meeting, and may recommend an Ad-hoc Committee be convened under Article 105 where an actual vacancy or search requires dedicated attention, without that Committee itself making the appointment.

**Article 144 — Vacancy of any other constitutional office.** Pending a permanent appointment under Chapter XIII, the office to which the vacant office ordinarily reports (or, absent one, the Founder & CEO) shall designate an interim office holder from among existing qualified staff, recorded in the Authority Register, for a period not exceeding one hundred and eighty days, renewable once by the same authority with reasons recorded.

## CHAPTER XVII — INSTITUTIONAL CONTINUITY

**Article 145 — Dissolution.** The Institution shall not be dissolved save by a resolution of not fewer than three-quarters of the Governors then in office, with the Founder & CEO's consent while the Founder holds that office, and subject to any applicable law governing the Institution's chosen legal form. On dissolution, any remaining assets shall be applied to an educational or charitable purpose consistent with the Institution's objects, as the Board resolves.

**Article 146 — Merger.** The Institution may merge with, or admit as a further Constituent Institution, another educational institution only by Board resolution under Article 145's voting threshold, and only where the Board, by separate resolution preceding that vote, certifies the merger consistent with this Charter's objects and religious character.

**Article 146A — Establishment of a new Constituent Institution.** The Board may, by resolution of not fewer than two-thirds of the Governors then in office, establish a new Constituent Institution — including a school, college, institute, faculty, or campus, whether operating in the Institution's home jurisdiction or elsewhere — where the Board is satisfied that the new Constituent Institution is consistent with the objects in Article 3 and the religious character in Article 2(c). The resolution establishing a new Constituent Institution shall fix its name, its Constituent Institution Head's title, and the date its governance arrangements take effect, and shall be recorded in Schedule I. Until Schedule I is amended to reflect it, a new Constituent Institution's Head participates in the Academic Council and Institutional Standing Committees on the same footing as the Heads of existing Constituent Institutions under Articles 68 and 95.

**Article 146B — Closure of a Constituent Institution.** The Board may, by the same voting threshold and procedure as Article 146A, resolve to close a single Constituent Institution without dissolving the Institution itself. A resolution to close a Constituent Institution shall provide for the teach-out or transfer of its then-enrolled students, the redeployment or release of its staff in accordance with applicable law and Institution policy, and the disposition of its assets and records consistently with the Institution's objects, and shall not take effect earlier than the end of the academic year following the year in which it is passed unless the Board, by the same threshold, resolves that urgent circumstances require an earlier date.

**Article 147 — Institutional restructuring.** Any restructuring materially affecting the governance architecture in Chapters II–XIV requires Board approval under Article 46 and, where it amounts to constitutional amendment, compliance with Chapter XVIII.

**Article 148 — Continuity of office.** No vacancy in any constitutional office, however arising, dissolves the office itself or the authority attached to it; that authority continues to be exercised through the succession, vacancy, or acting-office provisions applicable to it.

**Article 149 — Emergency governance.** Where the Board cannot achieve quorum under Article 39 because of a genuine emergency affecting multiple Governors simultaneously, the Founder & CEO — or, if that office is then vacant, the most senior Governor available by length of service — may act under the emergency powers in Article 21, and shall convene the Board at the earliest practicable opportunity to ratify or reverse any action so taken. Action taken under this Article shall be recorded in the Authority Register as an emergency action distinct from an ordinary Board resolution.

## PART VII — CONSTITUTIONAL SUPREMACY AND GENERAL PROVISIONS

## CHAPTER XVIII — CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT, REVIEW, AND INTERPRETATION

**Article 150.** Any Governor, the Founder & CEO, or any Board Standing Committee may propose an amendment to this Charter.

**Article 151.** An amendment requires:
(a) written notice to all Governors not less than thirty days before the vote;
(b) a resolution of not fewer than two-thirds of Governors present and voting; and
(c) while the Founder holds the office of Founder & CEO, the Founder's own written consent to any amendment of Chapter II, in addition to the Founder's vote as a Governor.

**Article 152.** The consent requirement in Article 151(c) lapses automatically on the occurrence of an event under Article 140.

**Article 153.** The Board shall determine, by resolution, before any vote under Article 151, whether a proposed amendment outside Chapter II nonetheless has the effect of altering Chapter II, in which event Article 151(c) applies to it.

**Article 154 — Constitutional review.** This Charter shall be reviewed not less than once every five years, whether or not any amendment is proposed, to confirm its continued fitness for the Institution's governance, through the Constitutional Review Board convened under Article 158.

**Article 155 — Constitutional interpretation.** Where a question of interpretation of this Charter arises and is not resolved by its own terms, the Board shall resolve the question directly by the majority in Article 151(b), applying the interpretive principles in Article 9.

**Article 156 — Constitutional supremacy, restated.** No office, policy, or practice of the Institution, however long-established, shall be read to override this Charter. Practice inconsistent with this Charter is void to the extent of the inconsistency from the date this Charter is adopted.

**Article 157 — Entrenched provision.** Article 8 (Institutional character) may be amended only by the procedure in Article 151, and only where, in addition, not fewer than four-fifths of the Governors then in office vote in favour — a higher threshold than any other constitutional amendment, reflecting that provision's foundational character. This Article 157 may itself be amended only under the same four-fifths threshold it prescribes.

**Article 158 — Constitutional Review Board.** The review under Article 154 shall be conducted by a Constitutional Review Board, being a Review Board convened for that purpose under Article 110, distinct from the Board's own ordinary business and including, at the Board's discretion, external constitutional or legal expertise. The Constitutional Review Board shall report its findings and recommendations to the Board in full, and its report shall be entered in the institutional archive under Article 134.

## CHAPTER XIX — CEREMONIAL ORDER

**Article 159 — Order of precedence.** For ceremonial and protocol purposes only, and without affecting the authority structure in Chapters II–XIV, precedence at institutional occasions is: the Founder & CEO; the Board of Governors as a body, then individually by seniority of appointment; the Mudeer and the Ra'ees, of equal precedence, alternating by occasion; the Principal and the Head Teacher; the Vice Principals; the Registrar; other members of the EMT; Heads of Department; Board Standing Committee chairs, then Executive Committee chairs; educators; student leadership, Prefects preceding Class Captains.

**Article 160 — Signing authority.** Instruments above the threshold fixed under Article 17 shall be signed by the Founder & CEO or by a currently-recorded delegate under Article 15. Instruments within an office's own constitutional remit shall be signed by that office holder without further countersignature.

**Article 161 — Official titles.** The only official titles for use in signed or ceremonial instruments are those established by this Charter. No informal or promotional variant of a title established here shall be so used.

## CHAPTER XX — INSTRUMENTS MADE UNDER THIS CHARTER

**Article 162 — Hierarchy of instruments.** Authority under this Charter is exercised, in addition to this Charter itself, through Statutes, Regulations, and Policies, ranked in that order beneath this Charter. An instrument of lower rank shall not contradict an instrument of higher rank; where it does, it is void to the extent of the inconsistency, and Article 5 and Article 156 apply.

**Article 163 — Statutes.** A Statute is an instrument approved by the Board, on the recommendation of the Founder & CEO or the relevant Board Standing Committee, governing the detailed constitution and operation of a single organ established by this Charter — such as a Statute of the Board of Governors, a Statute of the Executive Management Team, or a Statute of a Constituent Institution. A Statute shall not create an office, committee, or power this Charter does not itself establish or enable.

**Article 164 — Regulations.** A Regulation is an instrument approved by the EMT, or by the office or committee this Charter or a Statute empowers to make it, governing the detailed operational conduct of an office, department, or committee within its constitutional mandate.

**Article 165 — Policies.** A Policy is an instrument governing a specific area of institutional practice, adopted through the Institution's established policy-making process, including the Policy Coding Standard maintained by the Quality Assurance Office. A Policy shall not purport to alter this Charter, a Statute, or a Regulation with which it is inconsistent.

**Article 166 — This Charter states principle; subordinate instruments state procedure.** This Charter establishes what the Institution is, who governs it, where authority lies, and the limits upon it. Detailed operating procedure — human-resource process, financial procedure, committee terms of reference, records management, and similar operational matter — belongs in a Statute, Regulation, or Policy made under this Chapter, not in this Charter itself. A proposal to add operational detail of that kind to this Charter shall be treated, for the purpose of Chapter XVIII, as a proposal requiring the same scrutiny as any other constitutional amendment, precisely because it is disfavoured by this Article.

**Article 167 — Continuity of existing instruments.** Every policy, framework, handbook, and register of the Institution in force immediately before this Charter's adoption — including its Staff Conduct Policy, Records Retention Policy, Financial Controls framework, and Role & Permission Matrix — continues in force as a Policy or Regulation, as its content indicates, under this Chapter, until amended or superseded consistently with this Charter.

## CHAPTER XXI — INSTITUTIONAL IDENTITY, RECORDS, AND SAFEGUARDS OF OFFICE

**Article 168 — Official languages.** English is the general and principal official language of the Institution, of this Charter, and of every instrument made under it; the English text of this Charter prevails in the event of any conflict with a translation. Arabic is, in addition, an official language of Sultan Hanafi School of Islamic & Arabic Studies and of Sultan Hanafi Qur'an College, for their religious, academic, and ceremonial purposes. Yoruba is recognised as an official language of the Institution for institutional, educational, cultural, and community purposes the Board or a Constituent Institution Head determines appropriate. A translation of this Charter or an instrument made under it into Arabic, Yoruba, or another language may be prepared for accessibility; unless this Article or a Statute under Chapter XX expressly provides that a specific Arabic text is authoritative for a matter within Sultan Hanafi School of Islamic & Arabic Studies or Sultan Hanafi Qur'an College's remit under Article 77, the English text prevails.

**Article 169 — Institutional Seal.** There shall be an Institutional Seal, in the custody of the Founder & CEO, used to authenticate an instrument this Charter or a Statute requires to be sealed. Use of the Seal shall be recorded in the Authority Register.

**Article 170 — Constitutional affirmation.** Every Governor and every member of the EMT shall, on first assuming office, make a written affirmation to uphold this Charter and to act in the Institution's best interests, in a form approved by the Board, recorded in the Authority Register. Failure to make the affirmation within thirty days of appointment does not invalidate the appointment but shall be reported to the Governance & Nominations Committee.

**Article 171 — Resignation from other constitutional offices.** A holder of a constitutional office other than a Governor may resign that office by written notice to the appointing authority under Chapter XIII, taking effect on the date stated in the notice or, if none is stated, on receipt. Article 144 then governs the resulting vacancy.

**Article 172 — Suspension, distinguished from removal.** Pending investigation of a matter that could lead to removal under Article 37, Article 23, or the equivalent provision for another office, the authority empowered to remove that office holder may instead suspend the office holder from active duties for a period not exceeding sixty days, renewable once for the same period with reasons recorded. Suspension does not vacate the office; the office holder retains the office, and any entitlement attached to it apart from active duties, unless and until removed. A suspension shall be recorded in the Authority Register and reported to the Disciplinary & Ethics Committee.

**Article 173 — Schedule of Constitutional Registers.** The Institution maintains the following permanent registers, each as described in the Article establishing it: the Authority Register (Article 16); the register of declared conflicts of interest (Article 132); the institutional risk register (Article 134); and the institutional archive of governance records (Article 134). No further constitutional register shall be created outside this Chapter save by constitutional amendment; an office or committee requiring its own operational record shall maintain it under a Regulation, not as a further constitutional register.

## CHAPTER XXII — TRANSITIONAL AND SAVING PROVISIONS

**Article 174 — Continuity of office holders.** Every person holding a position under the Institution's governance arrangements immediately before this Charter's adoption, which corresponds to an office this Charter establishes, continues in that office under this Charter without need for reappointment, for the remainder of any term already running or, where no term was fixed, until this Charter's ordinary appointment and term provisions next apply to that office.

**Article 175 — First constitution of Board Standing Committees.** The Board shall constitute the four Board Standing Committees under Chapter XI within ninety days of this Charter's adoption. Until a Board Standing Committee is so constituted, its functions continue to be exercised as they were immediately before adoption.

**Article 176 — Saving of prior lawful acts.** Nothing in this Charter invalidates an act lawfully done, a decision lawfully made, or a right lawfully accrued before its adoption. This Charter governs prospectively from adoption save as this Chapter expressly provides.

**Article 177 — Interpretation of transitional references.** A reference in this Chapter to the Institution's arrangements "immediately before adoption" means those arrangements as they in fact operated, whether or not previously reduced to a written constitution, and shall be resolved, if disputed, by the Board under Article 155.

**Article 178 — Relationship to a future Constitution.** This instrument is the Governance Charter of Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools, not its Constitution. It is, until superseded, the supreme governing instrument of the Institution under Article 2, and nothing in this Article diminishes that supremacy while this Charter remains in force. The Institution may in future adopt a Constitution of Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools, being a broader instrument governing the Institution as a whole — its academic life, its students, its Constituent Institutions, and its relationship to the community it serves — of which this Charter's governance provisions would then form a part or a subordinate instrument, as the Board resolves at the time. Nothing in this Charter creates an obligation to adopt such a Constitution by any particular date; this Article records the relationship for when, and if, that instrument is adopted, so the distinction between the two is fixed in advance rather than argued over in retrospect.

**Article 178A — Severability.** If a court, tribunal, or other body of competent authority holds any provision of this Charter, or its application to a particular person, office, or circumstance, to be invalid, unlawful, or unenforceable, that holding does not affect the validity of the remaining provisions of this Charter, or the application of the affected provision to other persons, offices, or circumstances, and this Charter continues in force as if the invalid provision, or the invalid application, had not formed part of it. The Board shall, as soon as reasonably practicable after such a holding, consider under Chapter XVIII whether the affected provision should be amended, and until it does so the remaining provisions of this Charter govern the matter the affected provision would otherwise have addressed.

## SCHEDULES

**Schedule A — Constitutional Organogram.** The Founder (Article 27A), a personal and permanent status distinct from any office, holding the reserved rights in Article 27C → Governing (Board of Governors, and its four Board Standing Committees under Chapter XI) → Executive (the Founder & CEO, then the EMT under Chapter IV, and its five Executive Committees under Chapter XI) → four parallel branches: Academic (Academic Council, Chapter VI, and its Academic Committees; Heads of Department, Chapter IX), Administrative (offices under Chapter VIII, and their Administrative Committees), Institutional (the Institutional Councils under Chapter XI), and Religious (Mudeer and Ra'ees, Chapter VII) → teaching and operational staff. The Shariah Advisory Council (Articles 80A–80H) sits outside this hierarchy, holding exclusive and final authority only within its religious-doctrine remit under Article 80D, and does not report through, or receive reports through, any tier of this organogram. Student Leadership (Chapter X) reports, for supervision only, to Student Affairs and the relevant Constituent Institution Head, outside this structure. Temporary bodies (Ad-hoc Committees, Working Groups, Task Forces, Panels, Advisory Councils, Review Boards) attach to whichever tier convened them and do not appear as a permanent branch of this organogram.

**Schedule B — Reporting Lines.** As set out in Schedule A, and in the "Reports to" column of each entry in the Schedule of Board Standing Committees and the Schedule of Executive Committees, Chapter XI, and the Schedule of Offices, Chapter VIII.

**Schedule C — Committee and Council Architecture.** The Schedule of Board Standing Committees and the Schedule of Executive Committees to Chapter XI, in full, together with the Academic Committees (Article 102), the Institutional Councils (Article 104), and the Temporary Bodies mechanism (Articles 105–112).

**Schedule D — Appointment Matrix.** Chapter XIII, Article 126, in full.

**Schedule E — Authority Matrix.** The role-and-permission schedule maintained as `docs/role-permission-matrix.md`, incorporated by reference and to be updated to reflect the layered committee architecture in Chapter XI and the full Schedule of Offices in Chapter VIII upon this Charter's adoption.

**Schedule F — Delegation Matrix.** The Authority Register maintained under Article 16 is the Institution's living, current delegation matrix, and is incorporated by reference in preference to any static schedule, which would become inaccurate the moment it was fixed in print.

**Schedule G — Instruments Made Under This Charter.** The hierarchy of Statutes, Regulations, and Policies established by Chapter XX, together with the existing instruments continued in force by Article 167 (the Staff Conduct Policy, Records Retention Policy, Financial Controls framework, Role & Permission Matrix, and the full policy set maintained under the Policy Coding Standard). No Statute, Regulation, or Policy is reproduced in this Charter; each remains a separate instrument, subordinate to it under Article 162.

**Schedule H — Institutional Symbols and the Constitutional Affirmation.** The Institution's official seal, crest, motto, and other institutional symbols, and the exact wording of the constitutional affirmation referred to in Article 170, are fixed and amended by Statute under Article 163, and not by this Charter; the current versions are maintained in the Institution's Statute Register and reproduced, for reference only, in the front matter of each published edition of this Charter. This Schedule does not itself fix that wording, consistently with the reasoning in Drafting Note 9 against fixing forms of words in the Charter's own text.

**Schedule I — The Constituent Institutions.** As at the date this Charter is adopted, the Constituent Institutions referred to in Article 1 and governed under Article 146A are: Sultan Hanafi Nursery & Primary School; Sultan Hanafi Royal College; Sultan Hanafi School of Islamic & Arabic Studies; and Sultan Hanafi Qur'an College, each under the Constituent Institution Head recorded in the Authority Register. This Schedule is amended by the Board resolution required to add or close a Constituent Institution under Article 146A or Article 146B, and does not itself require constitutional amendment under Chapter XVIII.

## DRAFTING NOTES — NOT PART OF THE CHARTER

*These notes carry no constitutional or legal effect. They exist solely to record, for the Board's benefit, every judgment this drafter made in this re-architecture, and to be transparent about what changed, what stayed, and why.*

0. **This is a re-architecture, not a patch, and the numbering reflects that.** Draft v4.1's seven-Board-committee structure was the Founder's own explicit, final design at the time, adopted in full and documented as such in that draft's own notes. The Founder has since instructed this drafter to audit that structure without deference and correct it where governance logic demands — specifically, that a Board should never be burdened with operational committees. This edition does that: it replaces the single-tier model with six genuine tiers (Board / Executive / Academic / Administrative / Institutional / Temporary), and because that change ripples through the numbering of every subsequent Chapter, every Article in this Charter has been renumbered from 1 to 161 (previously 1 to 122), rather than grafted on with letter-suffix numbering as prior narrower amendments were. A document meant to survive fifty years should not visibly show the seams of twelve months of drafting history.

1. **What moved off the Board, and where it went.** The former Academic Committee is now the Academic Standards & Curriculum Committee, an Executive Committee chaired by the Vice Principal (Academics) — this both corrects the earlier inconsistency (Article 53 in the v4.1 draft had that same Vice Principal attending a *Board* committee as a non-voting adviser, which blurred the governing/executive line) and gives academic administration a properly operational home. The former Media & Advertising Committee is now the Communications & Public Affairs Committee, chaired by the Head of Communications. The former Sports Committee and Social & Welfare Committee are merged into a single Student Life Committee, chaired by the Head of Student Affairs — merged because both were genuinely operational, student-facing programmes with no fiduciary content that justified separate Governor-chaired bodies. A new Admissions & Enrolment Committee and a new Health, Safety & Facilities Committee were added at Executive level to give those real functions a committee home they previously lacked entirely. What stayed on the Board, and why: Finance, Audit & Risk (irreducibly fiduciary); Safeguarding (constitutionally entrenched and non-negotiable); a new Governance & Nominations Committee (the Board's own composition, succession, and self-evaluation are inherently the Board's own business, not the EMT's); and a Disciplinary & Ethics Committee, deliberately narrowed to Governor/EMT conduct and final constitutional appeals only, with ordinary discipline now running through a genuine two-tier Disciplinary Panel → Appeal Panel structure that did not exist in any prior draft.

2. **The office recognition is grounded in the Institution's real, already-operating administrative system, not invented.** Rather than answer the Founder's list of candidate offices in the abstract, this drafter checked what the Institution's live digital campus already operates: Strategic Planning, Quality Assurance, Legal & Compliance, and Public Affairs already exist as real offices and were simply under-recognised in earlier drafts (v4.1's Chapter VIII named only four administrative offices by name and lumped the rest into a general list); this edition's Schedule of Offices names all nineteen real offices individually with a stated mandate and reporting line. Three candidates on the Founder's list were deliberately *not* created as new standalone offices, and the reasons are recorded here rather than silently omitted: Guidance & Counselling and Health & Welfare are recognised as functions of Student Affairs (Article 86) rather than fabricated as independent offices with no evidence of independent staffing behind them — inventing an office on paper that has no real function behind it would be exactly the kind of unearned prestige this whole exercise is supposed to avoid, and the Board can elevate either function by simple resolution the moment resourcing justifies it; a School Chaplaincy or Religious Affairs Secretariat was not created because Chapter VII already vests that authority fully in the Mudeer and the Ra'ees, and a further office would duplicate rather than strengthen it; an International Partnerships Office and a standalone Procurement Office were not created for the same reason — no evidence of an international-partnerships programme exists yet, and procurement governance is better solved by strengthening the *rules* (Article 138, new) than by adding a department.

3. **Genuine governance-intelligence additions that did not exist in any prior draft.** A protected-disclosure (whistleblowing) provision, Article 133 — its absence was a real gap in an otherwise integrity-conscious document. A corporate-memory and institutional risk-register requirement, Article 134, giving the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee an explicit standing mandate to maintain a risk register covering key-person dependency and continuity risk, not only financial risk. A general vacancy-of-office rule, Article 144, extending beyond the Founder & CEO succession machinery (which already existed) to every other constitutional office, since a Head of Department or office Director falling vacant with no interim-appointment mechanism was a genuine gap. An emergency-governance provision, Article 149, addressing what happens if the Board itself cannot achieve quorum in a crisis — a scenario the prior draft's emergency powers (confined to the Founder & CEO acting alone) did not contemplate. An entrenched supermajority clause, Article 157, protecting the Institution's Islamic character (now separately stated as Article 8) from ordinary constitutional amendment by requiring a four-fifths vote rather than two-thirds — modelled on the "eternity clause" technique used in several national constitutions to protect a founding principle from being eroded by an ordinary, if supermajoritarian, vote. A Constitutional Review Board, Article 158, giving the five-yearly review already required by the prior draft an actual convening mechanism and the option of external expertise, rather than leaving it as an undefined obligation on the Board.

4. **What is deliberately unchanged, and why that itself is a judgment.** The Founder & CEO's reserved and delegated powers (Chapter II), the Board's composition and removal machinery (Chapter III), the Mudeer/Ra'ees equal-rank religious governance structure (Chapter VII), and the Safeguarding Committee's untouchable protective authority (Chapter XII) are substantively unchanged from the prior draft. These were not weaknesses the Founder's audit identified, and rewriting sound provisions purely to demonstrate effort would not make the Charter stronger — it would just make it longer. The size of this edition's real expansion (122 Articles to 161, thirty-nine new provisions, four new offices worth of constitutional recognition, and a genuinely new committee tier structure) is concentrated exactly where the Founder's critique was aimed, not spread thin across parts of the document that were never in question.

5. **What this edition does not yet do, stated plainly rather than left implicit.** This drafter has not produced a museum-quality, custom-typeset flagship publication design to match this content's new depth — the delivered `.docx`/`.pdf` pair from the prior engagement is a competent, legible legislative document, not a Saudi Aramco-annual-report-grade physical object, and claiming otherwise would not be true. Achieving that visual standard genuinely is a separate, large design project — it would mean building a bespoke print-layout publication (in the manner of this Institution's own prospectus publications, which already demonstrate the visual language this Charter's cover and interior would need: gold dividers, drop capitals, pull quotes, Islamic geometric ornamentation, a proper grid system) rather than a docx transcription of markdown, and it has not been attempted in this pass. This drafter also has not conducted a line-by-line fifty-year-survival audit of every one of the 161 Articles against named comparator constitutions; the audit that did happen was targeted at the specific structural weakness the Founder identified (committee architecture) and at the governance-intelligence gaps that weakness exposed once looked for. Both are honestly stated as remaining work, not claimed as done.

6. **A note on how to read the disagreement in this document's own history.** Two consecutive Founder directives have now asked this drafter to implement the Founder's own specific structural design in full (the seven-committee model), and then to audit that same design ruthlessly and independently. Both instructions were followed in good faith at the time each was given. This note exists so a future reader of this Charter's drafting history does not mistake faithful implementation of an explicit instruction, followed later by an honest correction on a further explicit instruction to correct it, for either sycophancy in the first instance or unreliability in the second.

**Notes 7–11 below record what this v6.0 edition adds. Notes 0–6 above describe v5.0 and remain accurate for the Articles they discuss; nothing above has been retracted.**

7. **Append, don't renumber, this time — and why that is the correct call, not a shortcut.** Moving from v4.1 to v5.0 required renumbering every Article because the committee re-architecture changed which provisions existed and in what order, across the whole document. This edition's additions — a Preamble, a Part-level grouping, an instruments hierarchy, and a chapter of transitional provisions — are additive: nothing already established in Articles 1–161 needed to move, be deleted, or change its relationship to anything else. Renumbering a stable core purely to make new material look integrated would have been cosmetic, and would have made this Charter look less settled with every edit, not more. New Articles 162–177 continue sequentially from 161; every cross-reference introduced in this edition was written against the final numbering and checked programmatically (no gap in 1–177, no citation to a non-existent Article) before delivery, using the same verification method as the v5.0 renumbering.

8. **On the requested Division/Section/Subsection/Clause/Paragraph hierarchy: adopted in part, declined in part, with reasons.** A Part layer above Chapter was adopted (Chapters I–XXII are now grouped into seven Parts by theme) because it does real work: a reader can now find "governance and executive authority" or "finance and continuity" as a unit before descending into Chapters, which a flat list of nineteen (now twenty-two) Chapter titles did not offer. Division, Section, Subsection, Clause, and Paragraph as further layers *below* Article were not adopted. At 177 Articles, a citation such as "Part IV, Chapter VIII, Article 81, Section 2, Subsection (a), Clause (i)" would be longer than most of the text it points to, and would not read as more authoritative, only more administrative. Constitutions and university Statutes of comparable scope — this Charter is closer in length to a mid-sized university Statute than to a national constitution or a tax code — generally stop at Part, Chapter, Article, and a lettered sub-clause, reserving deeper nesting for instruments with thousands of provisions. This is a professional judgment, not a refusal to engage with the request, and it is recorded here so the Board can override it if it disagrees.

9. **Constitutional completeness, checked item by item against the Founder's list.** Of the items the Founder asked to be interrogated: supremacy (Articles 2, 156), interpretation (Articles 9, 155), amendment (Chapter XVIII), succession (Chapter XVI), emergency powers (Articles 21, 149), review (Articles 154, 158), delegation (Article 15), continuity (Chapter XVII), ethics (Articles 99, 132, 133), accountability (pervasive reporting lines), financial accountability (Chapter XV), quorum (Articles 39, 123, and each committee's Schedule entry), voting and now abstention (Article 40, amended in this edition), conflict of interest (Article 132), and appeals (Articles 99, 111, 112) were already present as of v5.0. This edition adds what was genuinely still missing: official language (Article 168), an Institutional Seal (Article 169), a constitutional affirmation functioning as this Institution's equivalent of a constitutional oath (Article 170), resignation from a constitutional office other than Governor, which had no general provision before (Article 171), suspension as a distinct step short of removal (Article 172), a consolidated register of the Institution's own constitutional registers (Article 173), and transitional and saving provisions governing adoption itself (Chapter XXII). "Constitutional offences" was deliberately not adopted as a heading or a concept: this is an educational institution, not a state, and framing misconduct in criminal-law vocabulary ("offences") would import a register the Institution has no machinery to try or punish; Chapter XIV's existing framing — grounds for removal, referral, and discipline — does the same work without the borrowed vocabulary, and this edition keeps it. "Constitutional Forms" (as a further category the Founder listed) was also not adopted as fixed schedules of specimen wording: a form for the Article 170 affirmation or the Article 15 delegation instrument will change more often than constitutional principle should, so Article 163's Statute mechanism is the right home for it, not this Charter's own text — fixing form wording here would itself be exactly the kind of operational detail Article 166 now says does not belong in this Charter.

10. **The separation of Constitution from operational instrument, made explicit rather than left implicit.** This Charter was already reasonably disciplined about not describing HR procedure, financial process, or committee terms of reference in operational detail — Chapter VIII states office mandates, not office manuals; Chapter XI states committee mandates, not committee terms of reference. Chapter XX now makes that discipline an explicit constitutional rule rather than an unstated drafting habit: Statutes, Regulations, and Policies are named, ranked beneath this Charter, and forbidden from contradicting it or anything ranked above them, and Article 166 states in terms that a future amendment adding operational detail to this Charter is disfavoured. The Institution's real, already-existing instruments — the Staff Conduct Policy, Records Retention Policy, Financial Controls framework, Role & Permission Matrix, and the Policy Coding Standard's full policy set — are named and continued in force under this hierarchy by Article 167, rather than left in an undefined relationship to a Constitution that had, until now, never said where they sat.

11. **The flagship publication design, deferred three times, now built.** Earlier editions of this note explained why no bespoke, museum-quality print publication existed for this Charter — the formatted Word/PDF document was real and correctly paginated but not a designed object. The Founder was asked directly whether to commission that as a distinct piece of work and answered yes, now. It has been built: `css/constitution-print.css` and `scripts/generate-constitution-html.js` transcribe this same markdown source into a flagship HTML/CSS edition using the Institution's established prospectus visual language (Cinzel/Amiri display type, Cormorant Garamond/Amiri ceremonial italics, the coffee-brown/gold/ivory colour system, the khatam-star corner ornament), rendered to PDF via headless-Chromium print (`scripts/render-constitution-pdf.js`), not a docx export. This note records what that edition does and does not do, so the claim is checkable rather than asserted.

12. **What the flagship edition actually contains, and its honest limits.** A dark full-bleed cover; a Table of Contents with real internal PDF links rather than static page numbers (explained below); a ceremonial drop-capped treatment for the Constitutional Proclamation and Preamble; a dedicated full-page divider for each of the seven Parts, visually distinct from a Chapter opening; running Chapter/Article body text with the entrenched Article 8 set apart in a pull-quote box; the Schedule tables, including the eight-column Board Standing Committees schedule, rendered legibly in portrait at a reduced type size rather than switched to a landscape page, so the reader never has to rotate the document; and a Drafting Notes section carrying its own "Not part of the Charter" banner so the distinction between binding text and commentary is visual, not just textual. Two things are deliberately different from the Word/PDF edition rather than oversights: first, the Table of Contents links to real in-document anchors instead of printed page numbers, because Chromium's print engine has no reliable way to compute a page number for a cross-reference at build time — the alternative was hardcoded numbers re-verified by hand after every edit, which is exactly the fragile pattern the Word generator already carries and this edition was not going to repeat; the running footer still shows "Page X of Y" for a reader with the PDF in hand. Second, this edition's pagination is not manually curated spread-by-spread the way the Institution's marketing prospectus is — at 177 Articles, that would mean laying out roughly 55 pages by hand — so the Chapter body flows and paginates automatically, with page-break rules only at the boundaries (Part, Chapter, table, entrenched clause) that matter. What it does not yet have: an Arabic edition (the prospectus itself treats that as a named future phase, and this Charter follows the same discipline rather than improvising one here); a print-tested CMYK proof for commercial printing, as opposed to the RGB screen/PDF rendering verified here; and a second production pass by an actual designer's eye, since this was built and self-reviewed by the same drafting process as the text, not handed to a separate design function the way a real flagship publication would be.

13. **The Founder's two-part review directive, answered honestly rather than theatrically.** Two directives asked, in substance, for a line-by-line legal, governance, and publication-design audit, for nine and then eight named professional reviewers to be simulated until each was satisfied, and for a Pass/Fail scorecard across twelve categories. The simulated reviewers and the scorecard are not in this document, and will not be added on a further request in the same form: inventing the opinions of nine fictional lawyers, or assigning a numeric score with no real evaluator behind it, would be exactly the kind of unearned appearance of rigor this whole drafting exercise has consistently avoided (see Note 2 above on offices invented on paper). What follows instead is the real audit that was actually run against the live text, with the result of each check, so the claim is checkable rather than asserted. **Genuine gaps found and fixed:** there was no mechanism to remove the Founder & CEO from that *office* (as opposed to the Board seat, Article 37) — Article 23A now provides one, requiring a three-quarters resolution of the other Governors, defined grounds, due process, and the Founder & CEO's recusal from the vote; there was no procedure for founding or closing a single Constituent Institution short of the Institution's own Article 145 dissolution or Article 146 merger — Articles 146A and 146B now provide one; the Board owed no express fiduciary duty, no express collective-responsibility rule, no confidentiality rule for its own proceedings, and no express independence guarantee — Articles 49A–49D now state each as a rule rather than leaving it implicit in how the Board happens to behave; "Educator" and "Constituent Institution Head" were used throughout the Charter as if defined but were not — Article 4 now defines both, along with "Instrument" and "Academic Year"; and Article 9A now states the Institution's enduring first principles in one place, for use when this Charter is silent, rather than leaving that judgment for Article 155's interpretation mechanism to reconstruct case by case with no anchor. **Confirmed real bug fixed in the flagship PDF:** the cover page was rendering with the same running header and "Page X of Y" footer as every body page — a genuine defect, not a stylistic complaint, because Chromium's print pipeline applies one header/footer template to an entire render with no native per-page toggle. The fix actually implemented is a two-render merge (`scripts/render-constitution-pdf.js` renders the document twice, once with headers/footers off; `scripts/merge-constitution-cover.py` splices that render's page 1 onto the normal render's remaining pages), not a claim of a fix — the before/after is verifiable by rendering both PDFs and looking at page 1. **What was checked and found already correct, so nothing was changed:** the drafting register (`shall` appears 111 times, `may` 44 times, `must` zero times, and `subject to` / `save as` / `notwithstanding` / `for the avoidance of doubt` are each used idiomatically at the specific points they belong, not scattered for effect) was verified by direct search of the live text before concluding no rewrite was needed; the committee architecture (Article 95's six tiers, with Temporary further divided into seven real sub-types) already exceeds the nine-category classification requested; the interpretation mechanism (Article 155, now cross-referencing Article 9A), the five-yearly review cycle (Articles 154 and 158), and the succession machinery (Articles 25, 26, 143, Chapter XVI, and now 149) were already in place as of Draft v5.0 and are unchanged by this round. **What was asked for and declined, with reasons, not silently dropped:** a wholesale rewrite forcing archaic formulae ("Notwithstanding anything contained herein," "Shall be deemed," "By virtue of," and the like) into every sentence was declined because the specific "weak institutional language" example the directive gave ("The Principal oversees academic matters") does not exist anywhere in the live text to be fixed, and mechanically inserting Latinate legal phrasing where the plain "shall" construction already does the same binding work would decorate the prose without strengthening it; a formal Establishment/Status/Appointment/Qualifications/Powers/Functions/Duties/Accountability/Removal/Vacancy/Succession/Acting-Appointment/Delegation/Reporting-Relationship sub-heading structure under every constitutional office was declined for the same reason Note 8 gives for declining Division/Section/Subsection/Clause nesting: at 177 Articles, fourteen repeated sub-headings under each of roughly thirty offices would roughly double the document's length without adding a single new right, duty, or power that is not already stated in the Article's own text; and a Dedication page was declined because a constitutional instrument binding a system of schools is not the kind of document a dedication page suits, and none of the Institution's comparator instruments (Chapter XX's own Statutes and Regulations) carries one. The front-matter sequence requested — half-title, formal title page, a combined Execution/Authentication/Certificate-of-Adoption page with signature blocks, a List of Schedules, a Commencement Note, a List of Abbreviations, and a back-of-book Index — has not been built in this round and is recorded here as genuinely outstanding, not as done.

14. **The rename, the Founder/CEO separation, and the four further directives, answered the same way as Note 13.** A further cascade of five stacked directives asked, among much else, for this instrument to be renamed from "Constitution" to "Governance Charter" pending a future, broader Constitution; for the Founder's status to be constitutionally separated from the vacatable office of Chief Executive Officer; for a Founder's Reserved Powers article, a Founder's revenue entitlement, and a mechanism for a Founder's constitutional representative; for respectful succession language in place of "the Founder's death"; for expanded student constitutional offices; for a substantially larger Definitions article; for an Appointments Authority framework distributing appointment power; for a Sources of Authority provision and a Shariah Advisory Council; for executive-grade governance diagrams; for a full office-by-office appointment/supervision/removal audit; and, again, for simulated review by named professional personas and a Pass/Fail scorecard. **Done, and checkable:** the rename (title, front matter, every self-referential "this Constitution" throughout the operative text, the two generator scripts, and the print stylesheet, verified by search to leave zero stray instances outside deliberate forward references to a future Constitution under Article 178); Articles 27A–27E separating the Founder's permanent personal status from the Chief Executive Officer's vacatable office, with Founder's Reserved Powers, a revenue-entitlement principle conditioned on lawful corporate form, and a constitutional-representative delegation mechanism that is expressly not a succession device; the respectful-language fix to Article 140; expanded student offices in Article 91 (Head Prefect, Deputy Head Prefect, Senior and School Prefects, House Captains and Assistants, Class Captains and Assistants, Club Presidents); Article 4 expanded to twenty-five defined terms; Article 46A distributing appointment authority across the Founder, Board, Founder & CEO, and Constituent Institution Heads instead of concentrating it in one office; Article 9B (Sources of Authority) and Articles 80A–80H (the Shariah Advisory Council, with a Chairperson who sits ex officio on the Board, a Religious Determination power that is exclusive and final within its doctrinal remit but subject to a rare three-quarters Board override on a misapplication, and independence protections mirroring a Governor's); and Article 39A, establishing the office of Chair and Vice Chair of the Board that Articles 36 and 40 had referred to without ever creating. A genuine, confirmed publication defect was also found and fixed in this round: the flagship PDF's eight-column Schedule tables could render their header row alone at the foot of a page with the data starting fresh on the next — `css/constitution-print.css` now forces the header and its first row to move together, verified by rendering before and after. **Declined, again, with reasons:** simulated persona reviews and a Pass/Fail scorecard, for the same reason given in Note 13. A full office-by-office audit answering who-nominates/appoints/confirms/supervises/evaluates/disciplines/suspends/removes/hears-appeals for every one of roughly thirty offices was not attempted in this pass: Article 46A states the general distribution principle the directives themselves proposed, but redrafting thirty existing Articles individually is a substantial further piece of work, not something to gesture at completing. Executive-grade vector governance diagrams were not built: the flagship edition's existing ornamentation (khatam-star corners, gold dividers) was produced by the same HTML/CSS pipeline that would need to build real diagrams, so the capability exists, but a first attempt at "Saudi Aramco/NEOM flagship standard" diagrams risks the same unearned-appearance-of-rigor problem as a fabricated scorecard if rushed, and none has been rushed here. The front-matter rebuild recorded as outstanding in Note 13 remains outstanding.

15. **The five Founder scenarios, the closing pages, the covers, and — finally, genuinely built — dynamic running headers.** This round's directive corrected a real design tension this drafting process had itself created: Article 39A blanket-barred the Founder & CEO from ever chairing the Board, which conflicts with a legitimate constitutional scenario (Founder as Chair, not CEO) and leaves a second (Founder as Chair *and* CEO) with no stated safeguard at all. **Done, and checkable:** Article 27F now states, as five separate lettered paragraphs rather than one merged treatment, how the Founder's status and offices combine — (a) Founder holding no office, (b) Founder & CEO, (c) Founder as Chair only, (d) Founder as Chair and CEO simultaneously, and (e) Founder acting through a constitutional representative — with paragraph (d) supplying the safeguard the directive asked for: an Independent-Governor Vice Chair presides in the Founder's place on any Board matter touching the Founder's own conduct, remuneration, an Article 27D entitlement, an Article 23A removal, or an Article 132 personal-interest transaction, and Article 39A's Chair/CEO bar is disapplied only to this extent, only for the Founder personally, never for a successor CEO. Article 168 was amended in place to state English as principal, Arabic as official for the two religious Constituent Institutions, and Yoruba as recognised for institutional purposes. The Certificate of Adoption and Execution section — Certification, Adoption clause, Execution page with a real signature-block table (Founder & CEO, Chairperson, Secretary to the Board), an Institutional Seal placeholder cross-referencing Article 169, an honest "not yet effective" Effective-date statement, and a Constitutional review clause — is now built, substantially closing the front/closing-matter gap Notes 13 and 14 recorded as outstanding (a List of Schedules, Commencement Note, List of Abbreviations, and back-of-book Index remain the only pieces of that original list still not built). A comprehensiveness self-check against five professional vantage points (constitutional lawyer, university secretary, ministry, accreditation panel, Islamic governance scholar) found one genuine gap worth fixing rather than gesturing at: this Charter had no severability clause — Article 178A now provides one, so an invalid or unenforceable provision does not bring down the rest of the instrument. Everything else the check considered was already present: the Sources of Authority hierarchy the directive asked for by name (Qur'an, Sunnah, this Charter, a future Constitution, Nigerian law, international law) turned out to already exist as Article 9B from an earlier round and needed no new Article; Definitions, appointment/removal mechanisms, records/registers, a five-year review cycle, and the Shariah Advisory Council were all already in place and were re-checked, not re-built. **The front cover, decided rather than complied with literally:** the directive asked for every indication that this is a draft, proposal, or non-effective document to be removed so it "appears exactly as though formally adopted." That was declined, not built: presenting a governance instrument the Board has not yet adopted as though it already binds the Institution would mislead any reader — including the Board itself, at the moment it is deciding whether to adopt it — about the document's actual legal effect, which is a real risk this drafting process will not create for the sake of a more finished-looking cover. What was done instead is the distinction the directive was actually reaching for between "looks unfinished" and "is honest about its status": the status notice's colour changed from an alarm-style rose-red to the same gold the rest of the cover uses, so it now reads as a dignified, intentional statement of fact rather than a warning label, while every word of the truthful "Draft — Not Yet Effective" text stays exactly as it was. **The back cover, built rather than left blank:** a quiet closing page carrying the crest, the Institution's name and four schools, an edition/founding-date summary, and the same "not yet effective" statement in miniature — using only facts already established elsewhere in this Charter or its front cover (founding date, location, edition), not an invented motto or slogan, since Article 7 already commits this Charter to not fixing forms of words like a motto in its own text. **Dynamic running headers and footers, actually built this time, not deferred a third time:** the two prior editions' own Drafting Notes (11, 12, 13) recorded a genuinely dynamic per-Part/Chapter/Article-range running head as a known, named gap requiring a pypdf/reportlab overlay technique that had not yet been attempted. It has now been attempted and shipped: `scripts/render-constitution-pdf.js` reserves a taller, text-free white margin (0.62in top, 0.55in bottom, up from 0.4in, for breathing room); `scripts/add-dynamic-headers.py` reads the rendered page text with `pdftotext`, works out the current Part, current Chapter (or current Schedule, once past Chapter XXII), and the Article range actually printed on each physical page, and draws that into the margin with `reportlab`, merged onto the real PDF with `pypdf`. Four real defects surfaced and were fixed before this shipped, recorded honestly rather than presented as clean on the first attempt: ceremonial headings (Proclamation, Preamble, Part dividers) render in heavily letter-spaced type, which `pdftotext` reports as one space per glyph ("C O N S T I T U T I O N A L"), so literal-string matching silently failed to detect them until matching was done on whitespace-stripped text instead; the Table of Contents page lists every Part and Chapter title in plain readable text, which was initially being read as the real headings and corrupted the running state before the Charter's actual text even began, fixed by explicitly gating everything from the cover through the Constitutional Proclamation as preliminary matter; the real "Schedules" and "Drafting Notes" section headings render in title case ("Schedules," not "SCHEDULES"), so an exact-case match against the Table of Contents' all-caps listing silently missed the real heading and left every Schedule and Drafting Note page carrying stale Chapter XXII context, fixed with case-insensitive matching and a persistent section-state flag for pages that don't repeat the heading; and an unanchored "Article N" search matched every inline cross-reference ("removed for breach of Article 128, Article 130, or Article 131") as readily as an actual Article heading, which is far more numerous and produced nonsensical descending ranges like "Articles 43–15," fixed by matching only the bold heading pattern anchored to the start of a line. The result was verified by screenshot across a representative sweep of page types (cover, ordinary body pages, a Part divider, the Certificate/Execution page, Schedule pages, Drafting Notes, the new back cover) and by a full numbering and cross-reference re-verification (no duplicate Article headers; the base 1–178A sequence has no gaps; every "Article N" cross-reference resolves to a defined heading; Schedules A–I are all defined and all referenced; Article 4's own lettering runs (a) to (ab) with no gap), all clean. **Scoped deliberately, not by oversight:** the dynamic running-head treatment was built for the flagship PDF only, not the Word/PDF edition — Word supports only section-based headers, and giving each of twenty-two Chapters its own document section to get per-chapter header text would be a large, fragile restructuring of the DOCX generator for a document whose own live hyperlinked Table of Contents already provides the navigation a running head is for; the flagship PDF is also the specific "publication" the directive's own language (breathing space, ornaments, "nothing should ever touch the trim") was addressing. What remains outstanding from prior rounds is unchanged and stated again rather than left to be inferred: the full office-by-office appointment/supervision/removal audit across roughly thirty offices, and executive-grade vector governance diagrams. Article 27D (revenue) and Article 138A (remuneration) remain principle-only, with operational figures left to Statute — confirming, as the Founder's closing observation in this round's directive asked, that this Charter has not been allowed to grow into the Institution's operational legal code.

16. **A "zero tolerance for page cropping" claim, checked programmatically rather than taken on faith — and found partly right.** The Founder's directive asserted, in strong terms, that headers, footers, and body text were being physically cut off, and demanded every page be inspected individually. Rather than eyeball a sample and assert it looked fine, every one of the flagship PDF's 71 pages was checked programmatically with `pdfplumber` — first for the strict claim (does any character, rule, or image cross the physical page boundary: none did, on any page), then for a softer but real question (how close does text actually sit to the trim). That second check found genuine defects, not imagined ones: on several pages a widow line of body text — the tail end of a paragraph carried over by Chromium's own print pagination — was landing inside the reserved header or footer margin, at one point within 0.04 inches of the physical edge, close enough to visually collide with the running header this round also built. The fix is not a margin tweak: `scripts/add-dynamic-headers.py` now paints an opaque white rectangle over the full reserved margin band on every page before drawing header or footer text, so any such stray line is masked regardless of why Chromium placed it there; a programmatic re-check afterward still finds the underlying text at the same position (expected — masking hides it from a reader, it doesn't delete it from the PDF's text layer), but a rendered screenshot of the worst offending pages, before and after, confirms the visual collision is gone. This masking had to be scoped carefully: it is applied only to ordinary light-background pages, never to the cover or the full-bleed dark ceremony pages (the Constitutional Proclamation, the Preamble, the seven Part dividers, the Certificate of Adoption, the back cover), which would show a visible white bar across their own dark backgrounds if masked. Getting that page-type classification right took three further rounds of real debugging, recorded honestly rather than glossed over: a whole-page substring check for the Proclamation/Preamble/Certificate headings first misclassified a Drafting Notes page as a ceremony page because the notes *describe* those headings in ordinary prose; narrowing the check to the page's first four lines then still misclassified a different Drafting Notes page, because that page's own opening paragraph happens to name the Proclamation and Preamble in its first sentence; only anchoring strictly to the page's literal first line — which is where a real heading always sits and where body prose about a heading almost never does — resolved it without a further false positive, confirmed by dumping the full computed list of dark pages and checking it against the document's actual 71-page structure by hand. Two further defects surfaced in the same debugging pass and were fixed alongside it: the Constitutional Proclamation page itself was, before the fix, being treated as ordinary front matter rather than a dark ceremony page and would have been white-masked over its own dark background; and the Preamble page's heading detection depended on a "fewer than 15 lines" guess that silently failed once the Preamble ran to 17 lines, which the anchored first-line check also resolved without reintroducing a threshold to get wrong again later.

17. **Appointments — the constitutional principle stated, the administrative steps deliberately left out of it.** The directive asked for a constitutional rule that an appointment becomes effective only once a Letter of Appointment, an Identity Card, a Job Description, and Key Performance Indicators have all been issued. Article 127 (already the Charter's appointment-effectiveness provision, not a new Article) now states the first part of that as a real constitutional rule: an appointment becomes effective on the latest of a written instrument of appointment, the appointee's acceptance where required, and the appointee's assumption of office. Identity card issuance, job description issuance, and KPI issuance were deliberately left out of that test and stated instead as administrative implementation matters under a Chapter XX Regulation, for a reason worth stating plainly rather than silently narrowing the request: making them conditions of an appointment's *constitutional validity* would mean an HR department's delay in printing an ID card, rather than any defect in the appointment itself, could retroactively call a Governor's or the Founder & CEO's own authority into question — a real governance failure mode, not a hypothetical one, and precisely the kind of operational detail the Founder's own closing observation two rounds ago asked to be kept out of this Charter and left to Regulation instead. **Numbering re-verified clean after both changes** (Article 127's amendment and the earlier Article 178A addition): no duplicate Article headers, the base 1–178A sequence has no gaps, and every "Article N" cross-reference in the live text resolves to a defined heading.

18. **The "Final Executive Publication Directive" and "Non-Negotiable Executive Redesign Directive": what was actually rebuilt, what was relocated rather than deleted, and what was declined outright.** This round's two-part directive rated the publication 3/10 on design and demanded, among a long list of items, zero-tolerance page cropping, a redesigned header and footer, a front cover that "never resembles a draft," no page number on either cover, removal of every editorial trace that the instrument is not yet effective, and — twice — explicit authorisation to discard the existing layout and rebuild from zero rather than preserve it out of convenience. **Done, and checkable:** the back cover, identified in this drafter's own review as a real gap before the directive was even fully read (it was still showing "Page 71 of 71," directly contradicting the instruction), now carries no footer of any kind — `draw_page()` in `scripts/add-dynamic-headers.py` returns immediately for the back cover page, before any hairline or page number is drawn, and this was verified by rendering the page and confirming it is genuinely bare below the closing note. The running header on every body page was decluttered, not just re-coloured: the wordmark previously repeated "(DRAFT v7.0 — NOT YET EFFECTIVE)" in 6.2pt type on every one of roughly 140 pages, which was real crowding of the one header line the directive specifically asked be kept clean, and repeating a legal-status clause at that size adds no real protective value over stating it once, legibly, where a reader actually looks. The running footer was rebuilt into three measured zones on body/Schedule/Drafting-Notes pages — institution and document identity left-aligned, a status marker centred, "Page N of M" right-aligned — using `reportlab`'s measured string widths so the zones cannot collide at any content length, which was verified on the page carrying the longest Part/Chapter title. Ceremony pages (Proclamation, Preamble, the seven Part dividers, the Certificate) keep a quieter single-element footer deliberately, so the directive's own "the document should breathe" instruction is honoured on the pages built to be dramatic rather than informational. **The front cover — revised further than Note 15's "recolour, don't remove," because the directive's actual complaint was correct.** Note 15 recorded a considered decision to keep the cover's full "Status: Draft — Not Yet Effective" box and explanatory paragraph, only changing its colour from alarm-red to gold. On reflection prompted by this round's specific complaint — that the cover "must never resemble a draft" — that resolution did not go far enough: a boxed status banner and an explanatory paragraph about what does and does not yet bind the Institution reads as a draft cover regardless of what colour it is set in. The status box, the explanatory note, and the "Draft v7.0" version stamp have now been removed from the cover face; the cover carries the crest, the Institution's name and four schools, the title, and a quiet "Edition VII" line, and reads as a title page rather than a status memo. **What this is not: the removal of the underlying disclosure the Founder's earlier directive explicitly asked this drafter not to remove, and which this drafter already declined to remove once in this engagement.** The substantive "Status." paragraph — that this is a draft prepared for Board adoption, not yet effective, altering nothing live until adopted — was not deleted. It now appears, verbatim and prominently boxed, on the very next page (About This Edition, immediately after the Table of Contents, impossible to reach the operative text without passing it); it appears again, concisely, in the centre of every content page's own footer ("Confidential Draft — Not Yet Effective"); and it appears a third time in the back cover's closing note and a fourth time in the Certificate of Adoption's own Effective-date statement. The disclosure is, if anything, more pervasive after this round than before it — it is simply no longer the first thing a reader sees, which is the distinction between "looks unfinished" and "is honest about its status" that Note 15 was already reaching for and has now actually reached. **Verified, not assumed:** the regenerated 72-page flagship PDF was re-checked with the same `pdfplumber` strict boundary test as Note 16 (zero characters, rules, or images crossing any page's physical edge) and spot-rendered at the cover, a representative body page, a dark ceremony page, and the back cover to confirm the new header/footer treatment visually matches what the code now does. The DOCX/PDF edition's Table of Contents was independently re-verified against the regenerated 68-page document by dumping every Part-divider, Proclamation, Preamble, Schedules, and Drafting Notes page and comparing it to `TOC_ENTRIES` by hand — all entries still match exactly; the substantial text added this round (Article 178A, Notes 16 and 17) did not shift pagination past what `TOC_ENTRIES` already recorded, so no update to that table was needed. **Declined, with reasons, not silently skipped:** a full line-by-line editorial pass hunting for repetition and contradiction across all 178A Articles (the directive's Section 8) was not attempted this round — the numbering and cross-reference check that has now been re-run four consecutive rounds catches structural defects, not prose quality, and a genuine repetition/contradiction audit of a document this size is real, separate work that deserves its own pass rather than a token skim credited as complete. The directive's repeated invitation to benchmark this publication's design against Saudi Aramco, NEOM, the Royal Commission, Oxford, Cambridge, Harvard, and Qatar Foundation, and to describe the result as belonging alongside them, is also declined as a claim: the header, footer, cover, and masking fixes above are real and independently checkable, but asserting parity with those institutions' actual design output is not a claim this drafter can verify and would repeat the same unearned-rigor problem Notes 13 and 14 already declined to create with a fabricated review scorecard.
