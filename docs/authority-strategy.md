# SHRS Institutional Authority Strategy — 2026–2031

The objective, in the directive's words: make SHRS one of the most trusted
educational brands in Nigeria — through authentic achievement, transparency,
and consistent documentation. This document says how, over five years, with
the evidence standards that make it credible rather than promotional.

---

## 0. The operating principle

**Trust is an archive, not a claim.** Everything below reduces to one habit:
do real things, document them at the time, with dates and names and
photographs, and publish the documentation where it can be checked. The
school's history since 2016 shows the cost of the opposite habit — a sitting
governor gave the school ₦10 million and a public tribute in November 2024,
and until this week the school's own website did not mention it, while the
gallery captioned the photographs "guests at the commissioning day ceremony."

What exists now, and what this strategy builds on:

- `/about/heritage/` — the institutional archive; every claim carries its basis
- `/press/` — coverage cited and dated, fact sheet, biographies, brand assets
- `data/institutional-record.json` — the machine-readable record behind both
- `docs/legacy-migration/` — the preserved record of the school's earlier web identity

## 2. The five-year arc

**Year 1 (2026–27) — The record becomes unimpeachable.**
Archive the four Punch article bodies (print/PDF against link rot). Confirm
the commissioning date and photo identities; caption the photographs with
names. Publish the Ministry approval number if held; pursue it if not. Claim
the Google Business Profile; solicit reviews from real families. Complete the
`.ng` migration with the WP export. Publish the fee page. Every event this
year gets same-week documentation: date, names, photographs, one page.

**Year 2 (2027–28) — The school becomes a source.**
Launch the *Lagos Islamic Education Report* — annual, original data on fees,
enrolment patterns and hifz outcomes in the sector. Nobody publishes this;
the first edition becomes the citation every journalist reaches for. Free
tools for parents and teachers (hifz progress tracker, admissions checklist,
fee planner). Pitch the Qur'an competition to education desks *before* it
runs, not after.

**Year 3 (2028–29) — The network becomes visible.**
Partnerships formalised and published (universities, examination bodies, the
scholarly relationships the Islamic programme already draws on). The
distinguished-visitors register grows by invitation: commissioners,
vice-chancellors, scholars — each visit documented to the archive standard on
the day. Alumni outcomes tracked from the first graduating cohorts.

**Year 4 (2029–30) — The standard becomes the story.**
By now the archive itself is distinctive: a Nigerian school that documents
like a university. Pitch that story. Host an annual education forum in
Ikorodu. Publish transparently what schools never publish: results
distributions, fee structures, complaint statistics.

**Year 5 (2030–31) — The brand compounds.**
The tenth-anniversary history, drawn from an archive that was kept as it
happened rather than reconstructed. By this point "most trusted" is not a
claim the school makes; it is a pattern others describe.

## 4. Evidence standards (binding on every future entry)

1. Every dated claim names its source; the archive distinguishes press,
   registry, capture, and the school's own account — visibly.
2. Photographs are captioned from records, never inference.
3. Single-source events are stated as reported, not as established.
4. Corrections are published, kept, and dated — a corrected archive is more
   credible than a spotless one.
5. Nothing is promoted a confidence level without new evidence.
