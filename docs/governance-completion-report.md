# SHRS Governance Completion Report

**Prepared:** Phase F, at the close of the autonomous Tier 1 + Tier 2
retrofit programme and the first pass of the Website Publication
Requirement.
**Audience:** Board of Trustees and Executive Management Team.

> **How to read this report.** Per the same discipline the
> Institutional Readiness Report (Phase D) was built on: success here
> is not measured by document count. It's measured by whether SHRS's
> governance now depends on systems rather than individuals — and, new
> in this report, whether what's published matches what's actually
> true. Both questions get an honest answer below, including where the
> answer is "not yet."

---

## 1. Total documents drafted

**26 files in `docs/policies/`**, of which:
- **22 are true policy/framework documents** (the kind meant to hit the
  full 13-section architecture): 19 policies plus the three redeveloped
  live policies (SW-06, SW-07, SW-08).
- **3 are evaluation frameworks** (HR-03, FN-02, IT-06) — a distinct
  genre by design: they assess *other* policies rather than governing
  conduct themselves, so the 13-section policy architecture doesn't
  apply to them the same way.
- **1 is the Policy Code Index** — a meta/tracking document, no policy
  code of its own.

Of the 22 true policy documents, **20 are now retrofitted to the full
13-section architecture** (Policy Information / Purpose / Scope /
Definitions / Policy Statement / Roles & Responsibilities / Procedures
/ Monitoring & Compliance / Records & Documentation / Related Policies
/ Exceptions / Appeals & Complaints / Review & Amendment) and
substantially deepened beyond their original content. **Two remain at
the original architecture**: the Constitution & Governance Charter
(GV-01) and the Designated Safeguarding Lead Framework (SW-02) — both
real, substantial documents, just not yet retrofitted in this pass.

The Governance Master Register tracks **66 rows** total (documents,
partial-coverage items, and named gaps across all ten institutional
categories), of which **28 are marked DRAFTED, EXISTS, or RESOLVED.**

## 2. Total documents published

**14 documents now physically live** on the public website
(`pages/policies.html` / `.ar.html`, both languages, verified built):
the original nine live policies (Assessment, Anti-Bullying, Career,
Dress Code, Equal Opportunity, First Aid, Health & Safety, Visitors,
Complaint) plus the newly published **Child Protection & Safeguarding
Policy (SW-01)** — the single highest-priority document, published
first to prove the pattern rather than spreading effort thin across
many partial publications.

**13 more documents are classified Public and queued for publication,
not yet physically added:** Academic Regulations, Student Code of
Conduct, Student Handbook, Boarding Regulations, Parent Handbook,
Admissions Policy, Emergency Response Plan, Data Protection & Privacy
Policy, Acceptable Use Policy, Records Retention Policy, AI Usage
Policy, Hifz Regulations, Ijazah Governance Framework. Each needs the
same treatment SW-01 just received: an accordion entry, a metadata
block, correct category placement, verified EN/AR translation, and a
rebuilt/tested site. This is named as real remaining work in the Policy
Code Index, not implied as done by this report.

**9 documents are classified Internal** (Staff Conduct Policy, Staff
Handbook, the three evaluation frameworks, Information Security Policy,
Financial Controls Policy, and the still-missing Recruitment/
Performance/Leave/Discipline/Grievance/Procurement/Incident-Response/
Cybersecurity policies) — not withheld by oversight, but because
publishing internal HR specifics or exact security/financial-control
parameters would be counterproductive, following common institutional
practice rather than an arbitrary default.

## 4. Outstanding approvals required

Nothing in this entire governance library is Board-adopted yet — every
document is a draft. In priority order:
1. **Appoint a Designated Safeguarding Lead.** Every safeguarding
   document assumes this role is filled; it is not. This is the single
   highest-leverage action available, costs nothing to start, and
   unblocks the most Governance Resolution Register items.
2. **Obtain the real fee schedule.** Unblocks three Finance documents
   simultaneously and closes the one gap the live website already
   discloses to the public.
3. **Board adoption of the Constitution & Governance Charter**, since
   every other document's Approval Authority column assumes its
   authority structure is in force.
4. **Legal/regulatory review** of the six items the Governance
   Resolution Register's Section 6 names explicitly (NDPA 2023
   compliance, Child's Rights Act applicability, SHRS's corporate form,
   a site-specific fire-safety assessment, the Ijazah scholars'
   documented *sanad*, Nigerian labour law for staff discipline).
5. **Resolve the two governance-roster title conflicts** (Adebayo's
   real title; the phantom "Vice-Principal Academics").

## 5. Governance maturity assessment

**Developing, trending toward Established for the family-facing core.**
The safeguarding, conduct, and academic document clusters are now both
deep (13-section, substantively expanded) and cross-referenced
consistently — verified, not assumed, since every retrofit in this
programme triggered a citation sweep that found and fixed real drift
(stale section references in the Staff Handbook, Records Retention
Policy, Boarding Regulations, First Aid Policy, and Financial Controls
Policy, across multiple passes). The governance *structure* — Board,
CEO, named roles — is real and consistently applied. What holds this
back from "Established": zero real-world operating history. No
document here has been tested against an actual Board decision, an
actual safeguarding case, or an actual academic appeal.

## 6. Operational readiness assessment

**Foundational to Developing, uneven by domain.** Safeguarding
documentation is the most complete in the library, but the DSL
appointment gap means none of it is operational yet — this remains, as
the Institutional Readiness Report already found, the single most
important line in the whole governance ecosystem. Academic operations
have real structure but two open numeric decisions (the promotion
threshold, academic-integrity consequences) that need setting before
next year's promotion decisions, not after a disputed one forces the
question. Financial operations have real process (segregation of
duties, approval thresholds) but zero amounts — the fee schedule gap
is a business decision, not a governance one, and no further drafting
advances it.

## 7. Digital-campus readiness assessment

**Foundational.** The Parent Portal and AI assistant are both real,
coded, and security-audited, with governance now retrofitted to match
what they actually implement (verified against `sql/schema.sql` and
the codebase, not assumed). Neither system holds real production data
yet — no database provisioned, no Anthropic API key configured — so the
governance is ahead of the operational reality, which is the right
order (govern before you scale, not after). The AI Usage Policy's
vendor/model-change-management and pre-launch-review additions (this
phase) mean the next AI touchpoint SHRS adds has a real process to
follow, not a blank slate.

## 8. Multi-campus scalability assessment

**Structurally sound, two same numeric gaps as before, now joined by a
publication-scale question.** The Phase D Scalability Stress Test's
findings still hold: the DSL Framework's per-campus scaling model
closes the structural safeguarding gap; the academic-integrity/
promotion thresholds remain the concrete open item. New in this
report: if SHRS's governance library keeps growing at this programme's
pace (26 documents now, 38 more tracked as gaps), the website
publication workload will grow faster than any single pass can clear —
the Policy Code Index's Publication Status classification is the tool
that should scale with it, not a one-time label.

## The continuity test, revisited

The Institutional Readiness Report asked: would SHRS run consistently
if the CEO, Principal, or Registrar changed tomorrow? This report adds
a second version of that question, specific to what changed this
phase: **would a family reading the published policies get an accurate
picture of SHRS's actual governance, or a partial one?** Today, the
honest answer is partial — the single most important safeguarding
document is now genuinely public and complete, but thirteen more
Public-classified documents exist only in the repository. That gap is
smaller than it was at the start of this phase, and the path to closing
it is now explicit rather than implicit: one document at a time,
verified each time, not batched and assumed correct.
