# SHRS Board Handbook

*A practical guide for members of the Board of Governors of Sultan
Hanafi Royal Schools: how the Board is composed, how its meetings and
committees run, what the Chairman and Head of Schools / Administrator
offices actually do day to day, and how a new Governor gets oriented.
This Handbook implements the Governance Charter and Policy GV-01 — it
does not add to them.*

## 2. Purpose and Scope

### 2.1 What this Handbook is

This Handbook exists to make the Governance Charter of Sultan Hanafi
Royal Schools ("the Charter") practically usable by the people who sit
on the Board of Governors ("the Board") from meeting to meeting. The
Charter — certified as the Institution's supreme governing instrument
under Article 2, and restated in policy form as GV-01 — establishes
*what* the Board is, *what* it may decide, and the outer limits of its
authority. This Handbook does not restate that constitutional content.
It answers a narrower, practical question: once a person is a
Governor, what do they actually do — how are meetings called and run,
how does a resolution get proposed and recorded, what do the Chairman
and the Secretary do, how do the Board's committees fit together, and
how does a new Governor get oriented into the role.

### 2.2 What this Handbook is not

This Handbook is an implementation and guidance document, not a
constitutional instrument. It creates no office, no power, and no
procedure the Charter and the Statutes made under it do not already
establish. Where this Handbook appears to say something the Charter
does not, the Charter governs (Charter Article 5; Article 156), and
this Handbook is wrong and should be corrected, not the other way
round. Every substantive rule in this document is cross-referenced
back to the Charter Article, the Policy GV-01 section, or the Statute
provision it comes from, precisely so that tracing is possible.

This Handbook also does not duplicate the Statute of the Board of
Governors, which governs Board meeting procedure in greater
operational detail (agenda-setting, virtual/hybrid meetings, minute-
taking mechanics) — see `docs/statutes/statute-board-of-governors.md`.
Where the two overlap, this Handbook summarises for orientation only.

### 2.3 Scope

This Handbook applies to every Governor — the Chairman, the Secretary
to the Board, and the three Other Members — and to any co-opted
Governor appointed under Charter Article 33. It does not extend to the
Management Team's own internal operation, which is a distinct body
under Charter Chapter IV; Section 9 below states only how the Board
relates to the Management Team, not how the Management Team runs
itself.

## 3. Board Composition

### 3.1 Constitutional composition

Under Charter Article 29 (as amended 2026-08-04), the Board's core
constitutional composition is five Governors: the Chairman; the
Secretary to the Board; and three further Governors styled "the Other
Members." The Board may, beyond this core, consist of up to eleven
Governors in total, including any Appointed or Co-opted Governors
(Article 29(c)–(d); Article 30). This restated the Board's former
composition as a "Board of Trustees" — the body is now the **Board of
Governors** throughout the Charter and every document beneath it.

### 3.2 Current holders, as at this Handbook's drafting

The table below reflects the real, current Board roster as published
on the Institution's governance page and as recorded in the Governance
Resolution Register (Category 8). No name is invented for a seat that
is genuinely vacant.

| Seat | Current Holder | Constitutional Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Chairman | Zakariya Olanrewaju Anofi (the Founder) | Article 10, Article 27F(d) — the Founder holds the office of Chairman directly, without requiring election under Article 39A, for as long as the Founder holds it |
| Secretary to the Board | **Vacant — to be designated** | Article 32A (a new core Board seat created by the 2026-08-04 amendment); tracked as an OPEN item in the Governance Resolution Register, Category 8 |
| Other Member | Mr. Lukman Anofi | Article 32(b), Article 30(b) |
| Other Member | Mrs. Lasisi-Ahmed Olayinka Idayat | Article 32(b), Article 30(b) |
| Other Member | Dr. Ismail Seriki | Article 32(b), Article 30(b) |

The Secretary vacancy is a genuine, tracked governance gap, not a
drafting placeholder. Per Article 32A, the Board shall designate the
Secretary at the earliest practicable ordinary meeting; until it does,
the vacancy is governed by Article 144 and does not affect the
validity of a Board proceeding otherwise properly constituted under
Article 39. No Secretary should be named anywhere — this Handbook,
Board papers, or public material — until the Board actually designates
one and that is recorded in the Authority Register (Article 16).

### 3.3 Vice Chair

The Board additionally elects a Vice Chairperson under Article 39A.
Because the Chairman is also the Head of Schools / Administrator (see
Section 4), Article 27F(d) requires that the Vice Chair be an
**Independent Governor** under Article 49D — someone who is not, and
has not within the preceding two years been, an employee of the
Institution, a member of the Management Team, or a close relative of
the Head of Schools / Administrator. The Vice Chair presides in the
Chairman's place on any matter concerning the Founder's own conduct,
remuneration, the Founder's revenue entitlement, a removal proceeding,
or a transaction in which the Founder has a personal interest (Article
27F(d); Article 39A). This Handbook does not record a current Vice
Chair holder; that appointment should be confirmed from the Authority
Register before being stated anywhere as fact.

### 3.4 How each seat is filled

| Seat | Appointing Authority | Term | Reappointment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chairman | The Founder directly, under Article 10/27F(d), while the Founder holds it; thereafter, Board election under Article 39A | Two years if elected (not applicable while the Founder holds it directly) | Renewable once, if elected |
| Secretary to the Board | Board, by simple majority resolution, on the Governance & Nominations Committee's recommendation (Article 32A) | Four years, as for any Governor (Article 34) | One further four-year term (Article 35) |
| Other Members | Board, by simple majority resolution, on the recommendation of the Governance & Nominations Committee or any Governor (Article 32(b)) | Four years (Article 34) | One further term (Article 35), save by unanimous resolution of the other Governors for a further term |
| Co-opted Governor | Board resolution, to address a specific expertise gap (Article 33) | Up to two years | Renewable once |

### 3.5 Qualifications

No person is appointed a Governor unless they are of good standing
with no conviction for dishonesty or an offence against a child, have
no undisclosed material conflict of interest with the Institution, and
have relevant experience in education, finance, law, governance, or
Islamic scholarship (Article 31). Not less than a majority of Governors
in office must be Independent Governors under Article 49D.

## 4. The Chairman and the Head of Schools / Administrator — two distinct offices, one holder

This is the central practical distinction the 2026-08-04 amendment
introduced, and the one a Board member most needs to hold clearly in
mind. There is no longer an office of "Chief Executive Officer" under
the Charter (Article 10) — it does not exist and this Handbook will
never revive it. The Founder, Zakariya Olanrewaju Anofi, presently
holds **two separate constitutional offices**, not one fused role:

### 4.1 Chairman of the Board of Governors

As Chairman, the Founder:

- Presides at every Board meeting (Article 39A);
- Settles the meeting agenda, in consultation with the Head of Schools
  / Administrator and the Governance & Nominations Committee (Article
  39A; Statute of the Board of Governors, Section 3.1);
- Represents the Board, as distinct from the Institution, in any
  matter concerning the Board's own governance (Article 39A);
- Holds first ceremonial precedence together with the Head of Schools
  / Administrator role (Article 20, Article 159) — but the Chairman
  role itself carries no vote-weighting: the Chair has no casting vote,
  and a tied resolution fails (Article 40).

### 4.2 Head of Schools / Administrator

As Head of Schools / Administrator, the same person:

- Is the Institution's chief executive and administrative office
  (Article 10(a));
- Directs the executive management of the Institution through the
  Management Team (Article 13);
- Chairs and leads the Management Team (Article 10(c), Article 57);
- Is, ex officio, a member of the Board (Article 12) — separately from
  holding the Chair;
- Holds defined reserved powers (appointment/removal of Management
  Team members and Constituent Institution Heads; ratification of
  Board committee chairs; a limited veto over resolutions materially
  affecting the Institution's Islamic character or founding mission)
  under Article 14, and a wider set of delegated powers under Article
  47 covering everything not reserved to the Board or requiring Board
  approval.

### 4.3 Why the distinction matters in practice

A Board member should never treat "the Chairman said so" and "the Head
of Schools / Administrator directed it" as interchangeable, even
though both currently describe the same person. Board governance
(agenda, presiding, recusal arrangements) is a Chairman function.
Running the schools day to day (management direction, academic and
religious oversight, appointments under Article 22) is a Head of
Schools / Administrator function, exercised through the Management
Team, not the Board. When the Founder's own conduct, remuneration, or
personal financial interest is the matter under discussion, **neither**
hat presides — the Vice Chair does, under Article 27F(d) (Section 3.3).

The Founder additionally holds a third, wholly separate status: **the
Founder's personal reserved rights** under Article 27A and Article
27C, which are not an office at all and are addressed separately in
Section 7.4.

## 5. Meetings, Quorum, and Resolutions

### 5.1 Meeting cadence

| Meeting Type | Minimum Frequency | Notice | Convened By |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary meeting | Not fewer than 4 times per academic year (Article 41) | Not less than 14 days' written notice; agenda and papers circulated not less than 7 days before | Standing calendar, settled by the Chairman |
| Extraordinary meeting | As needed | Not less than 7 days' notice (Article 42) | Head of Schools / Administrator, or not fewer than one-third of Governors then in office |
| Emergency resolution | As needed | No fixed notice — by unanimous written consent of all Governors, communicated by durable electronic means (Article 43) | Any Governor may initiate |
| Virtual/hybrid participation | Permitted for any meeting type, at the Chair's discretion or on a Governor's request (Statute of the Board of Governors, Section 4) | — | — |

An emergency resolution under Article 43 cannot be used for a
constitutional amendment, a matter reserved exclusively to the Board
under Article 45, or a change to the Institution's vision, mission,
motto, core values, or objects under Article 7 — those always require
a properly convened meeting.

### 5.2 Quorum

The quorum for any Board meeting is a simple majority of the Governors
then in office, provided the Head of Schools / Administrator (or an
Acting Head of Schools / Administrator) is present — save where a
meeting is convened solely to appoint an Acting Head of Schools /
Administrator, or to constitute an Ad-hoc Committee, while that office
is vacant (Article 39). A vacancy in a Board seat, including the
current Secretary vacancy, does not itself invalidate a proceeding
provided quorum is met from the Governors remaining in office (Article
38).

### 5.3 How a resolution is proposed, voted, and recorded

1. **Agenda item.** Any Governor may submit an item for the agenda in
   writing to the Secretary function not less than 14 days before an
   ordinary meeting (or 3 days before an extraordinary meeting). The
   Chairman includes it unless the Chairman records a specific written
   reason it is better addressed elsewhere (Statute of the Board of
   Governors, Section 3.2).
2. **Standing order of business.** Confirmation of quorum; apologies
   and conflict-of-interest declarations; confirmation of previous
   minutes; matters arising; committee reports; the Head of Schools /
   Administrator's report; matters requiring Board approval (Article
   46); matters reserved exclusively to the Board (Article 45); other
   business; date of next meeting (Statute of the Board of Governors,
   Section 3.3).
3. **The vote.** Each Governor has one vote. A resolution is carried
   by a simple majority of Governors present and voting, save for a
   constitutional amendment (two-thirds under Article 151, or four-
   fifths for Article 8 under the entrenched procedure at Article 157).
   The Chair has no casting vote; a tie fails (Article 40). An
   abstaining Governor counts for quorum but not for or against.
4. **Recording.** The Secretary function drafts the minute — recording
   attendance, declarations of interest, each resolution and its
   result, and any dissent or abstention with reasons if the Governor
   wishes — circulates it within 14 days, and it is confirmed (with any
   correction) at the next ordinary meeting (Statute of the Board of
   Governors, Section 6). Every resolution is also entered in the
   **Authority Register** (Charter Article 16), the Institution's
   permanent, append-only record of appointments, delegations, and
   Board decisions.
5. **Tracking open items.** The **Governance Resolution Register**
   (`docs/governance-resolution-register.md`) is the Institution's
   living tracker of unresolved decisions across every governance
   document — the place to check what remains open, including the
   Secretary vacancy, the vacant Online & Distance Learning School
   headship, and the un-named sixth Board-Level Committee slot
   (Category 8 of that register).

### 5.4 Confirming the Secretary function versus the Secretary office

Two different things share the word "Secretary" and should not be
confused. The **office of Secretary to the Board** (Article 32A) is a
full, voting core Board seat, currently vacant. The **Secretary
function** (Statute of the Board of Governors, Section 2.2(b)) is an
administrative role — meeting logistics, papers circulation, minute-
keeping — held by a person the Head of Schools / Administrator
designates, and may or may not be the same person as the Secretary to
the Board once that seat is filled. Until the Board seat is filled, the
administrative Secretary function continues on its own footing and
does not itself carry a Board vote.

## 6. Board-Level Committees

### 6.1 The layered architecture, briefly

The Charter establishes six distinct tiers of committee (Article 95):
Board Standing Committees (Board-Level Committees), Management-Level
Committees, Academic Committees, Administrative Committees,
Institutional Councils, and Temporary Bodies. This Section covers only
the first tier — fiduciary and constitutional oversight bodies of the
Board itself, chaired by a Governor, created or dissolved only by
constitutional amendment (save for the one further committee the
2026-08-04 amendment reserved for Board resolution — see 6.3).

### 6.2 The full Schedule of Board Standing Committees

| Committee | Remit | Chair | Membership | Quorum | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Educational Technical Committee** *(named directly by the 2026-08-04 amendment)* | Board-level oversight of academic and educational technical standards across all five institutions; considers strategic academic policy referred by the Academic Council or a Constituent Institution Head | A Governor appointed by the Board | Not fewer than 3 Governors | Majority of members | Termly |
| **Finance Committee** *(named directly by the 2026-08-04 amendment; continues the mandate previously styled the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee)* | Budget; financial oversight; internal audit; procurement oversight; risk management; investments; the institutional risk register | A Governor appointed by the Board | Not fewer than 3 Governors, none holding a Management Team role | Majority of members | Quarterly |
| Governance & Nominations Committee *(continued in being, not abolished by the amendment)* | Governor recruitment and induction; the Board skills matrix; Board self-evaluation; succession-readiness monitoring; Board seat vacancies | A Governor appointed by the Board | Not fewer than 3 Governors | Majority of members | Quarterly, and as required |
| Disciplinary & Ethics Committee *(continued in being, not abolished by the amendment)* | Governor and Management Team conduct; the institutional ethics/conflicts-of-interest register; final constitutional or procedural appeal | A Governor appointed by the Board | Not fewer than 3 Governors | Majority of members | As required, not less than termly |
| Safeguarding Committee | Child protection; student welfare; staff safeguarding compliance (Chapter XII of the Charter, in full) | Designated Safeguarding Lead (once appointed) | Per Chapter XII | Per Article 123 | Monthly, and immediately on any disclosure |
| **One further Board-Level Committee** | **Not yet named — to be fixed by Board resolution under Article 97** | To be fixed | To be fixed | To be fixed | To be fixed |

The five Board-appointed committees report to the full Board; the
Safeguarding Committee, chaired by the Designated Safeguarding Lead
rather than a Governor, reports to both the Head of Schools /
Administrator and the Board (Article 120(d), Article 97 Schedule). This
Handbook does not create, rename, or reassign any of these committees;
it reproduces the Charter's own Schedule for a Board member's quick
reference.

### 6.3 The one further Board-Level Committee

Charter Articles 44 and 97 reserve one further Board-Level Committee
slot, which the Board — not this Handbook — will establish, name, and
staff by ordinary resolution, without needing a further constitutional
amendment for that specific step. This is tracked as an OPEN item in
the Governance Resolution Register, Category 8. No name should be
assumed for it until the Board actually resolves one.

### 6.4 Reporting line back to the full Board

A Board-Level Committee's authority is recommendatory or oversight in
character, not independent decision-making, unless the Charter
expressly says otherwise (as it does, narrowly, for the Safeguarding
Committee's Article 120(b) suspension power). The Board, not the
committee, makes the underlying decision on a matter reserved under
Article 45 or requiring approval under Article 46; a committee chair
briefs the Board at each ordinary meeting, per the standing order of
business (Section 5.3).

## 7. Delegation of Authority

### 7.1 The general principle

Authority under the Charter flows downward through explicit, written
delegation — never by assumption (Charter Article 15; Article 7.1 of
Policy GV-01). A delegation is valid only where it names a specific
delegate, states a specific and limited scope, carries a mandatory
expiry date, and is recorded in the Authority Register. This Handbook
states what the Board reserves, what it may approve on referral, and
what is already delegated — it does not itself delegate anything new.

### 7.2 What the Board reserves, approves, or delegates

| Matter | Authority |
|---|---|
| Constitutional amendment | **Reserved exclusively to the Board** — Article 45(a); Chapter XVIII procedure |
| Approval of the annual budget | **Reserved exclusively to the Board** — Article 45(b) |
| Appointment/removal of the external auditor | **Reserved exclusively to the Board** — Article 45(c) |
| Merger, dissolution, or fundamental restructuring | **Reserved exclusively to the Board** — Article 45(d); Chapter XVII |
| Removal of the Head of Schools / Administrator from the Board | **Reserved exclusively to the Board** — Article 45(e); Article 23A (three-quarters resolution) |
| Transactions above the Board-fixed threshold | **Requires Board approval**, brought forward by the Head of Schools / Administrator or the relevant committee — Article 46(a) |
| Change to the Institution's objects | **Requires Board approval** — Article 46(b) |
| Appointment of Appointed Governors | **Requires Board approval** — Article 46(c); Article 32(b) |
| Adoption/amendment of Institution-wide policy referred by a Board committee | **Requires Board approval** — Article 46(d) |
| Confirmation of Management Team members and Constituent Institution Heads | **Requires Board confirmation** of a Head of Schools / Administrator nomination — Article 46A(b)–(c) |
| Executive, academic, administrative, and religious governance not reserved or requiring approval above | **Delegated to the Head of Schools / Administrator**, exercised through the Management Team, the Academic Council, and the Chapter VIII/IX offices — Article 47 |
| Approval of a change to the Institution's name, emblem, motto, objects, vision, mission, or core values | **Reserved personally to the Founder**, for as long as the Founder lives, whether or not the Founder then holds the Head of Schools / Administrator office — Article 27C(a) |
| Consultation (not a veto) before establishing/closing a Constituent Institution, or amending Article 8 | **Reserved personally to the Founder** — Article 27C(b) |

### 7.3 A specific instance versus surrendering the power

Where the Head of Schools / Administrator delegates a specific instance
of a reserved power under Article 14 (for example, a specific
appointment decision) to a named delegate for a limited scope and a
mandatory expiry date, this does not surrender the reserved power
itself — it reverts automatically once that specific delegation
expires (Article 14, final sentence).

### 7.4 The Founder's reserved rights, distinct from delegation

The Statute of the Founder's Reserved Rights
(`docs/statutes/statute-founders-reserved-rights.md`) operationalises
Article 27D specifically — the Founder's revenue entitlement — distinct
from the Article 27C list in the table above and from the Head of
Schools / Administrator's Remuneration under Article 138A. The
entitlement does not depend on the Founder holding any office, save
that it is automatically suspended if the Founder is removed from the
Head of Schools / Administrator office under Article 23A. That Statute
does not itself fix a rate or sum — an actual figure is a separate
Board resolution the Statute enables but does not supply, and this
Handbook does not invent one either.

## 8. Onboarding a New Board Member

The following checklist is drawn directly from Charter Article 48 and
the Statute of the Board of Governors, Section 7 — it adds no step
either source does not already require.

1. **Confirm eligibility** against the qualifications in Article 31
   (good standing, no undisclosed material conflict, relevant
   professional experience) before appointment proceeds.
2. **Appointment resolution.** The Board appoints an Appointed Governor
   by simple majority, on the Governance & Nominations Committee's
   recommendation where the Board is actively recruiting, or on any
   Governor's recommendation otherwise (Article 32(b)).
3. **Receive the induction pack**, arranged by the Governance &
   Nominations Committee, comprising: the Governance Charter in full;
   the Statute of the Board of Governors; the Board Code of Conduct
   (once that document exists); the minutes of the Board's four most
   recent ordinary meetings; the current skills matrix; and a
   safeguarding-obligations briefing from the Chairman or the
   Chairman's delegate (Statute of the Board of Governors, Section
   7.1).
4. **Complete the induction** before casting a first vote on any
   matter other than confirmation of the new Governor's own appointment
   (Article 48).
5. **Make the constitutional affirmation** — a written undertaking to
   uphold the Charter and act in the Institution's best interests, in
   Board-approved form, recorded in the Authority Register (Article
   170). Non-completion within 30 days does not invalidate the
   appointment but is reported to the Governance & Nominations
   Committee.
6. **Declare any interests** at the outset, for entry in the standing
   register of declared interests maintained by the Disciplinary &
   Ethics Committee (Article 132).
7. **Locate the standing references** every Governor should know where
   to find: the Authority Register, the Governance Resolution Register,
   the minutes archive, and the current skills matrix — not to
   memorise them, but to know where to look.

The Board additionally maintains a skills matrix recording the
collective expertise required under Article 31(c), reviewed not less
than annually by the Governance & Nominations Committee (Article 48),
and conducts an annual self-evaluation of its own governance
effectiveness (Article 49).

## 9. Conflicts of Interest and Confidentiality

### 9.1 Fiduciary duty

Each Governor owes duties of good faith, loyalty, and care to the
Institution — not to whoever nominated or appointed them — and must
act as they honestly believe most likely to promote the Institution's
objects (Article 49A). A Governor must not use the office, or
information acquired through it, for private advantage.

### 9.2 Declaring and recusing

Every Governor must declare, in writing, any material personal,
financial, or family interest in a matter before the Board, and must
withdraw from the discussion and any vote on that matter (Article 132).
This applies equally to the Head of Schools / Administrator on matters
before the Board (Article 24) and specifically to the Founder on any
resolution fixing, reviewing, or altering the Founder's revenue
entitlement (Statute of the Founder's Reserved Rights, Section 5.4) —
where the Founder also chairs the Board, the Vice Chair presides for
that matter (Article 27F(d)). A standing register of declared interests
is maintained by the Disciplinary & Ethics Committee (Article 132).

### 9.3 Confidentiality of Board proceedings

A Governor must not disclose the substance of a Board or Board
committee deliberation, or a document circulated in connection with
it, outside the Board — except as the Chair or a Board resolution
authorises, as law or a regulatory authority requires, to the
Governor's own professional advisers in confidence, or where necessary
to comply with the anti-abuse-of-authority or anti-financial-misconduct
duties under Articles 128 and 131 (Article 49C). This does not restrict
publication of decisions or documents the Charter or a Board
resolution designates public.

### 9.4 Collective responsibility

A decision lawfully made by the Board binds every Governor, including
one who voted against it or was absent — without prejudice to that
Governor's right to have a dissent recorded in the minutes, or to raise
a genuinely unlawful or Article 9A-implicated decision afresh in
accordance with the Charter (Article 49B).

## 10. Relationship to the Management Team

The Board governs; it does not manage the Institution's day-to-day
operations, which are the Management Team's function under Chapter IV
(Article 28). The Board holds the Management Team accountable, but
**through the Head of Schools / Administrator, not directly**: no
individual Governor may instruct a Management Team member directly —
proper instruction passes through a Board resolution to the Head of
Schools / Administrator (Article 56). The Management Team reports to
the Board, through the Head of Schools / Administrator, not less than
once at each ordinary Board meeting, on institutional performance,
risk, and any matter requiring Board approval (Article 55). This
Handbook does not restate the Management Team's own internal operation
in fuller detail — that belongs to the Statute of the Management Team
(`docs/statutes/statute-executive-management-team.md`), not to a
Board-focused document.

## 11. Amendment and Review of this Handbook

This Handbook is reviewed not less than annually, or immediately upon
any material change to the Board's composition, its committee
structure, or any Charter provision this Handbook implements —
including resolution of the Secretary vacancy, the naming of the
Board's sixth Board-Level Committee, or the filling of the Online &
Distance Learning School headship, each of which would require this
Handbook's Section 3 or Section 6 to be updated to match.

Amendment of this Handbook is by ordinary Board resolution; it does
not require the Chapter XVIII constitutional-amendment procedure,
because this Handbook is not a constitutional instrument and confers
no authority the Charter does not already grant. Where a proposed
amendment would, in substance, change what the Charter provides rather
than merely describe it more clearly, that change belongs in the
Charter or a Statute instead — consistent with Charter Article 166's
own principle that the Charter and its Statutes state authority, while
a document like this one states how that authority is used in
practice.
