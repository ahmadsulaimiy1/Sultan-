## 0 · Why this document exists

Every other academic subsystem at Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools has its own
specification: the Master Academic Structure Register for the institution as a
whole, the Graduation Programme Editorial Bible for the ceremony, editorial
bibles for the certificates and the prospectus. The School of Islamic and
Arabic Studies has never had one of its own — its facts live scattered across
the master register (§4c, §4e, §5b), the top-level editorial bible, two
regulation documents, and three live pages that do not fully agree with each
other. This document is that missing specification: one place where what is
real, what is proposed, and what is simply unknown are each stated as what
they are.

**The rule this document follows is the register's own: a fact is either (a)
real and cited to its source, or (b) marked as a gap or a proposal. Nothing
here is invented to fill a silence.**

## 1 · What is locked

- **Two curricula, one standard.** The Royal College and Basic School deliver
  the Nigerian National Curriculum. The School of Islamic and Arabic Studies
  and Qur'an College deliver curriculum content and materials drawn from Saudi
  Arabian sources. Both report to one Board, one Head of Schools /
  Administrator, and one disciplinary and pastoral framework. This is stated
  as institutional fact in `docs/editorial-bible.md` Part I, and is the
  project's oldest documented claim about the school — it is not something
  introduced by this document.
- **This is a curriculum-content claim, not an accreditation claim, and the
  distinction is deliberate.** `docs/policies/hifz-regulations.md` §13 states
  that no specific external Qur'anic-education accreditation standard is
  cited there — including any formal alignment with Saudi curriculum
  certification bodies — "because that alignment has not been verified in
  enough detail to state as fact." Read together, the two documents are
  consistent, not contradictory: SHRS states that it draws its Islamic and
  Arabic programme's content and textbooks from Saudi Arabian sources; it does
  not claim certification by any Saudi accrediting body. **Every future
  restatement of this fact — on any page, in any language — must preserve
  this distinction.** See §5 for where the live pages currently blur it.
- **The School of Islamic and Arabic Studies is open to the wider community.**
  Attendance does not require the school's seven-step digital admission
  process (`academics/arabic-islamic-studies/index.html`). It runs on a
  weekday/weekend timetable distinct from the day-school calendar.
- **Sultan Hanafi School of Islamic and Arabic Studies and Qur'an College are
  two distinct academic entities**, per the Board's own directive
  (`docs/master-academic-structure-register.md` §5b), never collapsed into one
  generic "Islamic Studies" label.

## 3 · Proposed divisional structure (not yet confirmed as taught)

Per the Board's directive (`docs/master-academic-structure-register.md` §5b),
the requested component areas for the School of Islamic and Arabic Studies
are:

- Arabic Language
- Islamic Studies
- **Fiqh**
- **Aqeedah**
- **Hadith**
- **Seerah**
- **Islamic Leadership**

The five in bold do not currently appear against any named faculty member's
teaching areas (§2 above), and do not currently appear anywhere on the live
curriculum pages. **This is the actual gap this specification exists to name:
the Board has requested a structure the published curriculum has not yet
carried through.** Closing it needs one of two things, neither of which this
document can supply on its own:

1. Confirmation that Fiqh, Aqeedah, Hadith, Seerah, and Islamic Leadership are
   taught today, folded into an existing faculty member's stated "Islamic
   Studies" area rather than broken out — in which case the faculty table in
   §2 should be corrected to say so explicitly, or
2. Confirmation that they are not yet taught as distinct components, and the
   Board's structure remains a target for a future curriculum phase — in
   which case the live pages should say "proposed," not present the narrower
   real list as the whole of it.

This document takes no position on which. That is a question for whoever
holds the School of Islamic and Arabic Studies curriculum — Principal Shaykh
Abubakr Solah, per the sourcing in §4 below — not a gap to be silently closed
by guessing.

## 5 · Two findings, recorded rather than smoothed

### 5.1 — An unsourced operational claim on three live pages

`academics/arabic-islamic-studies/index.html`, `curriculum/index.html`, and
`pages/curriculum-architecture.html` all state, in near-identical wording:
*"the syllabus for any year group, textbook titles and publishers included,
is issued to its families in full at the start of term."*

This project's entire documentation discipline is to cite a real source for
every claim or mark it a gap. This claim has neither. A search of the full
repository history for any syllabus, textbook list, or scheme-of-work
document — for any institution, not only this one — returns nothing. That
does not mean the practice is false; term-start syllabus packs are exactly
the kind of thing a school hands to families on paper without ever putting it
in a repository. But as written, three public pages assert an operational
process this document ecosystem cannot back up. **This is left as an open
question for the school to confirm, not resolved either way here.**

### 5.2 — Inconsistent claim strength for the same fact, on the same page

`pages/curriculum-architecture.html` (and its mirror in
`curriculum/index.html`) presents the Islamic/Arabic programme twice, at two
different strengths, without reconciling them:

- As **"The Saudi Arabian Curriculum Programme"**: *"Islamiyyah School and
  Qur'an College adopt the Saudi Arabian educational curriculum, with
  Saudi-approved textbooks and materials"* — a wholesale-adoption framing.
- Two sections later, under Islamiyyah School's own stage card: *"carefully
  selected Saudi Arabian resources and SHRS-developed materials"* — a hybrid
  framing, naming SHRS's own material alongside Saudi sources for the first
  time.

Both may be true at once — a school can draw its content and textbooks from
Saudi Arabia while also writing some of its own material — but the page does
not say that; it reads as two different claims about the same thing. **This
document recommends the hybrid framing (§5.2's second quote) as the more
accurate one, on the grounds that it is the only place either framing names
SHRS's own authored material** — but does not silently edit the live pages to
match, since the underlying fact belongs to the school, not to this document.

## 7 · Role structure this maps onto

Per `docs/teacher-operating-model.md` §3, the proposed **Arabic Language
Instructor** and **Islamic Studies Instructor** roles are a proposed split of
the existing single `ARB` subject/role code, each reporting at the Head of
Subject (Islamic and Arabic Studies) tier — mirroring the mainstream Subject
Teacher tier for this institution specifically. `docs/role-permission-matrix.md`
already carries `ARB` as **Proposed**, scoped to "own assigned classes,
Sultan Hanafi School of Islamic and Arabic Studies," mirroring `TCH`'s scope.
Once §3's structure is confirmed one way or the other, this is the role
scaffold any new Fiqh/Aqeedah/Hadith/Seerah/Islamic Leadership teaching
assignment should be recorded against, rather than inventing a new code.
