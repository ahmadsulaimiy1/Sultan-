# SHRS Certificate Editorial Bible

**Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools — Flagship Academic Credentials**
**Version 1.0 — 5 August 2026. Governing authority for all certificate design.**

Drafted as the consolidated judgement of a multidisciplinary standards
board: academic-credential designers, royal-charter and warrant
designers, luxury editorial designers, master typographers (Latin and
Arabic), Arabic calligraphers, Islamic geometric-art specialists,
security-document and banknote/passport print engineers, university
registrars, historic-manuscript specialists, foil-stamping and premium
print-production experts, and brand identity directors. Benchmarked
against the *qualities* of internationally respected credentials —
ancient-university degree certificates, royal charters, national
security documents — never against any single country's artwork.

Every rule below states its reason. A design decision that cannot cite
a rule in this document is not approved.

---

## 1. Design Philosophy

1.1 **A certificate is a permanent legal instrument, not a poster.**
It will be read in fifty years, photographed for employers, framed on a
wall. Therefore: restraint over spectacle, engraving over decoration,
whitespace over filling. *Why:* every historic credential that still
commands respect is quiet; every certificate that shouts ages badly.

1.2 **Prestige is carried by four things only:** typography, paper,
composition, and one or two moments of ceremony (the name; the seal).
Ornament is a frame for these, never a competitor. *Why:* the world's
finest degrees are typographically austere; their authority comes from
discipline, not richness.

1.3 **The institution speaks in ink; the graduate shines in gold.**
Institutional text is engraved flat ink. Metallic treatment is reserved
for the student's name (and nothing else in type). *Why:* gold spent
everywhere is gold spent nowhere — selective foil is precisely how real
foil-stamped documents budget the die.

1.4 **Two languages, one document.** Arabic and English carry equal
dignity — equal weight, mirrored placement — but never crowd each
other. Each zone pairs the languages once; nothing is said three times.
*Why:* bilingual duplication in every element is the single fastest
route to a cluttered, "local" look.

1.5 **The credential certifies completion, never performance.**
No grade, class, or mark appears on the certificate. Performance
belongs on the Official Transcript, Statement of Results, or
Testimonial. *Why:* international best practice; it makes the document
timeless, dignified, and re-issuable without embarrassment, and it
centres the credential on the institution and the graduate's identity.
(Client-mandated, non-negotiable, 2026-08-05.)

## 2. Colour System

2.1 Palette (absolute; no colour outside this table may appear):

| Role | Name | Hex | Budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | Espresso ink | `#221A10` | primary text |
| Foundation | Coffee brown | `#3A2A18` / `#4B3420` | frame, secondary text |
| Metal | Royal gold range | `#6E5013→#8C6516→#B8860B→#D4AF37→#F1E3B2` | ornament, rules, foil |
| Field | Warm ivory/parchment | `#FDF6E3` / `#F6EDD8` | paper |
| Accent | Crimson | `#7A1F2B` | ≤ 4 small touches per document |
| Accent | Navy | `#1F2A44` | ≤ 2 minimal touches |
| Forbidden | Any green/teal, pure black, pure white, silver-as-colour | — | never |

*Why:* the brand triad (brown/gold/ivory) is the institution's identity;
crimson and navy exist to give the gold something to be precious
against; green is expressly prohibited by the Founder. Silver may exist
only inside the holographic feature's optical shift, never as a design
colour.

2.2 **Ink beats gradient.** Text is flat ink except the name foil.
Gradients live in metalwork (medallion, plaque frame) only. *Why:*
gradient text at small sizes reads as screen decoration, not print.

## 3. Typography System

3.1 **English.**
- Display/engraved lines: **Cinzel** (Trajan-class inscriptional
  capitals), letterspaced 8–20% at small sizes. Never Cinzel Decorative
  (its swashes read ornamental, not institutional).
- Text and ceremony lines: **Cormorant Garamond**, italic for
  conferral phrases.
- Utility (numbers, URLs, microtext): **Inter**.
*Why:* inscriptional capitals + garalde text is the canonical grammar
of engraved credentials; decorative display faces are the canonical
grammar of certificate makers.

3.2 **Arabic.**
- Display and text: **Amiri** (Bulaq-revival Naskh) — titles in bold at
  display sizes with generous leading (≥1.6). Names in Amiri bold.
- Small labels: **Reem Kufi** (geometric Kufic) sparingly.
- **Ruqʿah-style faces are prohibited for titles** — ruqʿah is
  handwriting register, not monumental register.
*Why:* classical Naskh at scale is how prestigious Arabic academic and
state documents set their titles; the ultimate ceiling is commissioned
Thuluth calligraphy (see 3.4).

3.3 **Hierarchy of sizes (single page, A4 landscape):** exactly one
element may exceed 20pt — the student's name. Titles 14–20pt.
Institutional lines 9–14pt. Body 10–11.5pt. Legal/security 5–7pt.
*Why:* one unmistakable peak creates hierarchy; two peaks create noise.

3.4 **Calligraphy upgrade path.** When the school commissions a khattāt
(Thuluth) for «شهادة إتمام المرحلة الإبتدائية» and the institution
name, the artwork (SVG) drops into the reserved title slots unchanged.
*Why:* no typeface equals commissioned calligraphy; the layout must be
ready for it.

3.5 **Institutional names never break mid-name.** "Sultan Hanafi Royal
Schools" and «مدارس السلطان حنفي الملكية» are each set on one line,
sized to fit. *Why:* a broken institutional name reads as a layout
accident and diminishes the marque.

## 4. Layout, Grid, Whitespace

4.1 **Grid:** A4 landscape (297 × 209.5 mm); content field inset
≥ 16 mm from the innermost rule; internal 12-column grid; a single
dominant vertical centre axis. Masthead uses the client-mandated
architecture: national + institutional crests above the English titles
(upper left); award medallion + certificate-identity plaque above the
Arabic titles (upper right); main titles side by side (EN left,
AR right).

4.2 **Whitespace ≥ 40% of the content field.** *Why:* air is the most
expensive-looking material on paper; crowding is the defining feature
of low-grade certificates.

4.3 **Vertical rhythm (top→bottom):** masthead → title band →
conferral → NAME (peak) → citation → execution (signatures/seal) →
security footline. Every zone separated by measurable air; nothing
floats ambiguously between zones.

4.4 **Symmetry with one asymmetry.** The composition is mirror-balanced
around the centre axis; the only sanctioned asymmetry is the discreet
verification block in the footline. *Why:* classical documents are
symmetric; the QR is modern apparatus and belongs at the document's
administrative edge, not its ceremonial heart.

## 5. Ornament & Border System

5.1 **Border stack (outside→in):** hairline gold on the trim edge →
coffee foundation band → engraved arabesque strapwork band (gold on
coffee) → crimson pinstripe → wide ivory margin → fine double gold
rule. Corner medallions: eight-fold khatam rosettes, gold with a
crimson centre. *Why:* layered rules with a dominant dark band is the
engraved-frame grammar of charters; the wide ivory margin between band
and rule is what separates "engraved frame" from "clipart border".

5.2 **All ornament is constructed geometry** — parametric curves,
patterns, and stars generated as vectors — never stock images, emoji,
or AI-textured raster. *Why:* authenticity survives magnification;
decoration that dissolves under a loupe reads as fake.

5.3 **Density budget:** ornament may not enter the content field except
as sanctioned background (watermark, rosette under identity panels,
central guilloché field ≤ 5% opacity). *Why:* rule 4.2.

5.4 **Client-supplied artwork slot.** A premium background/border
supplied by the institution replaces the constructed frame layer only
after passing this document's review (palette 2.1, density 5.3, print
test 10). *Why:* provenance does not exempt artwork from standards.

## 6. Security-Document System

Modelled on genuine security-print practice (banknote/passport
engineering), simulated honestly for digital + premium-print output:

6.1 **Guilloché:** true epitrochoid line-work at hairline weight —
border wave bands on all four sides, a rosette *behind the certificate
identity plaque* and *behind the seal* (as banknotes back their numerals
and seals), and a large ≤5%-opacity central field. Never decorative
swirls drawn by hand. *Why:* real guilloché is mathematics; its
regularity is the security feature.

6.2 **Microtext:** a perimeter ring and a rule beneath the student's
name, each carrying the live serial number repeated, legible only under
magnification. *Why:* microtext that encodes the actual serial is a
verifiable feature, not a texture.

6.3 **Tamper-evidence:** HMAC-SHA-256 content hash (12-char display),
anti-forgery serial suffix, live QR verification against the
institutional database, and the bilingual void-if-altered clause set
small and calm in the footline. *Why:* the strongest security is the
live register; the printed apparatus exists to point at it — loudly
printed "security" is theatre.

6.4 **Blind-embossed seal:** colourless relief, circular engraved
lettering, crest in low relief, backed by a guilloché rosette. In
production this is a real embossing die; on screen it is simulated with
light only (no colour fill). *Why:* an emboss is felt more than seen;
tinting it destroys the illusion and the dignity.

6.5 **Holographic feature:** one small gold↔silver optical-shift strip
integrated with the identity plaque zone — never a rainbow, never
large. *Why:* OVDs on real documents are discreet; a large hologram is
a toy.

## 7. The Student Name (Centrepiece)

7.1 The name is the document's only foil-stamped type: layered metallic
gradient + foil grain texture clipped to the letterforms + engraved
hairline edge + raised-emboss shadow. English (Cinzel, the largest type
on the page) over Arabic (Amiri bold), flanked by restrained gold
flourishes, above a serial microtext rule. *Why:* the client mandate —
the name must read as an inscription on a medal — and rule 1.3.

## 8. Signature & Seal Architecture

8.1 Two signatories, mirrored left/right: **Registrar / المسجّلة** and
**Head of the School / رئيس المدرسة**. Rule, then title in engraved
caps, Arabic beneath. The seal stands centred between them. *Why:*
registrar + academic head is the canonical two-office execution of a
credential; the seal between them is the institution itself presiding.

8.2 Signature lines remain honest blanks until real signature artwork
is supplied; no simulated handwriting ever. *Why:* a fake autograph on
a legal instrument is a forgery invitation and reads as one.

## 9. Verification Integration

9.1 QR (brown-on-ivory, themed to the document), serial, hash, and
verify URL form one discreet administrative block in the footline
corner. The QR never exceeds 15 mm and never sits in the ceremonial
field. *Why:* rule 4.4; modern registrars place verification at the
foot, where a verifier looks for it and a viewer's eye does not.

## 10. Production Specifications

10.1 **Digital master:** A4 landscape PDF, vector throughout,
`preferCSSPageSize`, no content within 6 mm of trim.
10.2 **Premium print:** 250–300 gsm archival cotton or parchment-laid
stock, warm white; the ivory field prints as paper colour wherever
possible. Real production upgrades: hot-foil die for the name zone and
plaque edge; blind-embossing die for the seal. *Why:* the digital
design is engineered so each simulated feature maps 1:1 to a real
print process — nothing designed here is unprintable.
10.3 **Consistency:** every certificate of a family renders from one
template module; batch and single output are identical.

## 11. Accessibility & Integrity of Data

11.1 All personal data (names, IDs, dates) set at ≥ 7pt equivalent;
contrast of ink on ivory ≥ 7:1 for body text. *Why:* a credential that
cannot be read by a registrar's scanner or an older reader fails its
function regardless of beauty.
11.2 Every rendered value comes from the immutable issued record —
nothing on the face is computed at render time except layout.

## 12. Never-Do List

- Never green/teal, pure black, or pure white.
- Never display performance grades on the certificate (rule 1.5).
- Never break an institutional name across lines.
- Never Cinzel Decorative, ruqʿah-register Arabic titles, or novelty
  faces.
- Never metallic-gradient body text; foil is the name's alone.
- Never stock clipart, emoji, AI-texture raster ornament.
- Never more than one visual peak; never a QR in the ceremonial field.
- Never simulated signatures, fabricated honours, or invented seals
  presented as real ones.
- Never fill silence — whitespace under 40% fails review.

## 13. Quality-Control Checklist (every release)

1. Palette audit: zero colours outside §2.1.
2. Name-break audit: institutional names on single lines.
3. Peak audit: name is the largest type; nothing competes.
4. Whitespace ≥ 40%; ornament density within §5.3.
5. Grade absence: no performance wording anywhere on the face.
6. Bilingual parity: AR/EN mirrored weight in every zone; bidi order
   verified.
7. Security: serial in microtext matches the live serial; QR resolves;
   hash displayed matches record.
8. Magnification test at 400%: guilloché, microtext, emboss hold up.
9. Print test: A4-landscape PDF, one page, no clipping, background
   prints.
10. Both sexes' Arabic grammatical forms verified on real renders.
