## Part 2 — Phase 2, checked item by item before drafting

Your proposed list for Phase 2 was: Board of Governors Standing Orders, Board Committee Regulations, Executive Management Team Charter, Shariah Advisory Council Charter, Quality Assurance Charter, Internal Audit Charter, Risk Management Charter. I checked each against what the Charter actually establishes (Chapter XI, Articles 95–125, and the Schedule of Offices at Article 81) before treating any of them as real, undone work.

| # | Item you proposed | What I found | What I recommend |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Board of Governors Standing Orders | **Already done.** This is exactly what the Statute of the Board of Governors is — Article 163 of the Charter names "a Statute of the Board of Governors" as its own worked example of this instrument type, and the Statute already covers agenda-setting, meetings, conduct of business, minutes, and induction. A separate "Standing Orders" document would duplicate it under a different name. | No further drafting. |
| 2 | Executive Management Team Charter | **Already done**, same reasoning — the Statute of the Executive Management Team. | No further drafting. |
| 3 | Board Committee Regulations | **Real, needed, and already flagged.** The Statute of the Board of Governors says explicitly (its own Section 1.5) that individual committee terms of reference are deliberately *not* covered there, and are left for this document. Nothing like it exists yet. | **Draft this. It's the one genuinely open item from your original seven.** |
| 4 | Shariah Advisory Council Charter | **The body doesn't exist in the Charter.** Chapter XI's four Board Standing Committees, five Executive Committees, and two Institutional Councils are an exhaustive, named list (Articles 96, 100, 104) — none of them is a Shariah Advisory Council. The closest body is the Religious Affairs Coordination Council (Article 78), which is an *internal* Mudeer/Ra'ees coordination body, not an external Shariah-scholar advisory body of the kind "Shariah Advisory Council" usually means. | **Cannot be drafted as a Charter/Statute yet without inventing an office the Charter doesn't establish** — that would violate Article 163's own constraint. The lawful path is Article 109 (Advisory Council): the Board or EMT can establish one by resolution, without constitutional amendment, whenever they actually want it. I'll flag this for your decision rather than draft ahead of it — see Part 4. |
| 5 | Quality Assurance Charter | Quality Assurance is a real Executive Support Office (Article 83), not a committee — it reports to the Founder & CEO through the EMT. | **Real, but it's a Regulation, not a "Charter."** I'll draft it as the Quality Assurance Office Regulation (see Part 3, it belongs with the other office Regulations) rather than inventing a "Charter" tier the Charter doesn't use for offices. |
| 6 | Internal Audit Charter | Internal audit is explicitly part of the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee's mandate (Article 97's own Schedule) — there is no separate Internal Audit body. | **Folded into the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee's own Statute** (see below) rather than drafted as a standalone document for a body that doesn't separately exist. |
| 7 | Risk Management Charter | Same finding — risk management and the institutional risk register are also the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee's mandate (Article 134). | Same disposition — folded into that Committee's Statute. |

**What this means concretely for Phase 2:** three of your seven items are already done, two (Internal Audit, Risk Management) collapse into one real document, one (Shariah Advisory Council) needs your decision before it can be drafted at all, and one (Board Committee Regulations) is real, needed, and ready to draft now. The actual Phase 2 document list, after checking, is:

1. **Board Committee Regulations** — covering the terms of reference for all four Board Standing Committees (Governance & Nominations, Finance/Audit/Risk, Safeguarding, Disciplinary & Ethics) in one instrument, since Article 97 treats them as one Schedule.
2. **Statute of the Finance, Audit & Risk Committee** — the natural home for what you called "Internal Audit Charter" and "Risk Management Charter," since both are that one Committee's mandate, not two separate bodies.

Two documents, not seven — because five of the seven were either already finished or not real gaps once checked against the Charter's actual text.

## Part 6 — How I'll continue, document by document

The same discipline used for every phase so far, repeated for each document below:

1. **Check before drafting.** For each document, re-verify its Charter cross-references and confirm it isn't already satisfied by something in the library, exactly as this note did for Phase 2 and 3.
2. **Draft one document at a time**, in the dependency order below — never several in parallel — so each one is audited before the next depends on it.
3. **Audit every document before moving on**: legal (every cross-reference checked against actual current text, not memory), governance (no new power or office invented beyond what the Charter or a prior Statute already establishes), editorial (no undefined terms, no duplication of an existing instrument), publication (consistent formatting and classification).
4. **Commit and push after each document**, with a status note appended to this file so the picture stays current rather than requiring you to re-derive it from chat history.
5. **Deliver each document to you as it completes**, not in one large batch at the end — the same pattern as Phase 1A, 1B, and Phase 1's three Statutes.
