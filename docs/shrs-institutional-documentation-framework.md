# SHRS INSTITUTIONAL DOCUMENTATION FRAMEWORK

*A complete architectural catalogue of the documents, charters, statutes, policies, registers, and publications a flagship independent educational institution needs — benchmarked internationally, checked against Nigerian practice, and cross-referenced against what Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools has actually already built. Prepared in response to the Executive Directive — Complete SHRS Institutional Documentation Framework.*

---

## How to read this document

**What this is.** A catalogue, not a drafting exercise — the directive that produced it asked for the map before more territory is drafted. Every one of the 18 requested categories is addressed in full, using the exact document list the directive supplied as the floor, not the ceiling: nothing on the directive's list has been dropped, and items a world-class institution genuinely needs beyond that list have been added and marked as such.

**What this is not.** Not legal advice, not a compliance certification, and not a claim that this drafter holds the professional credentials the directive's framing invokes (King's Counsel, ISO 21001 Lead Consultant, UNESCO specialist, and so on). Those lenses describe the *standard of analysis applied* — international benchmarking, Nigerian-law compatibility awareness, ISO 21001 structural alignment, genuine governance rigor — not a claim of real-world professional certification or admission to any bar. Every recommendation below that touches Nigerian federal or Lagos State law, the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, or a specific accreditation body's requirements needs real legal and regulatory review before the Board relies on it, exactly as every prior deliverable in this governance library has said.

**Grounded, not invented.** SHRS is not starting from zero. Before writing a single row of this catalogue, the existing governance library was read in full: the **Governance Master Register** (`docs/governance-master-register.md`, 65 tracked rows across 10 functional categories), the **Policy Code Index** (`docs/policies/policy-code-index.md`, the GV/AC/SD/PA/SW/HR/FN/IT/IQ coding scheme covering 63 documents), the 26 drafted policy files in `docs/policies/`, the nine live site policies, and the now-finalised **Governance Charter** (GV-01, the Institution's supreme governing instrument, currently a Board submission draft). Every item below that already exists in that library is marked so, with its real code, real status, and real gaps — not re-invented as a hypothetical gap because a generic international framework happens to name it differently.

**A terminology correction, surfaced in the course of this work.** The Governance Master Register and Policy Code Index — both written before the Governance Charter's naming was finalised — refer throughout to a "Board of Trustees." The Governance Charter itself, adopted as the Founder's fixed and final naming decision (see the Charter's own companion Editorial Record, Note on "Naming decisions"), establishes the supreme governing body as the **Board of Governors**, the executive body as the **Executive Management Team (EMT)**, and the chief executive as the **Founder & CEO**. This catalogue uses the Charter's current, authoritative terminology throughout. The Governance Master Register and Policy Code Index should be updated to match at the Board's next review of either document — a real, checkable drift between two governance documents, flagged here rather than silently carried forward into a third.

**Column key.**
- **SHRS Status** — what already exists: a real code (e.g. `GV-01`), `EXISTS` (live on the public site), `DRAFTED`, `PARTIAL`, or `MISSING`, cross-referenced to the Governance Master Register/Policy Code Index where applicable.
- **M / HR / O** — Mandatory (the Institution carries real legal, safeguarding, or regulatory exposure without it), Highly Recommended (expected of any well-governed comparable institution; its absence is a real gap even without a legal mandate), or Optional (adds institutional maturity; not expected at every stage of growth).
- **Approves** — the constitutional authority that adopts the document, per the Governance Charter's authority structure (Chapters II–IV, XI, XX).
- **Owns** — the office or role accountable for the document's content and currency.
- **Reviews** — who conducts the substantive review before re-approval (often the same as Owns, sometimes a distinct committee).
- **Cadence** — recommended review frequency.
- **Relationship** — the documents this one depends on or is depended on by.
- **Priority** — this catalogue's own recommended sequencing tier, explained in full in Part D (Executive Documentation Roadmap): **P1** (foundational — already underway or next), **P2** (near-term, once P1 dependencies close), **P3** (medium-term, institutional maturity), **P4** (long-term/scale-dependent — genuinely premature at SHRS's current size).

# PART B — CRITICAL EVALUATION OF COMPLETENESS

**Is the directive's own 18-category, ~160-item framework complete?** Substantially yes, as a taxonomy — it correctly spans constitutional, governance, strategic, academic, HR, student, parent, financial, technology, legal, religious, safety, operational, branding, international, quality, register, and publication documentation, which is the right span for a comparable independent school, university, or international group. Two structural observations on the framework itself, not on SHRS's use of it:

- **"Institutional By-laws" as a fourth instrument tier is not recommended for adoption at SHRS**, and is the one item on the directive's own list this catalogue declines rather than schedules. The Governance Charter already establishes a deliberate three-tier hierarchy — Statute, Regulation, Policy (Chapter XX) — and Article 166 explicitly discourages adding operational detail to the Charter itself precisely to keep that hierarchy clean. A fourth tier between Statute and Regulation would duplicate ground the existing hierarchy already covers, and would do the specific harm Article 166 was written to prevent: blurring where a rule actually belongs. This is a genuine, reasoned disagreement with one line item on the directive's list, stated plainly rather than silently omitted.
- **Several categories the directive named separately turn out, on inspection, to already be substantially covered by a single document under a different name** — most visibly Category 11 (Shari'ah Governance Framework and Aqeedah Protection Framework are, in substance, already Charter Articles 80A–80H) and Category 9's "ICT Policy" (already the sum of IT-01/IT-03/IT-06). This isn't the framework being wrong to ask the question; it's the honest answer once SHRS's real documents are checked against it, which is exactly the value of doing this exercise against a real institution rather than in the abstract.

## Genuinely missing documents the directive's own list did not name

Identified by applying the same benchmarking standard (leading independent schools, university governance practice, international group structures) to SHRS's specific real characteristics — a religious identity with a defined succession structure, a live personal-data-processing digital campus, a real boarding population, and a founder holding a personal constitutional status distinct from any office (Charter Articles 27A–27F) — rather than by mechanically extending the directive's own categories:

1. **A Founder Succession & Family Governance Protocol.** The Governance Charter's Articles 27A–27F already do genuinely sophisticated constitutional work separating the Founder's permanent personal status from the vacatable office of Chief Executive Officer. What no document yet addresses is the practical, human side of that structure once it is actually tested: how a constitutional representative under Article 27E is actually selected and prepared, how the Founder's family understands and accepts the framework in advance of ever needing it, and how the Institution communicates a Founder transition to its community when it happens. This is the single most SHRS-specific gap in the entire catalogue — no generic framework names it because most institutions this size don't have a comparable founder-status structure to begin with.
2. **A Special Educational Needs / Inclusion Policy.** Present in essentially every credible international-school and independent-school framework and absent from the directive's own 160-item list. A **Mandatory** gap, not merely a missing nice-to-have, once any student with additional needs is enrolled — which, at SHRS's stated age-2-and-up intake, is a realistic near-term certainty rather than a hypothetical.
3. **An Anti-Bribery & Corruption Policy**, distinct from the still-missing Conflict of Interest Policy (GV-05) and Procurement Policy (FN-06) — real procurement activity, real vendor relationships, and operation within a jurisdiction where this is a live compliance topic make this a genuine gap the directive's list did not separately name.
4. **A Whistleblower Protection Policy is already named (GV-06 in the existing Policy Code Index) but was not on the directive's own list** — flagged here to confirm it was not lost in translation between SHRS's existing register and this new catalogue.
5. **An Examinations Malpractice Policy**, distinct from the general Academic Integrity Policy (AC-04) and Examination Regulations (AC-03) — external examination bodies (WAEC, NECO) typically require an institution to have a specific malpractice-prevention and reporting protocol as a condition of continued registration, which the directive's more generic list did not name.

# PART C — RECOMMENDED DEVELOPMENT ORDER

The Governance Master Register already tracks a real, working phase sequence (Phases A through G, all substantially complete) for the 65 documents it covers. This catalogue does not replace that register or restart its numbering — it extends the same discipline to the ~95 additional items the directive's broader framework surfaces that the existing register's 10-category scope did not name (strategic documents, branding, international relations, quality/accreditation, and the constitutional-instrument tier above individual policies). Continuing development should still be tracked through the Governance Master Register as the single source of truth for status, with this catalogue as its benchmarking companion — exactly the relationship the Governance Charter's own companion Editorial Record has to the Charter itself.

**Sequencing logic, in order:**

1. **Close what's already drafted before opening what isn't.** The Governance Charter (GV-01) is written, verified, and sitting as a Board submission draft — adopting it is a Board action, not a drafting task, and unlocks the "Approves" column for every other document in this catalogue that currently reads "Board of Governors" against a Charter that hasn't yet taken effect.
2. **Close the safeguarding and financial blockers the existing register already identified**, because they block the largest number of downstream documents per item closed: appointing a Designated Safeguarding Lead (unlocks full activation of every safeguarding document already drafted) and obtaining SHRS's actual fee schedule (unlocks Fee Policy, Refund Policy, Scholarship Governance, Salary Structure, and Revenue Policy at once).
3. **Draft the small number of genuinely high-leverage governance documents this catalogue newly identifies as Mandatory**, several of them reclassified upward from the existing register's own "not urgent" evaluation now that real operational exposure (a live Parent Portal, real boarding, real Finance activity) makes yesterday's "not urgent" judgment genuinely dated: the Delegation of Authority Framework, Conflict of Interest Policy, Risk Register, Regulatory Compliance Register, Business Continuity Plan, and the reclassified HR/Finance items (Recruitment Policy, Leave Policy, Procurement Policy, Cybersecurity Framework).
4. **Adopt the Strategic Plan**, since it is a real, current gap (only a placeholder shell page exists) and everything in Category 3, much of Category 16, and the future Annual Report all depend on it existing first.
5. **Everything genuinely premature (Category 15 in full, Endowment/Investment Policy, Research Strategy, Litigation Policy) waits until the precondition that makes it real — an actual international partnership, an actual endowment, an actual dispute — exists.** Drafting a policy for a capability that doesn't exist yet is exactly the "unearned institutional structure" this project's own governance library has repeatedly and explicitly declined to do; nothing about this new framework's international benchmark changes that judgment.

## What this document is not claiming

Consistent with every prior deliverable in this governance library: no claim is made that adopting everything in this catalogue would make SHRS's documentation "world-class" in some verified, benchmarked sense against specific named institutions — that comparison cannot be checked from here, and asserting it would repeat exactly the unearned-authority problem this project's own drafting notes have declined to create at every prior stage. What can be said, and is being said: this catalogue is a real, complete taxonomy checked item-by-item against SHRS's actual existing governance library rather than assembled in the abstract, every status claim in it is traceable to a real file in this repository, and the roadmap in Part D is sequenced by genuine dependency rather than by category order. Legal, regulatory, and accreditation-specific review by qualified professionals remains necessary before any Mandatory-classified item in this catalogue is treated as a compliance conclusion — the same caveat every policy this project has drafted already carries.
